## MUST-ADD Items (ship-blocking gaps)

### MUST-ADD 1: Slocum's VI Corps division — the reinforcement is misidentified
**Gap:** The draft says McClellan sent "two brigades from Sumner's II Corps." This is incomplete and arguably inaccurate as the primary reinforcement. The pack's own §4 note says "Meagher's Irish Brigade and French's brigade" from Sumner; but the web research confirms that the larger reinforcement was Slocum's *division* from the VI Corps, which crossed Alexander's Bridge and fed two brigades (Taylor's and Bartlett's) directly into Porter's line — a bigger contribution than the two Sumner brigades. The draft's "Meanwhile in Washington" box names Slocum once (from §5 of the pack, context of the pursuit) but never explains his role *during* the battle. The narrative as written gives the reader the impression only a trickle of help arrived; in fact a full division crossed over. The SELECTIVE story can keep it brief, but it must be accurate: McClellan sent *both* Sumner brigades *and* Slocum's division — arriving too little too late, but still a larger commitment than the draft implies. **Risk: reader carries a wrong factual model of the reinforcement.**

### MUST-ADD 2: Longstreet's role in the final assault is invisible
**Gap:** The draft names Longstreet as one of Lee's commanders (§6 of the pack lists him clearly) but he does not appear in the narrative at all. Web research confirms: Longstreet's division attacked on Hood's right in the 7 p.m. assault; Pickett's brigade (part of Longstreet's division) contributed to the breakthrough on Hood's right flank. The pack's §4 note explicitly says "The 4th Texas's charge was supported on Hood's right by Brig. Gen. George Pickett's brigade of Longstreet's division." The draft presents the breakthrough as Hood alone, which is not wrong but is incomplete — it erases a key commander. A SELECTIVE telling need not dwell on Longstreet, but at minimum the 7 p.m. assault description should acknowledge the full Confederate front (Longstreet's sector included) rather than reducing it to Hood. **Risk: draft makes Hood the whole story when the coordinated assault was the point.**

### MUST-ADD 3: McClellan's pre-battle order — he told Porter to fight a rearguard, not a battle
**Gap:** The pack (§3, "McClellan's Pre-Battle Decision") states that before dawn on June 27 — before a shot was fired — McClellan had already issued orders to fall back and told Porter to bring his corps across the Grapevine Bridge once the attack developed. Porter was not fighting to hold; he was already in a designated rearguard posture. The draft implies McClellan decided to retreat *after* the battle broke his nerve further; in reality he had decided the night before. This is a meaningful factual inversion: the battle that "convinced" McClellan to retreat was actually the battle McClellan had already planned to retreat from. The censor telegram and the Pinkerton box are excellent, but they don't carry this point — the reader needs to know the retreat was decided *before* the battle to understand McClellan's psychology correctly. **Risk: draft implies the battle caused McClellan's breakdown; the truth is the breakdown preceded the battle.**

## SHOULD-CONSIDER Items (quality additions, not ship-blockers)

### SC-1: Name Magruder and Huger in the bluff
The draft describes the screen south of the Chickahominy ("a thin screen of troops") but never names the commanders. Web research confirms it was Magruder (~25,000 men, many green recruits) and Huger opposite McClellan's ~67,000. Magruder had just pulled the same theatrical bluff on the Peninsula itself. Naming him here (one clause) gives the bluff a face and a callback — Magruder repeating his con — which is exactly the kind of human story detail that makes the strategy vivid. Low word cost.

### SC-2: The balloon detail
The pack (§5) flags that Gaines' Mill was the first battle where observation balloons were used by *both* sides simultaneously. The Confederate balloon was made of ladies' dress silk and filled with coal gas; the observer was E.P. Alexander — the same artillery officer whose post-war quote is already in the draft. The draft already uses Alexander as a narrator-voice; connecting him to the balloon adds a sly layer (the man who witnessed the battle from both above and on the ground). This is a genuine period curiosity. Good for one Meanwhile box or a single sentence in the setup. Not ship-blocking if omitted; worth adding if space allows.

### SC-3: Butterfield / Taps origin
Dan Butterfield earned his Medal of Honor at Gaines' Mill (seized the flag of the 83rd Pennsylvania under fire to rally troops). During the army's recovery at Harrison's Landing — the very retreat this battle caused — he composed "Taps" as a replacement for the three-volley burial salute. The draft's aftermath ends at the censored telegram; the Harrison's Landing/Taps thread is a resonant coda available in the pack (§5 notes Butterfield/Taps). A SELECTIVE telling doesn't require it, but it's a striking irony: the battle that broke McClellan's army also gave the army the bugle call it would bury its dead by for the rest of the war and ever after.

### SC-4: Porter's court-martial and 23-year vindication
The pack (§5) has the full Porter aftermath: court-martialed November 1862 (for Second Bull Run, not this battle), cashiered January 1863, vindicated by the Schofield commission 1878, commission restored 1886. The draft ends with "Lee had his first victory." A one-sentence epilogue on Porter — the man who ran a near-perfect defense, was later scapegoated for a different disaster, and spent 23 years fighting to clear his name — is emotionally resonant and tells the reader something real about how the Union Army ate its own. Genuinely SHOULD-CONSIDER; would lift the section.

### SC-5: "Four narrow bridges" — specificity in the withdrawal
The draft says Porter "pulled his battered corps south across the narrow Chickahominy bridges" (plural). The pack (§9) gives the more specific detail: *four* narrow bridges, and names the Grapevine Bridge as the one McClellan specifically referenced in his pre-battle order to Porter. Adding "four" and naming the Grapevine Bridge costs two words and rewards careful readers.

### SC-6: Alternate name "First Cold Harbor" — one-sentence note worth dropping in
The battle is also known as First Cold Harbor (Confederate usage), distinguishing it from the catastrophic June 1864 battle a mile away. The pack (§10) has this. The draft uses "Gaines' Mill" throughout. A parenthetical mention — once — anchors the reader if they encounter the alternate name elsewhere and is a grace note for a SELECTIVE telling that aims to be the reader's complete orientation.
