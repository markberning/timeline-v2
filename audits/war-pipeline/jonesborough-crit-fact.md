# Jonesborough — GATE 1: FACT-CHECK

**Verdict: PASS (with 2 SHOULD-FIX). No MUST-FIX.**
Web-verified against Wikipedia (Battle of Jonesborough, Battle of Atlanta, Ebenezer
Creek, 1864 election), GPB/Parten, LOC blind-memo sources, and Castel secondary
coverage. The draft is unusually disciplined: every load-bearing number is a range,
the only printed quote is the verified four-word core, and the [VERIFY]-gated items
are handled exactly as flagged. The two high-risk items both pass.

## HIGH-RISK ITEM (a) — Sherman's telegram

**VERIFIED.** The draft prints ONLY `"Atlanta is ours, and fairly won."` inside
quotation marks, attributed to Sherman (North), described as "the telegram… To
Washington, in the first days of September."
- Four-word core: **VERIFIED** verbatim against [WIKI-ATL] and multiple secondaries.
- The draft does NOT assert a hard date (Sept 2 vs 3), does NOT assert recipient
  (Lincoln vs Halleck/War Dept), and does NOT print the "So" prefix. This is exactly
  the discipline the fact pack §7/§10 demanded.
- Best available evidence: [WIKI-ATL] = **Sept 3, to Washington**; Wikiquote =
  Sept 2 to Lincoln; the draft's "first days of September / to Washington" is true
  under every reading. **No assertion to correct.**
- Slocum occupying Atlanta Sept 2 and sending first word: **VERIFIED** (mayor
  surrendered the city to a Slocum staff officer Sept 2; Slocum's XX Corps was the
  left-behind force). Draft's phrasing is accurate. SHOULD-FIX-minor: the surrender
  was tendered by Mayor Calhoun to a Slocum staff captain; "the Union walked in /
  occupied Atlanta" is fine, not wrong.

## HIGH-RISK ITEM (b) — Ebenezer Creek timeline framing

**VERIFIED — framing is correct, NO timeline blur.** This was the highest-risk
trap and the draft clears it cleanly.
- The draft introduces Davis at Jonesborough (§3) with a forward-pointing flag:
  "we will be hearing his name again before this story ends, for reasons that turn
  the stomach" — a *promise of a later event*, not a same-battle event.
- The payoff (§5) is explicitly dated and explicitly relocated in time and place:
  **"Three months after Jonesborough, on December 9, 1864, during the March to the
  Sea, that same general…"** — VERIFIED. Ebenezer Creek was Dec 8–9, 1864, during
  the March to the Sea, months after the Aug 31–Sept 1 battle. The draft never
  implies it happened at or near Jonesborough.
- Pontoon bridge pulled up before the refugees could cross; trapped between deep
  water and pursuing Confederate cavalry (Wheeler's); men, women, children drowned:
  **VERIFIED** ([EBEN]). Draft says "many drowned" / "hundreds of refugees" and
  fixes NO drowning count — correct, sources give no agreed number ([EBEN] cites
  "over 600 freed people" present, not a death toll).
- **MUST-FIX: none.** The throughline is the one Wikipedia + Ebenezer Creek support.

## Claim-by-claim

- **Macon & Western RR = last uncut line** — VERIFIED. [WIKI]: "the last uncut
  railroad leading into Atlanta." Draft's spelling note (Jonesborough/Jonesboro) is
  correct.
- **Sherman's wheel south to cut the railroad rather than assault the works** —
  VERIFIED ([WIKI]/[ABT]).
- **Hood sends Hardee with two corps (Hardee's + S.D. Lee's)** — VERIFIED.
- **Aug 31: Howard's Army of the Tennessee reaches town first, entrenches near the
  Flint River** — VERIFIED.
- **~3 p.m. attack, artillery then infantry; Lee's corps overruns the picket line,
  stopped by entrenched fire** — VERIFIED ([WIKI]: Lee attacked prematurely, hit
  Hazen/Logan's front).
- **Infantry refused to renew the charge** — VERIFIED. [WIKI]: "the Confederate
  rank and file went to ground and declined to charge"; Lee told Hardee his troops
  "were in no condition" to renew. Draft's paraphrase (not a quote) is faithful.
- **Lowrey's division + Kilpatrick's dismounted cavalry with Spencer repeaters,
  veered west, crossed the Flint, halted by XVII Corps** — VERIFIED. (Minor nuance:
  [WIKI] says Lowrey *pressed the cavalry across* the Flint and was then halted; the
  draft's "veered off west, crossed the Flint, halted on the far bank by a XVII
  Corps division" is accurate.)
- **Aug 31 casualties ~175 Union vs ~1,300–2,200 Confederate, ~10:1** — VERIFIED as
  a range. [WIKI]: Union 172–179 (all but 18 in Logan's corps); Confederate
  1,300 (Lee's corps, Bailey) up to 2,200 (Castel). "Easily repulsed" phrasing
  supported. The ~10:1 framing holds.
- **Hood's ~2 a.m. recall of Lee's corps to Atlanta out of fear of a frontal
  assault** — VERIFIED. [WIKI]: order sent 6 p.m., march to start 2 a.m. Draft says
  "around two in the morning he recalled Lee's corps" — accurate to the march time.
- **Schofield & Stanley reached/cut the Macon & Western that afternoon (Aug 31)** —
  VERIFIED. [WIKI]: Schofield's XXIII Corps on the railroad 3 p.m., Stanley's IV
  Corps struck it 4 p.m.; Sherman "severed the railroad line."
- **Sept 1: Hardee ~12,000 on a ~2-mile front; salient apex held by Govan's
  brigade** — VERIFIED verbatim ([WIKI]: "Hardee had 12,000 troops to defend a 2 mi
  front"; "apex of the salient was held by Govan's brigade").
- **Davis's XIV Corps (Thomas's Army of the Cumberland) attacks the salient after
  ~4 p.m.; Carlin and Morgan divisions; thick woods/underbrush** — VERIFIED.
- **Estey's brigade ~1,139 men; 10th KY + 74th IN overran 6th/7th Arkansas; 14th/38th
  Ohio broke through** — VERIFIED. [WIKI]: "Estey's 1,139 soldiers… 10th Kentucky
  and 74th Indiana on the right… 38th Ohio and 14th Ohio on the left… 14th and 38th
  Ohio also broke into the Confederate defenses." Spellings correct.
- **Govan + ~600 captured; ~8 guns, Key's Arkansas + Swett's Mississippi
  batteries** — VERIFIED verbatim. [WIKI]: "Govan and 600 of his men were captured.
  The eight guns of Key's Arkansas Battery and Swett's Mississippi Battery were
  taken." (Draft prints "roughly 600" / "about eight" — defensibly conservative.)
- **Cleburne led Vaughan's Tennessee brigade into the gap, halted the advance** —
  VERIFIED. [WIKI]: "Cleburne led Alfred Jefferson Vaughan Jr.'s Tennessee brigade
  into the gap and halted the Federal advance." Eastern-sector rally also supported.
- **Stanley's IV Corps came up slowly, dug in instead of exploiting** — VERIFIED
  (Kimball/Newton built field fortifications rather than driving in).
- **Hardee slipped away to Lovejoy's Station undetected, night of Sept 1** —
  VERIFIED verbatim ([WIKI]: "completely undetected").
- **Hood evacuated Atlanta same night via the McDonough Road; burned stores;
  exploding ammunition train** — VERIFIED. [WIKI]: Stewart's corps + militia left
  via McDonough Road; "Five locomotives and 81 boxcars… put to the torch… 28
  boxcars… filled with ammunition… spectacular." Draft does NOT print the
  5/81/28 figures (per pack guidance) — correct restraint; the *event* is solid.
- **Gone with the Wind dramatization** — VERIFIED as a commonplace; the burning of
  the Atlanta depot/ammunition trains is the famous GWTW scene. Acceptable.
- **Atlanta fell Sept 2; "Atlanta is ours"** — VERIFIED (see item a).
- **Cleburne "Stonewall of the West," killed at Franklin Nov 30, 1864; Jonesboro
  cemetery bears his name** — VERIFIED. Cleburne killed Franklin Nov 30, 1864
  (gunshot, leading an assault on entrenchments); a Confederate memorial cemetery
  in Georgia is named for him. Draft hedges cemetery specifics — appropriate.

  SHOULD-FIX (optional): nothing to change; if a verbatim platform phrase is ever
  wanted, the plank's actual words are "cessation of hostilities," not "a failure."
- **McClellan repudiated the peace plank, splitting his party** — VERIFIED.
- **A negotiated 1864 peace = Confederacy + slavery left intact** — VERIFIED as the
  standard historical reading; honest.
- **Lincoln won 212–21, ~55% popular** — VERIFIED (212 to 21; 55.02%).
- **13th Amendment followed** — VERIFIED (passed Congress Jan 1865, ratified Dec
  1865). Draft says "followed" — true.

## Two SHOULD-FIX (neither blocks ship)

1. **Aug 31 Confederate casualty floor.** [WIKI]'s lower bound is ~1,700 (Castel's
   2,200; Bailey's 1,300+400=1,700), while the draft's lower bound is "1,300."
   1,300 is S.D. Lee's-corps-alone figure; the draft DOES say "Lee alone estimated
   roughly 1,300… with Hardee's corps losing several hundred more," so the total it
   implies is ~1,700+ — internally correct. The headline range "1,300 to 2,200" is
   defensible but slightly undersells the low end of the *total*. Optional: render
   the total as "~1,700–2,200" and keep the "1,300 in Lee's corps alone" gloss.
   SHOULD-FIX, not MUST.

2. **"the four words that turned a war" (§4 subhead) / "as much for the Union cause
   as any victory of the year."** Rhetoric, not a fact error, but verges on
   overclaim — the *battle/fall of Atlanta* turned the morale, the telegram
   announced it. The body prose correctly credits the event, not the sentence, so
   this is tone, handed to the framing gate. SHOULD-FIX-minor.

**Nothing in the draft is WRONG or UNSUPPORTED. No MUST-FIX.**
