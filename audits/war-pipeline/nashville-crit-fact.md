  ratio is sound against the Wikipedia figures (55k vs ~25k).
- **No false-precise pair printed.** Confirmed — the draft never writes "55,000 vs
  25,000" in prose; the numbers live only in the ledger as sourcing rationale. PASS.

## B. The two USCT brigades / two days — special scrutiny

**Claim:** First Colored Brigade = Dec 15 diversion (different regiments, different
commander); 2nd Colored Brigade (12th/13th/100th USCT under Col. Charles R. Thompson) =
Dec 16 Overton/Peach Orchard Hill assault. Not conflated.

- **VERIFIED.** Wikipedia: Dec 15 Steedman diversion used USCT regiments 14th, 16th, 17th,
  18th, 44th (the First Colored Brigade, Col. Morgan). Dec 16 Overton Hill assault =
  Thompson's 2nd Colored Brigade, 12th/13th/100th USCT (BNT + Iron Brigader confirm
  regiments + commander). The draft's MEANWHILE sidebar in §4 keeps them explicitly
  distinct ("two different commands doing two different jobs on two different days").
  Steedman commanded over both — also correct. PASS.

## C. The 13th USCT loss figure — special scrutiny

**Claim (draft §4):** "about 221 men killed, wounded, and missing out of roughly 576
engaged, near 40 percent." Sister regiments: 100th ~133, 12th ~114.

- **VERIFIED.** Iron Brigader: 13th USCT = 55 killed + 165 wounded + 1 missing = **221 of
  576 engaged, 38%**. 100th USCT = 133 (12k/121w). 12th USCT = 114 (10k/104w). BNT renders
  it "221 of 556, ~40%." The draft's "~221 … ~576 … near 40%" sits correctly inside the
  source range and matches Iron Brigader's engaged count. "Single heaviest regimental
  loss of the entire two days" — BNT: "the highest of any regiment across both battle
  days." VERIFIED.

## D. Verbatim quotes — the highest-risk surface

The draft prints exactly ONE quote inside quotation marks: the **Eicher** line. Every
other flagged quote is paraphrased. Checked each:

1. **Eicher — PRINTED VERBATIM.** Draft: *"If Hood mortally wounded his army at Franklin,
   he would kill it two weeks later at Nashville."* — **EXACT MATCH** to Wikipedia. The
   draft attributes it to "the historian David Eicher" and adds the in-text caveat
   "modern historian talking, not a soldier of the time." VERIFIED + correctly framed.
2. **Holtzclaw — PARAPHRASED, faithful.** Draft renders his report as substance ("dashing
   up to the abatis and being killed by the hundreds … never seen dead men lie thicker
   than in front of his two right regiments"). Source (Iron Brigader) verbatim: *"Placing
   a negro brigade in front they gallantly dashed up the abatis, forty feet in front, and
   were killed by hundreds … I have seen most of the battle fields of the West, but never
   saw dead men thicker than in front of my two right regiments."* The draft's paraphrase
   is faithful and — critically — does NOT reproduce the fact pack's mangled version
   ("I have never saw dead men thicker"), which dropped the "battle fields of the West"
   clause. Good catch by the author; the pack's verbatim string was slightly off and the
   draft was right to paraphrase. VERIFIED as paraphrase.
3. **Thomas "negro soldiers will fight" — PARAPHRASED.** Draft: "the question was now
   settled — that Black soldiers would fight," explicitly flagged in-text as "his meaning
   rather than as a sealed quotation." Provenance: attributed to Thomas at Overton Hill
   via Col. Morgan's 1885 account; two renderings exist (*"the question is settled; negro
   soldiers will fight"* / *"this settles it; the Negro will fight"*). The draft's
   substance is well-attested; handling correct. VERIFIED as paraphrase.
4. **Steedman "color made no difference" — PARAPHRASED.** Draft: "could not find that
   color made any difference in how his troops fought." BNT verbatim: *"I was unable to
   discover that color made any difference in the fighting of my troops. All, white and
   black, nobly did their duty as soldiers."* Faithful paraphrase. VERIFIED.
5. **"Probably the strongest works on the entire line" — PARAPHRASED, attributed to
   Steedman.** Draft: "in Steedman's own description, probably the strongest works on the
   entire line." MINOR ATTRIBUTION NOTE: BNT attributes this phrase to **Col. Thompson's**
   report (*"they assaulted probably the strongest works on the entire line"*), not
   Steedman's. The fact pack §8 also loosely tags it to Steedman/BNT. It is not inside
   quotation marks and the substance (the works were the strongest on the line) is
   uncontested, but the named speaker is most directly Thompson. **SHOULD-FIX:** soften
   to "by Union account" / attribute to Thompson, or drop the named attribution. Not
   ship-blocking (paraphrased, substance true), but it's a misattribution of a near-quote.
6. **Officer of the 12th USCT, "mow down … almost to a man" — PARAPHRASED.** Draft: "a
   portion of the 13th got onto the breastworks themselves before the Confederate
   reserves rose up and … were able to mow down those on the works almost to a man." Iron
   Brigader verbatim: *"A portion of the Thirteenth mounted the breastworks by
   themselves…the Rebel reserves…poured in so hot a fire as to mow down those on the works
   almost to a man."* Faithful paraphrase, not falsely quoted. VERIFIED.
7. **Thomas thaw telegram + Van Duzer "without skates" — PARAPHRASED.** Draft: "wired
   Washington that the thaw had come," and "the men could finally move 'without skates'"
   (the only fragment in quotes is the two-word colorism). Van Duzer line verified: *"the
   thaw has begun and to-morrow we can move without skates"* (telegraph officer J.C. Van
   Duzer). Quoting the two-word fragment "without skates" is defensible — it's verified
   verbatim — though the draft attributes it generically to "one of his telegraph
   officers" rather than naming Van Duzer (conservative, fine). VERIFIED.
8. **"Competing with one another to be first into a redoubt" — PARAPHRASED.** Draft renders
   the eyewitness recollection as substance, not a sealed quote. ABT/pack source =
   "competing with the others to reach the redoubt first." Faithful. VERIFIED.
9. **Grant "obviated all criticism" — PARAPHRASED.** Draft: "Grant would later admit, in so
   many words, that the victory at Nashville obviated all the criticism." Wikipedia
   (Thomas): *"Grant did, however, acknowledge that Thomas's eventual success at Nashville
   obviated all criticism."* The draft hedges with "in so many words" and does not seal a
   quote. VERIFIED.
