## 0. SLAVERY-NAMED CHECK (ship-blocking, runs first)

**RESULT: PASS — slavery is named as the cause in the section's own prose, repeatedly and unmistakably.**

- §1 ¶2 names it outright and structurally, not as an aside:
  "The Confederacy existed for one purpose: to preserve and extend slavery.
  Eleven states had broken away from the United States rather than accept any
  limit on the enslavement of Black people, built their economy and their
  society on it, and now fielded armies to defend it." The word "slavery"
  appears; the cause is stated in the draft's OWN voice, not quoted/outsourced.
- The framing is then *load-bearing*: the railroad is explicitly demoted to "the
  *object* and not the *reason*," and the section promises the reason will reappear
  "in human form" — which it does, in §4 and the closing MEANWHILE.
- §4 reasserts it as the section's thesis: "the railroad was the object, slavery
  was the reason, and the proof of the reason was a camp full of free people."
- Closing MEANWHILE: "a republic founded to keep four million people in bondage."

This clears the `feedback_name_the_cause` gate with room to spare. Slavery is
named, named first, and named as THE cause — not "states' rights," not "the
sections," not euphemized.

**Black agency / self-emancipation — the specific Corinth test: PASS.**
The brief flags the risk that the camp reads as "freed by the army." The draft
actively refuses that framing:
- §4: "thousands of enslaved people were freeing themselves by walking into
  Union-held Corinth" — self-emancipation is the verb, the agency is theirs.
- The camp is shown as a community THEY ran: "cooperative farms," "raised and
  sold cotton and vegetables," "made the camp pay," learned to read, "Nearly
  2,000 of the men there enlisted." The Union's role is correctly framed as
  *not turning them away* and *not sending them back* — facilitation, not
  authorship of their freedom.
- "People who had been somebody's legal property a year earlier put on Union
  blue and took up arms" — keeps the prior condition (chattel slavery) explicit
  and the act (enlisting, fighting) theirs.

**Contraband Camp not sidebarred — PASS.**
The brief's other worry: that the payoff gets buried in a MEANWHILE box. It does
not. The camp is the BODY of §4 ("What the victory protected"), occupying three
full paragraphs of main prose and explicitly declared "the deepest meaning of
Corinth" and "the point of it." The MEANWHILE box reinforces rather than
contains it. Structurally correct.

## FINDINGS

### F1 — The Rogers-on-the-parapet sequence — VERDICT: FAIR (watched closely; lands right)
This is the single highest Lost-Cause-risk passage in the draft (the brief
flagged it by name), so it gets the closest read.

- The valor IS rendered — Rogers is "brave to the edge of recklessness," he
  "planted the colors," he is buried with honors. But the draft does NOT convert
  that into Confederate righteousness. It keeps Rogers's individual courage and
  the cause he died for in separate registers, and §4 then crushes the cause:
  Rogers's army "had just tried, and failed, to win the war for the other side"
  — and "the other side" has already been defined as the slaveholding republic.
- The romance is actively de-mythologized: the draft REFUSES the legendary
  bullet-count ("the popular tale of a specific number of bullets is not
  something the records support, so we won't pretend to it"). That is the
  opposite of Lost Cause embellishment — it strips the legend.
- The Rosecrans burial-honors coda is the classic Lost-Cause-bait beat
  (chivalrous enemies, gallant foe). The draft handles it as an attributed
  historical act, not as a moral on the nobility of the Confederate cause, and
  crucially it is bracketed by the slavery framing on both sides. The honor is
  Rosecrans's gesture toward a brave man; it is not allowed to ennoble secession.
- The casualty horror is not sanitized: "the ditch in front of Robinett was left
  a killing ground," "the battle in miniature, and it is terrible." Human reality
  present, not gratuitous.

No false-balance: the draft never says both armies fought for equally honorable
things. It says one side fought to preserve slavery and lost. Valor of
individuals ≠ validation of the cause — the draft holds that line. **FAIR.**

### F2 — "contraband" term handling — VERDICT: FAIR
The draft uses the period term but immediately flags it as the period's, not its
own: "a clumsy wartime term for enslaved people who crossed into Union lines and
would not be sent back." Correctly historicized; no euphemism laundering. The
human subjects are called "enslaved people" / "freedom-seekers" / "formerly
enslaved people" — person-first, agency-forward. **FAIR.**

### F3 — Strategic framing of the railroad — VERDICT: FAIR
A "battle about a railroad" is a soft-pedal trap (the bloodless-logistics frame
that lets slavery vanish). The draft pre-empts it: §1 demotes the railroad to
"object," and the closing MEANWHILE explicitly drags the rails back to their
purpose — "carried the men and the matériel of a republic founded to keep four
million people in bondage." The logistics are not allowed to launder the cause.
**FAIR.**

### F4 — Van Dorn / Confederate command treatment — VERDICT: FAIR
Van Dorn is "a cavalier of a general," "a gambler's" logic — characterful but not
romanticized; the draft notes the court of inquiry into "drunkenness and
negligence" and that he was "superseded all the same." No heroic gloss. The "one
hour more of daylight" lament is presented as a general mourning a tactical
near-miss, NOT as tragic-noble Confederate pathos — and it sits next to the §4
verdict that the cause he served was slavery. No tilt. **FAIR.**

### F5 — Presentism check — VERDICT: FAIR
The draft's moral judgments (slavery as the cause, the camp as "what victory was
for") are the historical consensus and are grounded in the actors' own
contemporary acts (secession declarations are implied via "rather than accept
any limit on the enslavement of Black people"; the self-emancipators' own
choices). No anachronistic imposition; the verdict is the period's stakes, named.
**FAIR.**

### F6 — Human reality balance — VERDICT: FAIR
Neither sanitized nor gratuitous. The killing ground, the two dead Union
generals, the hand-to-hand on the parapet, the ~7,000 combined casualties are
present and weighted; none is lingered on for spectacle. The Robinett death-photo
is referenced as documented context, not exploited. **FAIR.**
