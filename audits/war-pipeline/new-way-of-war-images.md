Verified public-domain images for the "New Way of War" section (military technology/methods, 1861–1865). **1 hero + 10 inline figures.** Every direct URL was extracted from its live Commons File: page (or LOC equivalent) and fetched to confirm it returns the actual image; every subject was checked against the caption it carries.

**Note on verification:** Wikimedia's `upload.wikimedia.org` CDN rate-limited (HTTP 429) bulk `curl` HEADs from this IP mid-session. The URLs are nonetheless confirmed live — two resolved with HTTP 200 image/jpeg via `curl` before the throttle kicked in, and every URL below was independently fetched and returned real image bytes (the fetcher saved each as a JPEG/PNG of the expected size). Do NOT bulk-hammer the CDN at download time; pace the downloads (the project's known Wikimedia 429 lesson).

### 2 — minie-balls
- **filename:** `new-way-of-war-minie-balls.jpg`
- **URL:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fc/Minie_Balls.jpg
- **caption:** Not balls at all. The Minié "ball" was a cone-tipped lead bullet with a hollow base: it dropped easily down a rifled barrel, then the firing gas slammed into that hollow and flared the soft lead out to bite the grooves. Loose going in, tight coming out — the trick that made the rifle a mass infantry weapon. (Left to right: an Enfield, two Springfield, a Williams, and a .69-caliber Minié bullet.)
- **credit:** Mike Cumpston · photograph · 2008 · Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- **placement:** after `## What the bullet did to a body` (illustrates the bullet whose terminal ballistics the section is describing)
- **why PD:** Author Mike Cumpston released the work into the public domain ("grants anyone the right to use this work for any purpose, without any conditions").
- **subject-verification:** Commons page lists the five projectiles as Enfield/Springfield Minié/Williams/.69 Minié — genuine period-pattern Minié bullets matching the prose. Confirmed live (111.5 KB JPEG).
- **orientation:** landscape

The North could lose track to raids and keep running; the South, with a fraction of the engines and almost no works to build more, could not.

### 5 — spencer-rifle
- **filename:** `new-way-of-war-spencer-rifle.jpg`
- **URL:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/M1865_REPEATING_RIFLE.jpg
- **caption:** The Spencer repeating rifle. Where a muzzle-loader managed two or three aimed shots a minute, a soldier could work the Spencer's lever and empty seven metallic cartridges in well under one — the gun Confederates grumbled "they load on Sunday and shoot all week," held back from the infantry for years by an ordnance chief who called such things newfangled gimcracks.
- **credit:** National Park Service · photograph of artifact · n.d. · Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- **placement:** after `## The weapon that came too slowly`
- **why PD:** Work of the National Park Service (U.S. federal government employee, official duties) — 17 U.S.C. §105.
- **subject-verification:** Commons page (from Spencer repeating rifle Wikipedia lead) labels it an M1865 Spencer repeating rifle, whole weapon side-profile on plain background — the Spencer named in the prose. Confirmed live earlier (HTTP 200, 188 KB JPEG).
- **orientation:** landscape (1756×279, very wide strip)

### 9 — gatling-gun
- **filename:** `new-way-of-war-gatling-gun.jpg`
- **URL:** https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c5/GatlingGunDrawing.jpg
- **caption:** Richard Gatling's patent drawing for his hand-cranked, multi-barrel gun. It could spit out hundreds of rounds a minute, but only one Union general bought any — about a dozen, out of his own pocket — and whether they ever truly fired on the enemy is still unproven. The machine gun arrived at the very edge of the war, half-bought and half-used.

- **why PD:** U.S. patent document (1865); also PD under life-plus-70 (Gatling d. 1903).
- **subject-verification:** Commons page: "Battery Gun patent drawing by R.J. Gatling," U.S. Patent 47631, 1865 — Gatling's own gun. Confirmed live (returns 169.8 KB JPEG; image reads as a multi-barrel battery-gun mechanical drawing).
