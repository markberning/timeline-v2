# Framing / Lost-Cause Gate — Malvern Hill

**Gate:** Lost Cause / distortion critic
**Section:** `the-retreat` · `it-was-murder` · `richmond-saved`
**Date:** 2026-05-30

---

## 1. SHIP-BLOCKING CHECK — Does the section name slavery as the cause?

**PASS.**

The opening paragraph of `the-retreat` delivers it clearly and early:

> "…the thing the whole bloody week had been about — was the survival of a slaveholding republic. Richmond was the capital of a new country founded to keep four million people enslaved, and an entire campaign had been built to capture it and end the rebellion in a stroke."

The `Meanwhile in Richmond` interlude reinforces it:

> "The capital of the slaveholding republic was no longer in immediate reach."

Slavery is named as the foundational cause *in the section's own prose*, not buried in a parenthetical or fact ledger. Ship-block cleared.

---

## 2. SPECIAL TENSION — "Union won the field / Lee won the campaign"

**VERDICT: FAIR.**

This is the framing the gate asked to scrutinize most carefully. The section threads the needle correctly on every count:

**a. McClellan's failure of nerve is the engine, not Confederate destiny.**

The retreat is attributed to McClellan's decision — "he would not fight his way into Richmond. He would withdraw" — made before Malvern Hill, after Gaines's Mill. The section is explicit that the Union army *won the battle* and then McClellan ordered it to keep retreating anyway. His absence from the battlefield during his own victory, his subordinates wanting to counterattack, and his mockery during the 1864 campaign all land without any attempt to rehabilitate him. The prose does not treat Lee as fated to win; it treats McClellan as having quit.

**b. Lee's assaults are framed as costly aggression / blunder, not genius.**

The `it-was-murder` section is unambiguous. Lee's plan is called "a disaster in practice." The Confederate artillery effort is quoted as "most farcical." The infantry assault is described plainly: "a frontal assault uphill, across open ground, against massed artillery, delivered one fragment at a time with no support, is not really an attack at all." D.H. Hill's "It was not war — it was murder" is used as the section's verdict, delivered by a Confederate general who tried to talk them out of it beforehand. The casualty disparity (roughly 5,300–5,650 Confederate vs. ~3,000 Union) is stated plainly, with Lee having "gained not one foot of the crest." The section never romanticizes the charges. There is no language of courage redeemed, doomed gallantry, or noble sacrifice. The word "murder" is the frame the section lives inside.

**c. The strategic outcome is attributed to McClellan's retreat, not to Confederate superiority.**

The closing beat is precise: "Lee had won the week" because McClellan backed away from Richmond, not because Lee was the better general. The prose acknowledges the seven-day campaign went to the South "by the tally of the map," then immediately attributes that outcome to the retreat of a Union army that won its last battle. The `Meanwhile in Richmond` sidebar does quote the Richmond Dispatch lionizing Lee — but the section frames that as the Confederate press's reading, inside a "strategic triumph," not as the section's own verdict on Lee's quality.

## 3. DISTORTION HUNT — Additional surfaces

### 3a. Lee's letter to his wife | FAIR
> "Our success has not been as great or complete as we should have desired."

Quoted to show Lee's own dissatisfaction — he saved Richmond and still complained he hadn't destroyed McClellan. This is used to characterize Lee's relentless aggression, not to humanize him or generate sympathy. FAIR.

### 3b. Richmond Dispatch "no captain that ever lived" | FAIR
Attributed to the Confederate press, inside the `Meanwhile` sidebar, framed as the city's relief narrative. The section does not endorse the claim. FAIR.

### 3c. Jackson "reportedly wanted to pursue" | FAIR
Handled with appropriate hedging ("reportedly"). No sourced claim that Jackson would have destroyed the army. FAIR.

### 3d. "The South would take that trade every time" | SLIGHT TENSION — SHOULD-FIX
The closing italic kicker:

> "McClellan won the last battle and lost the campaign. He saved his army and surrendered the prize it had come for. Both halves were true at once, and the South would take that trade every time."

The first two sentences are correct and well-calibrated. The third — "the South would take that trade every time" — sounds confident from the Confederate POV without reminder of what that trade cost: ~5,300 casualties in a single day on top of an already brutal week. In isolation it reads like an approving military verdict on Confederate strategic success. It is not Lost Cause distortion — the battle *was* a Confederate strategic win — but given that the section has just spent an entire beat on "it was murder," ending on a phrase that sounds like "and they'd do it again" risks undercutting that message. A minor reframe — adding something like "at that price, and without knowing what came next" — would keep the kicker honest. This is a **SHOULD-FIX**, not ship-blocking.

### 3e. D.H. Hill's role | FAIR
Hill is used as a structural device: the man who tried to stop the attack, watched it fail, and named what it was. This is accurate and does not romanticize him. FAIR.

### 3f. Porter's role | FAIR
Porter is credited as the officer who actually ran and won the defense — a mild corrective to any McClellan-as-hero reading. FAIR.

---

## 4. OVERALL VERDICT

**PASS WITH ONE SHOULD-FIX.**

The section names slavery as the cause clearly and early. The Lee-won-the-campaign framing is handled correctly: McClellan's failure of nerve is the cause, Lee's assaults are framed as a blunder and a slaughter, and the Confederate strategic win is attributed to the retreat rather than Confederate genius. No romanticization of the charges; the "murder" verdict is the dominant frame. One kicker line ("the South would take that trade every time") has mild Lost-Cause-adjacent resonance that could be defused with a half-sentence acknowledging the cost — SHOULD-FIX but not ship-blocking.

**MUST-FIX:** None.
**SHOULD-FIX:** One — closing kicker line 3 in `richmond-saved`.
