# FRAMING CRITIC — Battle of Bennington draft (rev-bennington)

*Critic: framing (fairness, emphasis, interpretive honesty). Sources read: `rev-bennington-draft.md`, `rev-bennington-factpack.md`. 2026-06-11.*

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

No blockers. The draft's hardest framing jobs are done right: the Molly Stark line
is told entirely as legend with the 1819 anchor and the real-Molly counterweight;
the Brunswick dragoons are human without being sentimentalized; Baum is never
psychologized past the pack's hedges ("He may simply have been pinned and out of
options; he never got to say" is exactly the discipline the pack ordered); the
victory's scale is kept honest by arithmetic, with an explicit anti-inflation
sentence ("Bennington did not cause that by itself, but it shortened the road");
and every caption caveat the brief lists (Chappel century-later, Troiani modern,
Tenney copy, Warner no-life-portrait, Baum/Breymann no-likeness, NY-not-VT) is
present on its surface. What remains is a small set of emphasis overstatements and
unhedged superlatives.

## SHOULD-FIX

### SF1 · "almost none of it came back at all" overstates the destruction
Draft (Section 3): "It came back with nothing, because almost none of it came back
at all."
Pack: Breymann escaped with "roughly two-thirds to three-quarters of his men"
(~400–480 of 550–650); total expedition ~1,430–1,450, total lost ~900–1,000. About
a third of the combined force returned. "Almost none" is emphasis beyond the
numbers the same paragraph then gives correctly.
FIX: "It came back with nothing. Baum's column never came back at all, and
Breymann's came back broken — without its cannon, its wounded, or its baggage."

### SF2 · The Burgoyne supply quote is post-battle but staged as pre-battle
Draft (Section 1): "Burgoyne said it himself, in a letter to the government in
London that August: what he needed was transport and food on the hoof." followed by
the QUOTE ("The possession of the cattle and carriages would certainly have
enabled...").
Pack §7.3: the letter is Burgoyne to Germain, **Aug 20, 1777** — four days AFTER
the battle. The quote's own conditional ("would certainly have enabled") is
retrospective regret; placing it unmarked in the pre-battle section implies he
wrote it while planning the raid. Section 3's callback handles the timing honestly;
Section 1 should too.
FIX: "Burgoyne would say it himself, in a letter to London written four days after
the raid had already failed:" (or "...would write later that August:").

### SF3 · "neighbors of the men who had captured them" over-specifies settlement
Draft (Section 3): "Men who had been marched across an ocean to fight farmers
ended, in some numbers, as the neighbors of the men who had captured them."
Pack §5 [MED]: Brunswick prisoners "eventually stayed/settled in America" —
campaign-wide, with an explicit warning: "don't over-specify Bennington's share."
"Neighbors of the men who had captured them" implies they settled among the New
England militia communities specifically, which the pack does not say.
FIX: "...ended, in some numbers, as citizens of the country they had been sent to
fight." (The MEANWHILE block's "made America their home instead" is already
correctly scoped — no change there.)

### SF4 · "killed several hundred men" inflates the dead
Draft (Section 1): "And there was one more shortage, almost comic if it had not
killed several hundred men."
Pack §5: killed-and-wounded left on the field ~200–230 (Wikipedia: 207 killed);
the big number, ~900–1,000, is killed + wounded + captured. "Killed several
hundred" matches neither figure.
FIX: "...almost comic if the raid it spawned had not ended with most of a thousand
men dead, wounded, or captured." (Or "...if it had not gotten a couple hundred men
killed.")

## NIT

### N1 · Blurb superlative makes Stark's grievance a temperament
Draft (sections list, marching-for-horses blurb): "...1,500 New Hampshire militia
under the angriest veteran in New England."
The section prose gets this right ("one of the angriest officers in America,"
immediately grounded in the commission grievance). The blurb's flat superlative
trades the command-politics story for a temper. Suggest: "...under a Bunker Hill
veteran with a grievance" — or at least "one of the angriest men in New England."

### N2 · Three unbacked "of the war" superlatives
- "one of the strangest forces of the war" (Section 1)
- "one of the most famous things any American said in the whole war" (Section 2)
- "one of the great sentences of the war" (Section 2)
None are pack claims. The Molly-line fame is defensible as common knowledge; the
other two are authorial taste wearing fact's clothes. Keep at most one; "one of the
great sentences of the war" is the easiest cut ("Stark reported it to General Gates
six days later." stands fine on its own).

### N3 · Two-months phrasing inconsistent across surfaces
Locator 1 caption: "Two months later, almost to the day, Burgoyne surrendered" vs
Section 3: "exactly two months after Bennington." Aug 16 → Oct 17 is two months and
one day, so the locator's hedge is actually the more accurate; the pack authorizes
"exactly 2 months." Pick one rendering and use it on both surfaces.

### N4 · Breymann escape fraction differs between surfaces
Dossier bio: "escaped in the dark with perhaps two-thirds of his men." Section 2:
"perhaps two-thirds to three-quarters of his men." Pack gives the full range. Align
the dossier card to the range (or "with most of his men").

### N5 · "Nobody tell them."
Section 3's closing wink at Vermont's holiday. It is funny, but it is the narrator
stepping out of the story to elbow the reader (the no-meta-narration rule), and it
gently mocks the page's likeliest local readers. The preceding sentence already
lands the joke ("Vermont, which contains the monument, the depot site, and none of
the battlefield...") — consider cutting the tag line.

### N6 · "stayed where he judged the blow would fall" — hindsight halo
Section 1. Pack §3 says only that Stark "refused orders to march his brigade to the
Hudson and instead stayed to cover Bennington." The draft's phrasing credits him
with foresight the pack doesn't assert. Minor; "and stayed to cover Bennington"
alone is both pack-true and still flattering in hindsight.

## CRITERIA REVIEW (for the reviser's record)

1. **Both-sides honesty — GOOD.** Dragoons: boots, broadswords, "a country none of
   them had chosen," the breakout charge given dignity without gloating ("What they
   did next belongs to them"). Baum: every interior claim badged ("by most
   accounts," "by the standard account"), and the no-surviving-record anchor stated
   twice. Loyalists: named (Queen's Loyal Rangers), their prisoner treatment told
   with the "more relish than documentation" badge. Natives: flight is pack-HIGH,
   desertion framed through Burgoyne's loss ("cost him his eyes"), no cartoon.
2. **Victory proportion — GOOD.** Arithmetic carries the weight (one in seven, ~700
   prisoners "the firmest number," 30 days' provisions, Sept 13 crossing); "did not
   cause that by itself, but it shortened the road" is the model sentence. No
   "turning point" inflation. Residual overstatements are SF1/SF4.
3. **Legend framing — EXEMPLARY.** 1819 anchor quoted as exactly that; variants
   acknowledged; "nobody knows what he actually said"; real Molly (smallpox nurse)
   carried in the MEANWHILE; paper-in-hat and Baum-misread badged as
   tradition/most-accounts with the dead-in-two-days anchor; no flag mentioned
   anywhere; hell-hounds, Molly Stark cannon, Langdon, Breymann-shot-by-own-men all
   correctly absent per the ledger.
4. **Stark — GOOD.** Grievance told at pack evidence level (reported speech for the
   declaration), the NH-only condition and the refused Hudson orders presented as
   the command-politics fact they are, redemption closed with the Oct 4 commission.
   Neither saint nor prima donna in the prose; the blurb superlative is N1, the
   foresight halo is N6.
5. **Emphasis — MOSTLY GOOD.** SF1–SF4 are the full list of beats weighted past the
   pack. Prisoners' fate: traitor treatment badged, Brunswick settlement scoped
   campaign-wide ("among the Brunswick troops taken in this campaign") — only the
   "neighbors" flourish (SF3) drifts.
6. **Captions/dossier surfaces — CLEAN.** Chappel "Painted a century after the fact
   and heroic by design"; Troiani "modern (2000s) painting, not period art"; Tenney
   "19th-century copy... after a likeness made around 1790"; Warner "No portrait of
   Warner from life exists... inventions"; Baum/Breymann cards state no-portrait;
   winner line "decisively" matches pack-HIGH; New-York-not-Vermont accurate on the
   dossier note, place line, Section 2 opener, Section 3, the monument caption, the
   battlefield caption, and both locator captions. N3/N4 are the only cross-surface
   wobbles.
