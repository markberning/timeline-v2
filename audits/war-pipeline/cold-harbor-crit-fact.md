## A. THE CASUALTY LEGEND — the #1 trap. VERIFIED HANDLED.

The draft does NOT print "7,000 in 20 minutes" as fact. It explicitly labels it
"the legend, not the history," names Rhea as the corrector, and supplies the revised
ranges. Web verification confirms every revised figure:

- **June 3 morning assault ~3,500–4,000 Union** — VERIFIED. Rhea "can account for only
  3,500 to 4,000 Union killed, wounded, and missing" for the morning assault. Draft prints
  exactly this. ✔
- **Whole day of June 3 ~6,000 Union vs ~1,000–1,500 Confederate** — VERIFIED. Rhea:
  "about 6,000 casualties, compared to Lee's 1,000 to 1,500." Draft matches. ✔
- **"sputtered for about an hour, not eight minutes"** — VERIFIED verbatim-in-substance.
  Rhea: "the assault sputtered on for about an hour, not the eight minutes some writers
  have claimed." Draft says "sputtered for about an hour" and "did not happen in eight
  minutes." ✔
- **"many Confederates didn't even realize a major attack had been made"** — VERIFIED;
  this is in the Rhea/cwmemory summary. ✔
- **Whole-battle Union ~12,000–13,000** — VERIFIED. Wikipedia 12,738; ABT ~12,737. ✔
- **Whole-battle Confederate ~4,500–5,300** — VERIFIED as a defensible range. Wikipedia
  5,287; ABT ~4,595. Printing the range is the correct, safe call. ✔
- **Marye's Heights / Fredericksburg as the prior comparison** — REASONABLE. The draft
  frames it as "the most one-sided beating … since Marye's Heights." This is an
  interpretive comparison, well-supported in the literature; not a hard numeric claim.
  SHOULD-FIX (optional): it is a characterization, not a measured fact — fine as written
  because it is phrased as a comparison, not a statistic.

**Status: VERIFIED. The legend is corrected, not asserted. No fix needed.**

## B. THE COAT-PINNING STORY (Porter) — trap #2. VERIFIED HANDLED.

- **Quote verbatim** — VERIFIED. Porter, *Campaigning with Grant*: "calmly writing their
  names and home addresses on slips of paper, and pinning them on the backs of their coats,
  so that their dead bodies might be recognized upon the field …" Draft quotes this exactly
  (it stops the quote one clause early, dropping "and their fate made known to their
  families at home" — a clean truncation, not a misquote). ✔
- **Single-sourced / possibly apocryphal framing** — VERIFIED CORRECT and, if anything,
  the draft is *more* generous to Porter than the scholarship. Rhea explicitly calls it
  "no more than another of the sensational inventions that frequent his memoir," and a
  whole site ("Grant Under Fire") is devoted to Porter's unreliability. The draft says
  "single-sourced … may well be a story polished by memory … we just won't pretend it's
  bedrock." That is honest and within bounds. ✔
- **Mine Run (Nov 1863) precedent** — the draft cites the documented pinning practice at
  Mine Run to show "something like it surely happened somewhere." Carried from the fact
  pack; reasonable. SHOULD-FIX (optional): I could not independently re-verify the Mine Run
  detail in this pass beyond the fact-pack assertion; it is plausible and widely repeated,
  but if a reader-facing citation is ever wanted, confirm it. Not blocking — it is offered
  as corroborating color, not a load-bearing claim.

**Status: VERIFIED HANDLED. Told as Porter's single source, possibly apocryphal. No fix.**

## C. GRANT'S REGRET QUOTE — keystone. VERIFIED VERBATIM.

- Draft prints: *"I have always regretted that the last assault at Cold Harbor was ever
  made … No advantage whatever was gained to compensate for the heavy loss we sustained."*
- Primary text (Grant, *Personal Memoirs* ch. LV, via Perseus) — VERIFIED both clauses
  verbatim. "I have always regretted that the last assault at Cold Harbor was ever made"
  is exact. The "last" (not "very last") wording is confirmed against the primary; the
  ABT "very last" variant is the paraphrase, and the draft correctly used the primary
  wording. ✔
- The ellipsis bridges two genuine, near-adjacent clauses from the same retrospective
  passage. The join does not distort meaning. ✔ The `[VERIFY]` flag in the ledger is
  now RESOLVED: use "last," not "very last." Confirmed.

**Status: VERIFIED VERBATIM. Resolve the ledger flag in favor of "last."**
