## A. The Franklin quote — ✅ PASS

Draft text (line 132):
> "I had never heard any objection to the right of laying duties to regulate commerce; but a right to lay internal taxes was never supposed to be in parliament, as we are not represented there." — Benjamin Franklin, Examination before the House of Commons, 1766

- ✅ The fabricated "Before 1763…" opening clause flagged in the prior check is GONE. The quote now begins "I had never heard any objection to the right of laying duties to regulate commerce…".
- ✅ Substance, internal/external-tax distinction, and the "as we are not represented there" clause match the primary record of Franklin's February 1766 Examination before the Committee of the Whole of the House of Commons.
- ✅ Attribution correct (Franklin, 1766 Examination before the House of Commons). The examination is correctly described elsewhere in the draft as 13 February 1766 (line 130).

- ⚠️ Minor transcription variant (NOT an error, non-blocking): some modern renderings drop the auxiliary and read "I **never** heard any objection…". However, the fuller attested transcription — including the Digital History (University of Houston) edition of the Examination — renders it exactly as the draft has it: "**I had never heard** any objection to the right of laying duties to regulate commerce…". Both forms circulate in reputable secondary sources; the draft's "I had never heard" is an attested verbatim form, so no change is required. (If perfect uniformity with the most-cited Founders Online/Smyth wording is wanted, "I never heard" is the leaner variant — but this is editorial, not a correctness fix.)

## B. The Quartering Act of 1765 paragraph — ✅ PASS (all sub-claims)

Draft text (line 30): royal assent 15 May 1765; passed in the same session as the Stamp Act; required colonial assemblies to provide/pay for barracks, food, and supplies for British regulars; quartering in barracks, public houses, and empty buildings, NOT occupied private homes; Declaration of Independence echo.

- ✅ **Royal assent 15 May 1765** — confirmed (Avalon Project text header "May 15, 1765"; History.com "May 15, 1765"; LOC timeline). The draft does NOT use the wrong "24 May" date. Correct.
- ✅ **Same session as the Stamp Act** — confirmed; contemporary/period sources state the quartering provision was passed "in the same Session with the Stamp Act."
- ✅ **Required assemblies to provide/pay for barracks, food, and supplies** — confirmed (food, drink, quarters, fuel, transportation for regulars; colonial authorities to arrange/fund). Draft's "barracks, food, and supplies" is an accurate, fair summary.
- ✅ **Did NOT require quartering in occupied private homes in 1765** — confirmed. The 1765 Act establishes a hierarchy: barracks first, then public houses (inns, livery stables, alehouses, victualling houses, wine/spirit sellers), then uninhabited houses / outhouses / barns / other buildings. It did NOT authorize forced quartering in inhabited private dwellings. The draft's "barracks, public houses, and empty buildings, not in occupied private homes" is accurate and does NOT overstate private-home quartering.
- ✅ **Declaration of Independence echo** — confirmed verbatim: grievance reads "For quartering large bodies of armed troops among us." Draft quotes it correctly.
- ✅ Draft's "most explosive enforcement would come later, in 1767" is consistent with the later New York quartering crisis; not flagged for this re-check and supportable.

## C. The "[VERIFY: …]" bracket / £200,000 figure — ✅ PASS

- ✅ No "[VERIFY: …]" bracket appears anywhere in the final draft. The army-cost sentence (line 26) now reads: "It cost money, somewhere in the range of a few hundred thousand pounds a year, with the figure tied to the planning most often given as roughly £200,000 annually."
