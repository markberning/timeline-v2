# Fact Ledger — "Regulars & Provincials" draft

Every concrete claim in the draft mapped to its supporting fact-pack item. `[VERIFY]` flags collected at the bottom.

## Intro / definitions
- Two armies on the British side, mutual contempt → §11 thesis, §3, §4 (the section's framing premise).
- Regulars = full-time professional soldiers of the standing British Army, drilled in rigid discipline + European linear tactics (massed volleys in the open), sent across the Atlantic → §1 British regulars.
- Provincials = raised by a colony's assembly for a single campaign season, full-time + paid while serving, set term/terms; filled via bounties, militia-roster quotas, drafts → §1 Provincials.
- Militia = older institution, part-time citizen-soldiers of able-bodied free men, local defense, service a privilege of the settled/propertied; provincial regiments drawn from militia rolls but full-time/paid/single-campaign → §1 Militia.

## What the British thought of the provincials
- Braddock scornful of Native scouts; marched toward Fort Duquesne with only 8 Mingo guides → §3 Braddock / §9.
- Fort Duquesne = where Pittsburgh now stands → §9 (Monongahela ~10 mi east of present Pittsburgh; Fort Duquesne is the Pittsburgh site).
- Braddock meant to fight in European line "as gentlemen," ignored Washington's warning → §3 Braddock / §9.
- Franklin supplied the wagons; recorded Braddock's dismissiveness of Americans + Native allies → §3 (Franklin's Autobiography). Specific Braddock "these savages..." line NOT quoted, flagged → see VERIFY.
- Loudoun = John Campbell, Earl of Loudoun, commander-in-chief, arrived 23 July 1756, 35th + 42nd Foot, ~7,000 New England/New York provincials → §3 Loudoun + §7 (~7,000).
- Loudoun found colonists untrustworthy/ungrateful/incapable of sacrifice; reforms to lessen reliance on Americans → §3 Loudoun.
- Quartering: assemblies refused proper housing summer 1756; threatened to take quarters by force → §3 Loudoun quartering.

## What the provincials thought of the British
- The lash: cat o' nine tails; 25–500 lashes ordinary, up to 1,000 for mutiny/desertion; public → §1 British regulars (discipline) + §6.
- Gibson Clough of Lynn, Massachusetts, kept a journal → §6.
- Floggings recorded: 30 stripes (disobedience), 1,000 lashes, 500 each, 200 (artilleryman) → §6 (11 Jan, 28 Jan, 19 Mar entries).
- Used as laborers/teamsters, fatigue/hauling resentment → §4 (used as laborers/teamsters; Anderson A People's Army).
- Contract ethos: enlistment a contract kept to the letter, specific months/pay/rations/equipment/discharge date → §4 contractual enlistment ethos + §1 Provincials.
- Puritan covenant culture roots; "ordinary farmers led by neighbors," minimal social distance; can't impose regular discipline without dissolving the bonds → §4.
- Broke terms → men went home or refused duty = rights to them, mutiny to British → §4.
- "we be Englishmen born, we are debarred Englishmen's liberty" + regulars "but little better than slaves to their officers" → §6 / §12 (SAFE, attributed to Clough).

## The mutiny that was really a lawsuit (Louisbourg)
- Autumn 1759, Massachusetts men garrisoning Louisbourg, captured French fortress on Cape Breton, Nova Scotia → §5 + §10 (Louisbourg fell 1758).
- Clough enlisted six months under Governor Thomas Pownall proclamation, dismissed by first of November or sooner → §6 (Clough) / §5.
- Eight-month alternate figure noted; reckon by Clough's six → §5 CONTESTED + §6 (handled as discrepancy per pack directive).
- Commander refused to release them, kept them into winter → §5.
- 1 November 1759 ordered out; men "all swore that we would not do any more duty here" → §5 / §6 / §12 (SAFE).
- Next day all to guardhouse; round-robin petition (signed in a circle); officers labeled mutiny → §5.
- 3 November forgave the mutiny; 140 men shipped for Boston; rest ordered to stay a month → §5.
- Four-pound winter bounty → §6 (11 Feb entry).
- "God only knows who will see the end" (January 1760) → §6 (SAFE).

## The third army (Native + ranger woodland war)
- Native woodland war the only thing that worked in the forest → §9 premise / framing duty §13.
- "French and Indian War" centers British-colonial POV; British colonists named it for their enemies; Native nations fought on all sides and for themselves; their own aims (land, leverage, neutrality) → framing directive (top brief + §9 framing + §13).
- Monongahela 9 July 1755, ~10 mi east of present Pittsburgh; British flying column ~1,300 regulars+provincials → §9.
- Opposing force under Capt. Daniel Liénard de Beaujeu (killed); 891 total = 108 French marine troops, 146 Canadian militia, 637 Native warriors → §9.
- Nations: Ottawa, Ojibwa, Potawatomi, Abenaki, Lenni Lenape (Delaware), Huron; leaders Shingas (Lenape), Charles Michel Mouet de Langlade (French-Ojibwa) → §9.
- Pontiac (Ottawa) presence hedged "some accounts... others doubt it" → §9 CONTESTED.
- Warriors fought from cover; Braddock held tight road column = packed target → §9.
- British 457 killed, 450+ wounded; of 86 officers 26 killed/37 wounded; French/Native 39 killed, 57 wounded → §9 (flying-column figures, as instructed primary).
- Backwoodsmen had warned → §9 / §3.
- Washington found fault with regulars, restored order, formed rear guard in retreat → §9.
- Robert Rogers (NH) raised ranger companies 1755; long-range patrol/scout/ambush/raid → §9 Rogers' Rangers.
- Snowshoes, silent movement, cover, ambush, Native-derived tactics → §9.
- 28 Rules of Ranging, first written code; trained regular + provincial officers → §9.
- 1759 destructive raid on Abenaki village Saint-François (Odanak); people not scenery → §9 + framing note.
- Wolfe despised the rangers → §3 / §9.
- Sir William Johnson, Irish immigrant, settled Mohawk Valley 1738, learned Mohawk language/customs, Superintendent of Indian Affairs; job = win over Iroquois Confederacy (Six Nations / Haudenosaunee), then neutral → §9.
- Mohawk most pro-British; Hendrick / Theyanoguin brought warriors; Battle of Lake George 1755, ~200 Mohawks + ~1,500 colonial troops; Hendrick killed → §9.
- Confederacy divided, own interests; French Jesuits among Seneca + Onondaga → those nations leaned French; Six Nations weighed land/leverage/survival, leaned neutral; both empires wanted their land; principals not auxiliaries → §9 + §13 framing.

## The bargain changes (Pitt)
- Late 1757 into 1758, Secretary of State William Pitt the Elder directing the war; reimburse colonial governments for troop-raising costs; treat colonies as allies not subordinates → §10.
- Raised standing of provincial officers, easing rank humiliation → §10 (flagged VERIFY for precise rank rule).
- "Almost unlimited funds" / open purse → §10.
- Manpower poured in; conquest of Canada: Louisbourg 1758, Quebec 1759, Montreal 1760 → §10.
- The debt-and-taxation through-line (bill coming due) → §10 irony/through-line (kept light; full story handed to War Finance pill).

## What they carried home
- War ended 1763, France driven from mainland, Britain master east of Mississippi → §10 / general (1763 end-date; "east of the Mississippi" is the standard outcome — see VERIFY note).
- Catastrophe for Native nations; war ultimately over their land; no second empire left to play against; colonists pushed west; dispossession the truest result → §13 framing duty + top brief framing (human cost/dispossession SHIP-BLOCKING requirement).
- British colonists named the war for their "Indian" enemies → framing directive.
- 30%+ of Massachusetts military-age men served a tour (per Fred Anderson) → §7.
- Tens of thousands served across the colonies over nine years → §7 (qualitative, no hard total per GAP).
- Most deaths from disease not battle: smallpox, dysentery, louse-borne camp fever (typhus); far more buried from sickness than fell to French muskets → §8 (pattern, no F&I-specific ratio per CONTESTED/GAP).
- Two convictions: (1) regulars treated them as inferiors/second-class, flogged/outranked/worked/held past terms; (2) they could raise and lead armies, held by agreement not the lash → §11 thesis.
- A generation of Revolution officers, Washington above all, learned soldiering here → §11 + §2/§9 + Anderson "The War That Made America."
