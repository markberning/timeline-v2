## CLARITY (one-clause insertions at first use)
- **BLOCKERS:** define **delegate** ("the representatives each colony sent"); define **resolution/resolve** once ("a formal statement voted by a body, carrying its authority"); define **preamble** ("the introductory 'whereas' statement that gives the reasons for what follows" — needed to feel Adams's May 15 sleight-of-hand); explain **abstention vs unanimity** (New York neither yes nor no, so no colony voted no; NY's convention assented July 9, letting Congress engross it as "unanimous").
- SHOULD-FIX: gloss pamphlet (short, cheap, unbound booklet — the era's mass-market op-ed); broadside (a large single sheet printed on one side, posted publicly); petition's constitutional weight (the formal legal channel a subject had to the king — which is why refusing to receive it mattered); engrossed ("copied in formal script onto parchment as the official document"); preliminary vs formal vote (committee of the whole = relaxed-rules debate, then the recorded vote); bridge "provincial congress" at Halifax ("North Carolina's own extralegal assembly"); one concrete Association-enforcement beat (violator's name printed = neighbors stop trading with him); distinguish Dickinson STAYING AWAY (absent, not counted) from New York ABSTAINING (present, declining); one plain sentence on adopted (voted to approve, July 4) vs signed (names on parchment, mostly Aug 2); Hessians = soldiers RENTED from German princes, largest contingent from Hesse-Cassel.
- NITs: colonial secretary gloss; Richard Penn (grandson of Pennsylvania's founder); Caesar Rodney (a Delaware delegate, away on business); Colonel John Nixon (a Philadelphia militia officer); "express riders" → fast courier riders on first use.
