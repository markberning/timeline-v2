## SHOULD-FIX

### SF-1 · No-quarter passage: carry the pack's amplification caveat (Section 2, criterion 2)

Draft:

> "Some of the ugliest evidence of the morning comes from the winners' own
> mail: British and Hessian letters home boast of bayoneting rebels as they
> tried to give up, and the American camps filled with stories that the
> Hessians took no prisoners."

The pack (§4) instructs: "Handle plainly; **both panic and propaganda
amplified it**, but period British letters themselves supply the worst
lines." The draft keeps the second half of that discipline (the letters as
the primary evidence, the camp stories framed as stories) but drops the
first. As written, the camp stories sit directly after the letters and read
as *corroboration* — two independent streams confirming a no-quarter morning
— when the pack's framing is that the rumor stream was inflated by panic and
propaganda and the letters are the part that stands on its own. That
matters for fairness to the Hessians specifically: they are the figures the
American rumor mill demonized, and the page should not lend the rumors the
letters' evidentiary weight by adjacency.

**Exact fix:** one clause on the camp-stories half, e.g.:

> "...and the American camps filled with stories that the Hessians took no
> prisoners — stories that panic and propaganda swelled beyond what anyone
> could count, though the letters needed no swelling."

(Any phrasing works so long as the amplification caveat lands on the rumors
and the letters keep their evidence-level status. The QUOTE block itself is
fine: attributed to "one British officer's letter," printed source named,
no atrocity-porn elaboration, and the ledger already routes the byte-exact
re-verification to the fact critic.)

## NIT

### N-1 · Howe dossier bio: the Bunker Hill juxtaposition reads as the answer on a standalone surface (criterion 5)

Draft (Howe commander card):

> "A combat legend since Quebec in 1759 ... and the man who paid for Bunker
> Hill's frontal assaults, Howe executed the war's most elegant flanking
> victory at Brooklyn, then halted before the American works rather than
> storm them..."

In the prose, the halt debate is handled exactly right — the MEANWHILE block
gives Howe's stated reasons on record, names the critics, and badges the
Bunker Hill reading explicitly: "that reading is interpretation, not
record." But the dossier card is a standalone surface, and "the man who paid
for Bunker Hill's frontal assaults ... then halted" quietly implies the
causal reading the prose disclaims. Mitigations: the wording is the pack's
own §9 bio card verbatim, and the card closes on "Critics never forgave the
halt," which is attribution of a sort. This is therefore authorized and only
a nit — but if a one-word soften is wanted: "...then halted before the
American works rather than storm them, choosing siege spades over bayonets
— a choice critics tied to Bunker Hill ever after." That moves the linkage
to the critics on the card itself.

### N-2 · "The kind of move armies attempt only in books" (Section 2)

> "He proposed the kind of move armies attempt only in books: march the main
> body all night, around the far end of the American line, and appear behind
> it at breakfast."

Framing-wise this is fine — it credits Clinton's plan as real soldiering,
which criterion 2 asks for. But it is a meta-narrator flourish of the kind
the war voice rules sand down, and it slightly over-gilds a maneuver the
same page shows succeeding mostly because the door was left open. Flagging
for the storytelling critic's lane; no framing harm if kept.

### N-3 · Jamaica Pass blame lands mostly on Putnam — pack-authorized, but note the choice

Putnam's card: "The forward line was his to watch; the Jamaica Pass, his
open back door, was covered by five mounted officers." The card is the
pack's §9 wording, and the prose spreads the failure honestly (Washington
"outgeneraled, caught with his force split and his left flank open"; "Three
commanders in ten days, and the ground belonged to none of them"). The
pack's instruction to drop Sullivan's self-serving paid-patrol claim is
correctly honored, with the side effect that no one else shares the
five-officers decision on the page. Nothing to change — recording that the
blame distribution was a deliberate, pack-backed choice, not an oversight.

### N-4 · Marsh drownings carried in one clause

> "most made it, and some men drowned trying."

The pack itself gives the drownings exactly one clause (§4: "~80 yards of
water; some men drowned"), so the draft cannot say more without inventing.
Proportion is nonetheless honest — see the criterion-3 verdict below. No
change requested; recording that this was checked.

## PER-CRITERION VERDICTS

**1. Defeat honesty — PASS.** The defeat is stated flatly on every surface:
the dossier note ("The biggest battle of the war was lost in a morning to a
night march around an unguarded pass"), the sides note ("The forward line
was destroyed"), Washington's own card ("he was outgeneraled"), and above
all the Section 3 line that anchors the whole page: "The army had survived,
and that is the entire meaning of the Battle of Long Island. Everything
else about it was disaster." The command failures are all named in the
American prose, not euphemized: the unguarded pass, the five mounted
officers (stated three separate times), the split army, the feint
suspicion, the Greene→Sullivan→Putnam shuffle ("the ground belonged to none
of them"). The Maryland stand does not launder the rout: the same paragraph
that carries the charges carries the two-thirds losses, the lost casualty
list, and the unknown grave, and the section ends on a thousand-plus
prisoners and Howe's halt, not on glory. The post-battle paragraph (militia
deserting "in droves," the verified "dismayed, Intractable, and Impatient to
return" quote, the prison ships) forecloses any moral-victory reading.

**2. Both-sides — PASS (with SF-1).** Howe's plan and execution get full
credit ("the war's most elegant flanking victory," pack language); Clinton
is credited as the plan's author with his local knowledge explained; Grant's
feint is respected as competent soldiering ("under orders to pin rather than
press," "which he did all morning while the real blow landed behind it") and
his boast is correctly badged as reported speech. The Hessians appear as
professionals, not bogeymen — von Heister is named, dignified by Stirling's
choice to surrender to him rather than the British. The no-quarter material
is told at evidence level from the winners' own letters, one quote, no
elaboration — the only gap is the dropped amplification caveat (SF-1).

**3. Maryland 400 — PASS.** Textbook handling: "Posterity calls them the
Maryland 400" hands the label to posterity in so many words; the real count
(~260–270) is in prose AND in the section blurb ("about 270 Marylanders" —
the blurb resisting the famous number is the detail most drafts miss); "256"
is unpacked as killed-and-missing combined with the list lost and many
later proved captured; charges are "repeated"/"again and again," never a
number; the mass grave gets its own paragraph ending "The burial site is
genuinely unknown." The Washington "brave fellows" line and the Field 1869
line are both explicitly badged (anonymous/secondhand; "a historian's
verdict, not a participant's"). Proportion: the rout, the gauntlet, the
hand-to-hand woods fighting, the marsh escape, and the prisoners all stand
in the same section at honest weight.

**4. The escape — PASS.** Chance gets its full, explicit share, in order:
"What saved them first was weather"; the nor'easter that "kept the British
warships from beating up into the East River"; the adverse wind that
"nearly wrecked the timetable"; the ebb tide and rain covering the oars;
and "Toward dawn the timetable failed anyway. And then a dense fog settled
over Brooklyn and stayed past sunrise." Skill gets its share too (the ruse,
the Marblehead mariners, the Mifflin near-disaster caught) — but the draft
twice shows the plan *failing* and luck rescuing it, which is exactly the
pack's balance. Tallmadge's "providential" is his word, with the 1858
memoir gap flagged in prose.

**5. The Howe halt — PASS (N-1 nit on the bio card).** The MEANWHILE block
is the model: stated reasons on record, subordinates and Parliament as the
named critics, and "Historians have long read the shadow of Bunker Hill ...
that reading is interpretation, not record, but the halt itself changed the
war." No authorial fiat anywhere in prose; the only soft spot is the
standalone bio card's juxtaposition (pack-authorized).

**6. Loyalist Long Island + surfaces — PASS.** The Loyalists are agents,
not scenery and not traitors-by-default: "a reminder that this was a civil
war among Americans," the Queens 1775 vote as evidence of real allegiance,
Loyalist intelligence and guides as the battle-deciding contribution they
were, DeLancey's three battalions, and the occupation that "cut both ways
... Loyalist and Patriot alike." Woodhull is told at evidence level with
the "God save us all" legend correctly omitted entirely. Surfaces: the
Stirling caption says outright "No authentic life portrait of Stirling is
known; this is a later imagined likeness"; Sullivan and Howe carry the
speculative-print-series flags; Putnam's caption says "not a life portrait";
the Old Stone House caption discloses the 1930s reconstruction; the winner
line is an unhedged "Britain" with the casualty asymmetry shown plainly and
Howe's inflated 3,300 claim flagged in the footnote.
