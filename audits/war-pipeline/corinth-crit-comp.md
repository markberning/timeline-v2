## SHOULD-CONSIDER (non-blocking enrichments)

1. **Who *defended* Battery Robinett.** The draft renders Robinett almost entirely
   from the attacker's side (Maury, Rogers, the ditch). ABT's "Road to Corinth"
   names the defense: **Col. John W. Fuller's Ohio Brigade** held the works, and the
   **11th Missouri's bayonet charge** is credited with breaking the Confederate
   assault. The dual-POV mandate is technically met (Union *line* defends), but the
   Union *human* side of the climax is thinner than the Confederate side. A sentence
   naming Fuller's brigade / the 11th Missouri counter-charge would balance the
   battle's signature moment. *(NOT essential — the draft's Robinett is vivid and
   correct as-is; this is symmetry, not omission.)*

2. **Van Dorn's tactical incoherence.** Both ABT and AHC flag that Van Dorn planned
   on paper without ensuring his division commanders understood their assignments —
   a recurring "why the Confederates lost" thread (compounding the Hébert-ill and
   garbled-Hamilton-order beats already in the draft, though those are Union-side).
   One clause on Confederate command confusion would sharpen *why* the gamble failed
   beyond "ran out of daylight / threw infantry at earthworks." *(Optional color.)*

3. **Union engaged-strength nuance.** ABT gives Union strength **~15,000 engaged**
   vs the ~23,000 *present* the draft uses. Both are defensible (present-for-duty vs
   actually-in-line); the draft's ~23k vs ~22k framing is fine and matches WIKI/the
   pack, but a fact-checker may want to footnote that not all 23k were engaged. *(Pack
   already distinguishes "present" — no action needed unless precision is challenged.)*

4. **"Old Abe," the 8th Wisconsin eagle.** Corinth is one of the famous battles of
   the live bald-eagle regimental mascot. It is genuine local color but **correctly
   omitted** — it is trivia, not essential, and would dilute the cause-centered close.
   Listed only so the omission is a recorded decision, not an oversight.

## Fact-pack blind spots / verification flags (carry to fact-check, step 5)

- **LOC Robinett death-photo date error (confirmed live).** The LOC catalog item
  (loc.gov/item/2021644152) titles the Battery Robinett / Rogers image
  **"...killed on October 3, 1863."** That date is **wrong on two counts**: the
  battle was **1862**, and Rogers fell on **October 4**, not the 3rd. This is a
  known catalog transcription slip. The draft's §3 [VERIFY] flag on this photo is
  well-placed — if the photo is ever cited, **do not inherit the LOC date string**;
  cite the event date (Oct 4, 1862), not the catalog's "Oct 3, 1863." (Direct LOC
  fetch was 403-blocked, but the catalog title is visible in the search index and is
  the well-documented error.)

- **Confederate casualty total genuinely contested** — independent sources confirm
  the pack's split: **~4,233 (WIKI/AHC)** vs **~4,838 (ABT)**. Draft's range
  (~4,200–4,800) is correct; do not collapse to one number.

- **Union total** — WIKI/AHC ≈ 2,520; ABT ≈ 2,359. Draft says "about 2,500" — safe.

- **Quotes still needing verbatim/attribution confirmation** (carried from pack §7,
  not a comprehensiveness issue but flagged so step 5 owns them): Van Dorn "one hour
  more of daylight"; Rosecrans's burial words. Indirect fallbacks already drafted.

- **Contraband Camp "$5,000/month" + 300-acre cotton / 100-acre vegetable** figures —
  NPS page fetched confirms the **$4,000–5,000 profit by May 1863** and the
  church/school/hospital/6,000/55th-USCT facts (draft accurate). The wider
  acreage/monthly figures remain broader-interpretive; draft already flags them. OK.

- **AMA-by-name** — NPS says "benevolent organizations"; draft correctly does NOT
  name the American Missionary Association. Good; keep it generic unless step 5
  confirms.
