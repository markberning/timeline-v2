## MOST IMPORTANT MUST-ADD — EXPANDED NOTE

**The pursuit failure is the draft's one real gap.** Right now the draft says
Jackson's infantry was "exhausted" and he had "too little cavalry." That is
technically accurate but misses the vivid, ironic story:

- Ashby's cavalry — the very force Jackson needed to run down Banks — was
  scattered along the retreat route looting the captured supply wagons Banks
  had just abandoned. The soldiers who were supposed to pursue were busy helping
  themselves to the haul that made Banks famous as "Commissary Banks."
- Jackson, watching Banks escape, reportedly lamented: *"Never was there such
  a chance for cavalry"* and *"Oh, that my cavalry was in place."* This is a
  primary sourced quote from the NPS article on the battle.

The irony writes itself: the supplies that became "Commissary Banks" were also
the supplies that let Banks get away. One sentence in the pursuit passage
covering Ashby by name, the plundering, and Jackson's lament would make the
draft meaningfully stronger without any scope creep.

- Search result: "Ashby failed to cut off their retreat because his troopers were plundering captured wagons"
