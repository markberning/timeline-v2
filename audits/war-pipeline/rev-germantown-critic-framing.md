*Critic pass 2026-06-11 against `rev-germantown-draft.md` (all 372 lines, incl. FACT LEDGER
and compliance lists) and `rev-germantown-factpack.md` (sole source). Scope: fairness,

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

No blocker. The hard framing duties are met: the defeat is told plainly and assigned to
American decisions; the France debunk is told exactly at the pack's evidence level with
no re-smuggling on any surface (dossier note, winner line, outcome, blurb, and prose all
checked); Stephen is held at verdict level with the legend explicitly contrasted; Fiske
keeps his "is said"; Saratoga's ownership of the alliance is unambiguous twice; Musgrave
and Howe get their full due; the ~120-man garrison is honest everywhere and the 400
inflation is absent. Four should-fixes, all of the same species: the narrator
occasionally over-asserts past the pack's deliberately hedged frame, or adopts the
army's own exculpatory frame.

## BLOCKER

None.

## SHOULD-FIX

1. **"the famous wrong one" asserts a verdict the pack leaves argued.**
   Draft (S2): "Washington and his staff, coming up with the reserve, now had a decision
   to make, and they made the famous wrong one."
   The pack's frame is "the textbook advice that became **the battle's most argued-over
   decision**" (§3 Knox) — it never settles the argument. The draft is internally
   inconsistent on this: its own Knox bio says "the battle's most argued-over decision"
   and its dossier note says "historians have argued ever since," yet the prose narrator
   flatly rules the decision wrong. Asserting a contested interpretive verdict exceeds
   pack confidence.
   FIX: "…and they made the famous one, the most argued-over decision of the battle:
   the army stopped to take the castle." (The MEANWHILE Cliveden landing — "The harder
   lesson was what it cost to stop and besiege one" — already delivers the judgment at
   the evidence level; keep that.)

2. **"the battle's real enemy" launders the American failure into weather.**
   Draft (S2): "And then the battle's real enemy closed in. The dense ground fog…"
   This is the narrator adopting the army's own excuse — the draft itself reports, three
   paragraphs into S3, that "most of the men blamed the fog, the stone house, and
   accident, not the enemy, and certainly not themselves." The pack's causal frame is
   fog + too-complex plan + Chew House decision + friendly fire jointly (§1), and the
   brief's defeat-honesty bar is that the failure be told as substantially American
   decisions. Calling fog "the battle's real enemy" externalizes the loss AND
   undercredits the British soldiers who actually won the field.
   FIX: drop the epithet — e.g., "And then the fog closed in for good." Let the fog be
   the compounder the pack makes it, not the antagonist.

3. **"Somebody had to answer… The army's answer was Adam Stephen" insinuates a
   scapegoating past the record.**
   Draft (S3): "Somebody had to answer for the fog's worst disaster, the moment two
   American divisions fired into each other. The army's answer was Adam Stephen."
   Two problems in one bridge: (a) "somebody had to answer / the army's answer" implies
   a fall guy was selected, an interpretive insinuation the pack nowhere supports (and
   one that reads extra-loaded next to the bio's "prickly rivalry with his old chief");
   (b) "the fog's worst disaster" again assigns the collision to fog, where the pack's
   account is that Stephen's division drifted off its route (hedged "by most accounts" —
   which the draft carries correctly two sections earlier). Everything AFTER this bridge
   is exemplary — verdict byte-verbatim, legend contrasted not asserted, Lafayette
   stated without gloating. Only the on-ramp editorializes.
   FIX: a neutral bridge, e.g., "The collision of the two American divisions went to a
   court-martial within weeks, and the man on trial was Adam Stephen."

4. **"the largest city in America" — unpacked superlative.**
   Draft (S1): "Philadelphia, the rebel capital and the largest city in America."
   True historically, but it is not in the pack (§1/§2 give only "the rebel capital")
   and it is not on the draft's own deliberate-glosses list (which covers Cornwallis,
   Vergennes, Lafayette only) — so it is leakage, and a superlative at that.
   FIX: cut "and the largest city in America," or the fact critic packs it. Flagging
   cross-domain for the fact pass either way.

## NIT

1. **Winner stat flourish:** "the army that attacked marches away prouder than some
   armies after victories" — the morale paradox is pack-HIGH as mood, but this
   comparative has no pack referent. The pack's own winner-line material offers "the
   defeat that read like a promise," which is sharper and sourced. (The verdict line
   "British victory · the attack that mattered anyway" is proportionate as-is: result
   first, paradox second, Saratoga keeps the alliance.)

2. **Cliveden photo caption:** "the stone house that stopped an army" — overweights the
   house as the singular cause; the pack says it "swallowed the American reserve" while
   plan, fog, and the collision did the rest. "The stone house that swallowed the
   American reserve" (or "stopped the reserve") is the pack-true version.

3. **"Paoli, repaid."** The narrator briefly endorses the revenge ledger rather than
   reporting it. The pack sanctions the symmetry beat and the evidence-level form
   ("British officers remembered cries of…") is correctly used in the same sentence —
   the two-word narrator stamp on top is the only part that takes a side. Consider
   cutting and letting the remembered cries carry it. (Grey, for the record, is NOT
   cartooned: "the same 'No Flint' Grey of the Paoli bayonets" is identification, not
   indictment, and is the pack's sanctioned beat.)

4. **Garrison-number hedge consistency:** the ~120 figure is honest on every surface,
   but two spots render it bare ("a building defended by 120 men" in S2; "defended by
   120 men" implied again in the Cliveden MEANWHILE) where the pack's figure is "about
   120." Keep the "about" everywhere so the precision never exceeds the pack's.
