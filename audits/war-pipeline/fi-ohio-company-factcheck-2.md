## A. Pickawillany section (lines ~133–141) — ✅ CONFIRMED

- ✅ Pickawillany a Miami (Twightwee) town on the **west bank of the Great Miami River**,
  near **Piqua, Ohio**. [Wikipedia, *Pickawillany* — "west bank of the Great Miami River…
  near the modern city of Piqua, Ohio."]
- ✅ Founded by **Memeskia in late 1747** after breaking with the French. [Wikipedia,
  *Pickawillany* — "in late 1747."]
- ✅ **"Old Briton" (British) / "La Demoiselle" (French).** [Wikipedia, *Memeskia* — "known
  as 'Old Briton' by the British and as 'La Demoiselle' by the French."]
- ✅ **"roughly 400 families"** — CONFIRMED AS FAMILIES (not people, not traders).
  Wikipedia, *Pickawillany*: **"By 1750, Pickawillany contained four hundred Indian families"**
  (est. 1,200–1,600 people). The draft's "roughly 400 families" is correct.
- ✅ Largest center of British trade in the Ohio Country — supportable (see note 1 below).
- ✅ Reason for the break: French fur **monopoly = worse goods/prices**; turned to
  **Pennsylvania traders**. [Wikipedia, *Memeskia* — opposed French "dominance, monopoly
  and lower barter prices in the fur trade"; admitted "British traders and settlers from
  Pennsylvania."]
- ✅ Raid **21 June 1752**; **Charles Michel de Langlade**; **~240 Ottawa and Ojibwe** (see
  note 2); most Miami men **away hunting**; **~13 Miami + an English blacksmith** killed (see
  note 3); Pennsylvania traders **captured**; town **burned**. [Wikipedia, *Pickawillany*;
  American Battlefield Trust; Legends of America.] On "away hunting" specifically, Wikipedia
  *Pickawillany* states the attackers "found the town nearly deserted, **as most of the
  population was away hunting**" — draft's "most of the Miami men were away hunting" is fine.
- ✅ Memeskia killed and **reportedly boiled and eaten** — the draft's attributed framing
  ("by the accounts that come down to us, reportedly") is exactly right. The killing is
  settled; the boiled-and-eaten detail is what the standard sources report. [Wikipedia,
  *Pickawillany*: "killed, boiled and eaten 'within a hundred yards of the fort' in front of
  his Miami men"; Wikipedia, *Memeskia*: "boiled and ritually cannibalized" to "symbolize the
  victors' extreme distaste for his friendship with the English."] The draft presents it as
  reported + attributes the symbolic motive — correct handling, no embellishment.
- ✅ Survivors **abandoned Pickawillany and drifted back toward the French**; often called
  **an opening action of the war**. [Wikipedia, *Pickawillany* — "precursor to the wider
  British-French conflict that would become the French and Indian War"; Legends of America —
  "one of the events leading up to the French and Indian War."]

## B. Dual-claim clause (line ~51) — ✅ CONFIRMED
Virginia claimed the Ohio under its old **sea-to-sea (1609) charter** "from sea to sea, west
and northwest"; Pennsylvania claimed the same upper Ohio as a **westward extension** of its
**1681 charter**; the **Forks the crux**. [virginiaplaces.org, *Virginia–Pennsylvania
Boundary* / *Cessions* — VA "1609 charter… upper Ohio"; PA "westward extension of its colonial
boundaries"; Wikipedia, *Ohio Country*.] Draft's clause is accurate.

## C. Andrew Montour at Logstown 1752 / "Eghnisara" (line ~117) — ✅ CONFIRMED
Primary treaty record (*Treaty of Logg's Town, 1752*, VMHB 13 (1906); UNL Early Treaties):
**"Mr. Andrew Montour, Interpreter,"** and the Half-King "directing his speech to **Eghnisara,
which is Mr. Montour's Indian Name**… 'you are Interpreter between us and our Brethren, which
we are well pleased at.'" Exact match to the draft (interpreter + addressed as Eghnisara +
treated as more than a translator).

## D. "Race for the Forks" changes (line ~153) — ✅ CONFIRMED
- ✅ French force **"500 or more"** under **Claude-Pierre Pécaudy de Contrecoeur**. Wikipedia,
  *Fort Duquesne*: "a much larger French force of **five hundred** under… Contrecœur." SCWPA
  (colonialwarspa.org) gives **~600** soldiers and Indians. "500 or more" straddles both —
  accurate.
- ✅ **Arrived 17 April 1754 and demanded surrender.** SCWPA: "**on April 17, 1754, 600 French
  soldiers and Indians led by… Contrecœur, arrived with at least four cannons, demanding
  surrender of the fort.**"
- ✅ Ward **gave up the position the next day (18 April).** Ward "asked if the British might
  wait until the following day to leave, and Contrecœur agreed"; the Fort Duquesne article's
  "By April 18… forcing the small British garrison to surrender" is consistent. Draft's "17
  April arrival/demand, surrender the next day" is correct. (The bare Fort Duquesne Wikipedia
  line could read as a single April 18 event; the SCWPA day-by-day chronology confirms the
  draft's 17→18 sequence.)

## E. "1748/49" old-style-calendar gloss (line ~33) — ✅ CONFIRMED
Britain used the Julian/old-style calendar with the **legal year beginning 25 March (Lady
Day)**; dates between 1 Jan and 25 Mar were **double-dated** (e.g., "1748/49"). [Wikipedia,
*Old Style and New Style dates*; CT State Library; oldroots.co.uk — "25th February 1748/49…
shows the actual year (1748) and the year converted to the Gregorian Calendar (1749)."]
The draft's gloss ("year began in late March, so a winter date carried the year on either side
of that turn") is accurate. 16 March 1748/49 falls just inside the double-dating window. ✓
