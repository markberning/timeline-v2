## Claim-by-Claim Verdict

### SECTION: the-retreat

**Claim: July 1, 1862 — sixth and final of the Seven Days Battles.**  
✅ Confirmed across all sources (Wikipedia, CWSAC VA021, NPS, ABT, Encyclopedia Virginia).

**Claim: Lee's offensive June 25 to July 1, 1862.**  
✅ Confirmed. Seven Days = June 25–July 1. (Wikipedia Seven Days; NPS CWSAC.)

**Claim: Sequence — Mechanicsville, Gaines's Mill, Savage's Station, Glendale.**  
✅ All confirmed as named Seven Days engagements. Draft does not claim they are the complete list; names are accurate.  
⚠️ MINOR NOTE: The draft omits Oak Grove (June 25, the first engagement) and lists "Savage's Station" but not Garnett's/Golding's Farm. Neither is an error — the draft is a non-exhaustive name-drop for context — but Oak Grove as the actual opener is worth knowing for future cross-checks.

**Claim: After Gaines's Mill on June 27, McClellan made his decision to withdraw south toward the James.**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill," ABT: McClellan began withdrawing after Gaines's Mill.

**Claim: At Glendale (June 30), Lee nearly cut off the retreating column and failed.**  
✅ Confirmed. All sources.

**Claim: By July 1, entire Army of the Potomac united on one field — "for the first time in the whole week."**  
✅ Confirmed. ABT "10 Facts" Fact 2; Wikipedia.

**Claim: Malvern Hill — broad, flat-topped plateau, ~100 feet above surrounding terrain.**  
✅ Confirmed. Encyclopedia Virginia, NPS Richmond NBP, NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory.

**Claim: Strength from open fields of fire, not height.**  
✅ Confirmed. NPS Richmond NBP: "a modest elevation whose strength derived from open, gently sloping fields providing extensive fields of fire."

**Claim: North face — open, gently sloping farm field, "little cover for hundreds of yards, in places more than a mile."**  
✅ Confirmed. NPS Richmond NBP, ABT, Wikipedia: open ground of 800+ yards to over a mile in places.

**Claim: Boggy creeks, ravines, dense woods guarded the flanks.**  
✅ Confirmed. NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory: Western Run, swampy drainage bottoms, dense forest.

**Claim: James River lay "one to two and a half miles to the south."**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia, Encyclopedia Virginia, NPS.

**Claim: Col. Henry J. Hunt — "army's chief of artillery."**  
⚠️ PRECISION FLAG — NOT A MUST-FIX but worth flagging. Hunt's formal title was commander of the Artillery Reserve, not technically the army's "chief of artillery" — that post did not formally exist in its modern sense in 1862. However, Wikipedia "Henry Jackson Hunt" and the fact pack both describe him in this functional role. The draft's inline definition ("the officer in charge of the cannon") is accurate to his function. Sources treat him as the de facto artillery chief. This is acceptable shorthand for a lay audience.

**Claim: Hunt massed batteries "tier on tier along the crest," totals "up toward 250 guns altogether."**  
✅ Confirmed with correct framing. Wikipedia "Henry Jackson Hunt": "his 250 guns repelled repeated Confederate infantry assaults." Draft correctly frames this as "up toward 250" and explicitly describes definitional confusion among counts (forward line vs. reserve vs. total). Encyclopedia Virginia gives 107 cannon employed at the climax; ABT gives ~40 on the ridge crest. The draft's handling — acknowledging the confusion and giving the ~250 total as a total-across-all-positions figure — is accurate and appropriately hedged.

**Claim: Battery of heavy 32-pounder howitzers on the right flank.**  
✅ Confirmed. Iron Brigader; fact pack.

**Claim: Three Union gunboats including USS Galena added fire from the James.**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia: USS Galena, Jacob Bell, Aroostook. (The draft says "including the USS Galena" — accurate; Galena is also where McClellan went, making it the most notable of the three.)

### SECTION: it-was-murder

**Claim: Lee believed "one more hard blow" could destroy the retreating force.**  
✅ Confirmed. ABT, Wikipedia: Lee sought to catch and crush the Union army before it reached the James.

**Claim: Two-part plan — Confederate batteries deliver converging fire to silence Union guns, then infantry assaults.**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia, Essential Civil War Curriculum, ABT.

**Claim: Confederate guns engaged "one at a time instead of all together, never managed their crossfire."**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia: guns "engaged one-by-one instead of in unison"; Hunt fired first and prevented coordinating crossfire.

**Claim: D.H. Hill called Confederate artillery effort "most farcical."**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia, citing Battles and Leaders; ABT "10 Facts" Fact 5. This is D.H. Hill's own word, published in Battles and Leaders.

**Claim: Orders drafted by Lee's staff; Armistead's yell as signal for fifteen brigades.**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia: orders drafted by Col. Robert Chilton (Lee's chief of staff); Armistead's yell as signal for fifteen brigades — "a fatal command-and-control flaw."

**Claim: "Bad map mislabeled one of the key roads" — specifically Willis Church Road labeled "Quaker Road," delaying Magruder by hours.**  
✅ Confirmed. ABT "10 Facts" Fact 4: Lee's map mislabeled Willis Church Road as "Quaker Road"; local guides led Confederates down wrong route; Longstreet spotted the error. Essential Civil War Curriculum: guides led Magruder down wrong "Quaker Road." NPS CWSAC also confirms. Draft correctly calls it "Quaker Road" mislabel. The road is Willis Church Road; the Confederate map called it "Quaker Road." This is accurate.

**Claim: Piecemeal/uncoordinated assaults "across the afternoon and into the evening" — "around twenty separate Confederate brigades."**  
✅ Confirmed. NPS Richmond NBP; ABT: "approximately 20 separate Confederate brigades attacked at different times." Wikipedia confirms the piecemeal nature across afternoon (3:30 PM) through ~8:30 PM.

**Claim: Canister fire described as "tin cans packed with iron balls that turned a cannon into a giant shotgun."**  
✅ Accurate plain-language description of canister ammunition. Not a factual claim requiring a specific source — standard historical description.

**Claim: "Not one Confederate soldier reached the top of the hill."**  
✅ Confirmed. ABT article: "no Confederate reached the crest of the hill." ABT battle summary: Confederate attacks "stopped short." Wikipedia. NPS Richmond NBP: "most attacks sputtered and stalled well short of the hill's crest." (The ABT article adds that "some brigades penetrated to within 75–100 yards of Union cannons" — but did not reach the crest. Draft's claim is confirmed.)

**Claim: "A large share of those losses came from the artillery" — draft explicitly says no precise percentage exists.**  
✅ The draft's framing ("a large share," "no precise accounting") is correct and responsible. No secondary source reviewed provides a sourced percentage. The claim that artillery was the dominant killer is supported by the tactical record (charges broken before musket range), Captain Snow's Battery B firing 688 rounds (Iron Brigader), and Hunt's 250-gun deployment. The explicit no-percentage disclaimer in the draft is verified and should remain.

**Claim: D.H. Hill "led one of the largest of those charges himself — five brigades, more than eight thousand men."**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia: "Five brigades with approximately 8,200 men." Essential Civil War Curriculum: same figure. Both sources confirm Hill's charge was ~6 PM with five brigades, uncoordinated waves.

**Claim: Hill's verdict — "It was not war—it was murder." — attributed to "his own account," "written more than twenty years later," published in Battles and Leaders.**  
✅ SOURCE AND ATTRIBUTION: Confirmed via multiple secondary sources (Essential Civil War Curriculum, Wikipedia "Daniel Harvey Hill," Encyclopedia Virginia, ABT). The attribution to Battles and Leaders of the Civil War, vol. 2 is consistent across all secondary sources.

⚠️ QUOTE WORDING VARIANT (carry-forward VERIFY, not a Must-Fix): Wikipedia "Daniel Harvey Hill" renders the quote as "It wasn't war; it was murder" (semicolon, contraction). Encyclopedia Virginia, Essential Civil War Curriculum, and the fact pack render it as "It was not war—it was murder" (em-dash, no contraction). The Perseus/Tufts text of Battles and Leaders vol. 2 (chapter 7.52) did not return Hill's chapter — it returned Porter's account — so the original printed text could not be directly transcribed in this pass. Both variants refer to the same passage. The em-dash form is the majority scholarly rendering. The draft uses the em-dash form. This is acceptable but the original should be transcribed before final print (carry-forward, already flagged in both fact pack and draft).

**Claim: Hill "that same morning" had reconnoitered the Union position and warned "in so many words" that if McClellan was there in strength, the smart thing was to leave him alone. Rendered as paraphrase, not direct quote.**  
✅ Confirmed as paraphrase. The morning quote — "If General McClellan is there in force, we had better let him alone" — is confirmed by Essential Civil War Curriculum (citing Battles and Leaders) and ABT. Draft renders it as paraphrase explicitly ("in so many words"), which is the correct approach given that the exact wording was not transcribed from the original.

### SECTION: richmond-saved

**Claim: Union losses "roughly 3,000 men." Confederate losses "somewhere in the range of 5,300 to 5,650."**  
✅ CASUALTY RANGE — VERIFIED AND CORRECTLY HANDLED. 

The sources break down as follows:
- **Union:** Wikipedia infobox 3,007; CWSAC VA021 ~3,000; NPS Richmond NBP "roughly 3,000"; Encyclopedia Virginia 3,007. ABT summary/Essential Civil War Curriculum give ~2,100 (likely counting killed+wounded only, excluding 818 missing). Draft uses "roughly 3,000" — matches the most common authoritative figure (CWSAC, NPS, Wikipedia).
- **Confederate:** Wikipedia infobox 5,650; Encyclopedia Virginia 5,650; CWSAC VA021 5,355; NPS "more than 5,300"; ABT summary ~5,600; Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles" gives 5,355 at Malvern Hill. 

The draft uses "5,300 to 5,650" as a range, which accurately brackets the spread between the CWSAC official figure (5,355) and the Wikipedia/Encyclopedia Virginia detailed breakdown (5,650). This is the correct and honest approach.

**Claim: "McClellan had left the immediate battlefield before the main fighting, going aboard the gunboat USS Galena on the James River and riding off to inspect Harrison's Landing."**  
✅ Confirmed across all sources. Wikipedia: left for Harrison's Landing aboard USS Galena. ABT "10 Facts" Fact 3: spent June 30 and Malvern Hill aboard the Galena. Iron Brigader: "on one of the James River gunboats." Essential Civil War Curriculum: "boarded a gunboat to Harrison's Landing on the James River." 

⚠️ NUANCE NOTE (not a Must-Fix, but carry awareness): Wikipedia and Essential Civil War Curriculum note McClellan "returned to the field at Porter's urging, remaining on the right side away from the main action." The draft says he "left the immediate battlefield before the main fighting" and does not claim he was absent for the entire battle — this framing is accurate. The fact that he briefly returned to the right flank during the fighting (not the main action area) doesn't contradict the draft's claim.

**Claim: "Command on the hill fell to Brig. Gen. Fitz John Porter (North), his V Corps commander."**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia, ABT, Encyclopedia Virginia, Essential Civil War Curriculum.  
⚠️ MINOR PRECISION NOTE: Porter's V Corps is confirmed. His rank at Malvern Hill was Brig. Gen. (later Maj. Gen.). Sources consistently call him "Brig. Gen." at this battle. Draft says "Brig. Gen. Fitz John Porter" — correct.

**Claim: McClellan "mocked for sitting on a gunboat while other men fought" — critics during his 1864 presidential run.**  
✅ Confirmed. Iron Brigader ("The Craven" poem, Currier & Ives cartoons during 1864 presidential campaign).

**Claim: Army left Malvern Hill "around eleven at night" July 1, "marched away in heavy rain," reached Harrison's Landing "the next day," "tearing down a bridge behind it."**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia: army abandoned Malvern Hill at approximately 11 PM July 1. Essential Civil War Curriculum: "night of July 1–2, reaching Harrison's Landing by late July 2." Iron Brigader and Wikipedia confirm heavy rain. Wikipedia confirms Turkey Island Bridge destroyed behind the column.

**Claim: "Lee declined pursuit (rain and exhaustion); Jackson reportedly wanted to pursue and was overruled."**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; fact pack §5. Framed correctly as "reportedly" for Jackson — appropriate hedge since this is secondary-source consensus, not a primary document quote.

**Claim: "Roughly two years before Union forces came that close to Richmond again."**  
✅ Confirmed. Essential Civil War Curriculum: "approximately 23 months." Draft says "roughly two years" — accurate rounded expression of ~23 months.

**Claim: Lincoln losing faith in McClellan; Pope's new army forming; war shifting toward Second Bull Run (August 1862).**  
✅ Confirmed. Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles," ABT, Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill."

**Claim: Richmond Dispatch — "No captain that ever lived could have planned or executed a better plan."**  
✅ Quote confirmed present in Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill" (direct quote). Carry-forward: specific issue date/date of publication unconfirmed in any source reviewed. Draft uses it as a direct quote per pack — acceptable, but original issue should be confirmed before final print.

**Claim: Lee to his wife — "Our success has not been as great or complete as we should have desired."**  
✅ Quote confirmed in Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill" (direct quote attributed to Lee, letter to his wife after the Seven Days). Carry-forward: full citation (letter date, collection) not confirmed in sources reviewed. Draft uses it per pack — acceptable, but full letter citation should be confirmed before final print.

**Claim: "The campaign went, decisively, to the South" — Lee drove McClellan away in one week; Peninsula Campaign effectively over; ~two years before Union forces came close again.**  
✅ Confirmed. CWSAC VA021, Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles," ABT: the strategic result was Confederate despite the tactical defeat at Malvern Hill.

### SHOULD-FIX (recommended corrections before ship)

**SHOULD-FIX 1 — Carry the "It wasn't war; it was murder" wording variant as an explicit VERIFY before final print.**  
The draft uses the em-dash form ("It was not war—it was murder"). Wikipedia "Daniel Harvey Hill" renders it with a semicolon and contraction ("It wasn't war; it was murder"). The original Battles and Leaders vol. 2 text (Hill's own chapter) was not accessible via Perseus in this pass. The em-dash form is the majority scholarly rendering and is used by Encyclopedia Virginia, Essential Civil War Curriculum, and the fact pack. This is a low-risk carry-forward, not an active error, but the exact punctuation and wording from the printed primary source should be confirmed before final publication. No fix to the draft is required now — only a pre-print transcription check.

**SHOULD-FIX 2 — Carry the Richmond Dispatch and Lee's letter to his wife as VERIFY-before-final-print.**  
Both quotes are confirmed as appearing in Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill" and the fact pack, but neither source provides an original publication date (Dispatch) or full letter citation (Lee). Both are used as direct quotes in the draft. They are almost certainly authentic — they appear in multiple academic secondary sources — but the standard for direct quotation demands original sourcing. Flag these two in the draft's VERIFY section; do not alter the quotes themselves.

**SHOULD-FIX 3 — "Some of his subordinates objected" (counterattack advocates) — keep generic.**  
The draft already handles this correctly (generic "some of his subordinates," no names). Secondary sources note the sentiment but do not consistently identify specific subordinates. No change needed; flag is resolved by the draft's current phrasing.

### INFORMATIONAL (no action required)

- The ABT gives Union casualties as ~2,100 vs. Wikipedia/CWSAC ~3,007/~3,000. The discrepancy is likely ABT counting killed+wounded only (2,100 ≈ 314 killed + 1,875 wounded), excluding the 818 missing. Draft's "roughly 3,000" matches NPS/CWSAC/Wikipedia and is the better figure.

- McClellan's brief return to the right flank (noted in Wikipedia and Essential Civil War Curriculum) does not contradict the draft's claim that he left before the main fighting and that Porter ran the defense. The draft correctly attributes command on the hill to Porter.
- Oak Grove (June 25) is the actual first Seven Days engagement; the draft's name-drop of "Mechanicsville, Gaines's Mill, Savage's Station, Glendale" is accurate for the battles named but non-exhaustive (not an error).
