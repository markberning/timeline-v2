## SHOULD-CONSIDER (enrichments — author/integrator discretion, non-blocking)

1. **The "forlorn hope" storming party (June 14).** Web-confirmed (Wikipedia, 31st Mass.,
   49th Mass. regimental histories): Banks called for ~200 volunteers — a named "Forlorn
   Hope" storming party — to lead the June 14 charge and lay fascines across the ditches.
   It's a vivid, true human-cost detail that sharpens the futility of the second assault and
   produced Medal of Honor actions (e.g., Pvt. Frederick N. Deland, 49th Mass., filled the
   ditch with fascines under fire). **Currently absent from both draft and fact pack.** A
   one-clause add in `the-doomed-charges` (June 14 ¶) would deepen the slaughter image
   without bloating it. SHOULD-CONSIDER, not required.

2. **When the Vicksburg news actually reached Gardner.** Multiple sources (Warfare History
   Network, History Tools, ABT) say a boat reached Port Hudson **on July 7** carrying word
   of Vicksburg's July 4 fall, and Gardner opened surrender talks then, formalizing July 9.
   The draft's "When word reached Gardner that Vicksburg was gone … On July 9 he
   surrendered" is accurate but compresses a ~2-day gap. Adding "word came down the river on
   July 7" would be a precise, evocative beat (the news literally arriving by boat). Optional.

3. **Banks's near-week delay after investing.** ABT and others note Banks waited ~5–6 days
   after surrounding the works (May 22 → May 27) before assaulting, letting Gardner
   strengthen the line. The draft says "Five days into the siege he ordered the first
   attack" — so the timing is there, but the draft frames the delay as Banks's choice to let
   "the math settle it" rather than a lapse that gave Gardner prep time. Minor framing
   nuance; not a gap.

4. **The siege's "novel" / first-of-its-kind siege-warfare features** (sustained mining,
   continuous saps, the forlorn hope) — collectively this was an unusually modern, European-
   style siege for the war. The draft conveys the texture ("the part the manuals were
   written for," saps + 3 mines) so this is COVERED in substance; only the explicit "this
   was advanced/modern siege craft" framing is optional.

## FACT-PACK BLIND SPOTS (for the fact-checker, step 4)

These are places the fact pack itself is silent or soft — flagging so the gate chain
resolves them, not asking the author to invent:

- **B1 — The July 7 boat-news detail is NOT in the fact pack.** Fact pack §5 item 6 says
  only "Once Gardner confirmed Vicksburg was gone." Web sources are consistent on July 7 by
  boat. If the integrator wants the precise beat (SHOULD-CONSIDER #2), the fact-checker
  should add it to the pack with a source first — do not let the author add it from memory.

- **B2 — The forlorn hope / June 14 storming party is NOT in the fact pack.** (SHOULD-
  CONSIDER #1.) Same rule: source it into the pack before any prose uses it. The Deland MoH
  is a verifiable hook if a human-cost detail is wanted.

- **B3 — Surrender-acceptance officer is muddled in the fact pack.** Fact pack §5 item 6
  reads "Capt. Thornton A. Jenkins / Capt. (Union) accepted the surrender" — garbled and
  unsourced. The draft wisely does NOT name a surrender-accepting officer, so no error
  shipped, but the fact-checker should either fix/drop this fact-pack line. (Historically
  the surrender was arranged via commissioners; Brig. Gen. George L. Andrews is commonly
  cited as receiving it.) Non-blocking because the draft omits it.

- **B4 — Native Guard casualty figure absent from the draft.** Fact pack §6 / web give the
  1st & 3rd Native Guard ~600 casualties on May 27 (ABT: "nearly 600"). The draft describes
  the losses qualitatively ("torn apart," "heavy losses each time") but never states a
  number. This is a defensible storytelling choice (the figure itself is contested and the
  point is courage-under-fire), but if the integrator wants one quantified anchor for the
  Native Guard specifically, ~600 is the web-supported figure to add to the pack. Optional.

- **B5 — Casualty grand-total handling.** Already correctly hedged by the author (battle
  ~3,800 in two assaults vs. ~4,000–5,000 disease, no single Union total). Web check
  confirms the divergence is real (ABT 5,000 Union battle vs. NPS-CWSAC 10,000 campaign-
  wide). The draft's two-toll split is the right call; flagging only so the fact-checker
  does NOT "fix" it into one number.
