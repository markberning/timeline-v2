# Franklin — CRITIC GATE 3: LOST CAUSE / FRAMING

**Verdict: FAIR — PASS.** This is, if anything, one of the more Lost-Cause-resistant
Franklin treatments possible. Slavery is named, in the section's own prose, with the
single best on-battlefield proof available. Hood's motive is kept a debate. No MUST-FIX
on the cause gate. Two SHOULD-FIX tilt-risks worth tightening, plus one shared
perspective gap (see comprehensiveness crit).

---

## FIRST CHECK (ship-blocking): does it name SLAVERY as the cause, in its OWN prose?

**YES. PASS — emphatically.** §4 says it flatly: **"These men died to preserve slavery."**
It does not hide behind "Hood's invasion" or "a brave doomed charge." It then *proves*
it on this battlefield, three ways:

1. **The Cleburne proposal** — the battle's most famous dead man proposed arming and
   freeing the enslaved, named slavery as a "source of weakness," and was suppressed by
   Davis. The draft's framing — "a Confederate general suggested trading slavery for
   survival, and his own government chose slavery" — is the cleanest possible in-prose
   proof of the war's cause, delivered through the battle's own central figure. This is
   exactly the right move and it is historically sound (see fact crit: all elements
   verified).
2. **The 44 enslaved people at Carnton** — the war's cause made literal on the very
   ground of the death-house field hospital. "The men who bled in the ditch charged for
   a government built on that property line." Strong.
3. **The USCT at Nashville** — freedpeople helping annihilate the army that marched to
   keep them enslaved. The closing inversion ("put out of its misery … by the very people
   its existence depended on owning") is pointed and accurate.

This is the opposite of the Lost Cause failure mode. The cause gate is not just met, it is
the structural climax of the section.

---

## Lost Cause magnet check (the special care item)

Franklin is the classic Lost Cause shrine — the gallant doomed charge, six dead generals,
Cleburne the martyr, blood on the porch. The brief asks: does the *tragedy of the charge*
sanitize *why* they were fighting?

**It does not — and the draft explicitly guards against it.** The pivot sentence in §4
is doing the exact work required: *"because the porch full of dead generals can make
Franklin feel like nothing but a brave, doomed waste — and it was a great deal more
specific than that. These men died to preserve slavery."* The draft *names the trap and
walks the reader out of it.* That is precisely the anti-Lost-Cause move. The valor is
acknowledged (the men were brave, the charge was doomed) but is never allowed to launder
the cause. PASS.

The Cleburne martyrdom — the single biggest Lost-Cause sentimentality risk — is handled
with real care: instead of "beloved Irish hero cut down," the draft uses Cleburne *against*
the romance, as the man who tried to make his own side confront slavery and was buried for
it. That converts the war's most sympathetic Confederate figure into the proof of the
Confederacy's purpose. Excellent framing judgment.

---

## Hood's motive — kept a debate? **YES. PASS.**

The brief's red line: Hood's motive must stay a *debated interpretation*, not an asserted
fact. The draft nails this. §1: *"What he did next — and why — is one of the war's genuine
historical arguments, and it's worth being honest about where fact ends and interpretation
begins. Many historians read the assault … as an act of fury and humiliation … Hood himself
gave a colder, strategic reason … Both readings survive in the sources."* This is textbook
correct — it attributes the rage-reading to "many historians," gives Hood's own stated
rationale alongside, and asserts neither. No single-cause motive is stated as fact.

---

## Soft-pedaling / euphemism / false balance / presentism sweep

- **Euphemism:** none. "Slavery," "the ownership of human beings," "kept human beings in
  chains," "held as property" — direct, repeated, unsoftened. No "peculiar institution"
  hedging, no "states' rights" dodge.
- **False balance:** none. The draft does not "both-sides" the cause. It treats Union
  victory as a *Confederate self-inflicted catastrophe* and the cause as settled
  (slavery), while keeping genuinely-contested items (Hood's motive, casualty count) open.
  Correct calibration: open the things historians dispute, close the things they don't.
- **Presentism:** controlled. The moral judgment ("the South chose slavery over survival")
  is grounded in the *period's own* evidence (Cleburne's contemporaries' reaction, Davis's
  order) rather than imposed from 2026. The draft lets 1864 Confederates condemn the
  Confederacy in their own words. That is the right way to make the moral point without
  presentism.
- **Gratuitous vs. sanitized human reality:** well-balanced. The horror (blood-stained
  floors, generals on the porch, a son dying yards from home) is rendered with weight but
  not wallowed in; nothing is sanitized, nothing is gore-for-gore's-sake.

## TILT RISKS — SHOULD-FIX

**SHOULD-FIX 1 — the defenders are nearly absent (shared with comprehensiveness crit).**
The section's sympathy flows almost entirely to the *attackers* — the doomed, brave,
tragically-led Confederates. The Union soldiers who actually *did the killing* are a
"wall of dug-in guns" and one stubborn colonel. Because Franklin's emotional gravity
already pulls Confederate (six dead generals, the porch, Tod Carter, Cleburne), a section
with almost no Union human presence risks tilting sympathetic-to-the-South *by structure
even though its argument is anti-Lost-Cause.* A beat from inside the works — the defenders'
experience of mowing down rank after rank — would re-balance the human sympathy and keep
the section's emotional center from sitting entirely with the men charging to preserve
slavery. **Recommend adding.** (Not a cause-gate failure; a sympathy-distribution tilt.)

**SHOULD-FIX 2 — "without ever being promoted past division command."** This clause
(§4 close) sits adjacent to the suppression story and can read as asserting that the
Confederacy *punished* Cleburne for the proposal by denying him promotion. The fact is
true (he died a division commander); the *causal* claim is what BOFT itself calls
speculative. As written it's defensible (the draft frames it as the leadership's choice
to "bury the man, professionally"), but it leans slightly toward asserting a contested
causal link. Either soften to keep it descriptive ("he was still a division commander
when he was sent to the cotton gin") or add a one-word hedge. Minor.

**Note (not a fix):** the enslaved people who sheltered in the Carter cellar (verified)
would, if added, *strengthen* the framing — the war's victims literally in the center of
the line, on the contested property. Captured under comprehensiveness SHOULD-ADD 1.

---

## Bottom line

The cause gate passes hard. Hood's motive stays a debate. The Lost Cause romance is named
and dismantled rather than indulged, and the Cleburne-as-proof move is a model of how to
de-romanticize the war's most sympathetic Confederate. The only real framing risk is
structural sympathy: the section is all-Confederate-faces and would benefit from one
Union-defender human beat. FAIR / PASS, two SHOULD-FIX.
