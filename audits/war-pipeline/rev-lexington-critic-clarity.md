# NEWCOMER-CLARITY CRITIC — Battles of Lexington & Concord (`rev-lexington`)

Draft reviewed: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-lexington-draft.md`
Reader model: zero prior knowledge. Sections, dossier surfaces, bios, and captions are each read independently; first use resets per section.

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The in-section gloss discipline is the best of any war draft I've checked: regulars, militia,
minute companies, Provincial Congress, flank companies, trunnions, deposition, flanking
parties, 6-pounders, and Charlestown Neck all get clean parenthetical definitions at first
use in section 1 or 2, and the causal machinery (why the alarm worked, why militia kept
arriving in relays, why the road was the battle) is genuinely explained, not asserted. The
failures are concentrated, fixable with one-clause insertions, and fall in three clusters:
(1) per-section person resets are broken in sections 2 and 3 (Pitcairn, Smith, Percy, Gage
appear cold in the section that carries the battle); (2) "Emerson" silently switches from
the Reverend William Emerson (section 1) to Ralph Waldo Emerson (sections 2–3) and a
newcomer WILL conflate them; (3) three load-bearing geography facts live only in the locator
caption or nowhere: Lexington is never placed on the road in prose, the reason for crossing
the water first is never given, and Charlestown-as-safety is never explained.

---

## BLOCKER

**B1 — Section 2 opens cold on Pitcairn (no name/role/side).**
> "Pitcairn's advance reached Lexington a little before 5 a.m. and found about 77 militiamen drawn up in parade-ground lines..."
Section 2 is read independently; this reader has never met Pitcairn. Fix first sentence:
"Major John Pitcairn (British), the Royal Marines officer leading the column's advance
companies, reached Lexington a little before 5 a.m. ..." (This also carries the
"regulars are ahead of the main body" structure a newcomer otherwise has to infer.)

**B2 — Percy is never introduced at EITHER first use (section 1 or section 2).**
Section 1: > "That message would produce Percy's brigade, and it would save the column's life."
Section 2: > "Percy saved it. The relief brigade, perhaps 1,000 to 1,400 men with two 6-pounders..."
"Percy" is a bare surname both times; a newcomer doesn't know he exists, which side he's on,
or why a brigade is coming. Fix section 1: "That message would produce a relief brigade
under Brigadier General Hugh, Earl Percy, and it would save the column's life." Fix
section 2: "Brigadier General Hugh, Earl Percy (British) saved it. His relief brigade, the
reinforcements Smith had called for before dawn, perhaps 1,000 to 1,400 men with two
6-pounders (field guns firing a six-pound ball), met the column east of Lexington green
between 2 and 3 p.m." — the "reinforcements Smith had called for" clause also closes the
causal loop check 4 demands (section 2 never says why a rescue brigade shows up at all;
that mechanism currently lives only in section 1).

**B3 — Smith and Gage appear cold in section 2.**
First section-2 uses:
> "...the grenadiers searched the locations marked on Gage's map..."
> "Smith then sat in Concord for about two hours, gathering wounded and hiring carriages..."
Neither is introduced in this section. Fix: "...marked on the map General Thomas Gage (the
British commander in Boston, who had ordered the raid) had supplied..." and "Lieutenant
Colonel Francis Smith (British), the expedition's commander, then sat in Concord for about
two hours..."

**B4 — "Emerson" is two different people and the draft never says so.**
Section 1 introduces "The town's minister, the Reverend William Emerson" (the diary quote).
Section 2 then says:
> "The phrase is not contemporary: Emerson wrote it in 1837, for the hymn sung at the dedication of the bridge monument."
Section 3 repeats: > "And Emerson's shot that rang round the world belongs to the bridge, sixty-two years after the fact."
The 1837 author is the poet Ralph Waldo Emerson, not the minister (who was long dead by
1837). A newcomer will read this as the same man from section 1 writing a hymn 62 years
later. Fix section 2: "the poet Ralph Waldo Emerson wrote it in 1837..." and section 3:
"Ralph Waldo Emerson's shot that rang round the world..." (If the fact pack supports it,
"grandson of Concord's minister" is a lovely connective, but only with the fact-checker's
sign-off; "the poet" alone is safe and sufficient.)

**B5 — Lexington is never placed on the map in prose.**
First prose mention, section 1: > "From Lexington the word stopped depending on any one man."
Section 2 opens with Pitcairn arriving there. Nowhere in any prose surface does the reader
learn that Lexington sits ON the Boston–Concord road, about 11 miles from Boston and 6–7
short of Concord — that fact exists only in the Locator A caption, which a reader may never
expand. Without it, dawn-at-Lexington-then-on-to-Concord is unmotivated. Fix at the B1
sentence: "...reached Lexington (a farm town astride the Concord road, about 11 miles out
of Boston and 6 to 7 short of Concord) a little before 5 a.m. ..." and/or in section 1 at
first mention: "From Lexington, the next sizable town on the Concord road, the word stopped
depending on any one man."

**B6 — Why the troops crossed water first / Boston-as-peninsula is never explained.**
Section 1: > "...rowed across the Charles to Lechmere Point in East Cambridge about an hour later..."
The draft says Boston "was an occupied city" but never that it was a peninsula town with a
single land exit, so the boats read as arbitrary and "the town gate at Boston Neck" (Dawes's
route) floats unexplained. Section 3's "cut off by land" and the siege mechanism also lean
on this missing fact. Fix at section 1's opening: "In the spring of 1775 Boston was an
occupied city, nearly an island: a peninsula town joined to the mainland by one narrow neck
of land." Then the rowing motivates itself; one more clause seals it: "rowed across the
Charles to Lechmere Point in East Cambridge (the short way out of a peninsula town, skipping
the long march over Boston Neck)..."

**B7 — Charlestown is never located, so the day's destination is a blank.**
Section 2: > "At Cambridge, Percy made the day's smartest call, swerving off the expected route over the Great Bridge, where an ambush had been prepared, and making for Charlestown instead."
A mapless newcomer has no idea where Charlestown is or why going there ends the battle.
Fix: "...and making for Charlestown instead (a town on its own peninsula just across the
harbor from Boston, reachable without crossing the river, with the navy's guns to cover
its neck)." The Percy bio's "his swerve to Charlestown at the end of the day" needs the same
mini-gloss: "(the peninsula across the harbor from Boston)."

## SHOULD-FIX

**S1 — Section 2 never re-glosses the core terms of art (militia, regulars, grenadiers).**
First section-2 uses: "found about 77 militiamen drawn up in parade-ground lines";
"Some regulars swore it came from behind a hedge"; "the grenadiers searched the locations".
Sections read independently. Minimal re-glosses: "77 militiamen (the town's part-time
citizen soldiers)"; fold "regulars" into the B1 fix ("advance companies of regulars, the
British professionals"); "the grenadiers (the big assault companies) searched...". Light
infantry is covered later by the excellent flanking-parties gloss.

**S2 — North Bridge fight has no clock time (check 5).**
> "Concluding the town was burning, they formed up and advanced."
The timeline runs 5 a.m. (green) → 7 a.m. (Concord) → [no time] (bridge) → noon (march out).
The fact ledger has the bridge at ~9:30 a.m. Fix: "...they formed up and advanced, around
9:30 in the morning." Every other beat of the day is beautifully time-stamped; this is the
one gap.

**S3 — The gauntlet mechanism (no other road) is implied, never stated (check 4).**
> "The march for Boston began around noon, and the real battle began with it."
MEANWHILE 2 explains the flanker mechanics superbly, but nothing says the column was bound
to the one road it had marched out on. Fix: "The march for Boston began around noon, back
down the same single road the column had come out on, and the real battle began with it."

**S4 — Menotomy is on the road but not located along it.**
> "The worst came at Menotomy (now Arlington), between about 4:30 and 5:30 p.m., where fresh companies from Essex County waited in a town the road ran straight through."
"(now Arlington)" is good; the reader still can't place it between Lexington and Boston.
Fix: "...at Menotomy (now Arlington), the last big town on the road back before Cambridge..."

**S5 — Cambridge and the Great Bridge are unglossed, so Percy's swerve has no mechanism.**
Same sentence as B7. The reader needs to know the expected route home crossed a river there:
"At Cambridge (the river town where the road home crossed the Charles), Percy made the
day's smartest call, swerving off the expected route over the Great Bridge (the road's
river crossing), where an ambush had been prepared..."

**S6 — Hancock-Clarke House caption names two people the caption-reader has never met.**
> "The Hancock-Clarke House in Lexington, where Revere arrived from Boston with the warning on the night of April 18."
Captions are read standalone. "Revere" needs his role: "...where Paul Revere, one of the two
riders carrying the alarm out of Boston, arrived with the warning on the night of April 18."
And the house's name dangles — no Hancock exists anywhere in this draft. If the fact pack
supports it, add why a rider went THERE ("the parsonage where the rebel leaders John Hancock
and Samuel Adams were staying that night"); if it doesn't, the role-gloss for Revere is the
floor.

**S7 — Hero caption / dossier caption use "regulars" and "militia" unglossed.**
> "The regulars fire on Capt. John Parker's dispersing militia company on Lexington green at dawn..."
The dossier is the first surface a reader sees. Cheapest fix that carries side + meaning:
"The British regulars fire on Captain John Parker's dispersing company of Lexington
militiamen at dawn..."

**S8 — Pitcairn bio leans on three unglossed externals.**
> "...unusual in that Bostonians respected him as one of the fairer officers of the occupation."
> "At Fiske Hill his horse threw him... carried through the war by Israel Putnam."
> "Two months later he was killed leading marines up Breed's Hill, dying in the arms of his son."
Fixes: "the British occupation of Boston"; "At Fiske Hill (a rise on the retreat road east
of Lexington)"; "Israel Putnam (an American general)"; "killed two months later at the
Battle of Bunker Hill, leading marines up Breed's Hill (the hill where that battle was
actually fought), dying in the arms of his son."

**S9 — Parker bio: "French and Indian War" and "Bunker Hill" unglossed.**
> "...a veteran of the French and Indian War, and at 45 dying of tuberculosis..."
> "Too ill to fight at Bunker Hill, he died that September, age 46."
Fixes: "a veteran of the French and Indian War (Britain's earlier war against France in
North America)"; "Too ill to fight at Bunker Hill (the next battle, outside Boston that
June), he died that September..."

**S10 — Heath bio: Roxbury, Warren, "Continental," "skirmishers" unglossed.**
> "A Roxbury farmer... He reached Lexington in mid-afternoon with Dr. Joseph Warren and... directed the arriving companies into a moving ring of skirmishers..."
Fixes: "A farmer from Roxbury (the town just south of Boston)"; "Dr. Joseph Warren (the
Boston rebel leader who had launched the riders the night before)"; "a moving ring of
skirmishers (men fighting spread out in loose order)"; "a Continental major general (in the
colonies' new joint army)."

**S11 — Section 3 persons appear cold: Smith, Percy, Gage, Parker.**
First section-3 uses:
> "The bitter joke of it was that Smith's regulars had done their job."
> "Percy, who had watched the militia work all afternoon..."
> "They had executed Gage's written orders almost to the letter..."
> "Parker, too sick to fight by June, died of his tuberculosis that September..."
(Pitcairn, by contrast, gets a model re-intro: "the marine major whose advance companies had
walked onto Lexington green at dawn.") One-clause fixes: "Lieutenant Colonel Smith's
regulars"; "Percy, the relief commander, who had watched..."; "General Gage's written
orders"; "Parker, the Lexington captain, too sick to fight by June..."

**S12 — "Provincial Congress" not re-glossed in sections 2 and 3.**
Section 2: > "The Provincial Congress answered with sworn statements from the men who buried the bodies..."
Section 3: > "Within days the Provincial Congress had collected roughly a hundred depositions..."
Defined only in section 1. Add "(the rebels' own assembly)" at the first use in each section.
Section 3 should also re-gloss "depositions (sworn statements)" — its earlier "dueling sworn
statements" sentence nearly covers it, so this half is a nit if S12's congress gloss lands.

**S13 — Lincoln is never located, and three road beats hang on it.**
> "Revere himself was captured in Lincoln and released on foot..."
Then section 2: "a double bend in the Lincoln woods", "the Lincoln and Lexington town line."
Fix at first use (section 1): "captured in Lincoln (the next town between Lexington and
Concord) and released on foot."

**S14 — Samuel Whittemore has no role/identity.**
> "Samuel Whittemore, in his late 70s, killed three regulars before being shot, bayoneted, and left for dead..."
Who is he — militiaman, civilian? Minimum fix per the pack: "Samuel Whittemore, a Menotomy
man in his late 70s, killed three regulars..."

**S15 — Section 1 never says where Warren's riders were riding TO.**
> "Warren sent two riders by two routes: William Dawes overland through the town gate at Boston Neck, and Paul Revere across the water by the Charlestown crossing."
The destination is never stated; "From Lexington the word stopped depending on any one man"
forces the reader to back-infer it. Fix: "Warren sent two riders by two routes toward
Lexington and Concord, ahead of the column: ..."

## NIT

**N1 — Regiment shorthand: "the 4th Foot" / "the 23rd."**
> "Lieutenant John Barker of the 4th Foot wrote..." · "Lieutenant Frederick Mackenzie of the 23rd set down..."
"of the 4th Regiment of Foot" / "of the 23rd Regiment" reads cleaner for a newcomer; "Foot"
as infantry is otherwise unexplained (also in Smith's bio: "thirty years in the 10th Foot" →
"the 10th Regiment of Foot").

**N2 — "ring of skirmishers" unglossed in section 2.**
> "The militia ring of skirmishers that had hung on the column all afternoon broke off at the Neck."
"(militiamen fighting spread out, never massing)" — optional; the MEANWHILE block carries
most of the idea.

**N3 — Abner Hosmer gets no role.**
> "...killing Captain Isaac Davis of Acton, whose company led, and Abner Hosmer."
"and Abner Hosmer, another Acton man" (confirm against the pack).

**N4 — Artemas Ward: side/affiliation implied, not stated.**
> "Loose command of the gathering settled on General Artemas Ward the next day."
"on the Massachusetts militia general Artemas Ward."

**N5 — House rule says parentheses for definitions; the militia/minute-company glosses use comma apposition.**
> "Every town kept a militia, the part-time citizen soldiery of farmers and tradesmen mustered into companies under their own officers..."
Reads well as-is; flagging only because WRITING-RULES names parentheses as the convention.

**N6 — Locator A caption: "HMS Somerset" unglossed.**
> "...ending at Charlestown Neck under the guns of HMS Somerset."
"under the guns of the warship HMS Somerset" (the section-2 prose already does this right).

**N7 — Dossier sides/stats: "grenadiers & light infantry," "6-pounder field guns" unglossed.**
Space-constrained surface; acceptable, but "(elite assault and skirmish companies)" fits if
room allows.

**N8 — "Royal Marines" in section 1 unglossed.**
> "...garrisoned by regiments of regulars... and a force of Royal Marines..."
"(Britain's sea-service soldiers)" — optional; the term is near-self-explanatory.

## CHECKLIST SUMMARY

1. **Terms of art** — Section 1 and the second half of section 2 are exemplary
   (trunnions, flank companies, deposition, flanking parties, 6-pounders, Charlestown Neck
   all glossed at first use). Gaps: per-section re-glosses (S1, S12), bio externals
   (S8–S10), captions (S6, S7), regiment shorthand (N1).
2. **Persons role+side per section** — Section 1 near-perfect (Gage, Smith, Warren, Dawes,
   Revere, Prescott, Wm. Emerson, Pitcairn all introduced) EXCEPT Percy (B2). Section 2
   broken at the top (B1, B3) and at the rescue (B2). Section 3 cold on four names (S11).
   The Emerson identity switch is the single most misleading defect (B4).
3. **Geography for the mapless** — The big three gaps: Lexington unplaced (B5), Boston
   peninsula/why-boats (B6), Charlestown-as-safety (B7); plus Lincoln (S13), Menotomy's
   position (S4), Cambridge/Great Bridge (S5). Concord's 18 miles, Lechmere Point, Boston
   Neck (as a name), Menotomy=Arlington, and Charlestown Neck are all present and good.
4. **Causal chains** — Why the troops went (stores, trunnions, the map): excellent. Why the
   alarm worked (pre-built system, two riders + relay): excellent, the draft's best material.
   Why militia all day (relays, town by town, MEANWHILE 1 + sides note): excellent. Why the
   gauntlet: implied only (S3). Why Percy appears in section 2: unexplained there (folded
   into B2). Why the day ends in a siege: solid (camped, kept coming, Roxbury–Chelsea arc,
   cut off by land — lands fully once B6 establishes the peninsula).
5. **Timeline** — 10 p.m. muster, 2 a.m. march, ~5 a.m. green, 7 a.m. Concord, noon march,
   2–3 p.m. Percy, 4:30–5:30 Menotomy, 7:30 p.m. Neck: a newcomer always knows the hour
   EXCEPT at the North Bridge (S2).
6. **Other-chapter leans** — The two PILLs are labeled links, fine. Bunker Hill in section 3
   prose stands alone well ("its first great battle came just two months in... on the hills
   above Charlestown at Bunker Hill"); the bios' bare "Bunker Hill"/"Breed's Hill" do not
   (S8, S9). The Boston Massacre is never leaned on in prose. The siege handoff is
   self-contained.

*Critic note: any proposed gloss that adds a fact not in the fact pack (Hancock/Adams at the
parsonage, Emerson family link, Hosmer's town, Whittemore's identity, Menotomy's position)
must clear the fact-checker before integration; the clarity defect stands either way.*
