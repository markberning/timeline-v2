# Perryville Draft — NEWCOMER / CLARITY Critic Gate

War pipeline step 4. Read COLD as a sharp 15-year-old with ZERO Civil War knowledge.
Verdict scale: CLEAR / NEEDS-GLOSS / LOST. Findings tagged MUST-FIX vs SHOULD-FIX.

**Overall verdict: NEEDS-GLOSS.** The narrative spine is genuinely clear — the
slavery stakes, the drought-to-collision setup, the acoustic shadow, and the
won-the-field-lost-the-state irony all land for a cold reader. The prose sets scenes
well and side-tags people. What blocks a CLEAR is a cluster of undefined military
structure jargon (corps / division / brigade / battery / division commander / corps
commander / wing) that the draft uses heavily and never defines, plus a few
undefined terms (pickets is glossed but rearguard/rrick of "en echelon" half-glossed,
"general engagement," "salient" — see below) and one genuinely confusing leap. None
of these are LOST; all are fixable with short inline glosses.

---

## A. Undefined military-structure jargon (the big one) — MUST-FIX

The draft leans hard on the army org chart — **corps, division, brigade, battery,
"division commander," "corps commander," "wing"** — and never tells a zero-knowledge
reader what any of them are or how they nest (army > corps > division > brigade).
This matters MORE here than in most battles because the *entire point* of Perryville
is a numbers/structure story: "one corps plus parts of another" fought, "the bulk of
his army stood idle," "one corps' worth of men." A reader who doesn't know a corps is
roughly tens of thousands and a brigade a few thousand cannot feel the asymmetry the
whole narrative is built on.

| Term | First appears | Status | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **corps** | §2 ("Sheridan's corps commander") | NEEDS-GLOSS — used ~15× as the load-bearing unit, never defined | MUST-FIX. Gloss on first use: a corps = a big chunk of an army, tens of thousands of men, under one general. |
| **division** | §3 ("Cheatham's division"); "the division commander" | NEEDS-GLOSS | MUST-FIX (can fold into the one structure gloss): a division sits below a corps, several thousand men. |
| **brigade** | §3 ("Donelson's brigade") | NEEDS-GLOSS — used ~10×, the unit that actually charges | MUST-FIX (fold into same gloss): a brigade = a few thousand men, below a division. |
| **battery** | §3 ("Parsons' battery"); "the Union guns of Parsons' battery" | NEEDS-GLOSS | MUST-FIX: a battery = a cluster of cannon (and the men working them). Critical because the climax is "overran the guns" / "captured Parsons' guns." |
| **"corps commander" / "division commander"** | §2 / §3 | follows from the above | resolved once corps/division are glossed once. |
| **"wing"** ("directed on this wing by Polk"; "the Confederate left") | §3 | NEEDS-GLOSS | SHOULD-FIX: "wing" = one side (left/right) of the army's battle line. The left/right usage is mostly inferable from context but "wing" as a formal command is not. |

**Recommended single fix:** one short house-voice gloss the first time the chain
appears — e.g. when "corps" or "brigade" lands, drop a parenthetical that an army
breaks into corps (tens of thousands), corps into divisions, divisions into brigades
(a few thousand), so the reader has the ladder before the numbers start mattering.
The draft already does exactly this kind of inline gloss for "pickets" — it just
needs to do it for the unit ladder and for "battery."

## D. Unexplained leaps / confusing passages

1. **"the other Confederate force… under Edmund Kirby Smith" (§4)** — NEEDS-GLOSS,
   MUST-FIX. (Same as the Kirby Smith row above.) The draft never told the reader two
   separate Confederate armies invaded Kentucky. When Bragg goes to "join the other
   Confederate force," a cold reader asks "what other force?" This is the single
   clearest unexplained leap in the draft. One sentence earlier (ideally back in §1)
   establishing that Bragg's was one of two Confederate columns in the state would fix
   it cleanly.

2. **"essentially one corps plus parts of another" (§3)** — NEEDS-GLOSS, MUST-FIX.
   Depends entirely on the reader knowing how big a corps is (see Section A). Once the
   corps gloss exists, this line lands. Without it, the key asymmetry is just numbers
   on a page.

3. **Polk's identity across sections** — CLEAR but borderline SHOULD-FIX. Polk is
   "Major General Leonidas Polk (South)" directing the wing in §3, then in §4 he's the
   one who "blundered directly into that enemy regiment" and bluffs his way out. The
   reader can track him, but the §4 reference assumes you remember he's a senior
   commander, which makes the bluff (a top general personally riding into enemy lines)
   land harder if you recall his rank. Minor; acceptable as-is.

4. **"acting Major General… unconfirmed… the Senate never ratified it" (§2, Gilbert)**
   — NEEDS-GLOSS, SHOULD-FIX. The parenthetical about contested commissions and Senate
   ratification is inside-baseball for a 15-year-old and briefly stalls the read right
   as the Sheridan-held-back tension is building. The narrative point ("a Union
   commander held back a man who wanted to fight") survives without the rank-litigation
   detail. Consider trimming the parenthetical to a phrase ("a general whose rank was
   never officially confirmed") and moving the litigation to a footnote/ledger.

5. **The drought "more than any plan… set up the collision" (§1) and the water-trigger
   (§2)** — CLEAR. The causal chain (drought → both armies grope toward the same water
   → advance guards touch → it catches fire) is one of the best-explained things in the
   draft. No change.

## E. Overwhelming passages

- **§3 paragraphs 5–7 (the en echelon assault sequence)** — NEEDS-GLOSS, SHOULD-FIX.
  This is the densest stretch: within ~3 paragraphs the reader meets Cheatham,
  Polk, Donelson, Open Knob/Parsons' Ridge, the Chaplin River, Maney, Parsons'
  battery, Terrill, Jackson, and Starkweather, plus timestamps (12:30, 2:00, 2:45,
  3:00, 5:30). It's not LOST — the "three charges, thrown back twice, broke through on
  the third" spine is vivid and trackable, and the two-generals-die beat is a strong
  anchor. But it's the one place a cold reader could drown in names/places. The fix is
  mostly upstream: once corps/division/brigade/battery and "flank" are glossed (Section
  A), this passage gets much more legible because the units stop being noise. No
  restructure needed; the gloss does the work.

- Everything else reads at a comfortable pace. The "Meanwhile" sidebars are
  consistently CLEAR and actually help reset a tired reader.
