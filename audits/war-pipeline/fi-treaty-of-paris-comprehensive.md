## SHOULD-FIX (precision / weighting — NOT ship-blocking)

**SF-1 — Overstate of Pontiac→Proclamation causation.** The draft says the Proclamation was "the Crown's hurried attempt" and "an answer to a war the nations themselves had forced." Mainstream scholarship is more careful: the Proclamation (and the line) were *already in development* as part of post-treaty imperial reorganization before news of Pontiac's attacks reached London; the uprising **hastened** issuance rather than *caused* the policy. The draft already hedges once ("in part"), so this is not wrong, but two later sentences drop the hedge and assert causation. Suggest softening to "accelerated"/"hastened" framing. **No new facts needed** — the fact pack's own Beat 6 already says "partly," and the nuance is well-sourced (Encyclopedia Virginia, Mount Vernon).

**SF-2 — "roughly ten British forts" appears twice as the same fact** (Why-the-Line section and the closing Native section). Both are accurate, but the repetition slightly flattens the second, more important, use. Consider varying or consolidating. Cosmetic.

**SF-3 — Belle Île and Dominica.** The fact pack's Beat 1 lists Belle Île and Dominica among Britain's wartime captures; the draft mentions Dominica once (in the negotiating-hand list) and drops Belle Île. This is correct editorial selectivity for a theme section (Belle Île is a European-coast prize, off-topic) — flagging only to confirm it's a deliberate, fine omission, not a gap.

## BLIND SPOTS IN THE FACT PACK ITSELF

The fact pack is strong and well-sourced. Two minor blind spots, neither essential:

1. **Pontiac-causation nuance not flagged as contested.** Beat 6 says the Proclamation was issued "partly to reassure Native nations... during and after Pontiac's War" — true — but it does not surface the well-documented point that the line/policy *predated* the news of Pontiac and was only *accelerated* by it. Because the pack frames it loosely, the draft drifted into stronger causal language (SF-1). A one-line caution in the pack ("the line was in development before Pontiac's War; the uprising hastened issuance, not the policy") would have prevented the drift. **This is a fact-pack improvement, not a draft must-add.**

2. **Treaty of Niagara (1764) not even named as a dedup boundary.** The pack dedups Pontiac's War and native-alliances but never names the Niagara council, which is the single biggest Native-agency event tied to the Proclamation. It's correctly owned elsewhere, but the pack should *name* it as a dedup boundary so a future author knows it exists and where it lives, rather than risk it falling through the cracks between packs.

Neither blind spot blocks this draft.
