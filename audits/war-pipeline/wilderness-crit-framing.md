# Crit Gate 3 — LOST CAUSE / FRAMING · Battle of the Wilderness draft

**Gate question:** Is it fair? First and always: does the section name **slavery** as the
cause in its OWN prose? Then hunt slavery-soft-pedaling, false both-sides balance,
Confederate romanticization, "states' rights" euphemism, presentism, unfair sympathy
distribution, and sanitized-or-gratuitous human cost.

**VERDICT: PASS (FAIR throughout).** Zero MUST-FIX. The slavery-cause gate is cleared
emphatically — in the section's own prose, repeatedly, and as the structural climax rather
than a bolted-on disclaimer. A few SHOULD-FIX texture notes only.

---

## SLAVERY-NAMED gate (the automatic check)

**PASS.** The word "slavery" / "enslaved" / "enslavement" appears in the draft's OWN prose
multiple times, and not as a token mention — it is the section's thesis:

- "The Confederacy fought to preserve and extend **slavery**. That is the plain fact under
  everything else."
- "Eleven states had broken away from the United States rather than accept any limit on the
  **enslavement of Black people**."
- "Lee's Army of Northern Virginia was the military shield of that slaveholding republic —
  the thing standing between four million enslaved people and their freedom."
- "It was fighting to break the slaveholders' army for good, and **end the institution it
  protected**."
- Closing line: "The thicket was the object. **Slavery was the reason.**"

This is the strongest possible version of the gate: cause named plainly, in-prose, in the
section's own voice, attributed to the Confederacy as a whole AND to Lee's army specifically.

---

## Distortion hunt

### Confederate romanticization — FAIR
- Lee's "Lee to the rear" moment and Longstreet's rescue are told with drama but NOT
  hagiography. Lee is "so charged up," his soldiers grab Traveller's reins — it's a vivid
  human moment, not a marble-statue tribute. The draft never calls Lee noble, gallant, or
  tragic; it frames his generalship as *math* ("blind was the only way the math worked"),
  which is analysis, not glorification. **FAIR.**
- Longstreet's wound and the Jackson echo are handled as irony/tragedy of war, applied to
  the human being, not as a "doomed gallant cause" lament. The draft pointedly does NOT
  treat the Confederate near-victory as something to mourn. **FAIR.**
- "Old War Horse" appears in quotation marks as Lee's nickname for Longstreet — descriptive,
  not endorsing. **FAIR.**

### Slavery soft-pedaling / "states' rights" euphemism — FAIR
- **No "states' rights" framing anywhere.** The break is named as breaking away "rather than
  accept any limit on the enslavement of Black people" — the cause stated as slavery, full
  stop. **FAIR.**

### False both-sides balance — FAIR
- The draft does NOT present the two causes as morally equivalent. It explicitly frames
  Grant's army as fighting to "break the slaveholders' army … and end the institution," and
  Lee's army as "the military shield of that slaveholding republic." Sympathy is distributed
  toward the enslaved and toward the Union soldiers' relentlessness; the Confederate cause is
  named as what it was. This is correct, not "tilted." **FAIR.**

### Presentism — FAIR
- The moral framing (slavery = the cause, the USCT = the most literal stake) is grounded in
  *contemporary* fact (the 23rd USCT recruited from these very counties, Emancipation already
  in force, Black men in Union uniform in 1864) — it is not a 21st-century value imposed
  backward. **FAIR.**

### Human cost — neither sanitized nor gratuitous — FAIR
- The fires passage ("Men too badly hurt to crawl away were burned to death where they had
  fallen, and they were men of both sides") is unflinching and documented, attributed to
  NPS, with NO invented named victims or fabricated counts. It names the horror as falling
  on *both* sides — which is the honest reality of the fire, and does not undercut the
  cause-framing. The two are kept in their right registers: the fire is the shared human
  catastrophe; slavery is the moral cause. Correctly separated. **FAIR.**

### Sympathy distribution — FAIR
- The closing section deliberately gives the last and largest weight to the formerly-enslaved
  men marching "back across the land of their own bondage." The draft earns this — it is the
  group with the most literal stake, and the move is supported by the documented 23rd-USCT
  facts, not asserted for effect. **FAIR.**

## Findings sorted

### MUST-FIX
*(none — slavery-cause gate cleared in the section's own prose)*

### SHOULD-FIX
1. **"Either way, Grant had lost close to twice what Lee had."** Stated as cold arithmetic
   — which is the point — but appears twice (the-turn section and what-it-was-for section)
   in nearly the same words. Not a framing fault; just confirm the repetition is intentional
   emphasis and not accidental. (Texture, not fairness.)
2. **The cold-arithmetic framing of casualties** ("trade about 18,000 of his men for about
   10,000 of Lee's and keep marching") is historically accurate and is Grant's own logic, so
   it is fair to state — but make sure the prose does not let "acceptable trade" curdle into
   treating Union dead as mere ledger entries. The draft mostly avoids this because the fires
   passage and the USCT close restore the human stakes. As written it lands FAIR; flag only
   to keep an eye on it in any future trim that might cut the human-cost counterweight.

**Bottom line:** This is a model of the framing the gate wants — cause named plainly and
structurally, no Lost Cause euphemism, no false equivalence, no romanticization, human cost
honest on both sides, and the enslaved given the closing moral weight on documented grounds.
PASS.
