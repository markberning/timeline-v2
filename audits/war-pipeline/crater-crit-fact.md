## B. The contested / special-scrutiny items

### B1. Lines distance — MUST-FIX (numeric)
The draft twice asserts the lines were **130 yards** apart at the mine point. The
best-attested figure across ABT and Wikipedia is **~100 yards** (the 48th Pennsylvania at
the apex of "the Horseshoe" came within ~100 yards of Elliott's Salient; IX Corps ~100
yds west of the enemy works). The fact pack is itself self-contradictory (§1.5 "~130
yards"; §4 "~100 yards / ~400 feet"). 400 ft ≈ 133 yards, NOT 130 — and the dominant
sourced number is 100 yards. **MUST-FIX:** change to "~100 yards" (or "about a hundred
yards / a few hundred feet") and drop the "roughly 400 feet" gloss, which compounds the
error. Low-stakes factually but it is a printed hard number that disagrees with the
primary sources, and it appears twice.

### B2. The post-surrender massacre — VERIFIED, accurately stated, not exaggerated
The draft's central atrocity claim is **fully supported by the documented record,
including Confederate witnesses**, and is stated with accuracy, not inflation:
- It does **not** print a hard count of executed men (HistoryNet estimates "between 200
  and 300 or more"; the draft wisely says the casualty count "alone does not carry what
  happened" and never asserts a number). **Correct discipline.**
- "Murdered after surrender," "shot or bayoneted on the spot, or marched to the rear and
  executed" — **VERIFIED** (Alexander, McClellan, EV, ABT-CAL).
- "widespread, witnessed, and… in at least one officer's telling, celebrated" — **VERIFIED**
  and not overstated; Pegram's letter does celebrate it (see B6).

### B3. The rim cry — VERIFIED, handling correct
Draft renders it as a documented sorting command: "take the white man prisoner, and kill
the Black one," with the slur bracketed/euphemized. Wikipedia/EV carry the verbatim cry
with the racial slur ("Take the white man prisoner! Kill the n----r!"). The draft keeps
the racialized meaning intact (surrender offered to whites, refused to Blacks) and does
NOT soften it. **VERIFIED; ship-blocking requirement met.** Rendered as documented
command, not a verbatim block quote — appropriate, since the only verbatim form contains
the slur.

### B4. Grant's "saddest affair" — correctly PARAPHRASED, do NOT promote to quote
Draft paraphrases (no quote marks): "the saddest affair he had witnessed in the war…
such an opportunity for carrying fortifications he had never seen, and did not expect ever
to have again." Verbatim is *"It was the saddest affair I have witnessed in this war.
Such an opportunity for carrying fortifications I have never seen and do not expect again
to have."* **CONFIRMED that a wording variance is real:** Wikipedia/ABT-CAL render "in
**this** war"; Encyclopedia Virginia renders "in **war**." Because the sources disagree on
the exact wording, **KEEP IT PARAPHRASED** — do NOT promote to a block quote. The draft's
choice is correct. (If ever quoted, use the OR text "in this war" with the variance noted.)

### B5. Grant's Congressional "shoving these people ahead" line — correctly PARAPHRASED
Draft paraphrases: "shoving these people ahead to get killed because it did not care
anything about them" (no quote marks, recast to third person). Verbatim per the Joint
Committee transcript: *"it would then be said, and very properly, that we were shoving
those people ahead to get killed because we did not care anything about them."* The
draft's paraphrase is **accurate in substance** (note: source says "**those** people,"
draft says "these people" — which is exactly why it must stay paraphrased, not quoted).
**Do NOT promote to a verbatim quote without using "those people" and the full
"and very properly" clause.** Current paraphrased rendering is **VERIFIED-safe.**

### B6. Atrocity-witness attributions (Alexander / McClellan / Pegram) — VERIFIED
- **Alexander (South):** draft "some of the Black prisoners were shot by others, and that
  there was a great deal of unnecessary killing of them." Verbatim: *"Some of the Negro
  prisoners who were originally allowed to surrender… were afterward shot by others, &
  there was… a great deal of unnecessary killing of them."* **VERIFIED**, accurately
  paraphrased.
- **McClellan, 9th Alabama (South):** draft "worst-looking set you ever saw — bayoneted,
  clubbed with musket butts — … all of them would have been killed but for Mahone, with
  one man in his own brigade having killed several." Verbatim confirms: ~250 captured, all
  wounded, *"Bayoneted, knocked on the head by the butts of muskets. all would have been
  killed had it not ben for Gen. Mahone, who would beg our men to spare them. one fellow
  in our Brigade killed several."* **VERIFIED**, faithful. (The grislier "let me kill one
  more"/pocket-knife throat-cutting detail exists in the same letter and is correctly NOT
  used — restraint, not error.)
- **Pegram (South):** draft "the killings had a splendid effect on his men." Verbatim:
  *"it has a splendid effect on our men"*; he adds *"It seems cruel to murder them in cold
  blood, but… the men who did it had very good cause for doing so."* **VERIFIED;** the
  draft's "approving"/"celebrated" characterization is fair and if anything understated.

### B7. Mahone trying to stop it — VERIFIED, handled without exoneration
Draft: "Mahone himself appears to have tried to stop it… That does not soften the
verdict." Multiple accounts (McClellan, EV) confirm Mahone begged men to spare prisoners;
draft keeps Pegram's approval and the reported later killing alongside, so the
intervention does **not** exonerate the Confederate side. The literal "for God's sake
stop" is correctly NOT quoted (it's a contested/loose attribution). **VERIFIED; handling
correct.**

### B8. Ledlie & Ferrero drunk in a bombproof — VERIFIED ("by multiple accounts")
Wiki/EV/ABT-CAL support both generals behind the lines, reportedly drinking. Draft hedges
"by multiple accounts," which is correct (it is a reported, not court-proven-beyond-doubt,
detail). **VERIFIED-safe.**

### B9. "Turkey shoot," bayoneted muskets thrown like spears — VERIFIED
Wiki/ABT-CAL. Fine.

### B10. Shelby Foote / Ledlie — not used as a primary voice
Draft does not quote Foote; the "drunk in a bunker with a bottle" prose is narration, not
attributed to a participant. Correct (pack warned Foote is a historian, not a primary
source).
