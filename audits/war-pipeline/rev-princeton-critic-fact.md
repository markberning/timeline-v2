## BLOCKER

### B1. "his chestnut horse Nelson" at a January 1777 battle is an anachronism (PACK ERROR, propagated)
Draft (heroCaption, line 20, and IMAGES #1 caption, line 66):
> "Washington on his chestnut horse Nelson rallying the militia"

The pack's §8.3 image note says "Washington on his chestnut Nelson," and the Princeton University Art Museum's own story page does describe the painting that way ("accurately portrayed as mounted on his chestnut, 'Nelson'"). But Mount Vernon's digital encyclopedia and the standard references are unambiguous that **Nelson was given to Washington by Thomas Nelson in 1778** — a year after Princeton. Washington could not have been riding Nelson on January 3, 1777; the better-attested tradition has him on a chestnut/sorrel, with Blueskin the other war horse. The museum's caption is itself the suspect link. Asserting the horse's name flat, twice, on the most-read caption of the page is exactly the *Demoiselles*-class caption defect.
- Sources: Mount Vernon, "Nelson (Horse)" (gift 1778); Wikipedia "Nelson (horse)" (foaled 1763, gifted 1778); vs. artmuseum.princeton.edu/story/james-peale-and-battle-princeton (names Nelson).
- **Fix (either):** (a) drop the name — "Washington on horseback rallying the militia"; or (b) attribute — "Washington on the chestnut the museum identifies as his war horse Nelson". (a) is cleaner.
- **Also fix the pack** (§8.3) so the error doesn't re-propagate.

### B2. Charles Peale's sketches were made on the field TWO YEARS LATER — the caption drops the qualifier and implies battle-day eyewitness sketches
Draft (heroCaption line 20 / IMAGES #1 line 66):
> "working partly from sketches his brother Charles made on the field"

The pack (§8.3) says "with sketches by his brother Charles made on the field **two years later**." Web-confirmed: Charles Willson Peale returned to the Princeton battlefield in **1779** to sketch the site for his Washington portraits (Princeton University Art Museum story page). Sitting in apposition to "a veteran who fought in the battle," the draft's wording reads as sketches made *during* the battle — a meaning-changing omission that overstates the painting's documentary status on a gated caption surface.
- **Fix:** "...working partly from sketches his brother Charles made on the battlefield two years later: Washington on horseback rallying the militia, Mercer fallen, Moulder's guns at work, the Clarke farmhouse behind." (Combines with B1 fix.)

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1. James Peale painting date: draft/pack say "mid-1780s"; the holding museum says "ca. 1782" (pack error candidate)
Appears in heroSource (line 19), heroCaption (line 20), heroCredit (line 21), and IMAGES #1 (lines 66–68): "mid-1780s" ×5. The Princeton University Art Museum dates *The Battle of Princeton* **ca. 1782** (its story page says James "completed [it] in 1782"; some accounts run c. 1782–84). "Mid-1780s" is a ~3-year drift on a credit line. **Fix:** "c. 1782" with at-fetch verification against the Commons file page; if Commons and the museum disagree, the museum wins. Update the pack too.

### S2. "it never afterward came so close to dying again" — not in the pack, and contestable
Draft (line 211, section 3 closing): the pack supports "the cause survived the winter," not a war-long superlative. Valley Forge 1777–78, the 1779–80 Morristown winter, and the January 1781 mutinies are all candidate "as close." This is the only sentence in the prose asserting a claim with no pack line behind it. **Fix:** soften or cut — e.g. "The army that should have died in December was still in the field in January, and the war went on." 

### S3. Dossier duration stat pins the top of the pack's range as a point value
Draft (line 27): "Duration: ~1 hour for the field action (roughly 90 minutes including Nassau Hall)". Pack §1: the whole action "lasted roughly an hour to ninety minutes" [MED on minutes; "don't give minute-precision"]. The parenthetical converts a 60–90 range into "roughly 90." The section-2 prose gets it right ("roughly an hour to ninety minutes"). **Fix:** "Duration: ~1 hour for the field action; an hour to ninety minutes in all, including Nassau Hall".

### S4. The split "Militia are taking spirit" quote inserts a comma not in the verified text
Draft (line 207): «"The Militia are taking spirit," he wrote, "and I am told, are coming in fast from this State."» — rejoined, this adds a comma after "spirit" that the pack's verified §7.5 text does not have ("The Militia are taking spirit and I am told, are coming in fast from this State."). The pack's quotes are byte-exact-where-marked; don't let attribution-splitting re-punctuate one. **Fix:** "Washington saw it within days, writing that 'The Militia are taking spirit and I am told, are coming in fast from this State.'"

## NIT

1. **Ledger mis-cites the pack** (line 242): claims Hancock-as-"president of Congress" is "the pack's own addressee framing" — the pack never glosses Hancock. The gloss is true and harmless common knowledge; fix the ledger line so the audit trail is honest (mark it "common-knowledge gloss, not pack-sourced").
2. **Internal date tension on the Delaware crossing**: section 1 (line 159) "on the day after Christmas, he had crossed the icy Delaware and destroyed the Hessian garrison"; section 3 (line 201) "the night crossing of the icy Delaware on December 25." Both defensible (crossing the night of Dec 25–26, battle Dec 26), but the two sections shouldn't disagree. Suggest sec 1: "on the night after Christmas Eve he had crossed…" or simply "at Christmas he had crossed the icy Delaware and, on the morning of the 26th, destroyed…".
3. **Trumbull Mercer caption** (line 81): "begun in 1786" vs pack's "begun 1786–87." Use "begun in 1786" only if the Yale page confirms at fetch; otherwise "begun in the 1780s."
4. **Bag-the-fox terminal punctuation** (line 165): the tradition quote ends `…bag him in the morning,"` — period swapped for comma by sentence flow. Conventional and tradition-grade, but trivially avoidable by ending the sentence at the quote.
5. **Locator 1 caption** (line 139): "marched the 11 miles down the Post Road" and "twelve miles behind him" in the same caption. Both are pack-sourced (11-mile road; "12 miles in his rear") but read as a discrepancy; consider "a dozen miles behind him."
6. **Frame-reuse hedge slightly under-claims** (lines 93, 192): Princeton's University Archives note the trustees' minutes record the cannonball pulling down the George II portrait, and the frame's reuse; "so the story goes" hedges a partly documented fact. Safe as written — optional tighten to put the hedge only on the Hamilton attribution (which the draft already badges correctly).
7. **"Around midnight"** (line 171): pack gives departure "~midnight–2 a.m."; draft picks the front edge. Fine; "around midnight, or a little after" would match the range better.
