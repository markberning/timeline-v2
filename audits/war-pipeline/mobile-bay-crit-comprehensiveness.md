the strategic stakes, the geography (bay mouth / channel / forts / minefield), the fleet composition and the
lashed-pairs tactic, the three crisis beats (Tecumseh sinks → Brooklyn stalls → Farragut decides), the lone
stand of CSS Tennessee, the aftermath (forts vs. city), the casualty asymmetry, the political payoff, and the
cause. I built that checklist independently before reading the draft; the draft hits every item.

## SHOULD-ADD (enrichment, not gaps)

- **Granger's land troops are under-weighted.** They appear (landed to besiege the forts; ~1 killed/7 wounded) but the reader never quite learns that the forts fell to a *combined* fleet-and-land squeeze, not the fleet alone. One clause noting Granger's troops invested Gaines and Morgan from the landward side would close the loop on why the forts surrendered rather than being stormed. SHOULD-ADD.
- **Why the mines failed to detonate** is asserted ("long submerged, fouled, defective") but not really explained. A half-sentence — many mines had been in the water for weeks and their powder had spoiled or their firing mechanisms corroded — would turn a lucky-sounding outcome into an understood one. SHOULD-ADD (the fact-pack supports "long-submerged, fouled, or defective").
- **The scale of Farragut's gamble could land harder.** The draft says the mines "failed to go off" but doesn't dwell on the fact that Farragut had *no way of knowing* they would — he drove a wooden flagship over a field that had just swallowed an ironclad in 30 seconds. The prose gestures at "reckless decision" but could make the reader feel the dice-roll. SHOULD-ADD (tone only).

## FACT-PACK BLIND SPOTS (noted for the record, not draft defects)
- The fact-pack seeded the **West Gulf "1 in 4"** error (see fact gate MUST-FIX). The draft faithfully carried it; the blind spot originates upstream.
- The fact-pack offers no detail on **the broader Mobile land campaign of 1865** (Spanish Fort / Fort Blakeley) — appropriately out of scope for an 1864 battle, but worth a one-line pointer if a "what happened to the city" beat is ever wanted.

## PERSPECTIVE
Both sides are represented: Buchanan's hopeless courage gets real weight (the lone ironclad against a fleet), the
Confederate fort commanders are named and their surrenders dated, and the Union side is not triumphalist. The
human cost is carried on both sides (Tecumseh's ~90 dead; Buchanan's broken leg). No perspective gap.

**Bottom line:** Comprehensive for a naval action. The omissions are enrichment opportunities (Granger's role, why the mines failed), not missing essentials. PASS.
