# CLARITY GATE — "Freedom Seekers & the USCT"
Critic: NEWCOMER/CLARITY — sharp 15-year-old, zero prior knowledge
Draft: `audits/war-pipeline/usct-draft.md`

---

## Overall verdict: NEEDS-GLOSS

The draft is long and dense but largely readable. Framing is strong. Several terms are undefined that a 15-year-old will hit cold and stall on. Side-tag compliance has gaps for named military actors. No single passage is a catastrophic wall, but "no quarter" (eyebrow header only, never explained in the body) and the missing side tags are the worst structural failures.

## Unexplained leaps / dense passages

1. **The "three firsts" run (Beat 5)** — 1st Kansas → 1st Louisiana → 1st South Carolina in three dense paragraphs, each with its own date, location, personnel, and "first" distinction. A newcomer tracking three regiments in three states with overlapping timelines will struggle. The draft is aware of this ("three distinct 'firsts'… shouldn't be blurred together") but the acknowledgment comes *after* the reader is already confused. A one-sentence orientation before the trio begins would help: "This happened in three separate places at almost the same time, and each one was a 'first' in a different sense."

2. **The Crater / no-surrender dynamic** — The Crater passage references the Confederate policy of killing Black soldiers who tried to surrender, and white officers "tore off their USCT insignia." This is the reader's first encounter with the policy in action, but the Confederate policy itself was laid out several sections earlier (in "Remember Fort Pillow"). The Crater passage assumes the reader remembers it. A short callback ("because of the Confederate policy that Black soldiers would not be treated as prisoners of war") would close the gap.

3. **General Order No. 252 vs. General Order No. 143** — Two general orders are cited in close succession (143 in Beat 5, 252 in the Fort Pillow section). First-time readers may confuse them. No fix needed if the orders are separated by content — they are — but worth flagging as a potential stumble.

## MUST-FIX

These are comprehension failures: a reader is either lost or misled without the fix.

1. **"No quarter" — eyebrow says it, body never defines it.**
   > Eyebrow: "No quarter" appears over the Fort Pillow section. The body never uses the phrase or defines it. A 15-year-old reading the eyebrow thinks: "no quarter of what?" The concept (refusing to let surrendering soldiers live; killing instead of capturing) is the central point of the entire section, and the label for it never appears in the prose. Fix: use the phrase in the body and define it — e.g., "offering no quarter — refusing to let them surrender and live."

2. **Sgt. William Walker — missing (Union) tag.**
   > "Sgt. William Walker of the 3rd South Carolina Volunteers — a formerly enslaved man now wearing sergeant's stripes — led the men of his company to stack their arms"
   > Gloss needed: add "(Union)" after "Sgt. William Walker."

3. **Brig. Gen. Edward Ferrero — missing (Union) tag.**
   > "a division of USCT under Brig. Gen. Edward Ferrero"
   > Gloss needed: add "(Union)" after "Ferrero."

4. **Gen. Meade — missing (Union) tag.**
   > "General Meade overruled the plan"
   > Gloss needed: add "(Union)" after "Meade," or write "Union commander General Meade."

5. **New Market Heights four named soldiers — missing (Union) tags.**
   > "Sgt. Maj. Christian Fleetwood, 1st Sgt. Powhatan Beaty, Sgt. Maj. Milton Holland, and 1st Sgt. Edward Ratcliff"
   > Each needs "(Union)" on first mention.

6. **"abolitionist" — never defined, used as a known term in multiple critical places.**
   > "Butler was no abolitionist" (the reason his ruling had weight) and Higginson as "a Massachusetts abolitionist and minister." The reader needs to know this means someone who campaigned for the total abolition (end) of slavery. Without it, "no abolitionist" reads as a neutral descriptor and the stakes of Butler's reasoning are lost.

## SHOULD-FIX

These are stumbles that a sharp teenager will notice but can push through.

1. **"garrison" — undefined.**
   > "overran the Union garrison of roughly 600 men"
   > Gloss: add "(the soldiers stationed there to defend it)" after "garrison."

2. **"mutiny" — undefined, used to explain an execution.**
   > "convicted him of mutiny"
   > Gloss: add "(the military crime of refusing orders or leading soldiers in refusing duty)" after "mutiny."

3. **"court-martialed" — undefined.**
   > "the army court-martialed him"
   > Gloss: add "(put him on trial in a military court)" after "court-martialed."

4. **"servile insurrection" — partially explained but not enough.**
   > "the crime of 'inciting servile insurrection,' meaning for the crime of leading them"
   > The gloss "meaning for the crime of leading them" is too thin. Recommend: "meaning the crime of leading enslaved people in armed revolt against their enslavers."

5. **"fatigue duty" — used as if synonymous with labor, but it's a military technical term.**
   > "handed more of the exhausting fatigue and labor duty"
   > Gloss: change to "handed more of the non-combat labor duty the army called 'fatigue work' — digging fortifications, hauling supplies, building camps."

6. **"Gullah-Geechee" — undefined.**
   > "Gullah-Geechee freedpeople from the coastal islands of South Carolina, Georgia, and Florida"
   > Gloss: add a brief parenthetical — "(an enslaved community with their own distinct West African-rooted language and culture)."

7. **"broadside" — unclear to most 15-year-olds.**
   > "most famous recruiting broadside"
   > Gloss: change to "most famous recruiting broadside (a printed sheet posted publicly, the era's equivalent of a flyer)."

8. **"retroactive" — likely unknown to the target reader.**
   > "made it retroactive"
   > Gloss: add "(meaning it covered time already past, so soldiers could collect back pay from their enlistment date)."

9. **"prisoner of war" — worth a one-phrase definition given the section's stakes.**
   > "handled as ordinary prisoners of war"
   > Gloss: add "(captured enemy soldiers kept alive under rules both sides were supposed to follow)."

10. **"cavalry" — undefined on first use.**
    > "Confederate cavalry under Maj. Gen. Nathan Bedford Forrest"
    > Gloss: add "(soldiers who fight on horseback)" on first use.

11. **"division" — undefined.**
    > "a division of USCT under Brig. Gen. Edward Ferrero"
    > Gloss: add a parenthetical — "(a large military unit, several regiments combined)."

12. **"Creole community" — undefined.**
    > "New Orleans's old Creole community"
    > Gloss: add a brief parenthetical — "(a mixed-race community of French and Spanish descent, often free and propertied, with deep roots in New Orleans)."
