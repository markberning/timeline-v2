## HERO IMAGE

### Reynolds's Political Map of the United States (1856)

**Why this spot:** The perfect opening frame. Published in 1856 at the height of Bleeding Kansas, this is a contemporary period artifact — a map explicitly designed to show the comparative area of free vs. slave states and territory "open to slavery or freedom by the repeal of the Missouri Compromise." It carries the exact argument the section makes: the West as contested ground, the 36°30′ line recently erased, the crisis live and screaming. Bold colors, dramatic statistical annotations at the borders, and portraits of Frémont and Dayton (the Republican ticket) in the margins — it reads like a campaign document for the fight. A hero image of this map immediately signals to the reader: this is a story about land, and the land was the argument.

## SPOTS WITHOUT A GOOD PD IMAGE

**Daniel Webster portrait:** The Commons file `Daniel_Webster.jpg` has unknown artist and unknown date — the metadata gives no reliable date of publication, making the PD basis uncertain. There are better-documented options: the Library of Congress holds dated Webster photographs, but they are not on Commons with clear attribution. The `Daniel Webster addressing the United States Senate 1850 LCCN2004670675.jpg` print (a 1860 engraving from LOC, confirmed by API) shows Webster at the Senate podium during the very debate the narrative describes — this could replace or supplement the portrait. If a Webster face-portrait is wanted:
- `File:Daniel_Webster.jpg` is likely PD (it appears to be a daguerreotype of pre-1860 vintage) but the undated/unattributed metadata is a verification gap.
- Recommended: use the 1860 Senate-floor print as the Webster illustration, OR accept the `Daniel_Webster.jpg` portrait on the practical grounds that all daguerreotypes of Webster predate 1853 (he died that year) and are thus clearly pre-1929.

**"Sack of Lawrence" contemporaneous image:** No clean period print of the May 21, 1856 Sack of Lawrence surfaced on Commons. The Border Ruffians engraving (Figure 7) serves the Bleeding Kansas section adequately, but a specific Lawrence illustration would strengthen that beat. A Library of Congress search for "sack of Lawrence Kansas" may yield something via direct LCCN lookup.

**Nashville Convention (1850):** No period image found for the Nashville Convention. This is a relatively minor narrative beat and probably doesn't need an image.
