## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name:** Second Battle of Corinth (a.k.a. the Battle of Corinth, 1862; distinct from
  the **Siege of Corinth** / "First Corinth," the bloodless May 1862 Union advance). **[WIKI]**
- **Dates:** **October 3–4, 1862** (two days). **[WIKI] [ABT] [AHC]**
- **Location:** **Corinth, Alcorn County, Mississippi** (NE corner of the state, near the
  Tennessee line). Coordinates ~34°56′N 88°31′W. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Theatre:** **Western Theater.** Part of the **Iuka–Corinth Operations** (Sept–Oct 1862). **[WIKI]**
- **Result:** **Union victory.** **[WIKI] [ABT] [AHC]**

### Why Corinth mattered — the rail junction (strategic spine)
- Corinth was the **crossing of two strategic railroads**: the **Mobile & Ohio** (running
  **north–south**, linking the Gulf at Mobile to the Ohio River) and the **Memphis &
  Charleston** (running **east–west**, the only continuous rail line linking the Atlantic
  coast to the Mississippi River). The two **"linked up there."** **[ABT]** Holding the
  junction meant controlling Confederate logistics and troop movement across the western
  theater — it was, in the period phrase, a **"crossroads of the Confederacy."**
  *(The "crossroads of the Confederacy" epithet is widely used; treat as period framing,
  the two-railroad fact is the hard claim. [VERIFY] the exact epithet wording if quoted.)*
- **Prior history:** After **Shiloh (April 1862)**, Union Maj. Gen. Henry **Halleck** advanced
  on Corinth in a slow, entrenched **siege (the May 1862 "Siege of Corinth")**; the
  Confederates under Gen. **P.G.T. Beauregard evacuated the town in late May 1862**,
  abandoning it to Union control. **[ABT]** Corinth had been Union-held ever since.
- **Prelude — Iuka:** The **Battle of Iuka (September 19, 1862)**, ~20 mi east, was the
  opening move of the same campaign (Rosecrans vs. Price); it set up Van Dorn's October
  attack on Corinth itself. **[WIKI]** *(Iuka detail is prelude context — author may
  reference lightly; full Iuka facts are out of scope of this pack.)*
- **Why Van Dorn attacked:** to **retake the rail junction**, exploit the **scattered Union
  garrisons** across northern Mississippi, and **support Gen. Braxton Bragg's invasion of
  Kentucky** by tying down / driving back Grant's forces in the western theater. **[ABT]**

  **Army of the Mississippi** (the Corinth garrison). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant (North)** — **overall departmental command** (District/Army
  of West Tennessee); not on the field at Corinth but directing the theater and the
  reinforcement/pursuit. **[ABT]**

  (center division), **Brig. Gen. David S. Stanley** (reserve, then counterattack),
  **Brig. Gen. Charles S. Hamilton** (right division), **Brig. Gen. Thomas J. McKean**

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (north = up):

- **The town of Corinth** sits at the **rail crossing**: the **Mobile & Ohio RR runs
  north–south** through it; the **Memphis & Charleston RR runs east–west** through it. The
  two cross at the depot — that crossing is the literal objective. **[ABT]**
- **Three concentric defensive lines** (Rosecrans inherited a three-tiered system) **[ABT]:**
  1. **Outer works** — the **old Confederate entrenchments** (built by Beauregard in spring
     1862), several miles N/NW of town; **too long a front to hold**, used only as a forward
     tripwire on Oct 3.
  2. **Halleck Line (intermediate)** — about **1.5 miles** out, built during Halleck's siege;
     also too long.
  3. **Inner "College Hill Line"** — the close-in battery line **immediately outside town**,
     anchored by **five batteries** including **Battery Robinett** and **Battery Powell**.
     This is where the battle was decided Oct 4. **[ABT]**
- **Battery Robinett** — a **redan** (arrowhead-shaped earthwork) on the **west/northwest**
  side, **commanding the Chewalla Road** approach; armed with **three 20-pounder Parrott
  rifles**, fronted by a **five-foot ditch**. The focal point of the Oct 4 assault. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Battery Powell** — a battery to the **north/northeast** of Robinett along the inner line;
  briefly **captured by the Confederates (Green's brigades)** Oct 4, then retaken by Union
  counterattack. **[WIKI]**
- **Chewalla** — village/road **northwest** of Corinth; Van Dorn's army **approached from the
  northwest down the Chewalla Road**, which is precisely the axis Battery Robinett covered.
  **[ABT]** *(Chewalla is the direction of the Confederate approach — author/map should place
  it NW, with the attack arrow striking Robinett's front from the NW.)*
- **Approach roads:** the **Chewalla Road (NW)** is the main attack axis; the battle also
  references roads/rail leading toward **Bolivar/Jackson (NW/N, Union reinforcement direction)**
  and the **Hatchie River crossing to the west** (the retreat route — see §5). **[WIKI]**
- **Map orientation summary:** Town + rail-cross center; Robinett on the **NW/W face**
  guarding the **Chewalla Road**; Powell to its **N/NE**; old Confederate outer works
  arcing across the **N/NW** several miles out; Confederate attack arrows come **from the NW
  (Chewalla)** and N; retreat arrow exits **W toward the Hatchie/Davis bridges.**

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup:** Van Dorn **(South)** moved against Corinth to retake the junction Rosecrans
**(North)** held, gambling he could overwhelm the garrison before Grant concentrated. **[ABT]**

### October 3 — driving the Union back through the old works
- Van Dorn **attacked ~10 a.m.** along the **old Confederate outer entrenchments N/NW of
  town.** **[WIKI]**
- Union deployment: **three forward divisions** — **McKean (left) (North)**, **Davies
  (center) (North)**, **Hamilton (right) (North)** — with **Stanley (North)** in reserve
  south of town. **[WIKI]**
- Van Dorn attempted a **double envelopment**: **Lovell (South)** hit the Union left
  (McArthur's brigade); **Maury (South)** pressed Davies's left. **[WIKI]**
- **~1:30 p.m. — a gap opened between Davies and McKean**, forcing the **whole Union line
  to fall back** to within **half a mile of the inner redoubts (toward the Halleck/inner
  line).** **[WIKI] [ABT]** The day went to the Confederates — they pushed the Federals out
  of the outer works.
- Union generals **Hackleman (North) killed** and **Oglesby (North) severely wounded** in
  the Oct 3 fighting. **[WIKI]**
- **Turning point that wasn't:** Hamilton **(North)** was ordered ~3 p.m. to flank the
  Confederates, but **"through a misunderstanding of the order ... so much time was lost
  that it was sunset before the division was in position."** Daylight ran out with the
  Confederates short of a decisive breakthrough. **[WIKI]** Van Dorn's lament (see §7):
  *"One hour more of daylight and victory would have soothed our grief."* **[WIKI]**

### October 4 — the bloody repulse
- **~4:30 a.m. — Confederate artillery bombardment** opens. **[WIKI]**
- **~7 a.m. — Hébert (South) reports ill;** **Green (South)** takes his command — a delay
  that cost the morning's momentum. **[WIKI]**
- **~9 a.m. — the infantry assault on the inner line begins** (four brigades). **[WIKI]**
- **Battery Powell:** Green's **(South)** brigades **captured Battery Powell** — the
  Confederates' high-water moment — but a **Union counterattack (Davies + Hamilton, North)
  drove them out and recaptured the guns.** **[WIKI]**
- **Battery Robinett (the climax):** **Maury's division (South)** assaulted Robinett — *"a
  redan protected by a five-foot ditch, sporting three 20-pounder Parrott rifles."* The
  attackers met **"a terrific musket and artillery fire"**; **desperate hand-to-hand fighting
  on the parapet.** **Col. William P. Rogers (South, 2nd Texas)** and **Col. John Daly (South,
  18th Arkansas)** were both **killed** here. Maury withdrew **"with heavy losses from
  arguably the hottest action of the two-day battle."** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Phifer's brigade (South) briefly broke into Corinth itself** — reached the town — but was
  **driven back by Sullivan's reserve brigade (North)** and **crossfire from the Union
  batteries, "utterly routing them."** **[WIKI]**
- **~1 p.m. — the battle is effectively over.** **~4 p.m. — McPherson's reinforcements (North)
  arrive from Jackson.** **Van Dorn orders a general retreat.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### The retreat & pursuit
- Van Dorn **(South)** withdrew **west toward the Hatchie River**, aiming for the crossings.
- **October 5 — Battle of Hatchie's Bridge (Davis Bridge)**: a Union force under **Maj. Gen.
  Edward Ord (North)** (with Hurlbut) blocked Van Dorn at the Hatchie crossing; Van Dorn was
  forced to find another ford and escaped south to **Holly Springs, Mississippi.** **[WIKI]**
- Rosecrans **(North)** was **criticized for not pursuing hard on Oct 4** (see Grant's quote, §7).
- **Aftermath for Van Dorn:** a **court of inquiry cleared him** of drunkenness/negligence
  charges, but he was **superseded** — command in Mississippi passed to **Lt. Gen. John C.
  Pemberton (South).** **[WIKI]**
- **Aftermath for Rosecrans:** became a **Northern hero**; promoted to command the **Army of
  the Ohio (soon renamed the Army of the Cumberland)**, replacing **Don Carlos Buell (North).** **[WIKI]**

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the local hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and each side — was ultimately fought over. Omission is
> the default Lost Cause failure; "states' rights" / "a railroad town" framing alone fails the
> gate. Corinth makes this *easy and concrete*, because the throughline is literally on the
> ground here.

### The documented throughline
- The Confederacy fought to preserve and extend **slavery**; Van Dorn's **(South)** army was
  trying to retake the rail junction that fed the Confederate war effort — an effort waged to
  defend a slave society. The Union held Corinth as part of breaking that society apart.
- **The proof is the camp at the edge of town:** while two armies fought over the railroad,
  **thousands of enslaved people were freeing themselves by walking into Union-held Corinth.**
  The battle and the emancipation it protected are the same story.

### STRONG LOCAL HOOK — the Corinth Contraband Camp **[NPS-CAMP]**
The Union turned occupied Corinth into one of the war's **model freedmen's camps** — the single
best illustration on this battlefield of what the war was *for*:
- **Established 1862 by Brig. Gen. Grenville M. Dodge (North)** to shelter freedom-seekers
  ("contrabands") flooding into Union lines. Operated **1862 to December 1863**, when it was
  **relocated to Memphis.** **[NPS-CAMP]**
- A real community, not a holding pen: **"numerous homes, a church, school and hospital."** **[NPS-CAMP]**
- **Self-supporting free enterprise:** freedmen and women **operated cooperative farms**,
  growing and selling **cotton and vegetables** — *"By May 1863, the camp was making a clear
  profit of $4,000 to $5,000"* from its enterprises. **[NPS-CAMP]** (A wider account: ~**300
  acres of cotton + ~100 acres of vegetables**, profits ~$5,000/month — *attribute to NPS/
  Shiloh interpretive material; [VERIFY] the acreage line before printing as exact.*)
- **Education:** *"By August, over 1,000 African American children and adults gained the
  ability to read through the efforts of various benevolent organizations."* **[NPS-CAMP]**
  *(Benevolent/missionary bodies — the American Missionary Association is commonly credited;
  [VERIFY] AMA-by-name before asserting it, as the NPS page doesn't name it.)*
- **Scale:** **~6,000 formerly enslaved people** passed through the Corinth camp. **[NPS-CAMP]**
- **The camp fed Black regiments — the payoff for the war's cause:** **nearly 2,000 of the
  freedmen here enlisted**, forming the **1st Alabama Infantry Regiment of African Descent,
  later re-designated the 55th United States Colored Troops (USCT).** **[NPS-CAMP]** People who
  had been property a year earlier put on Union blue and fought for their own freedom — within
  sight of the battlefield where Van Dorn's army had tried to win the war for the other side.
- **Today:** an NPS site (part of **Shiloh National Military Park**) with a **walkway and
  six groupings of life-size bronze statues** of the camp's residents. **[NPS-CAMP]**

**Author directive:** weave the cause in where the section sets its stakes — the railroad was
the *object*, slavery was the *reason*, and the contraband camp on the town's edge is the human
proof. The camp is the section's emotional anchor and satisfies the NAME-THE-CAUSE gate concretely.

## 9. Significance

- **Secured Corinth and West Tennessee for the Union** — the Federals **held the junction for
  the rest of the war**, denying the Confederacy the western theater's key rail crossing. **[ABT]**
- **Wrecked Van Dorn (South)** — his army was bloodied (~2:1 losses), he was superseded by
  **Pemberton**, and the Confederate bid to retake the line collapsed. **[WIKI]**
- **Iuka + Corinth were the last serious Confederate offensives along the Mississippi** in this
  theater. **[HOW]**
- **Freed Grant to turn toward Vicksburg** — *"From the Confederate retreat from Corinth, to
  the final surrender of Vicksburg on 4 July 1863, the initiative on the Mississippi belonged
  to Grant and the Union."* **[HOW]** Corinth secured Grant's rear and let him pivot to the
  campaign that would split the Confederacy.
- **Made Rosecrans (North) a national figure** — promotion to the Army of the Cumberland. **[WIKI]**
