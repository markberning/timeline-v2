## SHIP-BLOCKING FIRST-CHECK

### (a) Are Native nations first-class actors with their own aims, not props? — PASS

The draft treats the Ohio nations as a third power with their own strategy, not a
backdrop or a tool of either empire. This is not a token gesture; it is the spine of
the piece.

- The opening frames the land as belonging to the resident nations: "The people who
  did live there, Shawnee and Delaware and Mingo and Wyandot families, had already
  moved once to get away from men exactly like these." The section closes its own
  intro with "the people whose ground it was all along."
- The dedicated section "The land does not belong to either of you" states it
  outright: "Here the Ohio nations step forward in their own right, because they were
  never the backdrop to this story. They were a third power inside it, playing the two
  empires against each other to hold their own ground."
- Tanaghrisson is given his own voice at length — three verbatim quotes from
  Washington's Journal — and the draft is careful to flag the provenance ("filtered
  through a British envoy's ear and pen... but it is the genuine primary record").
- The Iroquois-vs-resident-nations tension (who actually had the right to sell the
  Ohio) is dramatized exactly as the fact pack asks, with the British preference for
  dealing through the Iroquois named as a mechanism of dispossession: "Buy the land
  from a council that did not live there, and you never had to ask the families who
  did."
- The nations' aims and leverage are stated as their own: "Their own aim was never to
  choose a European master. It was to keep both empires at arm's length and hold their
  ground." Leverage is named as "real but bounded" and "running out" — agency without
  romanticizing it as limitless.

This is a model treatment of the rule. PASS.

### (b) Is the human cost / dispossession named in the section's OWN prose? — PASS

Named plainly and repeatedly, not euphemized:

- "The settlement condition and the dispossession are the same fact stated two ways.
  The Company existed to take that ground and sell it."
- Full closing section "What was already being taken": "The contest for the Ohio was a
  contest over Native land, and the people who lost it were the people who lived
  there."
- "The dispossession that the rest of this war would bring down on the Ohio nations
  was already visible, already in motion, before the war was even fully lit."
- "the land grab came first. The war came after. And the families on the ground at the
  Forks were about to pay for both."

The cost is in the section's own voice, not buried in a quote or a caption. PASS.

### The "French and Indian War" name acknowledgment — PASS (handled where natural)

The draft explicitly addresses the loaded name: "The colonists who fought this war
called it the French and Indian War, naming it for the enemies they expected to face.
The name centers their point of view and it flattens everyone else into one word. The
Native nations were not one thing and not one side. They fought on all sides and, more
than anything, for themselves." This satisfies the third framing directive directly.
