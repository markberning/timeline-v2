**Verdict: PASS with MUST-FIX corrections (2 must-fix, several should-fix).**

## B. Strengths & casualties (as ranges)

- **~31,000 Union / ~21,000 Confederate, "half again larger"** — VERIFIED. WIKI 31,610
  vs 21,102; ABT ~31,945 vs ~21,000. "Half again" (1.5×) is accurate. Good.
- **~5,700 Union / ~2,900 Confederate; "winner lost ~twice as many"** — VERIFIED. WIKI
  5,665 / 2,910; ABT 5,764 / 3,060. Ratio ~1.9–2.0×. Draft correctly rounds and
  attributes the bulk of Union loss to the morning rout. Good.
- **"more than 1,300 prisoners" (Confederate take) / "more than a thousand captured"
  (Union loss share)** — VERIFIED as a defensible split (Union captured/missing = 1,591).
  Draft correctly does not assert a precise camp-capture number. Good.

## C. Named officers / human cost

- **Ramseur (South †): shot through both lungs near the Miller house ~5–6 p.m.; third
  horse, first two killed; captured (1st Vermont Cavalry); carried to Belle Grove
  (Sheridan's HQ); died next day** — VERIFIED (NPS, RAMSEUR-WIKI). NPS: "two horses shot
  beneath him … before … his fatal wound," i.e. he was on his third when hit — the
  draft's "third horse … the first two already killed" is exactly right.
- **West Point friends Custer, Merritt, + Capt. Henry DuPont at the deathbed** — VERIFIED
  (NPS: "West Point classmates … Custer and Merritt, plus Captain Henry DuPont … former
  academy colleagues"). Draft says Custer/Merritt "had been classmates and friends" and
  DuPont was present — accurate. **SHOULD-FIX nuance:** DuPont (West Point 1861) was a
  near-contemporary but the marquee classmate bond is Custer (1861) / Ramseur (1860);
  the draft's grouping is fine, no change required.
- **"DuPont remembered Ramseur grasping his hand" — NO quote printed** — VERIFIED and
  CORRECT discipline. The actual line ("Du Pont, you know how I suffer") is NPS-sourced
  but the draft deliberately did not print it verbatim. Clean.
- **Daughter born "the day before"; flower worn; never met child; widow + newborn** —
  VERIFIED (NPS: daughter Mary born days before; "flower in his lapel to honor his new
  child"). **SHOULD-FIX:** NPS says the daughter was born "just days before the battle,"
  not strictly the day before; RAMSEUR-WIKI is where "learned the day before" comes from.
  The draft's "The day *before* the battle, Ramseur had learned that his wife had given
  birth" is defensible (he learned of it then), but soften to "shortly before" if the
  fact-checker can't pin "the day before" the **learning** vs the **birth**. Low risk.
- **Thoburn (North) mortally wounded in the dawn rout** — VERIFIED (WIKI).
- **Bidwell** — the panel was asked to scrutinize Bidwell (North). **NOTE / SHOULD-ADD
  (see comprehensiveness gate):** Bidwell (Brig. Gen. Daniel D. Bidwell, North) was
  *killed* at Cedar Creek per WIKI and the fact pack §6, but the **draft never names
  him.** Not a factual error (nothing wrong is said), but the brief flagged him; the
  draft uses Thoburn for the Union-death beat. Not ship-blocking on fact grounds.

## D. SPECIAL SCRUTINY ITEMS

### (a) Sheridan's Ride — myth discipline — VERIFIED, CLEAN
- Poem **"Sheridan's Ride" attributed to Thomas Buchanan Read (1864), never to
  Sheridan** — VERIFIED. Draft is explicit and correct.
- **~12 miles vs the poem's "twenty miles"** — VERIFIED (multiple sources: real distance
  ~12 mi; poem inflates to 20). Draft names the gap explicitly. Correct.
- **"Turn, boys, turn! We're going back!" labeled Read's invention, NOT documented
  speech** — VERIFIED. The line is the poem's; the draft prints it only as the poem's
  invention and explicitly says it is not "anything Sheridan is recorded as saying."
  Exactly the required handling. The poem refrain and closing ("Here is the steed that
  saved the day … From Winchester, twenty miles away!") are verbatim from Read. Clean.
- Sheridan's actual rally rendered as **paraphrase**, variants described not quoted —
  CORRECT. (Documented variants e.g. "Retreat, hell…" exist; draft does not assert one.)
- **"an electric shock"** as a reported soldier's impression — VERIFIED (WIKI/NPS), used
  unattributed-to-a-name as the fact pack advised. Clean.

### (b) "Stopped to plunder" — VERIFIED as a LEADING INTERPRETATION, CLEAN
- Draft presents the plunder/"battle that stopped for breakfast" explanation as "a
  leading interpretation, not a proven cause," and names the competing explanations
  (exhaustion, Early's caution). Matches the scholarly state. Correct.
- **Gordon flagged as an interested party** — VERIFIED and CORRECT. Draft states the
  Gordon–Early feud and labels the memoir "a brief for the prosecution." Good.

### (c) Belle Grove — VERIFIED (core), one MUST-FIX-adjacent sourcing caveat

## E. ERRORS / UNSUPPORTED ITEMS

1. **WRONG — MUST-FIX: "in Frederick County … close to a third of households enslaved
   people."** This figure is **not supported** by the cited source. Belle Grove's
   enslaved-community page (the fact pack's [BG] cite) contains **no** Valley-wide or
   Frederick-County percentage at all (verified directly). Independent sources put
   Frederick County's **enslaved share of population ~15% in 1860** — about half the
   draft's number. "Households that enslaved" is a different (and plausibly higher)
   metric than "population enslaved," but the draft prints a hard "close to a third"
   with no locatable source, and the only nearby published figure (~15%) contradicts a
   population reading. **FIX:** cut the "close to a third of households" claim, or
   replace with a sourced figure and state clearly whether it is population-share or
   households-owning-share. Same applies to the **"~a fifth of the Valley's population …
   held in bondage (1810–1860)"** line — directionally true (the Valley was a slave
   society) but the specific ~20% figure is **UNSUPPORTED by [BG]** as the fact pack
   claimed; it needs a real cite or should be softened to "a substantial share." This is
   the draft's one genuine factual-precision failure and it sits in the cause paragraph,
   so it must be fixed before ship.

2. **SHOULD-FIX: "Frederick County — where this battle would be fought."** The
   battlefield spans **Frederick, Shenandoah, and Warren** counties; Belle Grove/
   Middletown sits in Frederick. Saying the battle "would be fought" in Frederick County
   is close enough for the house-at-the-center beat but slightly overstated. Minor.

3. **SHOULD-FIX: Gordon "fatal halting" quote.** Draft prints: the "fatal halting" had
   "converted the brilliant victory of the morning into disastrous defeat in the
   evening." VERIFIED against Gordon's *Reminiscences* — the full sentence is "the fatal
   halting … and the isolated movements … lost us the great opportunity, and converted
   the brilliant victory of the morning into disastrous defeat in the evening." The draft
   stitches two non-adjacent fragments ("fatal halting" + the "converted…" clause) with
   an ellipsis-free join. The words are all Gordon's and the meaning is faithful, but to
   be clean either (a) insert an ellipsis between the two quoted fragments, or (b) quote
   only "converted the brilliant victory of the morning into disastrous defeat in the
   evening" and paraphrase "fatal halting." Currently reads as a single continuous quote
   when it is two. SHOULD-FIX for verbatim hygiene.

4. **SHOULD-FIX (typo, breaks meaning): "close to a third of households enslaved
   people"** and (§1 ¶2) the breadbasket sentence read as if a verb is missing
   ("households enslaved people" — should be "households **enslaved** people" i.e. "owned
   enslaved people," or "households **held** enslaved people"). Copy error; fix alongside
   #1.

5. **VERIFIED, no change: "third horse," 1st Vermont Cavalry capture, Rienzi→"Winchester"
   rename, statue in Washington, "only eclipsed by Grant and Sherman," Lincoln overjoyed,
   Mobile Bay (Aug) + Atlanta (Sept), Tribune front page Election Day Nov 8 1864, "may
   well have affected Lincoln's margin."** All check out.
