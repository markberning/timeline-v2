**Gate verdict: FAIL**
**❌ WRONG: 5 | ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED / NEEDS HEDGING: 6**

## MUST-FIX (❌ WRONG)

### ❌ 1 — Achilles Clark quote is SELECTIVE in a way that actively distorts the premeditation question

**Draft quotes:**
> "The poor deluded negroes would run up to our men, fall upon their knees and with uplifted hands scream for mercy but they were ordered to their feet and then shot down."

**What the draft says about Clark:** "This is not a Northern propagandist describing the enemy. This is a Confederate describing what his own side did."

**The problem:** The draft presents Clark as evidence that the massacre occurred while simultaneously saying the section "leaves Forrest's premeditation an honest open question." But Clark's *full letter* — well-documented across multiple sources including Wikipedia, Tennessee State Parks, and History.com — contains an additional sentence that the draft does not quote and does not acknowledge:

> "I with several others tried to stop the butchery and at one time had partially succeeded **but Gen. Forrest ordered them shot down like dogs and the carnage continued.**"

This sentence directly bears on the premeditation question. The draft cannot (a) cite Clark as its single hardest piece of evidence, (b) present the quote as if complete with nothing omitted, and (c) simultaneously frame premeditation as a genuinely open historical question — without disclosing that the same letter contains a sentence directly alleging Forrest gave the kill order. The omission is not editorially neutral. Either:

- The full Clark letter must be disclosed (including the "Forrest ordered them" sentence), **with honest acknowledgment that defenders dispute its interpretation** (soldiers escaping, Clark's vantage point, etc.), OR
- The section must not present Clark as "the single hardest piece of evidence" while suppressing the most explosive part of the same letter.

**Correct action:** Disclose the full letter. The sentence can be contextualized (it is disputed as to whether Forrest gave a direct verbal order or whether Clark misread command confusion), but the reader must know it exists. Its suppression is worse than its inclusion with appropriate hedges.

**Sources:** [Battle of Fort Pillow — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Pillow) · [Tennessee State Parks](https://tnstateparks.com/parks/history/fort-pillow) · [History.com Fort Pillow Controversy](https://www.history.com/articles/fort-pillow-controversy-lingers-150-years-later)

### ❌ 2 — Population displaced by General Order No. 11: "~20,000" understates the consensus figure

**Draft says:** "The result was a forced exodus of roughly 20,000 people."

**Sources say:** The most commonly cited and consistently used figure across authoritative sources (Wikipedia, Civil War on the Western Border, Legends of America, general scholarly consensus) is approximately **25,000**. The 20,000 figure appears occasionally but is the lower bound; the consensus estimate is 20,000–25,000 with 25,000 cited most frequently.

**Correct range:** "roughly 20,000 to 25,000 people" — or use "approximately 25,000" per the dominant source consensus.

**Sources:** [Wikipedia — General Order No. 11 (1863)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Order_No._11_(1863)) · [Civil War on the Western Border](https://civilwaronthewesternborder.org/encyclopedia/general-order-no-11)

### ❌ 3 — Lawrence Massacre raider force size: "~300 to 400" is the low estimate; better-attested figure is 400–450

**Draft says:** "Quantrill led roughly 300 to 400 raiders into Lawrence."

**Sources say:** The National Park Service and multiple secondary sources (Lawrence Journal-World, Civil War on the Western Border) give 300–400; BlackPast and additional sources give 400–450. The raid force is most consistently put at **400 to 450** in the more detailed accounts, with 300–400 being the NPS lower-end figure. The draft's choice of the lower range without noting the higher is not flat wrong, but is the least-supported option. Should be written as "roughly 400 to 450 raiders" or "between 300 and 450 raiders, sources vary."

**Sources:** [NPS battle detail](https://www.nps.gov/civilwar/search-battles-detail.htm?battleCode=ks001) · [BlackPast — Lawrence Massacre](https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/quantrills-raid-on-lawrence-kansas-1863/) · [Lawrence Journal-World](https://www2.ljworld.com/news/2013/aug/18/bloody-predawn-raid-shaped-lawrences-history/)

### ❌ 4 — Lawrence property damage understated; "$1M–$1.5M" is the low range; "$2M" is the dominant figure

**Draft says:** "did something on the order of a million to a million and a half dollars in damage in 1863 money."

**Sources say:** BlackPast, multiple secondary sources, and local historical records cite approximately **$2 million (1863 dollars)** as the property damage figure. "A million to a million and a half" is the low estimate and is not the figure most sources support.

**Correct fact:** "approximately $2 million in 1863 dollars" (some sources say $1.5M–$2M; no reliable source puts the primary figure as low as $1M).

**Sources:** [BlackPast](https://blackpast.org/african-american-history/quantrills-raid-on-lawrence-kansas-1863/) · [Lawrence Massacre — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Massacre)

### ❌ 5 — Centralia: Union soldiers on the train described as "returning from the Atlanta campaign" — this is ambiguous/partially wrong

**Draft says:** "24 unarmed Union soldiers — men on furlough, heading home from the Atlanta campaign."

**Sources say:** Multiple sources (Wikipedia, Civil War on the Western Border) confirm the soldiers were on furlough and heading home, but specifically they were from the 39th Missouri Infantry (newly formed, not returning veterans in the field sense) — the men were furloughed soldiers heading home through Missouri, not all "returning from the Atlanta campaign" as a unit. The specific description "returning from the Atlanta campaign" appears in some sources but others describe them more simply as soldiers on leave heading home. The furlough/leave detail is confirmed; the specific "Atlanta campaign" attribution is the contested element. Wikipedia states they were "returning home on leave after the Battle of Atlanta" — so that attribution is used in some sources, but the unit identifications don't fully match a single campaign. The draft's phrasing echoes what sources say, though the factpack should verify unit IDs. This is a borderline WRONG/UNSUPPORTED but is flagged here because the count is also contested (the draft says "24" but Wikipedia says "23 Union soldiers" were pulled off the train and 22 were killed; the 24th figure appears in some sources). **The soldier count should be hedged: "23 or 24."**

**Sources:** [Centralia Massacre — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralia_Massacre_(Missouri)) · [Civil War on the Western Border — Centralia](https://civilwaronthewesternborder.org/encyclopedia/centralia-massacre)

### ⚠️ 1 — KKK founding date: draft hedges correctly ("December 24, 1865, but 1866 is the year the thing took shape") — but the hedge is slightly confused

**Draft says:** "The Ku Klux Klan was founded by six Confederate veterans in Pulaski, Tennessee, in 1866 — some accounts date the very first meeting to December 24, 1865, but 1866 is the year the thing took shape."

**Sources say:** This is broadly defensible as written. The December 24, 1865 date is confirmed as the first meeting; "took shape" in 1866 is the mainstream view; some sources call it "May or early June 1866." The hedge structure is reasonable. No correction required but the reader might benefit from a slightly tighter framing: "first met December 24, 1865; took organized form in 1866."

**Status:** Acceptable as hedged; no mandatory fix.

### ⚠️ 2 — Forrest elected Grand Wizard: draft says "spring of 1867" — confirmed, but the draft also says he "joined in the fall of 1866"

**Draft says:** "Nathan Bedford Forrest joined in the fall of 1866, and in the spring of 1867 he was elected its first Grand Wizard."

**Sources say:** Multiple sources confirm the spring/April 1867 Maxwell House Nashville meeting and election as Grand Wizard. The "fall of 1866" joining date is plausible but less precisely confirmed — some sources say he was recruited before the April 1867 meeting and was sworn in at the Maxwell House. This may be right but should be flagged as a detail needing factpack citation. The spring 1867 Grand Wizard date is solid.

**Status:** Grand Wizard spring 1867 ✅. "Joined fall 1866" ⚠️ — plausible but confirm against factpack.

### ⚠️ 3 — Achilles Clark's letter date: draft calls it "two days after the fight"; one search result says April 14, another April 15

**Draft says:** Clark "two days after the fight sat down and wrote home to his sisters" (battle was April 12).

**Sources say:** The Tennessee State Archives document apparently dates the letter to April 15, 1864 (three days after), while other sources cite April 14 (two days after). The draft's "two days after" claim is consistent with April 14 but may be off by one day. This is a minor point but since the letter's authenticity and date are load-bearing evidence, it should be confirmed. The factpack cites "Apr 14, 1864" which is within the documented range.

**Status:** Minor — verify April 14 vs. April 15 against the primary source.

### ⚠️ 4 — Forrest "slave trader and planter" description — accurate but incomplete

**Draft says:** "a self-made Memphis slave trader and planter before the war."

**Sources say:** Confirmed accurate. Forrest was indeed a Memphis slave trader and planter. However, his biography also shows he was a millionaire largely through the slave trade specifically, which is already implied. No error — just note that all sources confirm this.

**Status:** ✅ Confirmed. No change needed.

### ⚠️ 5 — "Only two executed for war crimes" hedge — the draft handles this well, but the Magruder claim needs sourcing verification

**Draft says:** "Ferguson and Wirz are the two men *usually named*, among the very few ever held to that account" and names Henry C. Magruder as a possible third.

**Sources say:** The NPS Andersonville site explicitly notes that Wirz was NOT the only person tried for war crimes (a direct mythbuster), and both Ferguson and Magruder are confirmed as also executed for war crimes. The draft's hedge ("usually named … among the very few") is the correct and honest framing. Magruder is confirmed as a real third case.

**Status:** ✅ Draft's hedge is correct and sourced. No change needed.

### ⚠️ 6 — Front Royal: the draft says "some sources say seven" — the true count is six at Front Royal + one captured in early October = seven total triggering the retaliation

**Draft says:** "Union forces under the command of Philip Sheridan and George Custer executed six of Mosby's captured men — some sources say seven — at Front Royal, Virginia."

**Sources say:** At Front Royal itself, six Rangers were executed on September 23, 1864. A seventh was captured and executed separately in early October in Rappahannock County. Mosby selected seven prisoners for retaliation to match the total of seven. The "some sources say seven" hedge as applied to Front Royal itself is slightly misleading — the seventh was not at Front Royal. The draft should say "six at Front Royal, with a seventh captured separately in early October — seven total — prompting Mosby to select seven for retaliation."

**Sources:** [Iron Brigader — Front Royal Executions 1864](https://ironbrigader.com/2010/10/19/johns-s-mosby-george-a-custer-front-royal-executions-1864/) · [Executed Today — Six of Mosby's Rangers](https://www.executedtoday.com/2009/09/23/1864-six-of-mosbys-rangers/)

## ADDITIONAL NOTE ON POISON SPRING PHRASING

**Draft says:** "of roughly 300 Union casualties that day, the great majority were from that single Black regiment."

**Sources say:** Union total losses at Poison Spring were 301 (killed, wounded, missing). The 1st Kansas Colored Infantry suffered 117 killed + 65 missing (presumed murdered) = ~182 of 301 total casualties. That is a majority (60%) but "great majority" implies more like 80%+. The phrasing should be "the majority" not "the great majority." This is a ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX, not a hard error, but it's imprecise.

**Sources:** [Battle of Poison Spring — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poison_Spring) · [Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/engagement-at-poison-spring-37/)
