# Champion Hill — FACT-CHECKER gate findings (war pipeline step 4)

Web-verified against: Wikipedia "Battle of Champion Hill"; American Battlefield Trust
battle-facts + both long articles ("Champion Hill" / "Battle of Champion's Hill");
Warfare History Network "Savage Encounter"; Grant *Personal Memoirs*; NPS Vicksburg NMP
"The Crossroads at Champion Hill"; mississippiencyclopedia.org. Every date, number, name,
rank, place, and quote in the draft was independently checked. This gate CAN fail the
draft; it identifies one MUST-FIX.

---

## ❌ MUST-FIX

**1. "The hill of death" is misattributed to Hovey's *men* — it was Hovey *himself*.**
The draft (§2 prose + the Meanwhile/OUTCOME beats + Fact ledger #1) repeatedly states
that *Hovey's soldiers / Hovey's men* named the height: "afterward they gave the height a
name. They called it the hill of death." The source record is specific and different:
the phrase is Alvin P. Hovey's own, written after the battle.
- **Correction:** Render as Hovey's own phrase, e.g. "the division's commander, Alvin P.
  Hovey, called it — in the words he wrote afterward — 'the hill of death.'" This also
  *upgrades* it from a paraphrase to a verifiable near-verbatim quotation, so the Fact
  ledger #1 hedge ("paraphrased attribution… not a single verbatim quotation from a named
  soldier") is now resolvable and should be rewritten.
- **Source (exact wording):** ABT, "Battle of Champion's Hill": *"Hovey was almost in
  shock as he surveyed the battlefield. 'Champion Hill was, after the battle, literally
  the hill of death,' he wrote."*
  https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/battle-champions-hill
  (Corroborated: search snippet "Alvin P. Hovey of the 12th Division, XIII Corps, later
  referred to Champion Hill as the 'hill of death.'")
- **Why it's a MUST-FIX:** It is a concrete, checkable attribution error — words put in
  the wrong mouth (a named division of soldiers vs. their named general). This is exactly
  the caption/attribution failure class the pipeline gate exists to catch.

## ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX

**A. Bowen's counterattack time — "around two in the afternoon" is on the early edge.**
Draft §3 says "Around two in the afternoon." Sources split: ABT long article says "Around
2 p.m."; Warfare History Network says "Bowen's men charged… at 2:30 p.m." The draft's
"around two" is defensible but skews to the earlier figure. Safest fix: "around two to
two-thirty in the afternoon" or "in the early afternoon." Not a hard error.
Sources: https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/champion-hill ;
https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/article/the-battle-of-champions-hill-savage-encounter/

**B. Grant "between Johnston and Pemberton" quote — over-hedged; it is verbatim Grant.**
Draft §4 (block quote + Fact ledger #2) flags the wording as "a secondary attribution"
needing [VERIFY]. The wording is genuine and verbatim from Grant's *Personal Memoirs*:
*"We were now assured of our position between Johnston and Pemberton, without a
possibility of a junction of their forces."* The [VERIFY] is **resolved** — the draft may
quote Grant directly and drop the "secondary attribution" caveat if it wants. (Leaving it
as substance is not an *error*, just unnecessary caution.)
Source: https://www.historyonthenet.com/battle-of-champion-hill (quoting Grant's memoirs);
substance corroborated throughout.

**C. ABT battle-facts strength figures differ (54,000 vs 32,000) — note, not error.**
The draft uses ~32,000 (US) vs ~22,000–23,000 (CS), matching Wikipedia's infobox and the
WHN "32,000-strong" framing. ABT's battle-facts page lists 54,000 vs 32,000 (campaign-area
totals). The draft's numbers are well-sourced and it correctly hedges ("something on the
order of"). No change required; flagging the source discrepancy for the record.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Champion_Hill (32,000 vs 22,000) ;
https://www.battlefields.org/learn/civil-war/battles/champion-hill (54,000 vs 32,000).

## ✅ VERIFIED — the gate's required confirmations all hold

- **Near-even fight, NOT "Grant's much larger army" — CONFIRMED, and the draft frames it
  correctly.** WHN: *"With Sherman out of range and McClernand idle, the Army of the
  Tennessee had lost the advantage of superior numbers. McPherson's corps, about equal in
  strength to Pemberton's forces, would have to bear almost all of the fighting for the
  Federals."* The draft (§1) explicitly does the right thing — "forget the picture of a
  much larger army steamrolling a smaller one… a near-even, savage fight." No false
  "Grant's much larger army" claim anywhere. ✓
- **Sherman absent — CONFIRMED.** XV Corps stayed at Jackson; "out of range." (WHN; WP.) ✓
- **McClernand idle / barely engaged — CONFIRMED.** WP: "weakly attacked"; Grant himself:
  "Had McClernand come up with reasonable promptness… I cannot see how Pemberton could
  have escaped." ✓
- **Crest changed hands "several times" — CONFIRMED as three times.** "During several
  hours of bitter fighting, Champion Hill changed hands three times" (search/ABT/map).
  Draft's "several times" + narrated three-stage sequence (Union → Bowen → Union) is
  accurate. ✓
- **Casualties — CONFIRMED.** Union ~2,457 (410 K / 1,844 W / 187 M); Confederate ~3,840
  (381 K / 1,018 W / 2,441 captured); combined ~6,297. Matches WP + ABT exactly. Draft's
  ~2,457 / ~3,840 / ~2,441 missing / "something like" hedging all correct. ✓
- **Loring's division cut off — CONFIRMED.** ABT: cut off during the retreat, could not
  cross Baker's Creek, marched around to join Johnston near Jackson, never rejoined
  Pemberton / never entered Vicksburg. ✓
- **Tilghman killed by a Union artillery shell at the Baker's Creek rearguard —
  CONFIRMED.** ABT long article: "struck in the body by a Federal artillery shell." ✓
- **Stephen D. Lee atop the hill, spotted the Jackson-Road flank column, warned it would
  cut the army off; not Robert E. Lee — CONFIRMED** (paraphrase, no words put in his
  mouth — correct). ✓
- **Champion Hill = 75-foot bald crest — CONFIRMED.** ABT: "a 75-foot-high bald-crested
  hill." Draft's "about seventy-five feet high" correct. ✓
- **Crossroads ~⅓ mile / ~600 yards below the crest, where Jackson Rd & Middle Rd met —
  CONFIRMED.** NPS "The Crossroads at Champion Hill" + ABT (600 yds ≈ one-third mile).
  Draft's "perhaps a third of a mile down from the crest" correct. ✓
- **Three roads (Jackson / Middle / Raymond), Union main effort up the Jackson Road,
  Loring on the Raymond Road south end — CONFIRMED.** ✓
- **Crest seized ~1:00 p.m., Stevenson's left broken/in disorder; Grant arrived ~10 a.m.;
  skirmishing opened ~dawn (≈7 a.m.) — CONFIRMED** (WP). ✓
- **Crocker's division as Grant's reserve stopping Bowen — CONFIRMED.** Search/ABT: Boomer's
  brigade of Crocker's division plugged the breach from Bowen's counterattack; Crocker's
  7th Division, XVII Corps committed. Draft's "Brigadier General Marcellus Crocker's men"
  correct (rank Brig. Gen. correct). ✓
- **Logan's division on the Jackson Road with Hovey — CONFIRMED.** ABT: Hovey "supported by
  the men of Maj. Gen. John A. Logan's division"; Grant: "Hovey's and Logan's divisions
  brought on the battle." ✓
- **Grant's "well selected / highest points / commanded all the ground" position quote —
  CONFIRMED verbatim** from *Personal Memoirs* (WP). Draft's block quote is accurate. ✓
- **Champion plantation framing — SUPPORTED, not overstated.** Draft asserts the hill was
  the Champion family's plantation land (correct — origin of the name) and that the county
  was worked by enslaved people / valley-wide self-emancipation (NPS-solid). It does NOT
  assign a Champion-family headcount. Framing is supported; the §5 [VERIFY] flag is
  resolved as "general framing OK, no over-claim." ✓
- **Slavery named as the cause in every section's own prose — CONFIRMED** present in all
  four sections (framing gate satisfied). ✓
- **Bloodiest single day of the Vicksburg campaign — SUPPORTED** (commonly cited; draft
  hedges "frequently called"). ✓
