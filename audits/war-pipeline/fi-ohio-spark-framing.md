# Framing / Fairness Gate — "The Ohio Spark"

**Gate:** French and Indian War, Phase 1 narrative
**Critic role:** Framing, fairness, sympathy distribution, sanitization check
**Date:** 2026-06-02

---

## FIRST-CHECK RESULT

**1. Native nations as first-class actors with their own aims?**
PRESENT AND STRONG. Tanaghrisson is the chapter's structural argument. The opening paragraph announces him explicitly — "the man who turned that skirmish into a killing was not Washington and not the French. He was a Native leader pursuing his own war, for his own reasons, using both empires as his instruments." The full treatment in "Fifteen Minutes in a Ravine" gives his biographical arc, his contested "Half-King" title, his eroded power base, and — most importantly — the full architecture of his statecraft: the deliberate provocation, the blaming of Washington, the goal of British expulsion as the path back to Mingo relevance. The closing section names the Shawnee, Delaware, and Iroquois Confederacy as distinct actors with distinct strategies. This is genuine first-class treatment, not decorative mention.

**2. Human cost named?**
PRESENT. Jumonville's killing is recounted with care — the surrender that did not save him, Tanaghrisson's act, the killing of the French wounded. Washington's giddy "something charming in the sound" letter is quoted immediately after, which is an honest and unflattering juxtaposition. The closing paragraphs name directly that "the people who lived there would be the ones with the most to lose" and follow Tanaghrisson to his death (pneumonia, October 1754). The bill the Ohio nations would pay is stated plainly, not buried or softened.

**3. Does the chapter address what the NAME "French and Indian War" centers and erases?**
PRESENT. The final section ("The Spark and the World It Burned") addresses this directly and without hedging: "The name centers the British colonial view and flattens everyone else, lumping dozens of distinct nations into a single word, 'Indian,' and casting them as the enemy or the auxiliary, never as players with aims of their own." This is clear, plain, and correctly placed at the end as a capstone rather than a defensive throat-clearing up front.

**FIRST-CHECK VERDICT: PASS — all three checks met.**

---

## OVERALL RATING: **FAIR**

The draft does not tilt toward Washington or the British colonial POV in any structural way. The French perspective is given fair weight (the diplomatic case for Jumonville, Saint-Pierre's measured firmness, the legitimate French interest in the Ohio). Native perspectives are the interpretive center of the most important section. No false "both sides equally right/wrong" glibness appears. The violence is shown honestly and without gratuitousness.

## FINDINGS

### MUST-FIX

None identified. The First-Check tests all pass. No finding rises to structural distortion, sanitization, or erasure.

---

### SHOULD-FIX

**SF-1 — FAIR | Tanaghrisson's sentence count vs. Washington's sentence count in the early sections**

The first three sections ("The Land in the Middle," "The Forts and the Ultimatum," "The Forks Are Lost") are largely Washington-POV narration. Tanaghrisson appears at the end of "The Forts" section in two sentences, with a promise ("He had his own reasons… Washington did not fully grasp them") that is redeemed later but leaves a gap in the early movement. This is structurally defensible — the chapter earns the payoff in "Fifteen Minutes in a Ravine" — but a single early sentence about what Tanaghrisson was *trying to accomplish* at Logstown (not just that he joined the party) would anchor his agency earlier and reduce the risk that a casual reader stops before the payoff paragraph. Low priority; the structure works as is.

**SF-2 — FAIR | Delaware (Lenape) and Shawnee named in closing, but their distinct positions are not introduced earlier**

The draft introduces the Ohio Country as home to "several Indigenous nations" but names only Tanaghrisson and the Mingo specifically until the final section. The Delaware and Shawnee leaning toward France because of settler farming is stated in "The Land in the Middle" as a general point (FAIR — it is there) but without naming those nations yet. The closing section retroactively attaches the names. This is a light coherence issue, not a distortion. A reader who reads the chapter in order gets the point, but slightly delayed. Acceptable.

**SF-3 — FAIR | Washington quote ("charming in the sound") placement**

The quote is placed just after the Jumonville killing section, as a tonal juxtaposition. It works well and does not sanitize. One small note: the draft attributes it with "written days later to his brother" and the fact pack confirms the exact date (May 31, 1754 — three days after the battle). The draft's framing is accurate. This is not a fairness issue; flagged only because the juxtaposition is so effective it should stay exactly where it is.

**SF-4 — TILTED (minor) | Dinwiddie conflict of interest named for British/Virginia side; no analogous structural critique of French motives**

The draft correctly and explicitly names Dinwiddie's Ohio Company stake as a driver of Virginia's aggression — good. But the chapter does not offer a parallel structural observation about French imperial ambitions (e.g., the fur-trade economic interest of the French merchant class and colonial administrators; the Marquis Duquesne's career advancement stakes). This asymmetry is minor — the war was genuinely driven more by British land hunger than French commercial interests, so equal treatment would be false balance. The current framing is defensible. Still worth a brief acknowledgment that French forts served economic and political interests too, not just geopolitical strategy, to avoid the implicit "France = rational strategy / Britain = greed" binary.

**SF-5 — FAIR | Albany Congress: Iroquois diplomatic crisis mentioned, but Hendrick is named only in the fact pack**

The Covenant Chain rupture (Mohawk declaring the alliance broken after 800,000 acres of fraudulent land seizures) is mentioned in the Albany section. The draft correctly attributes this to Native grievance. However, the specific Mohawk chief Hendrick, who made the declaration, is named in the fact pack but not in the draft. Hendrick's absence is not a distortion — the point lands without him — but naming him would give the Iroquois side a human face in the Albany section the same way Tanaghrisson has one in the Jumonville section. A "should" not a "must."

### NOT FINDINGS (things that looked like candidates but are not)

- **"Tu n'es pas encore mort, mon père" treatment:** The draft acknowledges this comes from "French-derived accounts" and notes graphic details are uncertain. Honest handling, not sanitization and not credulity toward the hostile source.
- **Washington's "charming" quote:** Not romanticized. The draft lets the quote speak for itself; the reader has just read the killing and the aftermath. No narrative voice celebrates it.
- **Jumonville's status (diplomat vs. spy):** The draft presents both cases and correctly concludes the ambiguity was "built in" — neither false-balancing nor dismissing the French claim. Clean.
- **Tanaghrisson's anti-French personal grievance (his father):** The draft includes the claim but frames it as "He claimed" and notes the killing was also "a calculated act of statecraft." Neither dismissing the personal dimension nor letting it stand as full explanation. Good.
- **Fort Necessity defeat:** Washington's tactical failures (the overlooked hillsides, the flooded trenches) are stated plainly. Not softened.
- **The "assassinat" document:** The draft correctly notes the word was "very likely seeded with the word 'assassinat' on purpose" by Louis Coulon de Villiers — which is what historians believe. Not whitewashing the French manipulation.
- **The Walpole quote:** The draft correctly flags attribution as "doubtful, and probably wrong" and skips the direct quote, exactly as the fact pack recommended. Clean.

## SUMMARY

The draft passes the framing gate. Native agency is the chapter's structural argument, not a garnish. The human cost — Jumonville's killing, the Ohio nations' eventual dispossession, Tanaghrisson's death — is named without gratuitousness. The name problem is addressed directly at the end. No British/Washington sympathy crowding found. The SHOULD-FIX items are light coherence and depth notes, not distortions.
