# Cedar Creek — GATE 3: LOST CAUSE / FRAMING

**Verdict: FAIR — PASS. No framing MUST-FIX. One SHOULD-FIX (shared with fact gate).**
This is, on framing grounds, a model war section. The romantic Sheridan's-Ride material
— the single biggest sanitization risk at Cedar Creek — is explicitly subordinated to
the cause rather than allowed to crowd it out. The draft repeatedly says, in its own
voice, what the war and the Confederacy were *for*.

---

## FIRST CHECK (ship-blocking) — is slavery named as the cause, in the section's own prose?

**YES — emphatically, in the author's own voice, in multiple places.**

- §1 ¶2: *"The Confederacy existed to preserve and extend slavery, and the Valley was a
  slave economy that proved it."* — direct, unhedged, the section's own assertion.
- §1 ¶2: the wheat *"was grown by enslaved people"*; the Union attacked *"the food supply
  of a slaveholding army, raised on land worked by the very people the war was being
  fought to free."*
- "The Burning" §: *"the goal was cold and explicit: starve Lee's army by destroying the
  slave-worked farms that fed it."*
- §what-it-won: *"slavery was the reason the breadbasket mattered"*; Belle Grove *"was a
  slave plantation … the most concrete proof on this entire battlefield of what the war
  was about."*
- Closing MEANWHILE: *"what was at the center of the field was slavery."*

The word "slavery"/"enslaved" appears throughout, and the cause is **named as the
cause** (not merely as background economics). Passes the locked NAME-THE-CAUSE gate
without a hint of softness. This is the opposite of the default Lost Cause omission.

---

## Hooks — honest and non-tokenizing?

- **Breadbasket / The Burning** — FAIR. The draft refuses to let "breadbasket" stand as
  a neutral granary: it explicitly states the breadbasket is the *object* and slavery the
  *cause*, and that the Burning was "a deliberate strike at the food supply of a
  slaveholding republic," "not vandalism." This is the honest reading and avoids the
  common trap of romanticizing the Valley as pastoral.
- **Belle Grove** — FAIR and load-bearing, not tokenizing. The plantation is not dropped
  in as a one-line gesture; it is the structural payoff the section is built toward ("a
  house with a name … impossible to miss," set up in §1 and paid off in §what-it-won).
  The enslaved are named as people-who-were-freed-by-the-war's-aim, not as scenery.
- **1864 election** — FAIR. Framed as the *emancipation referendum* ("a negotiated peace
  that could have left slavery standing"; "the war to end slavery"), not as generic
  political drama. The "may have affected Lincoln's margin" claim is correctly hedged as
  uncountable. Non-triumphalist.

## Does the romance crowd out / sanitize the cause? — NO.

This was the explicit worry in the brief, and the draft pre-empts it deliberately:
- The closing line — *"The dramatic cavalry ride is the part everyone remembers. The
  slave plantation at the center of the field is the part that explains why anyone was
  riding at all."* — actively *inverts* the usual hierarchy, demoting the legend beneath
  the cause. That is the strongest possible anti-sanitization move.
- The ride is repeatedly de-mythologized (poem ≠ speech; 12 ≠ 20 miles), so the romance
  is never allowed to inflate into heroic myth that displaces the stakes.

## Soft-pedaling / false balance / euphemism / presentism — clean.

- **No false balance.** No "both sides fought bravely for what they believed" equivocation;
  no states'-rights euphemism; the Confederacy's purpose is stated flatly.
- **The cross-enemy-line Ramseur deathbed** (Custer/Merritt/DuPont) is the one spot where
  romanticization could creep in — a tender "brothers in arms" beat. The draft keeps it
  human without sanitizing the cause: Ramseur is "a general who fell defending it [the
  slaveholding Valley]" (closing MEANWHILE). The friendship is rendered as tragedy *within*
  a war fought over slavery, not as proof the war was a noble shared misfortune. This is
  the correct handling — it does not let the pathos launder the cause. **Watch-item only,
  not a defect:** ensure the integration step does not later trim the "fell defending it"
  framing and leave the deathbed as pure unframed sentiment; as drafted it is fine.
- **No presentism.** The slavery framing is grounded in documented period economics and
  the explicit Union war aim, not retrofitted moral judgment.

---

## SHOULD-FIX (shared with fact gate — framing-relevant because it's *in* the cause ¶)

- The **unsourced "~fifth of the Valley enslaved / close to a third of [Frederick County]
  households" figures** are a framing liability as well as a fact one: planting an
  unverifiable hard statistic inside the name-the-cause paragraph gives a hostile reader
  the one thread to pull to discredit the (correct) larger point. Replace with a sourced
  figure or soften to qualitative ("a substantial enslaved population," "a slave society")
  so the cause rests on the *verified* Belle Grove specifics (276 enslaved; 103 in the
  1810 census; built and worked by enslaved labor), which are unimpeachable. SHOULD-FIX.

---

## TOP framing finding
**No DISTORTED or TILTED content — the section is FAIR and, on the cause, exemplary.**
The only action is to shore up the *one unsourced demographic figure* sitting in the
cause paragraph so the (correct, well-named) slavery framing cannot be attacked on a
sourcing technicality.
