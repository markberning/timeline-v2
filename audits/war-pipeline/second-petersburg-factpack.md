## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name:** Second Battle of Petersburg (a.k.a. the **Petersburg "Initial Assaults"** or
  "First Offensive," June 15–18, 1864; distinct from the **First Battle of Petersburg** /
  Battle of Old Men and Young Boys, June 9, 1864, and from the **9½-month Siege of Petersburg**
  it began). **[WIKI] [BTC]**
- **Dates:** **June 15–18, 1864** (four days of Union assaults). **[WIKI] [ABT] [BTC]**
- **Location:** **Petersburg, Virginia** (and Prince George County), on the south bank of the
  **Appomattox River**, **~24 miles south of Richmond.** Coordinates ~37°13′N 77°23′W. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Theatre:** **Eastern Theater.** Opening phase of the **Richmond–Petersburg Campaign /
  Siege of Petersburg.** **[WIKI]**
- **Result:** **Confederate victory / Union failure to take the city** (Wikipedia hedges
  "Union strategic failure"; ABT calls it a **Confederate victory**; some summaries say
  "inconclusive/stalemate" tactically — *the unambiguous outcome is that the Union assaults
  FAILED to take Petersburg and a siege began*). **[WIKI] [ABT]** *(State the substance — the
  city held, the siege began — not a single contested label. [VERIFY] which label the
  fact-checker prefers.)*

### Why Petersburg mattered — the rail hub (strategic spine)
- Petersburg was a **railroad junction** **~24 miles south of Richmond**: it was the point
  where **five railroads converged**, feeding the Confederate capital. **[ABT]** ABT's framing:
  the **"junction point of five railroads that supplied the entire upper James River region."**
  **[ABT]** *(The "five railroads" figure is ABT's; the load-bearing fact is that Petersburg
  was the supply throat of Richmond.)*
- **The logic Grant (North) saw:** Richmond could not be fed or supplied except through
  Petersburg. **Take Petersburg and Richmond falls; with Richmond falls Lee's (South) army.**
  After a year of head-on bloodletting in the Overland Campaign (the Wilderness, Spotsylvania,
  **Cold Harbor**), Grant chose to **cut the supply line instead of charging the capital.** **[WIKI]**
- **The stolen march (the maneuver that set up the battle):** rather than attack Lee head-on
  after **Cold Harbor (June 3, 1864)**, Grant **disengaged secretly, marched the Army of the
  Potomac southeast, and crossed the James River** — over a **2,200-foot pontoon bridge at
  Windmill Point** (one of the war's engineering feats) — to strike Petersburg from the east
  **before Lee realized he was gone.** **[WIKI] [ABT]** For two days **Lee did not know where
  Grant's army was** (see §5). The whole battle is the story of whether Grant could exploit
  that head start before Lee caught up.

## 2. Forces & Commanders (SIDE-TAGGED)

> Side-tag rule: every named person tagged **(North)** = Union / **(South)** = Confederate
> on first mention per section. Civil War: **North = Union; South = Confederate (CSA).**

### Union (North)
- **Lt. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant (North)** — overall Union commander (general-in-chief),
  directing the campaign; designed the cross-the-James / seize-Petersburg plan. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. George G. Meade (North)** — commanding the **Army of the Potomac** (the main
  body crossing the James). **[WIKI]**
- **Maj. Gen. Benjamin F. Butler (North)** — commanding the **Army of the James** (already
  near Bermuda Hundred; XVIII Corps was drawn from his army for the opening assault). **[WIKI]**
- **Brig. Gen. William F. "Baldy" Smith (North)** — commanding the **XVIII Corps**; led the
  **June 15 opening assault** and **hesitated that evening** instead of pressing into the city
  (the battle's central might-have-been — see §5). **[WIKI] [ABT] [ECW]**
- **Brig. Gen. Edward W. Hinks (North)** — commanding the **U.S. Colored Troops division of
  the XVIII Corps**; his USCT division **spearheaded the June 15 assault** and **captured a
  string of Dimmock Line batteries** (the cause-hook — see §8). **[WIKI] [ABT] [WIYS]**
  *(Spelled "Hincks" in some sources; [VERIFY] preferred spelling — Wikipedia uses "Hinks.")*
- **Maj. Gen. Winfield S. Hancock (North)** — commanding the **II Corps**; arrived the
  evening of June 15 but was **uncertain of his orders** (the "lost order" / map confusion)
  and **deferred to Smith's decision to wait** (see §5). On **June 18 he was incapacitated by
  his unhealed Gettysburg wound** and handed off command to **Birney.** **[WIKI]**
- **Maj. Gen. David B. Birney (North)** — took over the II Corps from Hancock on June 18. **[WIKI]**
- **Maj. Gen. Ambrose E. Burnside (North)** — commanding the **IX Corps** (engaged June 16–18). **[WIKI]**
- **Maj. Gen. Gouverneur K. Warren (North)** — commanding the **V Corps** (arrived for the
  June 18 assault; halted before Rives's Salient). **[WIKI]**
- Other engaged commanders **(all North):** **Brig. Gen. Francis C. Barlow** (division,
  II Corps, took Redans 13–15 June 16); **Brig. Gen. Robert B. Potter** (IX Corps, dawn

  June 18); **Brig. Gen. August Kautz** (cavalry); **Col. Joshua L. Chamberlain** (brigade,

### Confederate (South)
- **Gen. Robert E. Lee (South)** — commanding the **Army of Northern Virginia**; **out of
  position and uncertain of Grant's whereabouts** for the battle's first three days; began
  **rushing divisions to Petersburg starting ~3 a.m. June 18** once the crossings were
  confirmed by his son. **[WIKI]**
- **Gen. P.G.T. Beauregard (South)** — in **immediate command of the Petersburg defenses**;
  held the city with a **skeleton force** by bluff, shifting troops and digging new lines
  faster than the Union pressed. The defense of Petersburg is **Beauregard's finest hour.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Brig. Gen. Henry A. Wise (South)** — commanded the **~2,200 men initially holding the
  Dimmock Line** on June 15 (a brigade of reserves, militia, and home guard). **[WIKI] [BTC]**
- Reinforcing division commanders **(all South):** **Maj. Gen. Robert F. Hoke** and **Maj. Gen.
  Bushrod R. Johnson** (arrived June 16–17); **Maj. Gen. Joseph B. Kershaw** and **Maj. Gen.
  Charles W. Field** (Lee's First Corps divisions, arrived ~noon June 18). **[WIKI]**
- **Maj. Gen. W.H.F. "Rooney" Lee (South)** — R.E. Lee's son; his cavalry's **confirmation of
  the James River crossings** is what finally convinced Lee where Grant was. **[WIKI]**
- **Col. Dennis D. Ferebee (South)** — led the **cavalry roadblock at Baylor's farm** (~850
  riders + artillery) that delayed Hinks's USCT on the morning of June 15. **[WIYS]**
  *([WIYS] popular-source for the Baylor's farm detail; [VERIFY] strength/commander.)*

### Strengths (each grows day-by-day as reinforcements arrive — give as ranges)
- **June 15:** **Smith's ~16,000 (incl. Hinks's ~3,700–5,000 USCT)** vs. **Beauregard's
  ~2,200 (Wise's brigade).** The disparity is the whole point — the city was **nearly
  undefended.** **[WIKI] [BTC] [WIYS]**
- **June 16:** **~50,000 Federals** vs. Beauregard's **~14,000** (he had concentrated
  Hoke + Johnson). **[WIKI]**
- **June 18:** **~67,000 Federals** (with Warren's V Corps up) vs. Beauregard's **over 20,000**
  (Lee's divisions arrived). **[WIKI]**
- **Campaign totals quoted by ABT:** **Union ~104,000 / Confederate ~62,000** — *these are the
  full available forces in theatre, NOT all engaged at Petersburg on any one day; the
  day-by-day figures above are what mattered.* **[ABT]**

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (**north = up**):

- **Richmond** sits **~24 miles to the NORTH**; **Petersburg** is the city being fought over,
  on the **south bank of the Appomattox River.** **[ABT]** The strategic logic runs vertically:
  Petersburg below, Richmond above, the rail lines connecting them.
- **The Appomattox River** runs roughly **west-to-east along the NORTH edge of Petersburg**
  and **anchors both ends of the Confederate Dimmock Line** (the line's east and west flanks
  both rest on the river). **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **The James River** is the **larger river to the NORTH/NE** that Grant's army crossed
  (pontoon bridge at **Windmill Point**) to reach the south side — the crossing that stole the
  march. The James and Appomattox **join east of the battlefield** (City Point, Grant's supply
  base, sits at their confluence — *[VERIFY] before naming City Point if used*). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **The Dimmock Line** — the Confederate fortified ring: **a ~10-mile arc of 55 artillery
  batteries (numbered) connected by infantry earthworks**, curving **east, south, and west**
  around Petersburg, **both ends anchored on the Appomattox River.** **[ABT] [EV]** The June 15
  Union assault struck its **EASTERN/NORTHEASTERN face.** Beauregard's 2,200 men held the
  sector **"between Redan number 1 on the Appomattox River and Redan number 23"** on June 15,
  with infantrymen **spaced ~10 feet apart** — far too thin to hold. **[WIKI]**
- **The captured batteries (the cause-hook geography):** on June 15 the Union assault **"swept
  over the earthworks on a 3.5-mile front, capturing Batteries 3 and 5–11"**; **Hinks's USCT
  division captured Batteries 6 through 11** on the **eastern face** by evening. **[WIKI] [ABT]
  [EV]** *(Sources vary slightly — "Batteries 3–12," "5–11," "6–11"; the firm claim is that
  the USCT carried a long stretch of the eastern Dimmock works including Batteries 6–11.
  [VERIFY] the exact battery numbers — see §10.)*
- **Baylor's farm** — east of the Dimmock Line on the **Jordan Point Road** approach; the
  Confederate cavalry roadblock here delayed Hinks's USCT on the **morning of June 15.** **[WIKI] [WIYS]**
- **Harrison's Creek** — the **secondary line Beauregard fell back to and entrenched on the
  night of June 15–16**, just west of the lost Dimmock batteries. **[BTC]**
- **Taylor's Branch / Hatcher's lines** — the **new defensive line Beauregard's engineers
  laid ~a mile WEST of the Dimmock Line** by June 17–18, running along a creek to the
  Appomattox; this is the line the disastrous June 18 assaults broke against. **[WIKI]**
- **Rives's Salient (Battery 27)** — strongpoint at the **Jerusalem Plank Road** (present-day
  U.S. Route 301), on the **SOUTH** part of the line; **Warren's V Corps was halted by
  "murderous fire" here on June 18.** **[WIKI]**
- **Map orientation summary:** Petersburg center, on the Appomattox's south bank; Richmond
  labeled ~24 mi **N**; James River crossing arrow coming from the **NE** (Grant's march);
  the Dimmock Line as a **10-mile arc** wrapping the city **E–S–W**, both ends on the
  Appomattox; June 15 Union attack arrows striking the **E/NE face** (Batteries 6–11, USCT);
  Beauregard's fallback lines (**Harrison's Creek**, then **Taylor's Branch**) just **W** of
  the lost works; June 18 assault arrows breaking against the new **W/SW** line and
  **Rives's Salient** at the Jerusalem Plank Road (**S**).

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup:** Grant **(North)** stole a march from Cold Harbor, crossed the James, and aimed to
**seize Petersburg before Lee (South) arrived.** Lee, for three days, **did not know where
Grant's army had gone.** Beauregard **(South)** had to hold the city with almost nothing. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### June 15 — the open door (and the hesitation)
- **Dawn:** **Smith's XVIII Corps (North)** crossed the Appomattox and advanced on the
  thinly-held **eastern Dimmock Line.** **[WIKI]**
- **The transport confusion:** vessels landed Smith's divisions **"almost at random"** at the
  landing sites, scrambling his organization. **[WIKI]**
- **Baylor's farm (morning):** **Hinks's USCT division (North)**, advancing via the **Jordan
  Point Road**, hit a **Confederate cavalry roadblock at Baylor's farm** (Ferebee, South);
  the USCT **launched two attacks, captured a cannon**, but the fight **delayed the advance
  until early afternoon.** **[WIKI] [WIYS]**
- **The assault (evening, ~7 p.m.):** after a long reconnaissance, Smith finally attacked. The
  assault **"swept over the earthworks on a 3.5-mile front,"** with **Hinks's USCT carrying
  Batteries 6–11** of the Dimmock Line. **[WIKI] [ABT] [EV]** *By ~9 p.m. the eastern works
  were Union's; **Petersburg lay open.***
- **THE HESITATION (the hinge of the battle):** with the city all but in his grasp, **Smith
  (North) "decided to wait until dawn to resume his attack"** rather than push the remaining
  mile into Petersburg in the dark. **[WIKI] [ECW]**
- **Hancock arrives, confused:** **Hancock's II Corps (North)** came up that evening — but
  **Hancock "was uncertain of his orders"** (he had not been told Petersburg was the day's
  objective; the orders/map miscommunication is the famous "lost order" failure) and
  **"uncharacteristically deferred to Smith's judgment to wait."** **[WIKI]** *Two corps stood
  outside an undefended city and did not go in.*
- **Beauregard's verdict (see §7):** **"Petersburg at that hour was clearly at the mercy of the
  Federal commander, who had all but captured it."** **[WIKI / Beauregard]**

### June 16 — the window closing
- By morning Beauregard **(South)** had **concentrated ~14,000 men** (Hoke + Johnson) against
  **~50,000 Federals.** **[WIKI]**
- **Grant (North) arrived** with **Burnside's IX Corps.** **[WIKI]**
- **Hancock's assault** opened **~5:30 p.m.**; the Confederates **"fought fiercely, erecting
  new breastworks"** as breakthroughs occurred. **Barlow's division (North) took Redans 13,
  14, 15** but was **driven back**, prisoners taken. **Col. Patrick Kelly (North)**, commanding
  the **Irish Brigade**, was **killed.** **[WIKI]**

### June 17 — piecemeal attacks, drunken failure
- **Dawn:** **Potter's IX Corps brigades (North)** attacked, **captured nearly a mile of works
  and ~600 prisoners**, then **stalled at a second line** under enfilade fire. **[WIKI]**
- **2 p.m.:** **Hartranft's brigade (North)** went forward at a bad angle and was flanked. **[WIKI]**
- **Evening:** **Ledlie's division (North)** attacked and failed; **Ledlie "was observed to be
  drunk."** **[WIKI]** *(The same Ledlie whose drunkenness would doom the Crater two months
  later — author may foreshadow lightly; [VERIFY] before extending.)*
- **That night** Beauregard **(South) withdrew to the new line ~a mile west (Taylor's
  Branch),** trading ground for a stronger position. **[WIKI]**
- **Lee finally moves:** Lee **(South)** got **confirmation of Grant's crossings only when his
  son "Rooney" Lee (South) verified them**, and **"immediately dispatched two divisions … to
  Petersburg, beginning at 3 a.m. on June 18."** **[WIKI]**

### June 18 — the door slams shut
- **Lee's divisions arrive:** **Kershaw and Field (South)** reached Petersburg ~noon, giving
  Beauregard **over 20,000 men**; Grant now had **~67,000** (Warren's V Corps up). **[WIKI]**
- **Dawn:** **II and XVIII Corps (North)** attacked the Union right — Hancock incapacitated,
  **Birney (North) in command.** They **advanced fast into the empty Dimmock works** (not
  realizing Beauregard had pulled back), then **halted against the new second line** under
  heavy fire. **[WIKI]**
- **Noon:** **IX and V Corps (North)** attacked. **Willcox's division (North) "emerged with
  only 1,000 men standing."** **Warren's V Corps (North)** was **halted by "murderous fire
  from Rives's Salient."** **Col. Joshua L. Chamberlain (North) severely wounded** (battlefield
  promotion to brigadier general by Grant). **[WIKI]**
- **6:30 p.m. — the catastrophe:** the final assault failed; the **1st Maine Heavy Artillery
  (North)** — ~900 men just converted from garrison to infantry — lost **632 men in minutes,
  "the heaviest single-battle loss of any regiment during the entire war."** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Aftermath:** Grant **(North) called off frontal assaults.** The **Siege of Petersburg
  began** — **9½ months** of trench warfare, lasting until the **breakthrough of April 2,
  1865** and Lee's surrender at Appomattox **April 9, 1865.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**

## 6. Human cost — named people / vivid documented detail

- **The USCT on June 15 (the day's — and the war's — point):** men of **Hinks's division
  (North)**, **many of them formerly enslaved**, charged and **carried fortified batteries of
  the Dimmock Line** in their first major combat. The achievement is documented and celebrated
  (see §8). **Col. Joseph B. Kiddoo (North)** reported the troops **"behaved in such a manner
  as to give me great satisfaction and the fullest confidence in the fighting qualities of
  colored troops."** **[ABT]** USCT casualties on June 15 ≈ **378** **[WIYS]** *(popular
  source; [VERIFY]).*
- **Sgt.-Maj. Christian A. Fleetwood (North), 4th USCT** — a **free Black man from Baltimore**
  who enlisted in **August 1863**; present in the XVIII Corps' USCT division at Petersburg.
  He is reported to have written that the men **"swept like a tornado over the works."**
  **[WIYS] — [VERIFY]** the exact wording and that it refers to June 15 specifically (the
  "tornado" phrasing is from a popular source; Fleetwood's *diary/letters* are the primary —
  confirm before quoting). Fleetwood went on to **win the Medal of Honor at Chaffin's Farm /
  New Market Heights (Sept 29, 1864)** for seizing the colors **"after 2 color bearers had
  been shot down."** **[ABT]** *(The Medal of Honor was for a later battle, NOT Petersburg
  June 15 — keep that straight. Fleetwood is the human face of USCT valor in this campaign;
  the MoH is context for who these men were.)*
- **Col. Patrick Kelly (North)** — commanding the **Irish Brigade**, **killed June 16.** **[WIKI]**
- **Col. Joshua L. Chamberlain (North)** — the hero of Little Round Top — **severely wounded
  at Rives's Salient June 18** (a wound thought mortal; he survived), **promoted brigadier
  general on the field by Grant.** **[WIKI]**
- **The 1st Maine Heavy Artillery (North)** — garrison troops thrown in as infantry on June 18;
  **632 men lost in ~10 minutes**, the **single bloodiest regimental loss of the war.** This
  is the human image of the four days' futility — the assault era ending in slaughter, the
  siege beginning because there was no other way. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Brig. Gen. James H. Ledlie (North)** — **drunk** during his failed June 17 attack; the
  same incompetence would help wreck the Battle of the Crater that July. **[WIKI]**

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the local hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and each side — was ultimately fought over. Omission is
> the default Lost Cause failure; "states' rights" / "a railroad town" framing alone fails the
> gate. Petersburg makes this *unusually concrete and powerful*, because the cause is standing
> in the assault line on June 15.

### The documented throughline
- The Confederacy fought to **preserve and extend slavery**; Petersburg was the **last shield
  of Richmond, the capital of the slaveholding republic.** The Union was breaking that republic
  apart. Holding or taking Petersburg was, finally, about whether the slave society survived.

### THE HOOK — the USCT take the slaveholders' fortifications (June 15) **[WIKI] [ABT] [EV] [WIYS]**
This is one of the war's clearest, most concrete demonstrations of why it was fought, and it
needs no reaching — **it is the opening event of the battle:**
- The **June 15 assault was spearheaded by United States Colored Troops** — **Brig. Gen.
  Edward W. Hinks's (North) USCT division** in **Smith's XVIII Corps** — **roughly 3,700+
  Black soldiers, many of them formerly enslaved**, most of them **green, in their first major
  combat.** **[WIKI] [ABT] [WIYS]**
- They **stormed and CAPTURED a string of fortified batteries on the Dimmock Line — Batteries
  6 through 11** — carrying **nearly a mile of the enemy's works** in roughly two hours of
  fighting. **[WIKI] [ABT] [EV] [WIYS]** Regiments named in the assault include the **1st,
  4th, 5th, 6th, and 22nd USCT** (e.g. **Maj. John Cook's (North)** four companies of the
  **22nd USCT** working around the flank of **Battery 7**). **[WIYS] — [VERIFY] regiment list
  and per-battery credit against the Official Records.**
- **The image is the argument:** **men who had been held as property** — some of whom may have
  been enslaved within miles of where they now stood — **stormed over the earthworks built to
  defend the capital of the men who had owned them**, and took the guns. **Col. Kiddoo's (North)**
  report — *"the fullest confidence in the fighting qualities of colored troops"* (§7) — is the
  white officer corps registering, in real time, what the freedmen had just proved. **[ABT]**
- **The Dimmock Line itself was built by enslaved labor** — **over 1,000 enslaved people from
  North Carolina and 264 from Virginia's Eastern Shore** were forced to construct it. **[EV]**
  *So on June 15 the works that enslaved hands had been forced to dig were taken by the hands
  of the formerly enslaved.* That is the war's cause made physical, in earth and gunfire, in a
  single afternoon.

**Author directive:** make the USCT June 15 assault the **emotional and thematic spine** of the
section, not a sidebar. The railroad hub was the *object*; **slavery was the reason**; and the
formerly enslaved men carrying the slaveholders' batteries — works that enslaved people had been
forced to build — are the proof. This satisfies the NAME-THE-CAUSE gate **concretely** and is
also the section's strongest story. (Pair it with the tragedy of §5: that valor opened the door,
and white hesitation — Smith's, Hancock's — let it close.)

## 9. Significance

- **It began the Siege of Petersburg** — the war's **longest siege (~9½ months)**, which ended
  only with the **April 2, 1865 breakthrough**, the fall of Richmond, and Lee's surrender at
  **Appomattox (April 9, 1865).** The four failed days of June 15–18 are the **pivot from
  open-field war to trench war** in the East. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **The "Grand Opening Squandered"** — the consensus tragedy: **Petersburg could have been
  taken on June 15 almost without a fight.** Grant's march was brilliant; Smith's hesitation,
  Hancock's confusion over a lost order, and Lee's belated arrival converted a war-shortening
  opportunity into a **nine-month bloodletting** that cost both armies far more than a June 15
  rush would have. **[WIKI] [ECW] [ABT]**
- **It proved USCT combat valor on a major field** — the June 15 assault is a **landmark in the
  record of Black soldiers in the war**, registered even by their own (often skeptical) white
  officers. **[ABT] [WIKI]** *(This is co-primary with the strategic significance — see §8.)*
- **It locked Lee's army in place** — once the siege lines set, the Army of Northern Virginia
  was **pinned defending Petersburg/Richmond**, unable to maneuver; the initiative passed
  permanently to Grant. **[WIKI]**
