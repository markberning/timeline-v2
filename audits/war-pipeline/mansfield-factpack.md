## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name:** **Battle of Mansfield** (Union name) / **Battle of Sabine Crossroads** (Confederate
  name); NPS alt-name **"Pleasant Grove."** **[WIKI] [ABT] [NPS]**
- **Date:** **April 8, 1864** (one day; the fighting that decided it was the late-afternoon
  attack ~4 p.m. to nightfall). **[WIKI] [ABT] [NPS]**
- **Location:** **DeSoto Parish, Louisiana** — about **three miles south of the town of
  Mansfield**, in **northwest Louisiana**, on the road from Pleasant Hill / Natchitoches toward
  Mansfield and Shreveport. Coordinates ~**32°00′44″N 93°39′55″W.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Theatre:** **Trans-Mississippi Theater.** The **decisive battle of the Red River Campaign**
  (March–May 1864). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Result:** **Confederate victory** — a lopsided one; the rout that broke the campaign. **[WIKI] [ABT] [NPS]**

### Why it mattered — the Red River Campaign (strategic spine)
- The **Red River Campaign** was the Union's failed 1864 drive **up the Red River into
  northwest Louisiana toward Shreveport and Texas** — a **combined army-and-navy expedition**:
  **Nathaniel Banks's (North)** Army of the Gulf marching overland, **Rear Adm. David Dixon
  Porter's (North, naval)** gunboat fleet steaming up the river alongside. **[WIKI-RR] [ABT]**
- **Shreveport** was the **headquarters of the Confederate Trans-Mississippi Department** — the
  campaign's military object. By **April 1** Banks had occupied **Grand Ecore and Natchitoches**;
  he had advanced **~150 miles up the Red River** by early April. **[ABT] [NPS]**
- **The campaign's aims were tangled — this is the heart of the section (see §8):**
  1. **Capture Shreveport** and **destroy Taylor's army** in west Louisiana. **[WIKI-RR]**
  2. **Plant the U.S. flag in Texas** — a **warning to French-occupied Mexico**, where Emperor
     **Maximilian** had **~25,000 French troops**; Washington feared French intervention and
     wanted a Union presence on the Texas border. **[WIKI-RR]**
  3. **Seize cotton** — "**as much as 100,000 bales**" from the **plantations along the Red
     River** — for Northern manufacturers and the U.S. Treasury; **cotton speculators rode
     along** and **Porter's gunboats took cotton as naval "prizes."** **[WIKI-RR]** (The cotton
     throughline is the cause hook — see §8.)
  4. **Establish a pro-Union government** in Louisiana under Lincoln's "ten percent" plan. **[WIKI-RR]**
- The campaign was the **Trans-Mississippi piece of Grant's (North) coordinated 1864 offensive**
  — pressure on Confederate armies across multiple fronts at once. **[ABT]** Reinforcing troops
  came from **Sherman (North)** under **Brig. Gen. Andrew J. Smith (North)**, on loan only until
  the end of April — a clock that shaped the whole operation. **[WIKI-RR]**

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (**north = up**; Mansfield is to the NORTH, the Union column comes from the SOUTH):

- **The single stage road through pine woods is the whole story.** The army was advancing
  **north up one narrow road** from Pleasant Hill / Natchitoches toward **Mansfield (to the
  north)** and Shreveport beyond, through **dense pine forest** — a *"forest tunnel."* Banks's
  column was **strung out for two to three miles or more** along this one road: cavalry at the
  head, then **320–350 supply wagons clogging the road**, infantry behind. **[ABT] [HOW]**
- **The clearing / Honeycutt Hill (the stage):** about **three miles south of Mansfield** the
  road broke out of the woods into a **large clearing** (open farmland, on/near the **Moss
  plantation** — *[VERIFY] the "Moss" name before printing*). A low rise in the clearing's
  center, **Honeycutt Hill**, is where the Union van halted and formed; Taylor had earlier
  placed **cavalry there as bait.** **[ABT] [WIKI]**
- **Taylor's line (the crescent):** Taylor positioned **~9,000 men in a rough crescent / "L"**
  along the **western, northern, and eastern edges of the woods** rimming the clearing — i.e.,
  he held the **far (north) tree line and both flanks**, with the open field in front of him and
  the Union column emerging into it. **Mouton's division on the LEFT (east of the road)**;
  **Walker's Texas division on the RIGHT (west of the road)**; **Green's cavalry on the flanks.**
  **[ABT] [WIKI]**
- **The attack axis:** Taylor **attacked the HEAD of the Union column** — striking south/into the
  clearing against the Union van as it deployed, **Mouton hitting east of the road**, **Walker
  wrapping the Union (west) flank.** The Union line **collapsed and was driven back south down
  the road**, the rout colliding with its own **wagon train** jamming the road behind it. **[ABT] [HOW]**
- **Emory's stand (the backstop):** **~2–3 miles back down the road (south)**, **Emory's
  division (North)** formed a **fresh line** (the **"Pleasant Grove"** position) that **halted
  Taylor's pursuit at dusk.** This is the southern edge of the action. **[ABT] [HOW] [NPS]**
- **Map orientation summary:** **Mansfield top (north); the single road runs north–south through
  pine woods; the clearing + Honeycutt Hill in the middle as the battle stage; Taylor's crescent
  along the north + east + west tree lines; Confederate attack arrows strike SOUTH into the
  clearing against the head of the Union column (Mouton east of road / Walker wrapping the west
  flank); Union rout arrow runs SOUTH back down the road into its own wagon train; Emory's
  backstop line forms 2–3 mi south.** A second/zoom map can show the strung-out column on the
  one road (cavalry → wagons → infantry) to make the "couldn't get its strength up" point.

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup:** Taylor **(South)**, outnumbered overall and falling back, chose to **stand and fight
south of Mansfield**, betting he could beat the **head** of Banks's column before its mass came
up. Banks **(North)** had his army **strung out on one road**; he chose to **fight where the van
made contact rather than withdraw.** **[ABT] [NPS] [HOW]**

### Late morning–midday — contact at the clearing
- Around **noon**, the Union cavalry van (**Lee, North**) pushed Confederate skirmishers back
  and **deployed across Honeycutt Hill / the clearing**, then **called for infantry support.**
  **[ABT] [WIKI]**
- **Lee (North) had warned Banks against pressing on** (see §7 quote). Banks decided to fight
  there. **[ABT]**
- A **long standoff (~2 hours)** followed: the two sides faced each other across the clearing
  while each rushed troops forward — **"a race to see which side could bring its forces to the
  front first."** Taylor **(South)** waited, **adjusting his line** (the cigar-on-a-stump image,
  see §6). **[ABT]**

### ~4:00 p.m. — the Confederate attack and the rout
- After ~two hours, **Taylor ordered the assault.** **Mouton's division (South)** charged first,
  **east of the road**, straight at the Union line along the **rail fence / Honeycutt Hill** —
  and was **met by heavy fire; Mouton was killed**, along with several regimental commanders;
  his charge was bloodily repulsed in its first rush. **Polignac (South)** took over the
  division. **[WIKI] [ABT] [WIKI-MOUTON] [WIKI-POL]**
- **West of the road, Walker's Texas division (South) wrapped around the Union position and
  collapsed it.** **Green's (South)** cavalry pressed the flank. The Union line **broke.** **[ABT] [WIKI]**
- **The rout:** the broken Union front fled **south down the single road** and **collided with
  its own cavalry supply train** jamming the road — turning a retreat into a catastrophe. Men
  **threw away weapons and ammunition** running back through Confederate **crossfire**; **~1,500
  were captured**; **~20 guns and ~156 wagons** were taken. **Ransom (North) was wounded** trying
  to rally them. **[WIKI] [ABT] [HOW]**
- **Banks (North)** tried to stem it — *"Form a line here! I know you won't desert me!"* — but
  the men fled anyway (see §7). **[ABT]**

### Dusk — Emory's backstop
- The Confederate pursuit drove **~2–3 miles** south until it ran into **Emory's fresh division
  (North)**, which formed the **third Union line** and **halted Taylor's attack** after more than
  an hour's fighting (~6:00 p.m. onward); **nightfall ended the combat.** **[ABT] [HOW] [NPS]**
- Taylor's **after-dark attempt to turn Banks's right flank failed.** **[NPS]**
- **Taylor's reaction** when told (late) that reinforcements / orders to avoid a general
  engagement were coming: *"Too late, sir. The battle is won."* (see §7). **[ABT]**

### Aftermath — the campaign breaks
- **Banks (North) fell back** that night **to Pleasant Hill.** **[ABT] [HOW]**
- **April 9 — Battle of Pleasant Hill:** Taylor **(South)** attacked again; **A.J. Smith's
  (North)** veterans **repulsed him** — a **tactical Union win**. **But Banks retreated anyway**,
  abandoning the drive on Shreveport. **The Red River Campaign was over and failed.** **[WIKI-RR]
  [ABT] [HOW]** *(Pleasant Hill is a SEPARATE battle — keep it to a one/two-line tie-off, do not
  narrate it here; see §10.)*
- The Union army withdrew down the river (the fleet nearly trapped by falling water at Alexandria
  — *out of scope here, [VERIFY] if referenced*), ultimately ending the expedition. **[HOW]**

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the local hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and this campaign — was ultimately about. Omission is the
> default Lost Cause failure; "a botched expedition over a river town" framing alone fails the
> gate. Mansfield makes the cause **unusually literal**, because the prize Banks was reaching for
> was **cotton — the slave-grown staple itself.**

### The strongest, best-documented hook: the cotton was slave-grown, and everyone knew it
- **One of the campaign's four stated objectives was to seize cotton** — *"as much as 100,000
  bales"* from the **plantations along the Red River.** **[WIKI-RR]** That cotton was grown by
  **enslaved people** on those plantations: the Union's drive up the Red River was, in plain
  terms, a march **toward the wealth of a slave economy** — to take its great crop for Northern
  mills and the U.S. Treasury.
- **The greed was real and documented.** **Banks (North) let cotton speculators ride along** with
  the army; **Porter's (North, naval) gunboats seized cotton as "prizes of war"** and **Porter
  personally made a large sum** from selling it. **[WIKI-RR]** An **1865 congressional
  investigation** aired the cotton-speculation scandal — a **speculator even telegraphed
  President Lincoln** urging him not to let Porter's expedition go up the river until he'd
  heard back. **[WIKI-RR]** The campaign was so shot through with cotton money that historians
  debate whether the **cotton or the military aims** were primary (Ludwell Johnson's *Red River
  Campaign: Politics and Cotton in the Civil War*). **[WIKI-RR]** *(Name the debate, don't
  resolve it; see §10.)*
- **Author directive:** the cause writes itself here — **the object Banks reached for was the
  product of slavery.** A war fought to preserve a slave society (the Confederate side) collided
  with a Union expedition partly chasing the slave economy's signature crop. **Name slavery
  plainly in the section's own prose**, hung on the cotton: the bales rotting in the Red River
  warehouses were grown by enslaved hands, and that is what two armies were maneuvering over.

### Reinforcing hook: Black soldiers in the campaign — the Corps d'Afrique
- **Banks (North) himself had created the Corps d'Afrique on May 1, 1863**, as commander of the
  **Department of the Gulf**, organizing regiments of **Black troops** (infantry, cavalry,
  artillery). **[WIKI-CDA]** **Units of the Corps d'Afrique / U.S. Colored Troops served in the
  Red River Campaign** — including **engineer regiments (3rd and 5th Engineers, Corps d'Afrique)**
  and a **Corps d'Afrique cavalry brigade under Col. William H. Dickey (North)** in the order of
  battle; **recruiting in occupied Alexandria in April 1864 raised more companies of freedmen.**
  **[WIKI-RR] [WIKI-CDA]** Of the USCT infantry in the campaign, the **84th and 92nd saw combat**,
  the **84th at Pleasant Hill (April 9).** **[WIKI-RR]** **Formerly enslaved Louisianans were
  marching in Union blue on the same campaign** — the war's emancipationist edge, on the ground.
  *(Use as the human counter-image to the cotton: the same soil grew the slave-cotton Banks
  wanted AND the free Black soldiers now fighting for the other side.)*

> **DO NOT CONFLATE (critical):** the **racial-massacre atrocity of this campaign is at Poison
> Spring, Arkansas (April 18, 1864)** — Confederates murdering surrendering / wounded Black
> soldiers of the **1st Kansas Colored** — which is a **DIFFERENT battle, in a different state,
> ten days later**, in the **Camden Expedition** wing of the campaign. **It did NOT happen at
> Mansfield.** The author may reference the campaign's **wider racial dimension** ONLY if cleanly
> sourced and clearly separated in time/place — never as a Mansfield event. **[VERIFY]** any
> Poison Spring reference against a dedicated source before printing. See §10.

**Author directive:** lead the cause with **cotton (the slave-grown staple Banks was reaching

## 9. Significance

- **The decisive battle of the Red River Campaign** — Mansfield **broke the Union offensive**;
  it was **the first of the two battles (with Pleasant Hill) that ended any chance of Union
  success.** **[ABT] [WIKI]**
- **It forced Banks's retreat** — back to Pleasant Hill, then (despite the tactical Union win
  there April 9) a full **withdrawal toward Alexandria / down the river**, **abandoning the drive
  on Shreveport and Texas.** **[ABT] [NPS] [HOW]**
- **All four campaign aims failed** — **no Shreveport, no Texas flag, no 100,000 bales of cotton,
  no expanded pro-Union government.** **[ABT/search]** The **cotton and Texas designs collapsed.**
- **One of the most lopsided Confederate victories in the Trans-Mississippi / West** — the
  outnumbered-overall attacker routed the larger army by beating its strung-out head, capturing

- **A humiliation for Banks (North)** — the campaign **effectively ended his military career**;
  the cotton-speculation scandal dogged him into a postwar congressional investigation. **[WIKI-RR]**
- **Prolonged the war in the theatre** — the failed expedition tied up Union troops and **"may
