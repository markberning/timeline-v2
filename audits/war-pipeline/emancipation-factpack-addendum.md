## MUST-ADD 1 — Contraband & self-emancipation (the enslaved force the issue FIRST)
This is the structural fix for the framing problem — it must come BEFORE the Confiscation Acts
in the narrative, because the human pressure produced the legislation.
- **May 23–24, 1861** — more than a year before Lincoln drafted the Proclamation — three
  enslaved men, **Frank Baker, Shepard Mallory, and James Townsend**, rowed to **Fort Monroe,
  Virginia**, and presented themselves to **Maj. Gen. Benjamin Butler (North)**.
- Butler refused to return them, declaring them **"contraband of war."** Fort Monroe became
  known as **"Freedom's Fortress."**
- Within weeks hundreds, then thousands of enslaved people walked to Union lines wherever they
  reached. **~500,000 self-liberated** via contraband camps by 1865. `[VERIFY ~500k]`
- The **First Confiscation Act (Aug 6, 1861)** ratified Butler's logic in law.
- Framing (W.E.B. Du Bois, *Black Reconstruction*, 1935): the enslaved waged what he called a
  "general strike" — their mass action was a primary driver, not background. Present them as
  agents who changed the war's purpose, not passive recipients.
- Sources: NPS "Fort Monroe and the Contrabands of War"; Encyclopedia Virginia; Britannica;
  Wikipedia "Contraband (American Civil War)".

## MUST-ADD 2 — USCT pay discrimination (the fight within the fight)
Fort Wagner / the USCT can't stand on heroism alone — these men simultaneously fought a second
war for their own dignity.
- Black soldiers were paid **$10/month minus a $3 clothing deduction = $7 net**, vs. white
  soldiers' **$13/month** (plus clothing). `[VERIFY exact figures]`
- The **54th Massachusetts refused ALL pay for over a year** in protest, rather than accept
  unequal pay.
- **Cpl. James Henry Gooding** of the 54th wrote directly to **President Lincoln in 1863**
  protesting the inequity.
- **Congress equalized the pay in 1864** (made retroactive), partly through Frederick
  Douglass's lobbying. `[VERIFY 1864 + retroactive]`
- Sources: National Archives ("The Fight for Equal Pay"); Military Times; History Matters (Gooding letter).

## MUST-ADD 3 — Juneteenth & the human reality of freedom
The section's own title promises the "slowest liberation" but ends on Lincoln's hand. Land it
on what freedom actually meant, and end Black-centered.
- **June 19, 1865** — Maj. Gen. **Gordon Granger (North)** issued **General Order No. 3** at
  **Galveston, Texas**, informing the enslaved there they were free — **2½ years after** the
  Proclamation took effect. This is the origin of **Juneteenth**. `[VERIFY date/order/place]`
- Liberation tracked the army's boots: the Proclamation freed people only as Union forces
  reached them.
- Within months, former Confederate states passed **Black Codes (1865–66)** designed to
  re-create plantation-labor conditions by law — freedom arrived contested and incomplete.
- The **13th Amendment** (passed by Congress Jan 1865; ratified **Dec 6, 1865**) was the
  actual legal abolition of slavery nationwide. `[VERIFY ratification date]`
- Sources: Galveston Historical Foundation; National Archives; Britannica "General Order No. 3".

## FRAMING FIX (apply throughout, not a separate beat)
- De-center Lincoln as sole agent. Frederick Douglass was a **pressure-applier throughout
  1861–62** (editorials demanding Black enlistment + emancipation; met Lincoln Aug 1863), not
  just a celebrant at Tremont Temple. Show Black agency in the first half of the story.
