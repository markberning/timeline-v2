# Cold Harbor — LOST CAUSE / FRAMING GATE

> War content pipeline, step 4. First and ship-blocking check: does the section name
> **slavery** as the cause, in its OWN prose? Then a sweep for soft-pedaling, false
> balance, romanticization, euphemism, presentism, unfair sympathy, and whether human
> reality is neither sanitized nor gratuitous.

**VERDICT: PASS (FAIR).** The NAME-THE-CAUSE gate is satisfied, twice, in the section's
own voice — and unusually well. No MUST-FIX. Two SHOULD-FIX balance notes below.

---

## 1. FIRST CHECK — is slavery named as the cause, in the section's own prose? YES.

This is the gate's hard floor, and the draft clears it decisively, in two places, both
in the author's own assertion (not a hedged "some historians say"):

- **§1, paragraph 3 (the stakes-setting):** *"The Confederacy existed to preserve and
  extend the enslavement of Black people; the army dug in west of these crossroads … was
  the shield of a slaveholding republic … The Union army grinding toward it had a war aim
  beyond a city: break the army that kept four million people in bondage."* This is a flat,
  declarative, own-voice statement of the cause at the moment the section sets its stakes —
  exactly what the gate requires, and placed early, not buried.

- **the-pivot (the closing spine):** *"The Confederacy fought Cold Harbor to defend
  slavery … a war, by 1864, explicitly fought to break a slave society … the war that
  freed four million people."* The cause is not a throat-clearing line; it is the
  structural payoff of the whole section, anchored to the concrete, documented USCT-at-
  Petersburg hook.

The word **"slavery"** appears in the section's own prose (not only "enslavement,"
"bondage," "slaveholding republic," "slave society" — all present, but the literal term
"slavery" appears, e.g. "fought Cold Harbor to defend slavery" and "with it, slavery").
The auto-fail trigger (slavery never named) does NOT fire. **PASS.**

---

## 2. The USCT hook — strong, concrete, forward-looking, NOT tokenizing.

The closing makes Black soldiers the *answer* to the question the section opened with,
and does it with documented specificity (Hinks's division, ~3,500, many formerly enslaved,
spearheading June 15 at Petersburg, taking batteries, heavy loss at Baylor's Farm). Crucially:

- It explicitly says they "were not a sideshow. They led." — pre-empting the tokenizing
  move of mentioning USCT as a footnote.
- It draws the two-trenches/one-war line plainly: white Union dead at Cold Harbor and Black
  Union soldiers storming Petersburg "were not in two different wars."
- It names the bitter symmetry: **enslaved laborers forced to dig the Confederate works**
  vs. **free Black men a march away fighting for their own freedom.** This is the single
  best anti-Lost-Cause sentence in the draft.

This is the gate's gold standard: the cause is concrete, on the ground, and load-bearing.

---

## 3. Soft-pedaling / euphemism / Lost Cause tropes — SWEEP.

- **"Confederate victory" stated plainly** — no euphemism, no "valiant defense" gloss. ✔
- **No romanticization of Lee or the Confederate cause.** Lee is "the shield of a
  slaveholding republic." His skill (turning the field into a fort) is credited as
  *tactics*, not virtue. No "gallant," no "noble," no Marble-Man treatment. ✔
- **No "war of Northern aggression" / states'-rights framing.** Absent. The cause is named
  as slavery, not "sovereignty" or "way of life." ✔
- **No sanitizing of the Union side either.** Grant's blunder is owned in his own words;
  the wounded left to die between the lines is told without flinching; "Butcher" is printed,
  not buried. The draft does not turn Grant into a flawless hero. ✔
- **The "regret = pointless tragedy" trap is explicitly disarmed.** The closing MEANWHILE
  ("what 'regret' does and doesn't mean") names the danger directly: "'a pointless
  slaughter' is only half the truth, and the dangerous half if it's the only half." This is
  the war-vertical's named failure mode (butcher's-blunder framing *alone* fails the gate),
  and the draft pre-empts it rather than falling into it. ✔

---

## 4. Human reality — neither sanitized nor gratuitous. FAIR.

- The horror is present and unsanitized: men butchered in a ravine, wounded left for days
  to die of thirst and exposure, the coat-pinning dread.
- It is NOT gratuitous: no lingering gore, no wallowing. The "black as coal" verbatim quote
  was deliberately dropped (fact gate confirms), and the prose carries the suffering without
  it. The balance is right.

---

## 5. Presentism / unfair sympathy — SWEEP.

- **No presentism.** The cause is named in period-true terms (the Confederacy's actual,
  documented war aim), not anachronistically. The Emancipation Proclamation timing is used
  correctly to show the war *already was* explicitly about slavery by 1864 — that is history,
  not hindsight moralizing.
- **No unfair sympathy** extended to the slaveholding side. Sympathy is reserved for the
  men in the ranks (both armies' soldiers as human beings), which is appropriate and not the
  same as sympathy for the cause — the draft keeps that line clean.

---

## 6. SHOULD-FIX (balance, non-blocking)

1. **The attrition counter-argument needs its leash kept short.** The draft twice frames
   the slaughter as "the one thing that could actually wear Lee's army down" and says the
   dead "were not spent for nothing in the long account." This is historically defensible
   AND the draft carefully brackets it ("on the morning of June 3, the counter-argument was
   no comfort to anyone"; "spent, terribly and avoidably on that one morning"). It does NOT
   tip into justifying the slaughter. But it is the one place where a careless edit *could*
   drift toward "the deaths were worth it" — keep the existing hedges intact in any revision;
   do not let the attrition argument lose its "terribly and avoidably" counterweight.
   SHOULD-FIX: preserve, don't strengthen, the attrition framing.

2. **The enslaved-laborers-dug-the-works detail deserves a hair more weight earlier.** It is
   the most concrete on-the-actual-battlefield link to the cause, and it lands only in the
   final section as a clause. Surfacing it once in the June 2 "works" section (the trench the
   Union charged was in part dug by enslaved hands) would tie the cause to the literal dirt of
   *this* battle, not only to the Petersburg pivot. SHOULD-ADD/SHOULD-FIX, strengthener only.

---

## VERDICT: PASS (FAIR). Slavery is named as the cause in the section's own prose, early
and again as the closing spine; the USCT hook is concrete and non-tokenizing; the
butcher's-blunder-alone trap is explicitly disarmed; no Lost Cause tropes, no
romanticization, no euphemism, no presentism, no unfair sympathy to the slaveholding side;
human reality unsanitized but not gratuitous. No MUST-FIX. SHOULD-FIX: keep the attrition
argument's hedges intact in any edit, and consider giving the enslaved-laborers-dug-the-
works detail a little more weight earlier.
