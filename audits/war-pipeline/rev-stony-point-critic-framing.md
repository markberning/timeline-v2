## SHOULD-FIX

**1. Wayne caption reads as a Paoli-vengeance setup that the restraint beat then claims
to transcend — the caption is doing chest-thumping the pack doesn't authorize.**

Wayne's commander caption (Images §6, also the bio closing line):
> "His own division had been bayoneted in the dark at Paoli two years before; at Stony
> Point his men did the bayoneting, and then gave quarter."

The rhythm here — "did the bayoneting, and then gave quarter" — presents the restraint as
the climax of a revenge arc completed. The pack's framing is specifically different: the
restraint is documented discipline, not a magnanimous choice by a wronged party who
decided to be bigger. "At Stony Point his men did the bayoneting" is also elliptical in a
way that could be misread as "they bayoneted freely and then stopped" rather than "they
used the bayonet to take the works and stopped when resistance ended." The pack says to
frame the restraint as "discipline + deliberate moral contrast, not softness" — and
explicitly not as a revenge-arc redemption story. The caption is short and will be the
most-read bio surface; its Paoli-→-bayoneting-→-quarter arc is exactly the national-
character narrative the pack warns against.

FIX: Rewrite the caption to keep the Paoli connection (which is documented and relevant)
without the arc that makes the restraint a moral trophy:
> "Anthony Wayne, the Pennsylvania general who led the Corps of Light Infantry at Stony
> Point. His division had been bayoneted in the dark at Paoli two years before; at Stony
> Point the garrison asked for mercy, and got it."

The fact is preserved; the arc is gone; the prose doesn't pre-narrate the story.

**2. Section 3 opening sentence inflates the restraint into a national-character claim
the pack's quotes do not support.**

Section 3, first sentence:
> "The restraint was not lost on anyone, least of all the enemy."

"Not lost on anyone" is a narrator's global claim — that every witness noticed and
acknowledged. What the pack actually licenses is: "contemporaries on BOTH sides remarked
on it" (Wayne's report + Collier's journal = two specific voices). The next sentence then
correctly cites those two. But "not lost on anyone" is the national-character
chest-thump the pack flags as a framing risk: it generalizes from two attested remarks to
a universal reception, which is editorializing. The pack says to "write the discipline"
and cites its specific witnesses; it does not assert a universal moral verdict.

FIX: Replace the global claim with the documented one:
> "Both sides remarked on the restraint, and said so in writing."

Then follow with Wayne's quote and Collier's journal as currently drafted. The meaning
is the same; the overshoot is cut.

---

**3. The Wayne Section 2 description of the "Carry me into the fort" beat correctly
tradition-frames the line but then adds an inference the pack does not support.**

Section 2, the Wayne wound passage:
> "Tradition has him gasping to be carried into the fort to die at the head of his column;
> what the record shows is that he was helped up, went on in with his men, and minutes
> later was inside it."

The pack's safe framing (DISPUTED §1) is: "what the record shows is that minutes later
he was inside it, writing to Washington at 2 a.m. with a bandaged head." The draft's
version adds "went on in with his men" as an intermediate step between "helped up" and
"inside it." That phrase is an inference — the record shows he was inside the fort at
2 a.m.; the record does not narrate his re-entry step by step. "Went on in with his men"
is a small but real elite-superman addition that is not in the pack, inserted to show
Wayne pressing forward heroically despite being knocked down. The pack's framing
deliberately elides that step and lands on the 2 a.m. dispatch as the record anchor.

FIX: Trim to the pack's safe framing:
> "Tradition has him gasping to be carried into the fort to die at the head of his column;
> what the record shows is that minutes later he was inside it, writing to Washington at
> two in the morning with a bandaged head."

**4. Johnson's court-martial framing is slightly stronger in the bio than the pack licenses.**

Johnson's bio (commanders section):
> "the court examined the loss and his career went on"

This is exactly the pack's HANDLE-WITH-CARE language (§12.7), which is correct. But the
bio also says Johnson "demanded a court-martial to clear his conduct" — and the word
"clear" asserts the purpose of the demand as exoneration, which is not exactly what the
pack licenses. The pack says the court-martial was demanded to clear "his conduct" in the
general sense; the verdict wording was NOT verified. The phrase as written implies the
court-martial worked as designed (he went in to be cleared, he emerged cleared). The
correct framing is just that he demanded the court-martial, without asserting the result
was what he intended.

FIX: Change:
> "After his exchange he demanded a court-martial to clear his conduct, testifying in
> February 1781; the court examined the loss and his career went on."

to:
> "After his exchange he demanded a court-martial — testifying in February 1781 — and his
> career went on. He died a full general and a baronet."

The demand is preserved; "to clear his conduct" (implies the purpose and result) is
dropped; the career outcome (which IS documented HIGH) does the work.

## POLISH

**5. The Collier quote is attributed "wrote in his journal" but the introductory sentence
slightly over-elevates it.**

Section 3:
> "The British said the same, from the other side of it. Commodore Sir George Collier
> (British), who had commanded the naval side of Clinton's operation that spring, wrote in
> his journal that the attackers had fought with a bravery they had never shown before, and
> 'showed at this moment a generosity and clemency which during the course of the Rebellion
> had no parallel.'"

The introductory sentence "The British said the same, from the other side of it" conflates
one officer's private journal with the British institutional voice. "The British said the
same" = all the British; what the pack licenses is that Collier's journal said so (MED-HIGH
— "not checked against a manuscript this session"). The attribution is correct later in
the sentence, but the intro sentence will fix the reader's impression before the
attribution lands.

FIX: Change the intro sentence to:
> "One British voice said the same, from the other side of it."

One clause, no fabricated institutional consensus.
