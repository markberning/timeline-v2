## ✅ VERIFIED — the two contested set-pieces (correctly framed; this is the required treatment)

- **Kirkland "Angel of Marye's Heights" — REQUIRED contested framing PRESENT and correct.** Independent verification confirms: the deed was first recorded by Gen. J.B. Kershaw in a letter to the Charleston *News and Courier* dated **Jan 2, 1880** (~17 years after the battle, after Kirkland was killed at Chickamauga 1863); **no contemporary letter, memoir, or official report names Kirkland**; historians (CWMemory, NPS, Mac Wyckoff) judge it "likely embellished" though probably based in some truth. The draft frames it exactly this way — "according to a single later account," "first written down by Kershaw in 1880," "no contemporary accounts," "possibly true, possibly embellished — and not as a verified fact." Rank (sergeant, Co. G, 2nd South Carolina, Kershaw's Brigade), the no-flag-of-truce detail, and the de Weldon statue all CONFIRMED. **This satisfies the MUST-FIX rule (must never be asserted as fact).** ✅
- **Lee "It is well that war is so terrible — lest we should grow too fond of it" — REQUIRED skeptical framing PRESENT and correct.** Independent verification confirms: provenance is shaky; earliest reference is John Esten Cooke (1871, Stuart's staff), then E. Porter Alexander (1907); **Longstreet — the supposed listener — never mentions it in his 1896 memoir** (*From Manassas to Appomattox*); some historians say Lee may never have said it. The draft attributes it as "traditionally said to have… remarked," explicitly flags the Longstreet-silence problem and "post-war recollection," and says "read as tradition, not transcript." **The pack's instruction (attribute as tradition or cut) is met by attributing-as-tradition with the provenance caveat in-prose.** ✅

1. **"turned it down twice before" (Burnside declining command)** — the draft's own ledger flags this as standard-but-[VERIFY]; the fact pack only says "newly appointed." It is widely reported that Burnside twice previously declined command of the Army of the Potomac, so it is defensible, but it is NOT in the pack and was not independently locked here. Either soften to "had been reluctant to take the job" or accept the standard claim knowingly. Non-blocking.
2. **"Lt. Col. E. Porter Alexander"** — Alexander's rank at Fredericksburg was Lt. Col. (he was Longstreet's chief of ordnance / artillery); CONFIRMED. No change. (Listed only because the draft's specific rank tag was worth confirming — it is correct.)
3. **"more than a hundred thousand men… better than a hundred thousand"** — fine as a round figure (engaged ~114,000). No change needed; flagged only for awareness that the precise engaged figure lives in §5.
