### ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX 1 — "another-such-victory" section, para 3: "against the advice of Sir Henry Clinton, the British commander in chief up in New York"

**Issue:** Clinton's title is rendered as "the British commander in chief up in New York." His precise title was Commander-in-Chief, North America (with headquarters in New York). This is accurate enough for the house voice but slightly loose — "the British commander in chief in America" or "his commander in chief, Henry Clinton in New York" is more precise and less ambiguous ("in New York" sounds like his nationality rather than his location). Minor but worth one word fix.
**Correction:** Change "the British commander in chief up in New York who wanted him back in South Carolina" to "his commander in chief, Sir Henry Clinton in New York, who wanted him back in South Carolina."
**Severity:** SHOULD-FIX (no false claim; word order ambiguity only).

### ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX 3 — Dossier casualties bar: `union: 532, csa: 320`

**Surface:** page.tsx, `casualties` object, lines `union: 532, csa: 320`.
**Issue:** The `union` / `csa` field names are Civil War holdovers (Union / Confederate). This is a Revolutionary War battle: the sides are American / British. More importantly, the `csa: 320` figure is unexplained — the American killed-and-wounded total (79 + ~185 = ~264, not 320) does not match any figure in the fact pack. The fact pack gives 79 killed + ~185 wounded = ~264 killed-and-wounded for Americans. The 320 figure appears to have been chosen to set the bar ratio, but it does not correspond to the documented American casualty return. The footnote correctly explains the ~264 effective casualty figure and the ~1,000 dispersal, but the bar value itself (320) is inconsistent with any cited number in the fact pack or sources. Even if 320 is intended as a "rounded visual proxy," the gate's standard is that every number on a reader-facing surface must correspond to a sourced figure.
**Correction:** Change `csa: 320` to `csa: 264` (the documented killed-and-wounded total of ~79 + ~185). Also update the `csaLabel` if needed: the current label reads "American ~264 killed & wounded (plus ~1,000 militia dispersed)" — which means the label already says 264 but the bar renders 320. Align the bar value to the label value: `csa: 264`.
**Note on field names:** The `union`/`csa` keys are presumably shared component props that accept any side's numbers; changing the key names would require a component change. The functional fix is the value; the key-name mismatch is a code debt note, not a reader-facing fact error.
**Severity:** SHOULD-FIX (the reader-facing bar does not match the documented figure; the footnote correctly states ~264 but the bar says 320 — internally inconsistent).

### ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX 4 — "race-to-the-dan" section, para 5: "his strategy was to fight, lose, recover, and fight again"

**Surface:** section-narrative.tsx, `race-to-the-dan`, block 5.
**Issue:** The fact pack explicitly flags Greene's "fight, get beaten, rise, and fight again" as an MED-confidence attribution — "widely attributed but original letter reference not pinned this session" — and mandates "Do not byte-quote; use 'as he had described his strategy to Washington.'" The section-narrative renders this as a paraphrase rather than a direct quote ("his strategy was to fight, lose, recover, and fight again"), which is appropriate. However, the preceding sentence says "He had long since told Washington, in substance, that his strategy was to fight, lose, recover, and fight again" — the phrase "in substance" does the hedging work, but the fact pack's instruction is to attribute it as "as he had described his strategy to Washington," which is what the draft does. This is broadly compliant, but the fact pack also warns against byte-quoting the exact phrase. The draft does not byte-quote, so this is technically compliant. Flag as a note: if any future revision tightens this to a direct quote, the letter reference must be verified first.
**Severity:** SHOULD-FIX (hedged correctly; note for future editors not to elevate to a direct quote without sourcing).

The section-narrative correctly names "Ramsey's Mill" for the post-battle camp. Both spellings are confirmed. No error; noting for reader awareness that these are distinct locations.
