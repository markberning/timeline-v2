8. **The turning point** — Pitt's 1758 reset of the bargain.
9. **The human cost** — disease over combat; the scale of participation.
10. **The throughline** — what the colonists carried home; the war as schoolhouse of the Revolution; and the Native catastrophe that the colonial "triumph" actually was.

## The Native nations' perspective and aims (the weighted question)

**This is handled well and is correctly weighted — it is not an afterthought.** Specifically:

- The section **names the framing problem itself** ("French and Indian War... tells you the war was being seen from one porch"), which is exactly the meta-move a comprehensive treatment owes.
- It states the aims plainly: "their own country to defend, their own borders, their own calculations... used the rivalry between France and Britain as leverage." This matches the standard scholarship (Delaware/Shawnee "use the French to defeat the British... drive away the French when we please").
- It refuses the auxiliary framing on **both** sides: French-allied nations at the Monongahela are "never simply anyone's tool"; the Iroquois are "principals in this war, not auxiliaries to it," divided (Seneca/Onondaga Francophile via Jesuits; Mohawk pro-British), playing the long game toward neutrality.
- It keeps Native people as **people, not scenery** — explicit at the St. François raid and again in the closing dispossession beat.
- The **closing reframe is the strongest single move in the section**: the colonial "triumph" of 1763 is named as a "catastrophe" for the Native nations, "a war over their land," with no second empire left to play against the first. That is the correct weighting for a war whose very trigger (Ohio Country) was a land contest.

This clears the bar comfortably. See SHOULD-FIX #1 for the one strengthening opportunity (the *cause* — Ohio Country land — is implied at the end but never stated up front, which would let the Native-aims thread land earlier and harder).

## SHOULD-FIX (strengthen; none block the ship)

1. **The war's cause/origin is never stated, only implied at the very end.** The Native-aims material would land harder if the reader were told early that the whole war began over the Ohio Country — land both empires (and the Iroquois, and the Delaware/Shawnee living on it) claimed. Right now "a war over their land" arrives only in the final section as an assertion. One early clause ("a war that began over who owned the Ohio Country") would seat the Native-principal framing from the start rather than as a late twist. *(Needs a small fact-pack addition — the Ohio Country / Jumonville-Fort Necessity origin is well-sourced but is NOT currently in the pack; see blind spot #1.)*

2. **Militia recedes after the setup.** The three-way frame promises militia as a co-equal third category, but after the definitions it largely drops out (the body is really regular-vs-provincial). This is acceptable for a storytelling-first theme, but a single later sentence tying provincial-from-militia-rolls back to the home-defense institution would honor the "a war owns three armies" promise the intro makes. Minor.

3. **Provincial economic motive is absent.** The pack and the live scholarship both stress that provincial service was, for many young men, one of the few ways to earn cash to start a household — bounties weren't just recruiting grease, they were the point for the enlistee. The draft mentions bounties mechanically but never the *why men signed*. Adding it would round out the provincial as a full person, not only a rights-bearer. Optional — the contractual-ethos read is the more important one and is intact. *(Light fact-pack support exists via §1/§4; the "cash to form a household" motive is in the standard Anderson reading but not explicit in the pack.)*

4. **"Tens of thousands" is doing a lot of unsourced work.** Correctly hedged per the pack's GAP note, so this is fine as-is — flagging only so the fact-checker/clarity critics don't re-litigate it. No action needed unless a hard number is wanted (none is pinned).

## Blind spots in the FACT PACK itself

1. **No origin/cause facts.** The pack has zero on the Ohio Country, Jumonville Glen (May 1754), or Fort Necessity (July 1754) — the war's actual trigger and Washington's first command. For a theme that (a) leans on Washington as its through-character and (b) is told as a land war from the Native point of view, the absence of the *cause* is a real gap. If SHOULD-FIX #1 is adopted, **add an origin micro-section to the pack first** (Ohio Country contested by Britain, France, Iroquois claim, and the Delaware/Shawnee actually living there; Jumonville/Fort Necessity 1754). Well-sourced (battlefields.org, mountvernon.org, Wikipedia). This is the only fact-pack gap that touches the weighted Native-aims question.

2. **Provincial enlistee motive (cash to establish a household) is not in the pack.** Present in the standard Anderson reading and the live scholarship; would support SHOULD-FIX #3. Optional.

Neither blind spot blocks the section as written — the draft does not over-reach beyond the pack, and every VERIFY/CONTESTED flag from the pack is faithfully carried into the draft (Braddock-Franklin line, Wolfe "worst soldiers," Pitt rank-rule, Pontiac-at-Monongahela "by some accounts," 6-vs-8-month Louisbourg term). That fidelity is itself worth noting: the author respected the pack's caution flags rather than laundering hedged claims into assertions.

## Bottom line

PASS. The section is selective in the right way for a storytelling-first theme, and the one place comprehensiveness most often fails this topic — flattening the Native nations into "the Indians" of the war's name — is exactly where this draft is strongest. The improvements are additive (seat the cause earlier, give the provincial a wallet as well as a conscience), and the only fact-pack work worth doing is a small origin/Ohio-Country addition if SHOULD-FIX #1 is taken.
