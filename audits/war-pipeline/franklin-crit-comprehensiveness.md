## OMISSIONS / underweighting / perspective gaps

**MUST-ADD (borderline — perspective gap): the Union side is thin as *people*.** The
draft is, appropriately, a Confederate tragedy — but the men who *did the killing* are
nearly faceless. Schofield is a planner, Opdycke a "stubborn colonel," Wagner a blunder.
We never feel the Union defender's experience of receiving a 20,000-man wave at point-blank
range in the dark — the thing that made Franklin a slaughter was a *defense*, executed by
soldiers who shot rank after rank to pieces over their own parapet. One or two sentences
from inside the Union works (the horror of the killing from the defenders' side, not just
the attackers') would close a real perspective gap and prevent the section from reading as
purely a Lost-Cause-adjacent elegy for the charge. Currently the only Union human note is
Stanley/MacArthur wounded. **SHOULD-ADD leaning MUST** — flag for reconcile.

**SHOULD-ADD 1 — the enslaved people in the Carter cellar.** The draft says the Carter
family "spent the night … sheltering in the cellar." Verified fact: the people in that
cellar included the Carters' *enslaved* men, women, and children (named in the BOFT
account: Jack, Calfurnia, John, Susie, Tom, Oscar, Gus). Given the section's whole thesis
is that this ground was about slavery, the enslaved people huddled in the literal center
of the Union line — on the property the battle was fought over — is a striking,
on-battlefield reinforcement the draft leaves on the table. A clause would do it. This is
a fact-pack blind spot (the pack flagged only "the family in the cellar").

**SHOULD-ADD 2 — Nathan Bedford Forrest's identity is under-loaded.** The draft names
Forrest as "his cavalry commander" who urged a flank. True and sufficient for the tactical
beat. But Forrest is one of the war's most consequential figures and (relevant to a
slavery-framed section) the postwar first Grand Wizard of the Klan / the Fort Pillow
massacre commander. Not required here, and adding it risks derailing the section — but
note it as a deliberate, defensible omission, not an oversight. **Leave as-is; flag the
choice.**

**SHOULD-ADD 3 — the civilian/aftermath toll beyond the named families.** The section
movingly covers Carnton and the Carters, but Franklin the *town* (~750 people) had a
battle fought through its dooryards; the broader civilian experience is compressed into
the two famous houses. Acceptable selectivity, but a sentence acknowledging the town
itself would round out the human picture. Optional.

## Fact-pack blind spots (independent of the draft)

- **The pack under-weights the Union defenders' experience** — it is rich on the
  Confederate tragedy and the cause, but offers the author almost nothing on what the
  defense *felt like* from inside the works. This produced the perspective gap above. Not
  the author's failure; the pack's.
- **The enslaved people in the cellar** — the pack flagged "the family in the cellar"
  for [VERIFY] but did not surface that enslaved people sheltered there too, despite it
  being directly on the pack's own slavery thesis. Blind spot.
- **Stephen D. Lee's corps largely not up** — correctly omitted by the author (pack
  guidance), good restraint. Mentioning it would not improve the reader's understanding.
- **The "second Battle of Franklin" / 1863 first engagement** — pack notes it; author
  omits. Correct (zero reader value here).
