# NEWCOMER-CLARITY CRITIC · Battle of Bennington draft (rev-bennington)

*Critic pass 2026-06-11 against `rev-bennington-draft.md`. Reader model: zero prior
knowledge — no other page read, no military vocabulary, no New England geography.
Surfaces checked: dossier note, stats, sides, casualties, commander bios, outcome,
section blurbs, all three sections, both MEANWHILEs, all captions, both locator captions.*

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The hard parts are already excellent. The supply-line arithmetic (Money's four-days-of-
provisions, 15 days to move depots 13 miles) is the best WHY-this-expedition mechanism a
newcomer could ask for; the NY-vs-VT signature fact is stated cleanly in the dossier note,
Section 2's opening, Section 3's closing paragraph, the monument caption, and both locator
captions, with miles each time, and never confuses; the Aug 14 / Aug 15 / Aug 16
(3:00 p.m. → ~4:30 → dusk) clock is always legible; the envelopment's WHY (strongpoints
too far apart to support each other + Baum reading the flankers as the promised Loyalist
rising) is explicit; the Saratoga arithmetic (one in seven of the army, the lost Native
scouts, the weeks the Americans spent getting Gates/Morgan/Bemis Heights) is explicit.

What fails the zero-knowledge reader is a small set of undefined terms of art — and two of
them (militia, Continental) are the very distinction the page's meaning rests on.

---

## BLOCKER

**B1 · "militia" is never defined until Section 3 — and it is the word the whole page turns on.**
First use, Section 1: "the rebel supply depot there was lightly held, by reportedly only
about 400 demoralized militia." The reader does not learn what a militia IS until the
third section ("they were not Continentals (the American regular army); they were
militia") — after reading two full sections about militia beating regulars, the dossier
note's "The militia it was told would scatter," and the outcome's "Militia raised in
about a week had beaten European regulars." Without the gloss, "militia raised in a week"
has no force.
→ Fix at first Section-1 use, or better at the Stark paragraph where the page can afford
the clause: "When New Hampshire raised a militia brigade in July 1777 (militia were
part-time citizen soldiers — farmers and tradesmen called out from home for an emergency,
not professional troops), about 1,500 men gathered in roughly a week..."
And a two-word touch in the dossier sides note: "About 1,500 New Hampshire militia
(part-time citizen soldiers) raised in roughly a week..."

**B2 · "Continental" is never defined until Section 3, but Sections 1 and 2 depend on it.**
First use, Section 1: "Stark had resigned his Continental commission and gone home." Also
load-bearing in the Section-1 MEANWHILE ("Colonel Seth Warner's Continental regiment"),
Stark's bio, Warner's bio, the sides note, and Section 2's hinge sentence ("It was Seth
Warner's Continental regiment... that came up at the critical moment"). The gloss
"(the American regular army)" exists — but only in Section 3. A newcomer cannot parse
Stark's grievance (Continental rank vs. answering to New Hampshire alone) or why Warner's
330 are different from Stark's 1,500 without it.
→ Fix at Section-1 first use: "...resigned his commission in the Continental Army (the
new full-time American regular army, run by Congress) and gone home." Then Section 2 and
the MEANWHILE can carry a terse "(the American regular army)" on their own first uses.

**B3 · "breastwork" is undefined (Section 2, the Faden caption, Locator 2).**
First use, Section 2: "Across the river, his Loyalist volunteers (Americans fighting for
the king) built a breastwork covering the bridge itself." The draft glosses "redoubt (an
enclosed earthwork)" in the same sentence-cluster but leaves breastwork bare, and the
word recurs ("the Loyalist breastwork was stormed," the map caption, the locator dot).
→ Fix: "...built a breastwork (a chest-high wall of earth and logs to shoot over)
covering the bridge itself."

## SHOULD-FIX

**S1 · "grenadiers" undefined everywhere it appears.** Section 1 ("small detachments of
German musketeers and grenadiers"), Section 2 ("roughly 550 to 650 German grenadiers and
light infantry"), Breymann's bio ("the Brunswick grenadiers and light infantry of
Burgoyne's advanced corps"). Jägers got a gloss; grenadiers didn't.
→ Gloss once per surface at first use: "grenadiers (an army's biggest and heaviest
assault infantry)."

**S2 · "rangers" undefined.** Stark's bio-paragraph ("a veteran of the French and Indian
War's ranger companies"), the Queen's Loyal Rangers, and "Vermont rangers under Samuel
Herrick" (sides note + Section 2).
→ Gloss at Section-1 first use: "ranger companies (light troops trained for scouting and
woods fighting)" — the other uses then read fine.

**S3 · The Burgoyne quote's "magazines" will misread.** "...enabled the army to leave
their distant magazines..." A newcomer reads gun magazines or print magazines. The quote
is verbatim and untouchable, so frame it.
→ In the lead-in sentence: "what he needed was transport and food on the hoof — a way to
stop living off distant supply depots (his 'magazines,' in the language of the day):"

**S4 · "double envelopment" needs its plain-words parenthetical.** Section 2: "a double
envelopment of a fortified position." The next two sentences DO show it concretely
(Nichols around the left and rear, Herrick around the right and rear), which is most of
the work, but the term itself should not be bare jargon at the moment it's named.
→ "a double envelopment of a fortified position (hitting it from both flanks and the
rear at the same moment as the front)."

**S5 · Burgoyne is not re-introduced in Section 2.** Per-section reset: Section 2's first
mention is "the column of about 800 men Burgoyne had sent" — no role, no side, for a
reader who landed on this section cold.
→ "...the column of about 800 men that General John Burgoyne, the British invasion
commander on the Hudson, had sent to seize..."

**S6 · "General Gates" lands undefined in Section 2.** "Stark reported it to General Gates
six days later" — Gates is only identified (Horatio Gates, the new northern commander) in
Section 3, a section later.
→ "...reported it to General Horatio Gates (the new commander of the American army facing
Burgoyne) six days later..."

**S7 · "Tory" appears only on the Locator-2 surface and is never defined — and it
contradicts the page's own vocabulary.** Locator 2 dot: "Walloomsac bridge & hamlet (the
Tory breastwork across the river)"; the prose and the Faden caption both say "Loyalist
breastwork." A newcomer has been taught "Loyalist," never "Tory."
→ Use "the Loyalist breastwork" in the locator dot and caption; or, if "Tory" is wanted
for color, define it in prose first ("Loyalists — Tories, the rebels called them").

**S8 · Dossier note: bare "Burgoyne" + an ambiguous pronoun.** "Burgoyne sent a column of
horseless German cavalry to seize an American supply depot. The militia it was told would
scatter destroyed it instead..." A cold reader doesn't know who Burgoyne is, and
"destroyed it" can grab "depot" instead of "column."
→ "A British army invading from Canada sent a column of horseless German cavalry to seize
an American supply depot. The militia the column was told would scatter destroyed the
column instead, twice in one afternoon, on a battlefield that is not in Bennington and not
in Vermont." (Or keep Burgoyne with a three-word tag: "Burgoyne, the British invasion
commander, sent...")

**S9 · Outcome text: "the road that ended at Saratoga two months later got shorter" —
a newcomer doesn't know what happened at Saratoga.** On the dossier surface nothing has
yet said "surrender."
→ "...and the road that ended two months later with Burgoyne's surrender at Saratoga got
shorter."

**S10 · The Section-1 MEANWHILE doesn't stand alone.** "Colonel Seth Warner's Continental
regiment, the Green Mountain Boys: about 330 men recruited in the Grants... the desperate
rearguard action at Hubbardton on July 7." Three undefined loads for a standalone block:
"Continental," "the Grants," "Hubbardton" (unplaced).
→ "...Colonel Seth Warner's regiment of the Continental Army (the American regular army),
the Green Mountain Boys: about 330 men recruited in the Grants (the disputed territory
that is now Vermont), the same regiment that had fought the desperate rearguard action at
Hubbardton (in the Grants, on July 7, covering the American retreat from Ticonderoga)."

**S11 · The storm's gift to Stark is implied, never stated.** Criterion: WHY the rain
delay mattered. The draft nails the anti-Breymann half (roads to soup, carts bogging) and
shows the infiltration ("Many of them were Stark's men, looking the position over from the
inside"), and Massachusetts militia are "newly arrived" — but no sentence says the lost
day worked FOR Stark. One clause closes it, using only facts already on the page:
→ End of the Section-2 fortifying paragraph: "The rain cost Baum a day he could not
afford and handed it to Stark, whose strength was still arriving — Massachusetts militia
came in during the lull — and whose men spent the wet day walking Baum's lines from the
inside." (Subject to the fact pack supporting the arrival-during-the-lull timing; if it
doesn't, the weaker "cost Baum a day and handed it to Stark" clause alone still does it.)

## NIT

**N1 · "Fort Ticonderoga" unplaced.** Section 1: "taken Fort Ticonderoga on the way" —
fine narratively, but three words place it: "(the lake fortress guarding the route south
from Canada)."

**N2 · Why Albany?** "pushed on toward Albany on the Hudson River" — the goal is named but
not motivated. If the pack supports it, half a clause ("the river town his whole campaign
aimed at") helps; if not, leave it — "the British war plan was an army moving south"
carries enough.

**N3 · "deputy quartermaster" unglossed.** "His deputy quartermaster, Captain John Money,
ran the arithmetic" — context (he counts provisions) mostly covers it; "(his supply
officer)" would close it fully.

**N4 · Section 3: Saratoga unplaced in prose.** "surrendered his army at Saratoga" — the
locator caption places it; the prose could add "on the Hudson, miles short of Albany."
Optional.

**N5 · Hero caption leans on page context.** "New England militia storming the hilltop
where Baum's German dragoons made their stand" — read truly cold (e.g., as a shared
image), militia/dragoons/Baum are all unglossed. Acceptable for a hero caption sitting
under the dossier, but worth knowing it doesn't stand fully alone.

**N6 · Faden map caption stacks three fortification terms.** "the dragoon redoubt on its
hill, the Loyalist breastwork across the river, and the pincers coming through the woods"
— once B3's prose gloss lands, this reads fine; "pincers" is plain enough.

**N7 · Warner's Section-2 first appearance has no side tag.** "It was Seth Warner's
Continental regiment, the Green Mountain Boys" — side is unambiguous from context
(counterattacked beside Stark), but a strict per-section reset would tag it "(American)."

**N8 · Manchester unplaced in Section 2.** "about 330 men marching from Manchester" — the
Section-1 MEANWHILE placed it ("North of Bennington, at Manchester"), but per-section
reset suggests "from Manchester, to the north." (Pack gives no mileage; keep it soft.)

**N9 · Breymann bio card.** "Burgoyne's advanced corps" (jargon) and "killed at Saratoga
defending the redoubt that still bears his name" assume Saratoga/redoubt knowledge.
Card brevity excuses most of it; "advance guard" instead of "advanced corps" is the one
cheap win.
