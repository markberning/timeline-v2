## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name:** Battle of Nashville. **[WIKI]**
- **Dates:** **December 15–16, 1864** (two days). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Location:** **Nashville, Davidson County, Tennessee** — fought across the hills
  immediately south and southwest of the city. Coordinates ~36°05′N 86°49′W. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Theatre:** **Western Theater.** The **closing battle of the Franklin–Nashville Campaign**
  (Hood's invasion of Tennessee, Nov–Dec 1864). **[WIKI] [WIKI-CAMP]**
- **Result:** **Union victory** — Wikipedia's infobox frames it as the **"end of the Army of
  Tennessee as an effective fighting force."** One of the most complete battlefield victories
  of the war. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### Why Nashville mattered — the last western Confederate army (strategic spine)
- This was the **annihilation of the Confederate Army of Tennessee as a field force.** It came
  **two weeks after the Battle of Franklin (November 30, 1864)**, where Hood **(South)** had
  already wrecked that army in a frontal assault — losing ~6,250 men including **six generals
  killed** and the irreplaceable division commander **Patrick Cleburne (South)**, plus 55
  regimental commanders. **[WIKI-CAMP]** Historian David Eicher's summary, widely quoted:
  *"If Hood mortally wounded his army at Franklin, he would kill it two weeks later at
  Nashville."* **[WIKI]**
- **The standoff:** after Franklin, Hood **(South)** marched the remnant of his army up to the
  southern edge of Nashville and **took up a thin defensive line** facing the city's powerful
  fortifications. He was **too weak to assault the works and unwilling to retreat** — believing
  a withdrawal would dissolve the army through desertion. **[WIKI-CAMP]** Wikipedia-campaign
  frames it: *"rather than retreat and risk the army dissolving through desertions, Hood
  advanced ..."* **[WIKI-CAMP]** *(The "too weak to attack, unwilling to retreat" framing is
  the documented strategic logic; author may state it. The army was indeed entrenched and
  waiting, not maneuvering.)*
- **The broader collapse of 1864:** by December, the Confederacy's strategic position was
  already shattered — **Atlanta had fallen (September 1864)**, **Mobile Bay had been sealed
  (August 1864)**, **Lincoln had been re-elected (November 1864)**, and **Sherman (North) was
  marching through Georgia** to the sea. Hood's Tennessee invasion was the Confederacy's last
  offensive gamble in the West. **[WIKI-CAMP] [ABT]** *(Atlanta / Mobile Bay / Lincoln /
  Sherman are well-documented 1864 context; author should set Nashville as the final act
  inside that collapse. The author may state these as background.)*

## 2. Forces & Commanders (SIDE-TAGGED)

> Side-tag rule: every named person tagged **(North)** = Union / **(South)** = Confederate
> on first mention per section.

### Union (North)
- **Maj. Gen. George H. Thomas (North)** — overall Union field commander, commanding the
  **Army of the Cumberland** and the assembled Nashville force. Famed as **"the Rock of
  Chickamauga"** (earned at Chickamauga, Sept 1863, for a stand that saved the army from
  rout — Garfield reported Thomas *"standing like a rock"*). Known also as **"Pap," "Old Slow
  Trot," "Old Reliable."** A **Virginian who stayed with the Union.** **[WIKI] [WIKI-THOMAS] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. John M. Schofield (North)** — commanding the **XXIII Corps** (held in reserve /
  committed on the Union right). He had fought the delaying actions at **Spring Hill** and
  **Franklin** that bought Thomas time to concentrate. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. Andrew J. Smith (North)** — commanding the **detachment of the Army of the
  Tennessee (XVI Corps)**, the heavy infantry mass of the Dec 15 right wheel against Hood's
  left. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Brig. Gen. James H. Wilson (North)** — commanding the **Cavalry Corps** (largely
  dismounted to fight as infantry on the far Union right, then leading the pursuit). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. James B. Steedman (North)** — commanding the **Provisional Detachment (District
  of the Etowah)** on the **Union left**, which made the Dec 15 diversion and the Dec 16
  assault on Overton/Peach Orchard Hill. **His command included the U.S. Colored Troops**
  (see §6, §8). **[WIKI] [ABT] [BNT]**
- **Brig. Gen. Thomas J. Wood (North)** — commanding the **IV Corps** (advanced via Hillsboro
  Pike / Montgomery Hill in the center-left of the line). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- USCT brigade command **(North):** **Col. Charles R. Thompson (North)** — commanding the
  **2nd Colored Brigade** (12th, 13th, 100th USCT) at Overton/Peach Orchard Hill. **[BNT] [IRON]**
  *([WIKI] lists "Colonel Morgan" with the First Colored Brigade — see §10. Thompson's 2nd
  Colored Brigade is the one whose Dec-16 Overton Hill assault is the USCT centerpiece.)*

### Confederate (South)
- **Lt. Gen. John Bell Hood (South)** — overall commander of the **Army of Tennessee**;
  architect of the failed Tennessee invasion and the bloody Franklin assault that preceded
  this battle. **[WIKI] [WIKI-CAMP] [ABT]**
- **Lt. Gen. Alexander P. Stewart (South)** — corps commander; held the **Confederate left**
  (west, toward Shy's/Compton's Hill — the Dec 15 collapse and Dec 16 break came here). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. Benjamin F. Cheatham (South)** — corps commander; held the **Confederate right**
  (east, toward Overton/Peach Orchard Hill). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Lt. Gen. Stephen D. Lee (South)** — corps commander; held the **Confederate center**. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. Nathan Bedford Forrest (South)** — Hood's cavalry chief, **absent from the main
  battle** — detached toward **Murfreesboro** (Dec 2–5) on a railroad raid, then **covering
  the broken army's retreat** (see §5). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- Brigade-level **(South):** **Brig. Gen. James T. Holtzclaw (South)** — Alabama brigade on
  the Confederate right at Overton/Peach Orchard Hill; his after-action report is the famous
  Confederate witness to the USCT assault (see §7). **[IRON] [BNT]**
- **Col. William M. Shy (South), 20th Tennessee Infantry** — killed defending the hill on the
  Confederate left on Dec 16; the height was **renamed Shy's Hill** (formerly Compton's Hill)
  for him. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

## 3. Casualties (both sides; scholarly range)

**Union (North) — well-agreed:**
- **Total ≈ 3,061** — **Killed 387 · Wounded 2,558 · Missing/Captured 112.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**

**Confederate (South) — contested at the captured-line, give a range:**
- **Total ≈ 6,000.** A common breakdown: **~1,500 killed/wounded** and **~4,500 captured/
  missing** (plus **several batteries / many guns captured**). **[WIKI] [ABT]** The loss was
  overwhelmingly in **prisoners** — the signature of an army that broke and dissolved rather
  than one ground down in attrition. *(The killed/wounded vs captured split is approximate;
  author should say "roughly 6,000, the bulk of them prisoners." [VERIFY] the exact captured
  count — it ranges in sources.)*
- **Combined both sides ≈ 9,000** (ABT prints **~9,061** total). **[ABT]**

**The bigger number — the army erased:** the discrete two-day casualty count understates the
outcome. The **Army of Tennessee was destroyed as an organized force.** Hood reported only
**~18,742 effective** on Jan 20, 1865; **Beauregard (South)** judged it had **"fewer than
15,000 men."** Hood retreated to **Tupelo, Mississippi**, and **resigned his command Jan 13,
1865** — never given another field command. **[WIKI] [WIKI-CAMP]**

**Author guidance:** state Union loss ≈ 3,000 and Confederate loss ≈ 6,000 (**mostly prisoners**),
then make the real point — the disproportion is not the headline; the **dissolution** is. This
is one of the few battles of the war that **destroyed an entire army as a field force.**

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (north = up):

- **Nashville sits to the NORTH**, behind a **7-mile semicircular Union fortification line**
  on the **south and west** sides of the city (works begun when the Union occupied Nashville in
  **February 1862**). The **Cumberland River** forms the natural barrier on the **north and
  east**. **[WIKI] [ABT]** *(Map: the city + its arc of forts at the TOP, river curling N/E.)*
- **Hood's Confederate line ran in an ARC to the SOUTH** of the city, **~4 miles** long, facing
  the south-facing portion of the Union works. From **(Confederate) right to left**:
  **Cheatham (South, east) — Stephen D. Lee (South, center) — Stewart (South, west).** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Dec 15 — the grand right wheel:** Thomas's plan was a **feint/demonstration on the Union
  left (Confederate right)** by **Steedman (North)**, masking a **massive wheeling assault by
  the Union right** — **Wilson's cavalry (North)** swinging west then south, with **A.J.
  Smith's (North)** infantry — that **crushed Hood's left.** The attack rolled up a chain of
  **five detached Confederate redoubts** (Redoubts 1–5, each ~2–4 guns, ~150-man garrisons)
  guarding the Confederate left. **[WIKI] [ABT]** *(Map arrows: big sweeping arrow from the
  Union right/NW curving down onto the Confederate left/SW; smaller feint arrow on the E.)*
- **Dec 16 — the two objectives:**
  - **Shy's Hill (a.k.a. Compton's Hill)** — on the **Confederate LEFT (W/SW)**; the key height
    whose fall broke the line. Its defenses were a **salient exposed to Union fire from nearly
    all directions**, and crucially the trenches sat on the **geographical crest, not the
    military crest**, so attackers were sheltered until nearly at the top. **Brig. Gen. John
    McArthur's (North)** three-brigade assault carried it; **Col. Shy (South) was killed**
    here. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
  - **Overton Hill / Peach Orchard Hill** — on the **Confederate RIGHT (E)**; the objective of
    **Steedman's (North)** assault, including the **U.S. Colored Troops** (see §6, §8). The
    Confederates **held** this hill, but the **collapse of the left at Shy's Hill rolled the
    whole line up west-to-east** into rout. **[WIKI] [BNT]**
- **Approach / retreat roads:**
  - **Hillsboro Pike** — IV Corps (Wood, North) advance axis via **Montgomery Hill** in the
    center. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
  - **Granny White Pike** and **Franklin Pike** — the two **roads SOUTH** that became the
    **Confederate retreat routes** as the army broke. Forrest's rearguard fought a night
    action in the rain on the **Granny White Pike.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Map orientation summary:** Nashville + its arc of forts at TOP (river N/E); Hood's 4-mile
  arc below it (Cheatham E / Lee center / Stewart W); Dec-15 big right-wheel arrow from the
  NW/W onto the redoubts of Hood's left; Dec-16 objectives = **Shy's Hill (W)** and **Overton/
  Peach Orchard Hill (E)**; retreat arrows exit S down **Granny White** and **Franklin Pikes.**

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup — the pressure on Thomas (the "sledgehammer" back-story):** Thomas **(North)**, true to
his deliberate reputation, spent days methodically refitting Wilson's cavalry and massing his
force before striking. Washington and Grant grew alarmed at the delay — and a **bitter ice
storm struck Nashville on Dec 8, freezing the ground and making any attack impossible through
Dec 12.** **[WIKI]**
- **The threat to relieve him:** Grant **(North)** — convinced Hood might slip past Thomas and
  drive north — sent a stream of increasingly sharp telegrams, then **on Dec 13 ordered Maj.
  Gen. John A. Logan (North) to Nashville to take command if Thomas had not yet attacked.**
  **Logan reached Louisville by Dec 15** — the day the battle began. **Grant himself left
  Petersburg on Dec 14**, getting only as far as **Washington** before the news of victory
  stopped him. **[WIKI] [ABT]** *(The "sledgehammer telegrams" framing is descriptive — the
  documented facts are: repeated Grant prods, the Logan relief order of Dec 13, Logan reaching
  Louisville, Grant en route to Washington. [VERIFY] before printing "sledgehammer" as a
  literal period word.)*
- **Thomas vindicated:** he attacked **before the relief arrived** and won overwhelmingly. Grant
  later **acknowledged that the victory at Nashville obviated all criticism.** **[WIKI-THOMAS]**
  His deliberateness was vindicated by the most complete result of the war. **[WIKI] [WIKI-THOMAS]**

### December 15 — the feint and the right wheel
- **Steedman's (North) diversion** struck **Hood's right (east)** across the **Nashville &
  Chattanooga Railroad** to fix Hood's attention. It did NOT fool Hood, but it **took works
  that made Hood's left untenable.** **[WIKI]**
- **The main blow:** **Wilson's cavalry (North)** moved west then wheeled south; **A.J. Smith's
  (North)** infantry followed, **XXIII Corps in reserve.** **~2:30 p.m.**, the Union right
  **assaulted the five redoubts** guarding Hood's left, **taking Redoubts 4, 5, 3, 2, and 1
  in sequence.** **[WIKI]** Eyewitnesses recalled regiments **"competing with the others to
  reach the redoubt first."** **[ABT]**
- **By nightfall** Hood's **left had collapsed**, forcing a **~2-mile withdrawal** to a new,
  shorter line anchored on **Shy's (Compton's) Hill** west and **Overton/Peach Orchard Hill**
  east. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### December 16 — the line breaks, the army dissolves
- **Overton / Peach Orchard Hill (~3 p.m.):** **Steedman's (North)** force — including the
  **USCT** — assaulted the Confederate right. The attack was **repulsed with heavy loss** (see
  §6, §8) — the Confederates **held** the hill. But the assault **pulled Confederate
  reinforcements eastward**, further thinning Hood's already-stretched left. **[WIKI] [BNT] [IRON]**
- **Shy's Hill (late afternoon):** with daylight fading, **Brig. Gen. John McArthur (North)**
  sent word he would attack within five minutes unless ordered otherwise — and his **three-
  brigade assault carried the salient.** The defenses' fatal flaw (geographical-crest trenches,
  fire from nearly every side) let the attackers reach the crest. **[WIKI]**
- **The collapse:** **"The Confederate left flank suddenly disintegrated. The Confederate line
  was rolled up west to east."** **[WIKI]** The Army of Tennessee **broke and dissolved into
  rout** — men streaming south down the **Granny White and Franklin Pikes.** One of the most
  complete routs of any field army in the war. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### The retreat & pursuit — Forrest saves the remnant
- **Forrest (South) had been absent** from the main battle — Hood had detached him toward
  **Murfreesboro** (Dec 2–5) on a raid against the **Nashville & Chattanooga Railroad**;
  Wikipedia-campaign calls this **"a serious strategic error"** that **"deprived his army of
  its strongest and most mobile unit."** (At Murfreesboro the Confederates were **driven off
  in the Third Battle of Murfreesboro / Battle of the Cedars, Dec 7.**) **[WIKI]**
- **Forrest rejoined** the broken army and **commanded the rearguard** of the retreat. **Rucker's
  Confederate cavalry brigade (South)** fought a **nighttime melee in the rain on the Granny
  White Pike**; over the next days Forrest **stymied Wilson's (North) pursuit** in hard
  rearguard fights at **Richland Creek, Anthony's Hill, and Sugar Creek**, and his two cavalry
  divisions **reached Columbia from Murfreesboro on Dec 18.** **[WIKI]** Forrest's rearguard is
  widely credited as the **only thing that saved any organized remnant** of the army during its
  retreat **south across the Tennessee River** to Tupelo. **[WIKI] [WIKI-CAMP]** *(The "only
  thing that saved it" is the standard interpretation; the hard facts are the named rearguard
  actions. Author may state the saving role; [VERIFY] if a stronger claim is wanted.)*

## 6. Human cost — named people / vivid documented detail

- **The 13th U.S. Colored Troops (North)** — the **heaviest regimental loss of the entire
  two-day battle.** In its **first major action**, attacking **Overton/Peach Orchard Hill**, it
  lost **221 men — 55 killed, 165 wounded, 1 missing — out of 576 engaged, a ~38–40%
  casualty rate.** **[IRON] [WIKI]** *(One sister-source rounds to "220" and ~40% of a 556-man
  regiment — use **~221, roughly 40%, out of ~576**; the figures agree within rounding.)*
- **The 2nd Colored Brigade (North)** at Overton Hill: the **12th, 13th, and 100th USCT** under
  **Col. Charles R. Thompson (North).** The **12th and 100th led**, the **13th followed in
  support** then charged hard; an **isolated thicket bunched the regiments** under frontal and
  enfilading fire. **Other brigade losses:** **100th USCT ~133**, **12th USCT ~114.** **[BNT] [IRON]**
- **Some of the 13th reached the parapet** — *"A portion of the Thirteenth mounted the
  breastworks ... the Rebel reserves rising up were able to give their undivided attention to
  them ... mow down those on the works almost to a man."* (officer of the 12th USCT). **[IRON]**
- **The Murfreesboro flag (vivid, [VERIFY] — strong if confirmable):** one account reports the
  13th carried a flag **emblazoned "Presented by the Colored Ladies of Murfreesboro,"** and
  that **five color bearers were shot down before the banner fell.** **[IRON]** *(This detail is
  in [IRON] but NOT confirmed by [TRAV] or [BNT]; mark **[VERIFY]** — confirm the exact flag
  inscription and the "five color bearers" count before printing as fact.)*
- **Col. William M. Shy (South), 20th Tennessee** — killed defending the hill on the Confederate
  left, Dec 16; the height was renamed **Shy's Hill** in his honor. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **The toll on the Army of Tennessee** — the human cost is best told as the army's death:
  ~6,000 lost in two days, the bulk surrendering; an army that started the campaign ~38,000+
  reduced to **fewer than ~15,000–18,000** effectives. **[WIKI] [WIKI-CAMP]**

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the local hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and each side — was ultimately fought over. Omission is
> the default Lost Cause failure. Nashville makes this *unmissable*, because the men who helped
> destroy the slaveholders' last western army were themselves formerly enslaved.

### The documented throughline
- The Confederacy fought to preserve slavery; **Hood's Army of Tennessee was the field force of
  the slaveholders' republic** in the West. At Nashville that army was **annihilated** — and a
  conspicuous part of the force that broke it was made up of **Black men in Union blue.**
- **U.S. Colored Troops were central to the battle, not a footnote.** Steedman's command
  **(North)** put the **12th, 13th, and 100th USCT** into the **Dec-16 assault on Overton/Peach
  Orchard Hill** — *"probably the strongest works on the entire line."* **[BNT] [IRON]**
- **The cost was real and conspicuous:** the **13th USCT** suffered the **single heaviest
  regimental loss of the battle** — ~221 men, ~40% of the regiment — many shot down **on and at
  the Confederate parapet.** **[IRON] [WIKI]**
- **Even the enemy testified to their valor.** Confederate Brig. Gen. **Holtzclaw (South)**
  wrote that the Black brigade *"gallantly dashed up the abatis ... and were killed by
  hundreds,"* and that he had *"never saw dead men thicker."* **[IRON]** Praise for the courage
  of the men they were fighting to keep enslaved — from a Confederate officer's own report.
- **The Union commander's verdict:** **Steedman (North)** — *"I was unable to discover that
  color made any difference in the fighting of my troops."* **[BNT]** And **Thomas (North)**,
  surveying the field: *"the question is settled; negro soldiers will fight."* **[TRAV]** *(both
  [VERIFY] for exact wording — see §7.)*

### The hook for the author
The concrete cause-hook is **the men themselves on Tennessee ground:** **formerly enslaved and
free Black men in Union uniform helping to destroy the army of the slaveholders' republic**,
many of them raised in Tennessee and the mid-South — assaulting Overton/Peach Orchard Hill,
dying on the parapet, and winning even their enemy's acknowledgment. The railroad and the
fortifications were the *terrain*; **slavery was the reason the war existed**, and the USCT
charge up Overton Hill is the section's human proof of what — and whom — the war was being
fought over. *(Note: [TRAV]/[BNT] document the regiments, the assault, the casualties, the
valor, and the commanders' verdicts; the specific "formerly enslaved / raised in Tennessee"
biographical claim is sound general USCT history but **[VERIFY]** the regiment-specific
recruitment detail before asserting a precise origin for these units.)*

**Author directive:** name slavery plainly where the section sets its stakes — the Army of
Tennessee was fighting for a slave society, and the men who broke it on Overton Hill included
the very people that society held in bondage. That is the cause, made flesh, and it satisfies
the NAME-THE-CAUSE gate concretely.

## 9. Significance

- **Destroyed the Army of Tennessee as a field force** — the infobox verdict: **"end of the
  Army of Tennessee as an effective fighting force."** **[WIKI]** It would never again take the
  field as a real offensive army. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Ended Hood's career** — Hood retreated to **Tupelo** and **resigned Jan 13, 1865**; never
  given another field command. **[WIKI] [WIKI-CAMP]**
- **Effectively ended major fighting in the Western Theater** — with the last western
  Confederate army gone, the West was decided. Give the **finality full weight**: coming after
  Atlanta's fall, Mobile Bay's sealing, Lincoln's re-election, and Sherman's march, Nashville
  **erased the Confederacy's last offensive hope in the West.** **[ABT] [WIKI-CAMP]**
- **One of the most complete battlefield victories of the war** — a rare instance of an entire

## 10. Blind spots / open items for the fact-checker
- **Troop strengths are genuinely contested.** Union: ~55,000 [WIKI] vs an 85,000 figure on
  [ABT]'s facts page (likely total-present/garrison-inclusive). Confederate: ~22,000–30,000
  [WIKI/ABT] from a campaign-start ~38,000–44,000. **Use "outnumbered ~2-to-1," not false-
  precise pairs.** Verify the preferred infobox numbers.
- **Confederate captured/killed-wounded split (~1,500 vs ~4,500 of ~6,000)** is approximate;
  the captured count varies by source. State "~6,000, mostly prisoners."
- **The USCT brigade naming.** [WIKI] mentions a "First Colored Brigade ... commanded by Colonel
  Morgan" (14th/16th/17th/18th/44th USCT) in the Dec-15 diversion; the **Overton/Peach Orchard
  Hill Dec-16 assault** is the **2nd Colored Brigade (12th/13th/100th USCT) under Col. Charles
  R. Thompson.** Don't conflate the two brigades/days. Verify which regiments were where on
  which day.
- **Quotes #1 (Holtzclaw), #3 (Thomas "negro soldiers will fight"), #5 (Van Duzer)** need
  verbatim/attribution confirmation against the Official Records / primary sources before
  printing inside quotation marks. Two renderings of the Thomas line exist.
- **The "Presented by the Colored Ladies of Murfreesboro" flag and the "five color bearers
  shot down"** appear in [IRON] only — confirm the exact inscription and the count before using.
- **The "formerly enslaved / raised in Tennessee"** characterization of these specific USCT
  regiments is sound general history but not confirmed unit-by-unit in the fetched sources;
  verify before asserting precise origins.
- **The "sledgehammer telegrams"** label is descriptive shorthand, not a period quote; the hard
  facts are the Logan relief order (Dec 13), Logan reaching Louisville (Dec 15), and Grant
  traveling to Washington. Don't print "sledgehammer" as a primary-source word.
- **Forrest "the only thing that saved the army"** is the standard interpretation; the hard
  facts are the named rearguard actions (Granny White Pike, Richland Creek, Anthony's Hill,
  Sugar Creek) and the Columbia rendezvous Dec 18. State the saving role as interpretation.
- **Franklin (Nov 30)** prelude facts are summarized for context only; pull a dedicated Franklin
  pack if the author wants more than a two-week-prior reference.
