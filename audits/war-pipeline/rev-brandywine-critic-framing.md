## Judgment by mandate

### 1. Defeat honesty — CLEAN
The defeat is plain everywhere, never laundered by the army-survived frame:
- Hero caption: "a stand-up fight in open farm country that the Americans lost and
  marched away from."
- Washington bio: "he read the battlefield wrong... and Howe turned his flank exactly
  as at Long Island."
- Section 2: "Fooled by the same man, with the same move, twice in thirteen months."
- Section 3 opens on Washington's own "masters of the field" admission and tallies
  "the ledger honestly, both columns" — Howe's column FIRST, at full length.
- Dossier note: "the army he needed to destroy marched away intact" — survival framed
  as Howe's limit, not as an American achievement that erases the loss.
The unguarded fords and the cancelled-attack intelligence sequence are told as an
American failure ("nobody was guarding either one," "the local intelligence he leaned
on failed him"), with Ewald's incredulous quote landing the indictment from the
enemy's own diary. "Beaten but unbroken" / "His redemption was the evening" are
earned because the beating is fully paid for first.

### 2. Both-sides — CLEAN
- Howe: "conceived... and executed both almost flawlessly," "arguably his
  masterpiece," the flank march "longer and bolder than Long Island's and worked
  completely" — craft fully credited, limits stated without sneering ("a habit of
  winning battles and not wars" is the pack's own line).
- Knyphausen: "His job was theater... He played it almost perfectly," "One of the
  most senior and respected German officers of the war" — the demonstration told as
  skilled soldiering, not trickery.
- The Hessians human: Ewald the named, quoted, thinking individual; Hessians defined
  neutrally ("German troops in British service") with no mercenary cartooning.
- Ferguson's riflemen: not in the fact pack; correctly absent from the draft (adding
  them would have been out-of-pack invention). No pack texture is cartooned.

### 3. The blame question — CLEAN
Section 1 handles Howe-abandons-Burgoyne exactly per pack §10.7: the no-order fact
("no order ever required him to go north"), then "Who deserves the blame for that,
Howe, London's sloppy coordination, or Burgoyne's own overconfidence, is an argument
historians have now been having for two and a half centuries, and it will not be
settled here. What is not argued is the fact pattern." That is the mandated
fact-pattern-only treatment, stated in so many words. Howe's bio keeps it at "while
leaving Burgoyne's northern army to its fate" — effect, not verdict. Sullivan's
scapegoating is carried at evidence level per pack §7: "Congress demanded an inquiry
into his conduct. Washington kept him, and he was cleared" — no adjudication of
whether the inquiry was fair, no piling on.

### 4. The Quaker community — CLEAN
- The two-meetings distinction is preserved exactly: the "quiet and peaceful within"
  quote attached ONLY to Old Kennett's morning meeting ("one of the Kennett Friends
  later wrote" — the pack's required attribution shape); Birmingham's meeting
  correctly displaced to the Sconnelltown wheelwright shop because the meetinghouse
  was already a commandeered American field hospital, with Townsend's fragments
  attributed as decades-later memory. No blending.
- Respect, not quaintness: "They did not stop" is the whole editorial gesture for Old
  Kennett — restraint, no cuteness. The Birmingham MEANWHILE ("One grave behind the
  wall") carries the civilian cost — hospital for both armies in turn, Friends tending
  the wounded of both, the common grave "inside the wall the battle had been fought
  over" — without atrocity inflation or sentimentality.

### 5. Paoli — CLEAN
- "massacre" examined as framing, per pack: "'massacre' was the American framing, and
  the multiple wounds and the dark made it easy to believe" — sympathy for why the
  label stuck, no endorsement.
- Modern ~53 primary, "older accounts ran the dead as high as 150 or more" — the
  pack's required rendering.
- Grey: "a piece of cold professional discipline," the flint mechanism explained,
  "Strictly by the rules of eighteenth-century war it was a lawful night attack,
  devastating because it was disciplined" — doctrine, not gothic villainy.
- Wayne's verdict byte-exact: "did every duty that can be expected from an active,
  brave and vigilant officer" / "acquit him with the highest honor."
- The Paoli image caption repeats the framing honesty ("Americans called it the Paoli
  Massacre; modern counts put the dead at about 53").

### 6. Captions and dossier surfaces — CLEAN with fixes

## SHOULD-FIX

**SF-1 · Unsupported analytic gloss on the Hessian casualty count (Section 3).**
> "(only about 40 of them Hessians, a measure of how completely Knyphausen's morning had been a show)"

The pack (§5) gives the ~40-Hessians number with no causal interpretation. "A measure
of how completely Knyphausen's morning had been a show" is the draft's own inference,
and it is shaky: Knyphausen's column also contained British brigades who did real
fighting in the afternoon assault, and Hessian jaegers fought hard under Cornwallis —
so the low Hessian count is not cleanly a measure of the demonstration. Fix: drop the
gloss or neutralize it — e.g. "(only about 40 of them Hessians)". The dossier
casualties line already carries the bare number correctly.

**SF-2 · Sullivan imagined-likeness caveat: instruction present, caption absent.**
> "MANDATORY caption caveat: the 1776 London print is an IMAGINED likeness; no from-life portrait of Sullivan exists." (IMAGES #8)

Every NEW image gets its caption text written verbatim in the draft, but the five
reuses get none — and Sullivan's is the one reuse where the caption IS the gated
caveat. An instruction to write a caveat at integration time is exactly how mandatory
caveats get dropped. Fix: write the actual caption in the IMAGES section, e.g.
"John Sullivan in a 1776 London print — an imagined likeness; no portrait of Sullivan
from life exists." (Same applies in milder form to the other four reuses: write the
fresh captions now, per the pack's "REUSE with fresh captions.")

**SF-3 · Paoli begging-quarter claims: "propaganda" softened to "newspapers and camp talk" (Section 3).**
> "American newspapers and camp talk said men had been killed begging for quarter"

The pack's evidence level (§6) is "American propaganda said men were killed begging
quarter." The draft's rendering both invents channel specificity the pack doesn't
give (newspapers, camp talk) and drops the pack's evidence-frame word, presenting the
claims one notch closer to reportage. Fix: track the pack — e.g. "American propaganda
said men had been killed begging for quarter (the formal word for mercy and
surrender)" — or the minimal "American accounts claimed," which neither invents
channels nor upgrades the evidence.

## NIT

**N-1 · Hero caption certifies the illustration's accuracy.**
> "but the day's texture is right, a stand-up fight in open farm country"

"The texture is right" is our own editorial certification of a c.1898 illustration,
pushing back against the just-stated caveat. The clause after the comma does the work
alone; consider "but it shows what the day was: a stand-up fight in open farm
country that the Americans lost and marched away from."

**N-2 · Wayne caption asserts "painted in the period" (IMAGES #5).**
The pack verifies the Commons file exists, not the portrait's date. Drop "painted in
the period" unless the file page verifies it at fetch.

**N-3 · "that fall it died at Saratoga" (Section 1).**
"Died" is the pack's own metaphor (§2 "the campaign that died at Saratoga") so it
passes, but coming one sentence after the careful not-adjudicating paragraph it reads
a half-step more fated/dramatic than "surrendered at Saratoga," which Section 3 uses.
Optional harmonization only.
