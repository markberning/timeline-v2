## Blind Spots in the Fact Pack

1. **Declaratory Act (March 18, 1766)**: §8 and §10 both omit it. The Stamp Act's repeal came simultaneously with an explicit assertion of Parliament's absolute taxing authority — arguably the harder provocation for the constitutional argument. The fact pack's forward-thread (§10) is incomplete without it.

2. **Fort Pitt / Ecuyer's nationality**: Fact pack §7 notes Ecuyer is "Swiss-born British officer." Draft says "British" throughout — correct by service, but the Swiss-born detail is interesting context (not essential).

3. **Neolin's 1762 second vision (the prayer)**: Fact pack §7 cites it; §6 (Neolin Wikipedia) is listed as a source, but the 1762 prayer is not integrated into the forward-thread of his movement spreading. Minor.

4. **Martinique and Saint Lucia returned to France**: §4 lists them (with Guadeloupe) as returned. Draft only makes this visible implicitly. The reader of the Canada-or-Guadeloupe debate never sees that France got its Caribbean islands back anyway — which is part of why Choiseul was content.

## Overall Assessment

The draft is strong and covers its core ground well. The Neolin/Pontiac sections are particularly well-handled — the moral-center requirement for Pontiac's War as a coordinated movement with coherent aims is substantially met. The biological warfare section's epistemic care is a model. The causal chain from debt → taxation → resistance is clean and concrete.

The four genuine gaps are: the Currency Act (completing Grenville's program), the Proclamation Line's double-meaning needs to be stated explicitly rather than left to be inferred, the dispossession thread needs to land in the Pontiac sections rather than only at the close, and the Declaratory Act is structurally necessary for the Revolution thread. The regulars-vs.-provincials point from the fact pack (§9) is silently dropped in the draft and should be recovered.

The fact pack's own blind spot is the Declaratory Act — the author needs a supplemental research note on it before revision.
