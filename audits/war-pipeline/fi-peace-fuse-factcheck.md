**Murray quote — ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX:**
The phrase "conquered yet dignified people, quiet, pliant, hard-working, and humble" appears only on canadahistory.com, a non-peer-reviewed secondary site. The authoritative Dictionary of Canadian Biography (biographi.ca) gives a different and clearly authenticated Murray quote about French Canadians: he called them "perhaps, the best and bravest Race on the Globe" who "could they be indulged with a very few Privileges...must in a very short Time become the most faithful & useful Set of Men in this American Empire." The "quiet, pliant, hard-working, and humble" phrasing could not be independently confirmed in any primary-source or peer-reviewed source.

**Recommended fix:** Either replace the quoted phrase with the Dictionary of Canadian Biography version (which is unambiguously sourced and more vivid), or strip the quotation marks and render it as paraphrase (Murray praised them as a conquered yet dignified people). Do not ship it as verbatim quoted text resting on a single weak secondary source — the author flagged this exact concern in the fact pack (§2 note), and independent verification confirms the caution was warranted.

**Havana fleet — ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX:**
The "roughly 51 warships" figure is not independently confirmed. Multiple authoritative sources (Warfare History Network, britishbattles.com itself, Wikipedia) describe the fleet as ~22 ships of the line + 24 lesser warships (= ~45 warships) + ~168 support vessels (transports, storeships, hospital ships), total ~180 sail. The 12,000 troops figure is confirmed and reliable. The "51" appears to be a conflation of warship types or an error in transcription from the fact pack's britishbattles.com citation. The draft would be more accurate and defensible with "roughly 20 ships of the line and a large fleet of transports, with 12,000 troops" or simply "a massive British fleet of roughly 180 sail, carrying 12,000 troops." Printing "51 warships" as a crisp number risks a factual challenge.

## SUMMARY OF FLAGS

### ❌ MUST-FIX
*None.*

### ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX (2)

**1. Murray "conquered yet dignified" quote (§ The Conquered and the Conquerors)**
The exact phrase "a conquered yet dignified people, quiet, pliant, hard-working, and humble" cannot be confirmed in any primary source or peer-reviewed reference. The only source is canadahistory.com. The Dictionary of Canadian Biography (biographi.ca) — the authoritative scholarly source for this person — gives a different Murray quote with a completely different register. The author's own hedging note in §2 of the fact pack ("note: rendered as quoted phrase; fact pack gives it as Murray's words via canadahistory.com") correctly flagged the weakness. **Action: either replace with the Dictionary of Canadian Biography quote, or strip quotation marks and render as paraphrase. Do not ship as verbatim.**

**2. "roughly 51 warships" at Havana (§ The War the Treaty Was Still Finishing)**
Multiple independent sources (Warfare History Network, Wikipedia, britishbattles.com) describe the Havana fleet as ~22 ships of the line + 24 lesser warships + ~168 transports and support vessels = ~180 total sail. The 12,000 troops figure is confirmed and correct. The "51 warships" cannot be reconciled with these figures and appears to conflate total warship types in a non-standard way. **Action: change to "roughly 12,000 troops and a large fleet" or "more than 20 ships of the line," dropping the "51 warships" number. The troop count is the vivid detail that counts here.**
