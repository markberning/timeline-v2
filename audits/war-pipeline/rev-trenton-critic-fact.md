## BLOCKERS

### B1 · Out-of-pack claim: "a river with no bridges" (Section 1)
Draft: "took every boat he could find with him, so that the British could not follow at once
**across a river with no bridges**."
The pack (§2) says only "seizing/securing the boats on the Delaware so Howe could not follow at once."
The no-bridges mechanism is nowhere in the pack → out-of-pack claim, blocker by rule.
**Web check: the claim is TRUE** — the first bridge across the Delaware (Lower Trenton Bridge) opened
January 30, 1806; crossings in 1776 were ferry-only (DRJTBC; structuremag.org; Wikipedia "Lower Trenton
Bridge"). **Fix (either):** (a) cut "across a river with no bridges" → "could not follow at once"; or
(b) add the verified fact to the pack (§2, HIGH, first Delaware bridge 1806) and keep the clause. (b) is
better — the clause is good explanatory mechanism and is sound history.

### B2 · Out-of-pack and imprecise: "the war went on for six and a half more years" (Section 3, closing paragraph)
Draft: "...and **the war went on for six and a half more years** because roughly 2,400 freezing men..."
No war-duration claim exists anywhere in the pack. It is also imprecise on its own terms: from December
1776, major fighting effectively ended at Yorktown (October 1781, ~4 yrs 10 mo) and the Treaty of Paris
came September 1783 (~6 yrs 9 mo) — "six and a half" matches neither anchor cleanly.
**Fix:** drop the number. E.g. "...and the war went on, because roughly 2,400 freezing men, about half
of whom then agreed to stay for ten dollars, made it possible for there to be a war at all." (If a
duration is wanted, it must go into the pack first with a stated anchor.)

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1 · Rank anachronism: "Brigadier General Henry Knox" on Christmas night (Section 2)
Draft: "**Brigadier General Henry Knox** (American), the 280-pound Boston bookseller turned artillery
chief, ran the embarkation..."
The pack (§3) and the draft's own dossier bio both state his brigadier commission is **dated December 27,
1776 — the day after the battle** (web-confirmed: Encyclopedia.com, americanhistorycentral.com). On the
night of the crossing he was Colonel Knox. The draft contradicts itself (bio vs prose).
**Fix:** "Colonel Henry Knox" in the Section 2 prose (the dossier role line "Artillery chief" is fine as is).

### S2 · Invented specificity: "would know by nightfall" (MEANWHILE: Bordentown, Section 2)
Draft: "...it meant every British post in New Jersey **would know by nightfall** what had happened at Trenton."
The pack supports the escape toward Bordentown carrying the news; the "by nightfall" timing is invented.
The ledger itself calls this "framing of that fact, no new claim" — but "by nightfall" IS a new temporal claim.
**Fix:** "...would soon know what had happened at Trenton" (or "within a day").

### S3 · Out-of-pack detail in the Monroe image caption
Draft (image 10): "At Trenton he was an eighteen-year-old lieutenant, **carried off the field** with a
severed artery."
The pack (§3) has the wound, the severed artery, and Dr. Riker's clamp — nothing about being carried off
the field. Captions are a gated surface; a short caption is not exempt.
**Fix:** "...an eighteen-year-old lieutenant, wounded by a musket ball that severed an artery."

### S4 · Time drift in the outcome block: "recrossed the Delaware by noon"
Draft (outcome text): "took roughly 900 prisoners, and **recrossed the Delaware by noon**."
Pack §6: "**By noon Dec 26 Washington was recrossing**" — the recrossing *began* by noon; the force was
not back over by noon (Knox sources have everything back across "by the afternoon"). The Section 3 prose
gets it right ("By noon on the 26th Washington was recrossing"); the outcome block overstates.
**Fix:** "...and was recrossing the Delaware by noon."

## NITS

1. **Outcome block, "fixed the blame on him alone"** — the pack (§6) says "blame fixed on Rall (dead and
   unable to answer)"; "alone" is added emphasis the pack doesn't carry (and London criticism also hit Howe
   and Grant). Suggest dropping "alone." Section 3 prose ("fixed the blame on Rall, who was dead and could
   not answer") is exactly right.
2. **Section 2 blurb vs prose duration** — blurb: "a fight in the streets that was over in **about an
   hour**"; prose and pack handling: decisive street fight "**under** an hour" / "less than an hour."
   Align the blurb to "under an hour."
3. **Fact-ledger misdescribes the draft (Stephen fury)** — the ledger claims Washington's fury at Adam
   Stephen is "rendered as 'by one account telling him he might have ruined all his plans'", but no such
   sentence exists in the Section 2 prose (the raid is told without the fury beat — which is fine per pack).
   Fix the ledger line so the ledger matches what shipped.
4. **"a month before" vs "six weeks before" (Fort Washington)** — Section 1 says Rall "led the decisive
   assault at Fort Washington a month before" (anchored to mid-December — true); the Rall bio and Section 3
   say "six weeks" (anchored to the battle — also true). Both defensible; consider harmonizing to "weeks
   before" in Section 1 to remove the apparent contradiction.
5. **The December 30 speech timing** — draft: "On December 30 Washington recrossed into Trenton yet again,
   and there the war came down to a speech." Pack gives "Dec 30–31"; most accounts (Mount Vernon) put the
   famous ride-along-the-line appeal on December 31. Within the pack's range as written (the recrossing is
   dated, the speech isn't), so acceptable — but "in the last days of the year the war came down to a
   speech" would be drift-proof.
6. **"many units a third within days" vs pack "24+ hours"** — pack §6 places the third crossing for many
   units inside "24+ hours"; draft says "within days." The draft's rendering is arguably the more accurate
   (the December 30 recrossing), and the ledger flags it; no change required, but note the divergence.
7. **Hessian wounded range "83–95"** — the pack is internally inconsistent (§5: "~83–95"; Known
   Uncertainty 2: "80–95"). The draft followed §5. Fine; flagging for the pack maintainer.
