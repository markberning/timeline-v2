# Chancellorsville — STORYTELLING critic gate (war pipeline step 4)

Judged AS A STORY (the #1 goal). Reference voice: the shipped Antietam sections
(`src/app/war-civil-war/eastern/antietam/s/[section]/section-narrative.tsx`).
This gate does NOT judge fact accuracy (that's the parallel fact-check gate) — only
whether it reads as a gripping, human, well-paced story in the house voice. Selective
narrative is treated as GOOD, not a gap.

**Overall grade: GOOD (one notch from STRONG).** This is a strong, voice-true draft
that lands the two pieces of drama it has to land ("Lee's perfect battle" and Jackson's
death). The bones, the hooks, and the human dimension are mostly there. What keeps it
from STRONG is concentrated and fixable: a few sections lean on telling-not-showing in
exactly the beats that should be the most cinematic, and a couple of OUTCOME/closer
pairs repeat themselves. None of the must-improves are structural rewrites.

---

## §the-plan — GOOD

**Hook & stakes:** Strong open. "a fresh plan, and a swagger it had not earned in a
long time" is house voice and earns the reader. The Antietam-style "the plan was
genuinely excellent, and that matters, because almost everything that goes wrong from
here goes wrong in spite of a good plan" is a great framing device — it sets dramatic
irony the whole article pays off. Keep it.

**Human dimension:** Thin here, but that's acceptable for a setup section. Hooker is a
character ("swagger," "boasted") more than a person; fine for the opener.

**Play-by-play tedium:** Watch the river-crossing geography paragraph (¶3). "cross the
Rappahannock far to the west at Kelly's Ford, then cross the Rapidan River… at Germanna
and Ely's Fords" is the right info but reads like a route list. Antietam handles the
equivalent (Lee splitting for Harpers Ferry) in one clean sentence and lets the *map*
carry the geography. **SHOULD-IMPROVE:** trust the `[FIG: campaign map]` to do the
fords; tighten the prose to the *idea* (a wide swing around Lee's left while Sedgwick
fakes the front).

**Voice:** On target. "not a town, just a single large brick house at a road junction
in the woods" is exactly the Antietam register ("not a town" / "a farmer named Miller").

**Pacing:** Good. The Wilderness reveal at the end ("the woods were about to do the work
of an extra Confederate corps") is the best line in the section and the right note to
exit on — except the OUTCOME + closer that follow partly *re-say it*. See below.

**MUST-IMPROVE — redundant landing:** The Wilderness paragraph, the `> OUTCOME` block,
AND the closer paragraph all make the same point three times ("erased the very edge,"
"set the table for… defeat," "lose his nerve the moment Lee refused to behave like a
beaten man"). Antietam's OUTCOME-equivalent is a single sharp italic line, not a
paragraph + blockquote + paragraph. Collapse: let the "extra Confederate corps" line be
the gut-punch, keep ONE of {OUTCOME, closer}, cut the other. Right now the section
deflates by restating its own thesis.

## §lee-divides — STRONG

**Hook & stakes:** Best-constructed section in the draft. "Lee did the opposite, twice,
in a way that has been studied by soldiers ever since as either reckless or perfect, and
possibly both" is a perfect house-voice thesis sentence.

**Human dimension:** Excellent and *specific* — "Lee and Jackson sat on cracker boxes by
a fire in the woods." That's the Antietam "three cigars / half-cooked breakfast" move:
one concrete object that makes a strategy meeting into a scene. The Wellford / Catharine
Furnace beat ("Lee bet his army on a furnace owner's knowledge of his own back roads")
is the single best human-dimension line in the whole draft — it turns a logistics fact
into character and stakes. Keep verbatim.

**Play-by-play tedium:** None. The `[I]` "do the arithmetic" beat is the *right* way to
handle numbers — it dramatizes the math instead of listing it ("If Hooker attacked the
thin force in front of him… Lee's army would simply cease to exist"). This is the model
for how the casualty math should read in the final section too.

**Voice / pacing:** Both strong. "close to insane" / "by the standards of military
prudence" is the informal-but-precise register the product wants.

**Dual POV:** This is the Lee-interior section and it earns it. No notes.

**SHOULD-IMPROVE (minor):** "in the air being the soldier's term for…" is a good
inline-define, but it lands mid-sentence inside the cracker-box paragraph and slightly
clogs the best scene in the piece. Consider setting the "in the air" definition one beat
earlier or later so the cracker-box image runs clean.

## §the-flank-march — STRONG

**Hook & stakes:** "for most of the day the most dangerous column in the Confederacy more
or less disappeared" — great. The "very nearly caught" Sickles/Catharine Furnace beat
gives the march real suspense instead of a foregone conclusion. Good.

**Human dimension:** Strong and this is the showpiece. "they were cooking supper,
stacking arms, settling in" directly mirrors Antietam's Hood "yanked off their
half-cooked food," and the payoff — wildlife bolting from the woods, then "the trees
themselves seemed to come apart" — is genuinely cinematic. The "Dutch"/immigrant framing
gives the victims a face. This is the most show-don't-tell section and it shows.

**Play-by-play tedium:** None, and notably the rout itself is told through *one soldier's
eye-level* ("the first thing many of them saw was the wildlife") rather than a unit
ledger. That's the Antietam West Woods technique. Good.

**Pacing:** Mostly excellent. **SHOULD-IMPROVE:** the Rodes-assault paragraph briefly
slips into telling — "a flanked line cannot fight back, because almost no one in it can
bring a weapon to bear" is *explaining* the rout twice (the inline-define is correct, but
it's stated, then the closer restates it: "less for any failure of its soldiers than for
being posted in the air"). Trust the wildlife/trees imagery to *show* the helplessness;
trim one of the two "a flanked line can't fight back" explanations.

**Voice:** "Lee was not running. Lee was reaching around his neck." — best two-sentence
turn in the draft. This is the product's voice at its peak.

**Note (positive):** The "Flying Dutchmen" Meanwhile sidebar does real moral work and
matches Antietam's habit of giving a wronged group its due (the Irish Brigade). Keep.

## §jackson-falls — GOOD

**Hook & stakes:** "It was Jackson's own aggression that killed him" is a strong cold
open and the right thesis — the irony (the engine of the masterpiece killed by the
masterpiece's momentum) is the emotional core of the whole battle. Lands.

**Human dimension:** The wounding is handled well and humanely. The Stuart handoff —
"the most unlikely man on the field… had never in his life commanded infantry in a
pitched battle" — is a good character beat that Antietam-readers will recognize as the
A.P. Hill "no business being there yet" move.

**Play-by-play tedium — MUST-IMPROVE:** This is the section most at risk. The May-3
combat (Hazel Grove → Alexander's guns → Fairview → Hooker's porch) is told as a
competent *sequence of events* but it's the densest stretch of cause-and-effect in the
draft, and the prose starts to read like a staff report in two spots: "opened on the
Union artillery posted at Fairview, the height just to the northeast. For once in
Virginia, Confederate guns dominated the duel. The Federal artillery at Fairview was
overpowered and the position fell around half past nine." That's three clauses of
artillery bookkeeping. Pick the one human/ironic hinge — Hooker *ordering Sickles off
the one hill that mattered* ("It was a gift") is already the dramatic pivot you have;
lean on it and compress the gun-duel mechanics behind it. The Hooker-concussion beat is
good and should stay vivid; the artillery duel around it should get *out of its way*.

**Dual POV:** This section actually carries the best dual-POV contrast available — the
Confederate side seizing a hill while, simultaneously, the Union commander is literally
knocked senseless on a porch and won't relinquish command. That juxtaposition is gold
and slightly buried. **SHOULD-IMPROVE:** sharpen the cut between "Alexander rushed his
guns up" (South ascendant) and "the Union army effectively lost its commander" (North
decapitated) so the reader feels both happening at once.

**Voice / pacing:** Good. "a concussed one that would not let go" is sharp. The Salem
Church Meanwhile ("The pincer closed on nothing") is a clean, voice-true sidebar.

**OUTCOME note:** "Lee won the field on May 3 — and paid for it with the one subordinate
he could not replace" is a strong OUTCOME line; here the OUTCOME and closer do NOT
redundantly repeat (closer adds Jackson "in a borrowed bed"). Good — this is the model;
fix §the-plan and §hooker-quits to match it.

## §hooker-quits — GOOD

**Hook & stakes:** "What broke at Chancellorsville, in the end, was not the Union army.
It was Joseph Hooker's will to use it." — excellent thesis open, possibly the strongest
in the draft. "an army cannot be braver than the general commanding it" earns its place.

**Human dimension:** The Jackson death (pneumonia, "thirty-nine years old," the Chandler
plantation outbuilding) is handled with restraint and lands hard. Good — it's the
emotional close the whole piece has been building toward and it doesn't oversell.

**Play-by-play tedium — SHOULD-IMPROVE:** The Sedgwick/Salem Church/Banks' Ford
withdrawal paragraph (¶2) is the one stretch of *map-movement* the section can't fully
dramatize, and it reads a little like a campaign summary ("Through May 3 and 4, McLaws
and then Early, coming back into the fight, hemmed Sedgwick in"). It's not bad, but it's
the flattest paragraph in an otherwise strong section. Consider trusting the `[FIG]` map
and tightening to the *point*: Lee fighting two directions at once and winning both
because the bigger army stopped believing it could win (you already say this — say it
sooner and cut the troop-shuffle detail).

**Casualty handling — STRONG:** The "but the word *masterpiece* hides the bill" turn is
exactly right, and the proportional framing ("a fifth of his entire force, against
roughly an eighth of Hooker's") dramatizes the numbers instead of dumping them — the
same technique that worked in §lee-divides. The Wilderness brush-fire beat ("wounded men
of both sides who could not crawl clear were burned where they lay") is harrowing and
earns its place. Keep all of it.

**Pacing — MUST-IMPROVE (the one real structural snag):** The section tries to do FOUR
endings in a row: (1) Hooker recrosses / "Lee's masterpiece," (2) the `[I]` masterpiece-
hides-the-bill casualty turn, (3) Jackson's death, (4) the `> OUTCOME` block, (5) the
"perfect battle was the beginning of the end" closer, (6) the Gettysburg-forward
Meanwhile. That's a lot of landings stacked on each other, and "the beginning of the
end" / "the perfect battle was the beginning of the end" appears as the closer AND is
foreshadowed in the OUTCOME AND in the Meanwhile. **Pick the order and cut the
repetition:** the natural climax is Jackson's death → the single best Gettysburg-pivot
line, ONE OUTCOME, and let the Meanwhile carry the slavery/Gettysburg throughline. Right
now the ending is the strongest material in the draft fighting itself for the last word.

**Voice:** On target throughout. "carried them back across beaten by an enemy he had
outnumbered the entire time" is house voice.

## Cross-cutting notes

**Dual POV (overall): GOOD.** The draft alternates POV by *section* (Hooker's nerve,
Lee's audacity, the XI Corps soldiers' eye-level, Jackson) rather than within scenes,
which suits a selective narrative. The one place to make it *simultaneous* is §jackson-
falls May 3 (South taking the hill / North's commander concussed) — see that section.
Don't force more; this is the right amount.

**Voice (overall): STRONG.** Consistently matches Antietam. The signature moves are all
present and used well: the concrete object that makes a scene (cracker boxes, half-cooked
suppers), the inline-define-on-first-use ("in the air," "in detail," "beaten in detail"),
the short punch-line italic beats, the "not a town / not running" parallel constructions.
The slavery throughline is named in the section's OWN prose (not just sidebars), matching
the directive. No sanding-down. This is the draft's biggest strength.

**Tedium (overall): mostly avoided.** The flagged risks are narrow and concentrated in
exactly two places — the river-fords route list (§the-plan) and the May-3 artillery duel
(§jackson-falls) — plus the softer Sedgwick-withdrawal paragraph (§hooker-quits). Each is
a "trust the map, compress the mechanics" fix, not a rewrite.

**The recurring snag worth naming once:** the OUTCOME-block + closer-paragraph pairing
repeats the section's thesis in 3 of 5 sections (the-plan, the worst; hooker-quits, the
most cluttered; lee-divides, mildly). §jackson-falls is the template that does it right
(OUTCOME and closer say *different* things). Make the others match it.
