## A. Trent smallpox-blanket quote — "regard TO them"

**✅ CORRECT.** The narrative (line 93) reads: *"Out of our regard to them, we gave them two Blankets and an Handkerchief out of the Small Pox Hospital. I hope it will have the desired effect."*

- Verified against the UMass Amherst transcription of the *Journal of William Trent, 1763* (people.umass.edu/derrico): the primary source reads **"Out of our regard to them we gave them two Blankets and an Handkerchief out of the Small Pox Hospital."** — confirmed **"regard TO them,"** NOT "regard for them."
- The full sentence including **"I hope it will have the desired effect"** also matches the primary source.
- The earlier draft's "regard FOR them" wording (and the Quote Inventory in the fact pack) was wrong; the revised final corrects it. Correction confirmed applied and accurate.

⚠️ (carried forward, out of scope): the UMass primary transcription dates this entry **May 24, 1763**, while the narrative dates the gift to the **June 24, 1763** parley (the conventional secondary-source citation for the Turtleheart parley). This May/June discrepancy was explicitly flagged in fact-pack §S6 and is a known, deliberately-handled item — not a new error introduced by the revision. No action required for this verification; noting for completeness only.

## B. "The Conestoga" section — every specific confirmed

**✅ CORRECT on all points** (verified against Wikipedia *Paxton Boys* and corroborating sources):

- **Susquehannock-descended, small, peaceful, neutral, near Lancaster, under Pennsylvania's protection** — ✅. A remnant Susquehannock group returned to the lower Susquehanna; "The Conestoga remained neutral during the Seven Years' War and Pontiac's War." Protection relationship traces to a treaty with William Penn's government (Wikipedia cites a 1701 confirming treaty recognizing the Conestoga's right to live on/use the land — Penn was the proprietor; the narrative's "a treaty with William Penn" is consistent).
- **Not part of Pontiac's War / not combatants** — ✅ (explicitly neutral through both wars).
- **Paxton Boys = Scots-Irish frontier vigilantes** — ✅. "Drawn from Scots-Irish Presbyterians who lived in the hill country"; vigilantes from Lancaster/Cumberland counties. (Narrative's "Paxtang hill country northwest of Lancaster" is consistent.)
- **14 December 1763 — about fifty attacked Conestoga Town, killed six, burned the village** — ✅. "Roughly 50 Paxton Boys attacked Conestoga Town, killed and scalped the six Conestoga... set the buildings ablaze."
- **27 December 1763 — broke into the Lancaster workhouse and murdered the ~14 survivors including women and children; ~20 dead total** — ✅. "The Paxton Boys broke into the workhouse and killed, scalped, and dismembered all fourteen of the surviving Conestoga, including the women and children." 6 + 14 = ~20 total. (Narrative's "about twenty Conestoga dead in total" is correct and appropriately rounded.)
- **February 1764 — several hundred marched on Philadelphia; turned back by a delegation led by Benjamin Franklin** — ✅. "A few hundred in total, marched on Philadelphia"; Franklin led the delegation that negotiated their dispersal on a promise their grievances would be heard. Narrative's "several hundred" and "met them and turned them back, on a promise that their grievances would be heard" are accurate. (Note: the precise act was persuade-to-disperse via promise, exactly as the narrative states — not a physical repulse. Wording is sound.)
- **No one was ever convicted** — ✅. "None of the Paxton Boys were ever held accountable"; Gov. Penn's reward yielded no arrests; widespread frontier sympathy.
- **The spy accusation was fabricated** — ✅. Targeting rested on "unsubstantiated claims" (Will Sock/Tenseedaagua aiding the Lenape/Shawnee) and Matthew Smith's unverified claim of "dozens of strange, armed Indians." Narrative's "fabricated"/"never anything but a lie" is supported.

No factual overreach detected in the section. The "intent on paper / settlers made it real" framing (lines 106, 110) is interpretive editorializing consistent with the fact pack's stated purpose, not an asserted fact, and does not overstate the record.

## C. "Peace not in unison" clause — confirmed

**✅ CORRECT** (narrative lines 126–127; verified against Wikipedia *Pontiac's War* and *Charlot Kaské*):

- **Resistance persisted in the Illinois country into 1764–65, where the British had not yet taken the last French fort** — ✅. The British did not take possession of Fort de Chartres (from the French) until October 1765; the Illinois country remained out of British hands ~two years; Indian calls for resistance continued there after the 1764 expeditions.
- **Charlot Kaské (Shawnee) rejected accommodation, sought French aid as far as New Orleans, crossed the Mississippi rather than submit** — ✅. Kaské broke with Pontiac over peace, sought French material support (Fort de Chartres / Louisiana governor), supplied the list of 47 villages "that desire to die attached to the French," was rebuffed by French officials, and ultimately took his family across the Mississippi rather than submit to British rule.
