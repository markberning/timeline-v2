## THIN items

### 1. The April 1 gun-spike raid — THIN (one line of context in pack, not in draft)
The pack notes (§4): "April 1: A raid by sailors and troops from the 42nd Illinois Infantry under Col. George W. Roberts spiked Battery No. 1's guns." This is a vivid human moment that belongs to the bombardment section — a small offensive jab during the weeks of stalemate, and it directly involves the same 42nd Illinois regiment the draft already names (the 23 sharpshooters on the *Carondelet*). Not a major omission but a missed texture beat inside the "three weeks of bombardment" passage, which currently reads as pure failure until the canal.

### 2. The Confederate naval squadron (Hollins) — THIN
The draft names the floating battery *New Orleans* correctly (and her fate — moorings shot away, drifted off). But Flag Officer George Hollins and his 6–7 Confederate river gunboats are not mentioned at all. The pack names Hollins (§6: Confederate naval squadron); his complete inaction and resulting disgrace (permanently transferred to administrative duty) is a meaningful counterpoint to Walke's boldness on the same river. Not a MUST-ADD, but his absence means the Confederate naval side is a phantom — the *New Orleans* just floats there with no one commanding it.

### 3. Why McCown was replaced by Mackall — THIN
The draft says Mackall "took over from McCown only about a week earlier, on March 31" but gives no reason. The pack (§6) says McCown was "replaced" without explanation. The actual reason is significant: Beauregard and Polk pulled McCown out to reinforce Johnston — they still believed the island was secure and shuffled him to a bigger fight. Mackall then inherited a command he had no chance to shore up. This adds a layer of Confederate overconfidence to the structural story.

## MISSING items

### A. The CSS *Red River* capture — the U.S. Navy's first hospital ship — MISSING
The pack (§2) lists the *Red Rover* / *Red River* as captured and "later became U.S. Navy's first hospital ship." The draft omits this entirely. It is a genuinely striking fact: a Confederate barracks ship seized at Island Number Ten became the first naval hospital ship in American history, treating nearly 3,000 patients during the war. It belongs in the surrender haul passage (which currently lists only guns and prisoners) as a single punchy sentence. This is a MUST-ADD.

### B. Confederate construction labor — enslaved workers used to build the fortifications — MISSING
The pack records that construction began in August 1861 "under direction of Captain Andrew B. Gray and a crew of several hundred requisitioned enslaved laborers" and that the project was "beset by shortages of labor and tools like shovels." The draft says only that "the Confederates had been digging in here since as early as August 1861." Who dug it — and under what conditions — is a meaningful surface for a war vertical whose stated core value is understanding why the war was fought. A sentence noting that the earthworks were built by impressed enslaved labor, with shortage of even basic tools, adds factual truth and connects the fortress to the war's cause. This is a MUST-ADD (brief; one sentence in the "river fortress" section).

### C. Confederate garrison morale / desertion before the trap closed — MISSING
Pack and web sources both note that by the time Mackall took over he was dealing with troops "demoralized and deserting." The draft depicts the garrison as a capable force that gets trapped and surrenders; it misses the texture of a garrison already dissolving before Pope's crossing sealed the exit. This human detail (men slipping away across Reelfoot Lake before the net closed, the ones who stayed "ragged and tired" marching into Tiptonville) adds weight to the surrender scene and clarifies why 5,000-plus men offered so little resistance. SHOULD-CONSIDER.

### D. Fortification labor shortages: shovels, wheelbarrows, soldiers refusing to work alongside enslaved people — MISSING
Distinct from (B) above and worth noting separately: Confederate soldiers resisted working "alongside slaves," meaning the garrison's own enlisted men refused some labor tasks — a crack in the Confederate social order, right there at the fortress. This is more pointed than a general labor note. SHOULD-CONSIDER (if the draft is expanded).

### E. Post-surrender prisoner disposition — MISSING
Pack §5 has specific detail: sick to St. Louis; officers to Fort Warren (Boston Harbor) or Columbus, Ohio; enlisted men to Chicago (~1,500), Springfield, IL (~1,000), Madison and Milwaukee, Wisconsin (remainder). The draft ends at surrender and the strategic opening. A sentence on what happened to 4,000-plus prisoners — dispersed to Northern cities as far as Boston Harbor — is a human grounding note for the haul. SHOULD-CONSIDER.

## MUST-ADD summary (3 items)

1. **CSS *Red Rover* / *Red River* capture** — one sentence in the surrender haul: seized Confederate barracks ship became the U.S. Navy's first hospital ship. Concrete, surprising, born-verified.

2. **Enslaved labor built the fortification** — one sentence in the "river fortress" section where the August 1861 construction is introduced: several hundred impressed enslaved workers, under Army direction, with chronic tool shortages (not even enough shovels). Factually accurate, directly relevant to the war's cause as the vertical frames it.

3. **The April 1 gun-spike raid** — one sentence in the bombardment-failure passage: 42nd Illinois troops raided Battery No. 1 ashore and spiked its guns. This gives the stalemate section a beat of offensive action and ties the regiment to the story before the *Carondelet* run.
