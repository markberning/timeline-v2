## 3. WEB SPOT-CHECKS (8) — PACK AND DRAFT HOLD UP

1. **Fords + flank route** — Wikipedia (Battle of Brandywine): Trimble's Ford on the West Branch
   then Jefferis Ford on the East Branch, both overlooked by Washington; 17-mile march in ~9
   hours. Matches pack/draft. ✓
2. **Intelligence sequence** — Mount Vernon digital encyclopedia + americanhistorycentral.com:
   Ross's report received ~11 a.m.; Washington begins a cross-creek attack; Spear's null report
   (~1 p.m.) cancels it as an apparent feint; Cheyney's contradicting report among those that
   finally convinced him. Matches the draft's Ross → attack orders → Spear → cancellation →
   ~2 p.m. confirmation. Elmer's "so Contradictory" line corroborated as a contemporary
   observation. ✓
3. **Birmingham Meetinghouse** — Wikipedia (Birmingham Friends Meetinghouse and School):
   "used as a hospital first for the Americans, and after the battle for British officers";
   cemetery stone wall an American defensive position; British and American dead in a common
   grave. All three draft claims confirmed. ✓
4. **British return 587** — Wikipedia: 93 killed, 488 wounded, 6 missing = 587 official; Greene's
   1,200–1,300 estimate and Howe's ~300/600/~400 claim also confirmed verbatim. ✓
5. **Lafayette's wound** — Colonial Williamsburg, ushistory.org (1825 interview), Encyclopedia
   Virginia: musket ball through the left calf/leg while rallying Stirling's breaking division;
   blood filling/pouring from the boot. ✓
6. **Yohn painting** — Commons file page: Frederick Coffay Yohn, published Scribner's Magazine
   June **1898**, photomechanical halftone print, Library of Congress (LCCN90713897), 4096×3278,
   public domain. Draft's "around 1898 / c. 1898" is correct (could safely say "1898"). ✓
7. **Paoli scholarship** — Wikipedia (Battle of Paoli) + PSU Center for the Book: McGuire's count
   of 53 American dead buried on the field (with his own uncertainty caveat), 71 captured, older
   higher counts; Wayne's Nov 1, 1777 verdict text confirmed byte-exact incl. "The Court do
   acquit him with the highest honor." ✓ (One web summary gives ~150 wounded vs the pack's ~113
   — both circulate; draft traces to the pack; see NIT-7.)
8. **Faden's title** — Wikipedia (William Faden) + London Museum: Faden appointed Geographer to
   George III in **June 1783**. The draft's 1778-map caption calling him "the official British
   cartographer" is anachronistic → BLOCKER-2 below. ✗ (draft, not pack — the pack never makes
   this claim)

**Pack-vs-web tension noted (not a draft error):** Wikipedia puts Cornwallis's column at ~9,000
vs the pack's ~8,400; the pack itself flags column splits as MED, ±500 by source. The draft's
"about 8,400" with the dossier's "vary ±500" acknowledgment is within the pack's stated band.

## BLOCKERS (3)

**BLOCKER-1 · §1 prose (line 169): "the next morning" for the Jefferis's Ford defile is a
not-in-pack time claim, and it is wrong.**
> "When the British vanguard poured through the narrow defile at Jefferis's Ford the next morning, the Hessian captain leading it, Johann Ewald, could hardly believe what he was looking at"
The pack gives no clock time for the defile, and its own timeline contradicts "morning": the
column left Kennett Square ~5 a.m. and emerged on Osborne's Hill ~2 p.m. after 9–10 hours of
marching, with Jefferis's Ford the SECOND crossing, near the march's end — i.e., around midday
to early afternoon, not morning. **Fix:** "the next day" (or "the next afternoon" if you want
precision, but "the next day" is the pack-safe rendering).

**BLOCKER-2 · IMAGES #2 / Faden caption (line 77): "the official British cartographer's version
of the day" — not in pack and anachronistic.**
> "William Faden's 1778 London map of the battle, the official British cartographer's version of the day"
The pack says only "the period British battle map." Web check: Faden was appointed Geographer
to George III in June 1783 — five years AFTER this 1778 map — so "official British cartographer"
is wrong for the map's date. **Fix:** "William Faden's 1778 London map of the battle, the
British version of the day: the creek line, the fords, and the long hook of the flank march..."

**BLOCKER-3 · IMAGES #5 / Wayne caption (line 91): "painted in the period" — not in pack,
unverified.**
> "Anthony Wayne, the Pennsylvania brigadier who held Chadds Ford, painted in the period."
The pack lists the file with no date or attribution for the portrait. The Commons file page
may or may not support a period date; as written this is an asserted-from-nothing claim on a
gated caption surface. **Fix:** drop the phrase ("Anthony Wayne, the Pennsylvania brigadier who
held Chadds Ford.") or verify the portrait's date on the file page at fetch time and caption
what it actually says.

## SHOULD-FIX (7)

**SF-1 · "Six weeks at sea" (4 places) contradicts the pack's "~34 days at sea" and the draft's
own prose.** Dossier note (line 16: "Howe spent six weeks at sea"), sides note (line 33: "Six
weeks at sea to the Chesapeake"), §1 blurb (line 55: "spent six weeks at sea"), Locator 2
caption (line 146: "Six weeks at sea put Howe's army ashore"), plus §1's closing "Six weeks of
seasickness and dead horses" two sentences after the same paragraph says "about 34 days at sea."
The pack blesses "six-week sea MOVE" (the whole operation) and "~34 days at sea" (Sandy Hook →
landing) — "six weeks AT SEA" is the conflation. **Fix:** "six-week sea move" / "Six weeks after
leaving New York" phrasings; keep "about 34 days at sea" as the only at-sea figure.

**SF-2 · Locator 1: the Kennett Square dot (39.844) falls OUTSIDE the frame (latMin 39.85).**
The dot would clip off the bottom edge, and its lon (-75.711) sits 0.009° from the left edge
(-75.72). **Fix:** extend the tactical frame to latMin ~39.82 / lonMin ~-75.74, or drop the
Kennett Square dot from Locator 1 (it's fully served by Locator 2).

**SF-3 · §1 (line 155): "the decisive battle the British had been chasing for a year" — "for a
year" is not in the pack.** The pack supports the decisive-battle hope, not a duration. **Fix:**
cut "the British had been chasing for a year" → "the decisive battle the British wanted" (or
similar).

**SF-4 · §3 Paoli (line 215): "American newspapers and camp talk said men had been killed
begging for quarter" — the pack says "American propaganda," not newspapers specifically.**
The channel specificity is invented. **Fix:** "American accounts said" or "American propaganda
said."

**SF-5 · Reuse-portrait captions (IMAGES #7–11) are not written, and Sullivan's mandatory
imagined-likeness caveat exists only as an instruction note.** The pack's §9 says reuse "with
fresh captions," and the Sullivan caveat is flagged MANDATORY in two places in the draft but no
caption text carries it. This is exactly how mandatory caveats get lost at integration. **Fix:**
write the five reuse captions now, Sullivan's including "an imagined likeness — no portrait of
Sullivan from life exists" (or equivalent).

**SF-6 · §2 (line 180): "a stream with no bridges" drops the pack's locality qualifier.** Pack:
"not bridged HERE in 1777"; §1 gets this right ("no bridges along this stretch in 1777"), §2's
re-gloss universalizes it. **Fix:** "a stream with no bridges along this stretch."

**SF-7 · Hero/dossier caption says "around 1898" — the Commons/LOC page dates the Yohn print to
1898 exactly (Scribner's Magazine, June 1898).** Not an error (the pack says c. 1898), but since
the file page is definitive, tighten to "in 1898" at fetch time and consider crediting
"published in Scribner's Magazine" if the integrator wants it; pack-safe either way.

## NIT (8)

1. **Dossier sides (line 32): American strength "~14,600"** — the pack's own use-instruction
   says 'Use "~14,000 Americans vs ~15,500 British"' even though ~14,600 is the pack's standard
   figure. Internal pack tension; either is defensible. Note for the integrator.
2. **§1 (line 159): chevaux-de-frise gloss** "(rows of sunken, iron-tipped spikes designed to
   tear out a ship's hull)" — not in the pack but an accurate definitional gloss of the term
   (the Delaware obstructions were sunken timber frames with iron-tipped spikes). Acceptable as
   inline-define-everything house style; flagging for the record.
3. **§3 (line 209): Hancock glossed "the president of the Continental Congress"** — not in the
   pack; true for Sept 1777 (Hancock served until late Oct 1777). Accurate role gloss; fine.
4. **§1/§2: "about seven miles" Kennett Square → the Brandywine** — not stated in the pack but
   derivable from pack §11 coords (~6.5 mi); fair rounding.
5. **§3 (line 219): "his official casualty bill for the battle was about half his enemy's"** —
   derived arithmetic (587 vs ~1,100–1,300 ≈ 0.49); sound, and "official" correctly anchors it
   to the return rather than the undercount question.
6. **Knyphausen bio (line 48): "once Cornwallis's guns opened behind the American line"** — the
   pack says only "once Cornwallis's guns opened"; "behind the American line" is consistent with
   the verified geometry (Birmingham was behind the right). Harmless extension.
7. **Paoli wounded ~113** — pack figure, carried correctly; one web summary circulates ~150
   wounded (McGuire totals 272 killed/wounded/missing). If the integrator ever revisits, "some
   113 wounded" could become "well over a hundred wounded," but no change required to pass.
8. **§3 (line 219): "(that story has its own page)" re Germantown** — meta-aside, not a factual
   claim; voice call for the storytelling critic, not this gate.
