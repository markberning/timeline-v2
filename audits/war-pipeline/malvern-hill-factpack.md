## 2. Casualties

### Union casualties
- **Total: 3,007** (314 killed, 1,875 wounded, 818 missing/captured)  
  *Sources: Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill" infobox; Encyclopedia Virginia; Iron Brigader*
- Alternative figures: ABT summary page gives ~2,100; Essential Civil War Curriculum gives ~2,100; CWSAC VA021 gives ~3,000. [See §10 for contested-numbers note.]

### Confederate casualties
- **Total: 5,650** (869 killed, 4,241 wounded, 540 missing)  
  *Sources: Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill" infobox; Encyclopedia Virginia; Iron Brigader*
- Alternative figures: CWSAC VA021 gives 5,355; ABT summary gives ~5,600; NPS Richmond NBP gives "more than 5,300"; Seven Days Wikipedia gives 5,355 at Malvern Hill. [See §10.]

### Combined
- CWSAC total: ~8,355. [Wikipedia; CWSAC VA021]
- ABT summary: ~7,700 total (2,100 US + 5,600 CS).

### Artillery's share of Confederate losses
- The Confederate loss was overwhelming from Union artillery fire; Confederate brigades were cut down before reaching Union infantry lines. Union artillery fired 688+ rounds per battery (Captain Snow's Battery B alone fired 688 rounds). [Iron Brigader]
- No single sourced percentage of CS losses specifically attributable to artillery fire exists in open secondary literature. [UNVERIFIED — percentage figure often cited in popular accounts but no original source located.]
- Primary effect: Confederate infantry attacks stalled "well short of the crest," driven back by canister and shell before engaging Union infantry at close range. [NPS Richmond NBP; ABT article; Essential Civil War Curriculum]
- D.H. Hill's division suffered ~20% casualties; Wright's and Gordon's brigades ~40% each; Mahone, Armistead, Cobb ~25% each. [Essential Civil War Curriculum]

## 3. What Came Before

### The Seven Days
- The Seven Days Battles ran June 25–July 1, 1862. Malvern Hill was the **sixth and final** engagement. Prior battles: Oak Grove (June 25), Beaver Dam Creek/Mechanicsville (June 26), Gaines's Mill (June 27), Garnett's and Golding's Farm (June 27–28), Savage's Station (June 29), Glendale/White Oak Swamp (June 30). [Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"; NPS CWSAC]
- Lee launched the campaign on June 26 to drive McClellan away from Richmond and prevent the fall of the Confederate capital. [ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill"; Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"]

### The retreat to the James
- After Gaines's Mill (June 27), McClellan began withdrawing the Army of the Potomac southward across the Virginia Peninsula toward the James River, where Union gunboats could provide protection. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- At Glendale (June 30), Lee attempted to cut off McClellan's retreating column and failed. [Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"; ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- By July 1, the Union rearguard had reached Malvern Hill. The Army of the Potomac — for the **first time during the Seven Days that the entire army was united on the same field** — held the plateau. [ABT "10 Facts: Malvern Hill," Fact 2]
- Harrison's Landing on the James River was McClellan's intended destination, ~10 miles further south, where Union gunboats (the James River Squadron) would protect the army. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT article]

### Hunt's artillery deployment
- Col. Henry J. Hunt massed the Artillery Reserve in close support of the frontline batteries near the Crew House. [Iron Brigader; Essential Civil War Curriculum]
- Gun count (contested — see §10): sources variously report ~100 guns deployed on the front slope with ~150 more in reserve and on flanks (total ~250); or approximately 107 cannon employed at the climax; or ~40 guns at the forward ridge with reserves behind. The Encyclopedia Virginia figure is 107 cannon; Wikipedia "Henry Jackson Hunt" cites 250 guns total; L. Van Loan Naisawald's *Grape and Canister* (cited in multiple sources) gives ~100 on the front with another 150 in reserve. [See §10.]
- Hunt also positioned a battery of heavy 32-pounder howitzers on the right flank; at the battle's climax these fired at close range to devastating effect. [Iron Brigader; Warfare History Network "Artillery Reserve"]
- Three Union gunboats — **USS Galena, USS Jacob Bell, USS Aroostook** — provided supporting fire from the James River, though with limited accuracy due to the range and troop movement. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]

### The ground
- Malvern Hill is a broad, level plateau approximately 100 feet above the surrounding terrain, with open, gently sloping farm fields on the north face running downhill toward the Confederate approach. [Encyclopedia Virginia; NPS Richmond NBP; NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory]
- The plateau's strength came not from great height but from the open fields of fire it provided across all approaches. NPS Richmond NBP: "a modest elevation whose strength derived from open, gently sloping fields providing extensive fields of fire." [NPS Richmond NBP; ABT article]
- The James River lay ~1–2.5 miles to the south, securing the Union rear. [Wikipedia; Encyclopedia Virginia; NPS Richmond NBP]
- Union flanks were protected by natural features: **Western Run** (a tributary of Turkey Island Creek) on the east side of the hill; steep ravines and swampy drainage bottoms and dense forest/wetland along its course helped shield the flanks. [NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory; ABT]
- **Willis Church Road** (also called the "Quaker Road" on some Confederate maps) bisected the Union position. [ABT "10 Facts," Fact 4 and Fact 6]
- The **Crew house** (Crew Farm) sat near the western ridge of the hill, west of Willis Church Road, and was a central reference point for the Union artillery line. The **West house** (Nathaniel West farm) stood on the east side of the ridge. [NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory]

## 4. The Battle (July 1, 1862)

### Lee's plan
- Lee hoped to catch and crush the retreating Union army before it reached the James River. After the partial success at Glendale, he believed one more blow could destroy McClellan's force. [ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill"; Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- Lee ordered a two-part plan: (1) Confederate artillery batteries at separated positions would deliver converging fire on the Union guns and silence them; (2) once the Union artillery was suppressed, infantry would assault. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; Essential Civil War Curriculum]
- The orders were drafted by Col. Robert Chilton (Lee's chief of staff) and designated Brig. Gen. Lewis Armistead's yell as the attack signal for fifteen brigades — a fatal command-and-control flaw. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]

### Confederate artillery failure (from ~1 PM)
- The Confederate artillery barrage failed completely. Union guns under Hunt fired first and prevented Confederate batteries from achieving their coordinating crossfire. Confederate guns were "engaged one-by-one instead of in unison" and were "cut to pieces by concentrated Union artillery fire." [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- D.H. Hill dismissed the Confederate artillery effort as "most farcical." [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill," citing the *Battles and Leaders* account]
- Cartographic error compounded the tactical failure: the map Lee used incorrectly labeled Willis Church Road as "the Quaker Road," delaying Magruder's arrival by several hours. [ABT "10 Facts," Fact 4]

### Piecemeal infantry assaults (from ~3–3:30 PM)
- Despite the artillery failure, Lee ordered discretionary attacks after receiving reports (later shown to be incorrect) suggesting the Union line was wavering. [Essential Civil War Curriculum; Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- **Armistead's advance (~3:30 PM):** Three regiments pushed Union skirmishers back but stalled in a ravine, unsupported. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- **Magruder's charge (~5:30 PM):** Arriving three hours late due to navigation confusion, Magruder launched approximately 5,000 men. Brigades under Wright and Mahone were pinned down or driven back; the division took ~25% casualties in some brigades. [Wikipedia; Essential Civil War Curriculum]
- **D.H. Hill's charge (~6 PM):** Five brigades with approximately 8,200 men attacked through dense woods in five uncoordinated waves. One brigade lost 41% of its strength. [Wikipedia; Essential Civil War Curriculum]
- **Later assaults (~6–8:30 PM):** Brigades of Toombs, Anderson, Barksdale, Ransom, Kershaw, and Semmes launched successive charges. Kershaw's men retreated in rout after taking friendly fire. Union sharpshooters and canister fire repelled all attacks. Fighting ended around 8:30 PM. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- Approximately 20 separate Confederate brigades attacked at different times across the afternoon and evening; no coordinated assault ever materialized. [NPS Richmond NBP; ABT]
- No Confederate soldier reached the Union crest. [ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill" article]
- Lee himself was absent from the main battlefield during much of the action, searching for alternative routes to cut McClellan off from the river. [ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill" article]

### D.H. Hill's verdict
- **Exact quote (widely cited):** "It was not war—it was murder."  
  *Source: D.H. Hill, "McClellan's Change of Base and Malvern Hill," in Robert Underwood Johnson and Clarence Clough Buel, eds., **Battles and Leaders of the Civil War**, vol. 2 (New York: The Century Company, 1887). Written more than 20 years after the battle.* [Essential Civil War Curriculum; Wikipedia "Daniel Harvey Hill"; multiple secondary sources]
- **Note on exact wording:** Minor punctuation variants appear across sources ("It was not war—it was murder" vs. "It wasn't war; it was murder"). The em-dash version matches the most frequently cited scholarly form. [Wikipedia "Daniel Harvey Hill"; Essential Civil War Curriculum; Encyclopedia Virginia]
- Hill also said that morning, on inspecting the Union position: "If General McClellan is there in force, we had better let him alone." [Essential Civil War Curriculum, citing Hill in *Battles and Leaders*]

## 5. The Aftermath

### Immediate tactical result
- Despite a clear Union tactical victory — Confederate assaults repulsed with ~5,600 casualties inflicted — McClellan ordered the retreat to continue overnight. [Wikipedia; CWSAC VA021; NPS Richmond NBP; ABT]
- McClellan's army abandoned Malvern Hill beginning at approximately 11 PM on July 1. The army marched through the night in heavy rain and reached Harrison's Landing on the James River by July 2. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill" article]
- The Turkey Island Bridge was destroyed behind the retreating column. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- Some Union subordinates reportedly favored counterattacking toward Richmond after the victory; McClellan overruled them and continued the retreat. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT] [VERIFY — specific subordinates who protested not consistently identified in sources reviewed]
- Lee considered pursuit but declined, citing rain and army exhaustion. Jackson reportedly disagreed with this decision. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]

### Strategic consequences
- **For McClellan:** His Peninsula Campaign was effectively over. The Army of the Potomac entrenched at Harrison's Landing, protected by gunboats, but made no further advance on Richmond. Lincoln began losing confidence in McClellan. General John Pope's Army of Virginia was soon tasked with a new overland advance. McClellan was not removed immediately but was effectively sidelined. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT article; Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"]
- **For Lee:** Despite the costly tactical defeat at Malvern Hill, the Seven Days as a whole represented a strategic Confederate triumph. Lee had driven McClellan from the gates of Richmond in one week of fighting. The Confederate press lionized Lee; the *Richmond Dispatch* declared: "No captain that ever lived could have planned or executed a better plan." [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- **Lee's own assessment:** Lee wrote to his wife after the Seven Days: "Our success has not been as great or complete as we should have desired." [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"]
- **Seven Days total casualties:** Union ~16,000 (1,734 killed, 8,066 wounded, 6,055 missing); Confederate ~20,000+ (3,494 killed, 15,758 wounded, 952 missing). [Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"]
- The war in the East shifted: Lee reorganized his army into two wings (Jackson and Longstreet) and reformed Confederate artillery into battalion-sized units. The next major Eastern Theater action would be Second Bull Run (August 1862). [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"]
- It would be approximately 23 months before Union forces approached Richmond again. [Essential Civil War Curriculum]

## 6. Key People

**Confederate:**
- **Gen. Robert E. Lee (CS)** — overall Confederate commander; launched the Seven Days offensive to relieve Richmond; ordered the Malvern Hill assault despite the failed artillery preparation; was absent from the main battlefield during much of the action. [Wikipedia; ABT; NPS]
- **Maj. Gen. D.H. (Daniel Harvey) Hill (CS)** — divisional commander; led the major infantry charge (~6 PM) with five brigades (~8,200 men); witnessed the slaughter firsthand; authored the famous "not war, it was murder" verdict in his *Battles and Leaders* account (written ~20+ years post-battle). [Wikipedia; Essential Civil War Curriculum; *Battles and Leaders* vol. 2, 1887]
- **Maj. Gen. John B. Magruder (CS)** — organized and led most of the Confederate assaults; arrived hours late due to map confusion (wrong "Quaker Road"); launched his ~5,000-man charge at ~5:30 PM. [Wikipedia; ABT "10 Facts"; Essential Civil War Curriculum]
- **Maj. Gen. Thomas J. Jackson (CS)** — commanded a large wing (four divisions); Jackson's forces advanced on the eastern approach through woods; his command did not play a decisive role in the failed assaults; Jackson reportedly argued for pursuit after Lee declined. [Wikipedia; NPS Richmond NBP; Essential Civil War Curriculum]
- **Maj. Gen. James Longstreet (CS)** — held in reserve after the engagement at Glendale (June 30); not heavily committed at Malvern Hill. [Essential Civil War Curriculum; Wikipedia]

**Union:**
- **Maj. Gen. George B. McClellan (US)** — overall Union commander; departed the battlefield before the main action to board the USS Galena and inspect Harrison's Landing; left Fitz John Porter in de facto command; returned to the field only briefly at Porter's urging, remaining on the right side away from the main action. Severely criticized for absence both immediately and during his 1864 presidential campaign. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT "10 Facts" Fact 3; Iron Brigader "The Craven"] [See §10 for contested details.]
- **Brig. Gen. Fitz John Porter (US)** — V Corps commander; assumed de facto field command in McClellan's absence; positioned Union infantry in a U-shaped formation; coordinated the defensive line with Hunt; directed the battle from the crest. [Wikipedia; ABT; Essential Civil War Curriculum; Encyclopedia Virginia]
- **Col. Henry J. Hunt (US)** — Army of the Potomac Artillery Reserve commander; massed Union batteries tier-on-tier along the crest, including 32-pounder howitzers; positioned the Artillery Reserve in close support of front-line batteries near the Crew House; his artillery coordination was the decisive factor in the Union victory. Rank at Malvern Hill: Colonel (later promoted to Brigadier General). [Wikipedia "Henry Jackson Hunt"; Iron Brigader; Warfare History Network "Artillery Reserve"; ABT]

## 7. What It Meant

- **End of the Peninsula Campaign.** Malvern Hill concluded the Seven Days Battles and effectively ended McClellan's effort to capture Richmond via the Peninsula. Despite the tactical victory, the strategic result was Confederate: Lee had saved Richmond. [CWSAC VA021; Wikipedia; ABT]
- **A strategic Confederate triumph despite tactical defeat.** Lee suffered far heavier casualties than McClellan across the Seven Days (~20,000 CS vs. ~16,000 US), but drove the Union army away from the Confederate capital — the objective. [Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"]
- **McClellan's failure and its consequences.** The Peninsula Campaign's failure damaged McClellan's standing with Lincoln and the Northern public, set in motion the appointment of Pope and ultimately McClellan's removal after Antietam (September 1862). [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles"]
- **The power of massed artillery.** Malvern Hill became a textbook demonstration of what massed, well-positioned artillery could do to frontal infantry assaults across open ground. Hunt's deployment — fresh batteries rotating in as others exhausted ammunition, converging fire across the open northern slope — was a template for later Union artillery doctrine. [Wikipedia "Henry Jackson Hunt"; Iron Brigader; ABT article]
- **Lee's command lessons.** The costly, uncoordinated assaults exposed severe deficiencies in Lee's army's command-and-control and staff systems; Lee subsequently reorganized and would not repeat the same errors. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT article]
- **Preservation.** The NPS has called Malvern Hill "the best preserved Civil War battlefield in central or southern Virginia," largely unchanged since 1862. The American Battlefield Trust acquired 1,441 acres between 1994 and 2021. [Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill"; ABT "10 Facts"]

## 9. Geography for a Map

**Orientation:** North up.

**Key terrain features:**
- **Malvern Hill plateau** — a broad, flat-topped elevation (~100 ft. above surrounding terrain) in Henrico County, Virginia; open farm fields cover the north face; steep ravines and woodland on the edges. Located approximately 1–2.5 miles north of the James River. [Encyclopedia Virginia; NPS; Wikipedia]
- **North face** — the main Confederate approach: gently sloping open ground rising southward to the plateau crest; no significant cover for ~800 yards to over a mile in most directions. This is the killing ground. [ABT article "Battle of Malvern Hill"; NPS Richmond NBP]
- **James River** — south of the hill (~1–2.5 miles); the Union's destination and protection; three Union gunboats (USS Galena, USS Jacob Bell, USS Aroostook) operated here and fired supporting rounds onto the battlefield. [Wikipedia]
- **Union line along the crest** — running roughly east-west across the top of the north face; massed artillery positioned to fire north down the slope; Porter's infantry formed a U-shaped line behind and among the guns. [Encyclopedia Virginia; Essential Civil War Curriculum; ABT]
- **Crew house / Crew Farm** — western side of the crest, west of Willis Church Road; reference point for Hunt's main artillery reserve deployment. [NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory; Iron Brigader]
- **West house / West Farm (Nathaniel West farm)** — eastern side of the ridge, east of Willis Church Road. [NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory]
- **Willis Church Road** — ran north-south through the position; divided Union line into east (West Farm) and west (Crew Farm) sectors; Confederate maps mislabeled it "Quaker Road," causing Magruder's delayed arrival. [ABT "10 Facts" Facts 4 & 6]
- **Western Run** — a tributary of Turkey Island Creek; ran mostly along the eastern side of the hill and into the northern approach; swampy bottomland and dense forest along its course helped protect the Union right/eastern flank. [NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory; ABT geography]
- **Confederate approach** — from the north, across open fields, uphill toward the crest; multiple Confederate brigades attacked in waves up this slope; woods to the north provided limited staging areas at 800+ yards. [Wikipedia; NPS Richmond NBP; ABT]
- **River Road** — ran along the James River to the south; T.H. Holmes's Confederate division (not heavily engaged) approached via this road; Union siege artillery covered it. [Essential Civil War Curriculum]

## 10. Contested / Uncertain Facts

### Casualty figures
- The two most common figures for the battle appear in these ranges:
  - **Union:** 2,100 (ABT summary; Essential Civil War Curriculum) vs. 3,007 (Wikipedia infobox; Encyclopedia Virginia; Iron Brigader) vs. ~3,000 (CWSAC VA021)
  - **Confederate:** ~5,300 (NPS Richmond NBP) vs. 5,355 (CWSAC VA021; Wikipedia "Seven Days Battles") vs. 5,600 (ABT summary) vs. 5,650 (Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill" infobox; Encyclopedia Virginia)
- **Likely explanation:** The lower Union/Confederate numbers (2,100 / 5,600) from ABT may count differently from the more granular 3,007 / 5,650 breakdown in Wikipedia's sourced infobox. The CWSAC figure (3,000 / 5,355) is a rounded official government estimate. The Wikipedia infobox figures (3,007 / 5,650) are the most granular and appear most fully broken down (killed/wounded/missing); these are used as the primary figures in §2 above, with the range noted.
- **Fact-checker action:** Cross-check against OR (Official Records), series I, vol. XI, and against Livermore's *Numbers and Losses in the Civil War* for the authoritative figures.

### The share of Confederate casualties from artillery
- Popular histories often assert that the "overwhelming majority" or "more than half" of Confederate casualties came from artillery fire rather than infantry. No secondary source reviewed here provides a sourced percentage backed by primary data. [UNVERIFIED — flag for fact-checker; do not assert a specific percentage in the narrative without a primary source.]

### McClellan's whereabouts
- Multiple sources confirm McClellan boarded the USS Galena and went to inspect Harrison's Landing during the battle, leaving Porter in de facto command. [Wikipedia; ABT "10 Facts" Fact 3; Iron Brigader]
- Sources differ on nuance: Wikipedia says he "left the battlefield before the main action to inspect Harrison's Landing aboard the USS Galena"; ABT says "despite being present, McClellan delegated battlefield management to subordinates...while he prepared supply operations elsewhere"; the Iron Brigader source states he was "safely smoking on a gunboat."
- The ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill" article states Lee was absent from the main battlefield searching for alternative routes — some sources conflate the two commanders' absences.
- Wikipedia states McClellan "returned to the field at Porter's urging, remaining on the right side" — timing unclear. [VERIFY: When exactly did McClellan leave? When did he return? Did he direct any battle decisions in person?]
- McClellan's physical absence was politically damaging — the satirical 1862 poem "The Craven" (modeled on Poe's "The Raven") mocked him for it; Currier & Ives cartoons depicted him on the Galena during the battle during his 1864 presidential run. [Iron Brigader "The Craven"]

### D.H. Hill quote — exact wording
- The quote appears in *Battles and Leaders of the Civil War*, vol. 2 (1887) — a named primary source in a named published work. The em-dash form ("It was not war—it was murder") is the most commonly reproduced in scholarly secondary literature. [VERIFY: Transcribe from the original *Battles and Leaders* text to confirm punctuation and surrounding context before the narrative goes to print.]

### Gun count: Union artillery
- Sources are inconsistent:
  - ~100 guns on the forward slope + ~150 in reserve = **~250 total** [Wikipedia "Henry Jackson Hunt"; multiple secondary sources citing this figure]
  - **107 cannon** employed at the climax [Encyclopedia Virginia]
  - **~40 pieces** at the forward ridge, more behind [ABT "Battle of Malvern Hill" article, describing "roughly 30–35 cannon" in the main line]
  - **"Mile-long line of 100 guns"** [cited in multiple sources as the front-slope count]
- The confusion is definitional: whether counting (a) guns in the immediate forward line, (b) guns of the Artillery Reserve only, or (c) all Union artillery on and behind the hill including corps batteries. The ~250 figure appears to represent the total; ~100 the front-slope deployment. [VERIFY — cite the specific deployment figure with a definitional note in the narrative]

- The Richmond Dispatch quote and Lee's letter to his wife are cited in Wikipedia "Battle of Malvern Hill" without original issue dates or full letter citations. [VERIFY before direct quotation in the narrative.]
