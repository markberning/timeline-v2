# FRAMING CRITIC — Battles of Lexington & Concord (`rev-lexington`)

Draft: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-lexington-draft.md`
Fact pack: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-lexington-factpack.md`
Critic lane: fairness, emphasis, interpretive honesty (not fact-checking, not voice).

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The hard framing requirements all hold. The first shot is never resolved on any surface
(prose, blurbs, outcome text, hero caption, MEANWHILE). Both deposition campaigns are
presented as instruments ("produced as weapons, not as evidence"), with Gould's
could-not-say line as the only thing left standing. Margaret Gage stays an unproven
suspicion with the specialists-call-it-a-myth counterweight. The atrocity ledger runs
both directions at evidence level: the hatchet killing is real and told, the scalping
elaboration is propaganda with the burial-party denial quoted, and the British
plundering/house-killings are attested through the British officers' own diaries
(Barker, Mackenzie), capped by the pack's even-handed line. The British day has real
humanity: the soaked midnight wait, ~21 hours under arms, the exhausted collapse inside
Percy's square, Pitcairn respected by Bostonians, Percy the professional who opposed the
policy and marched anyway, the police-raid-on-paper framing with Gage's restraint clause
quoted. No-likeness honesty holds on Parker, Pitcairn, and Heath, with honest-caption
requirements written into every fallback. The "system, not a minuteman" anti-romance
note is carried in the MEANWHILE and reinforced (never undercut) by the prose. All
superlatives checked trace to the pack ("most honest British verdict," "smartest call,"
"bloodiest single spot"). No blockers.

## SHOULD-FIX

1. **Plate IV caption drops the months-later Doolittle discipline the page sets for
   itself.** The draft's own fact ledger says the Doolittle caption framing requirement
   is "carried on every Doolittle caption." The hero (Plate I) and Plate III captions
   carry it; the Plate IV caption does not:
   > "Percy's relief brigade meets Smith's exhausted column east of Lexington green,
   > cannon deployed and houses burning, in Doolittle's Plate IV."
   Only the credit line has the December 1775 date, and credits are the least-read
   surface. **Fix:** end the caption with the same badge, e.g. "...the way they fought
   all afternoon. Engraved by Doolittle in December 1775, months after the day."

2. **Parker's deposition is quoted as straight evidence while the page brands the
   deposition campaign as weapons.** Section 2 introduces QUOTE 2 with "Six days later
   Parker swore a deposition (a formal statement made under oath) about his intentions" —
   presented as credible testimony. Section 3 then says of the same campaign: "they were
   produced as weapons, not as evidence" (Parker's April 25 deposition is one of the
   ~100 Provincial Congress instruments). The pack endorses using it (HIGH, archives.gov;
   "his real deposition is better anyway"), and it IS better testimony because it claims
   less, not more — but the page never says so, leaving an apparent double standard a
   careful reader will catch. **Fix:** one clause at either use, e.g. in section 3:
   "his actual sworn deposition, taken for the Congress's own campaign yet claiming
   only dispersal and no firing, is better testimony anyway." (The asymmetry — an
   instrument that under-claims — is exactly the honest point.)

3. **Section 3's "did their job" beat can read as battle-forcing the plunder.**
   > "...marched to Concord, destroyed what stores they could find, and mostly spared
   > the inhabitants and their property until the road home made a battlefield of every
   > house along it."
   Paired with "executed Gage's written orders almost to the letter," this risks framing
   the looting and house-killings as something the road did to the regulars, when the
   draft's own section 2 establishes the orders explicitly forbade it and were violated
   ("Gage's written orders had forbidden exactly this"). The pack's "almost to the
   letter" framing is HIGH and fine; the added "mostly spared... until" clause is the
   draft's own extension (the pack scopes correct behavior to the officers IN CONCORD).
   **Fix:** keep the bitter-joke beat but concede the exception in-line, e.g.
   "...executed Gage's written orders almost to the letter, the no-plunder clause
   excepted, and it did not matter:" — or trim "mostly spared the inhabitants and their
   property" back to Concord ("behaved correctly in the town itself").

## NIT

1. **Ledger/prose mismatch on Danvers.** The fact ledger claims `Danvers company 16
   miles in ~4 hours — MED ("about four hours" in prose)`, but Danvers and the four
   hours appear nowhere in the prose (Menotomy says only "fresh companies from Essex
   County"). Either add the Danvers beat (it strengthens the system-not-spontaneity
   frame) or correct the ledger line so the integration pass doesn't trust a phantom.

2. **"...the occupation was exactly the problem it was meant to solve"** (section 1,
   opening paragraph) is an interpretive aphorism with no pack line behind it. It is
   defensible house-voice and directionally true, but it is the one unattributed
   editorial verdict on the page; consider trimming or grounding it.

3. **"Percy's was the one that aged well"** (section 3) goes a half-step past the
   pack's letter, but the pack's "became British policy-grade respect" covers the
   direction; acceptable as-is. Noting it only so the fact critic doesn't relitigate.

## Checklist against the brief

1. **Both-sides honesty — PASS.** British humanity concrete and specific (soaked wait,
   21 hours, 35–40 miles, collapse in the square, Smith's wound, Pitcairn's fairness,
   Percy's opposition-yet-duty). Atrocities at evidence level both ways; the hatchet
   killing told plainly, British killings/plunder via Barker and Mackenzie's own words;
   the closing even-handed ledger sentence is verbatim the pack's required beat. Neither
   hymn nor mush — SHOULD-FIX 3 is the only soft spot.
2. **Propaganda honesty — PASS.** Both campaigns presented as instruments on every
   surface, including the dossier outcome text ("racing sworn testimony across the
   Atlantic, because each understood that who fired first mattered as much as who won
   the road") and the London MEANWHILE ("It was ammunition"). First shot never resolved
   anywhere — SHOULD-FIX 2 closes the one seam.
3. **Myth/legend framing — PASS.** Emerson 1837 badged twice (bridge, not Lexington);
   boulder line badged as much-later inscription with the deposition preferred; Margaret
   Gage "an unproven suspicion, nothing more"; the alarm SYSTEM is the hero in both
   prose and MEANWHILE, Revere captured/Prescott credited, lanterns demoted to backup;
   Parker's Revenge carries the one-account-50-years + archaeology caveat in prose;
   scalping = real killing + propaganda career + burial-party denial.
4. **No-likeness honesty — PASS.** Parker img NONE (bio says no likeness exists; Kitson
   statue fallback caption-mandated as idealized, not a portrait); Pitcairn img NONE
   (Trumbull-used-the-son noted; Plate II spyglass crop mandated honest caption; the
   unverified `File:John Pitcairn.jpg` explicitly barred); Heath NONE. Percy's Stuart
   portrait captioned as post-war.
5. **Emphasis — PASS.** Weight follows the pack: the road, not the green/bridge,
   carries the casualties and the page says so in stats footnote, prose, and section 3.
   Superlatives all pack-backed. The militia-system MEANWHILE is reinforced by "None of
   this was spontaneous. It was a system" and never undercut.
6. **Captions/dossier discipline — PASS with SHOULD-FIX 1.** Doolittle months-later
   provenance on hero and Plate III (Plate IV needs it); winner line honest both ways —
   "American strategic victory" with the outcome text giving the British tactical escape
   ("saved from destruction only by Percy's relief brigade") and the Percy-saved-it
   verdict in his card.
