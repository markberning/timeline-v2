## Summary Verdict

### MUST-ADD (essential, blocking — draft is meaningfully incomplete without these)

1. **Union casualty figures.** The draft names Confederate captured totals and materiel in detail but never states that ~507 Union soldiers were killed and ~1,976 wounded. Every battle story needs both sides of the ledger. One sentence.

2. **Johnston's reinforcement decision.** The draft correctly establishes Johnston's line and notes he was far away, but misses the key strategic irony: Johnston concluded the fort was probably indefensible after Fort Henry fell, and then poured reinforcements in anyway — trapping the army he sent to delay Grant. Without this, the reader does not understand WHY there were 12,000+ men available to surrender. The beat is implicit but needs to be made explicit. One to two sentences.

### SHOULD-CONSIDER (enrich the telling, not blocking)

3. **Floyd and Pillow's post-battle fates.** Davis dismissed Floyd from the Confederate Army (March 11, 1862) and suspended Pillow from command (April 16, 1862) for "grave errors in judgement." The draft builds to their flight but never lands the historical verdict. Given how much space it gives their decisions, the payoff belongs.

4. **Johnston's "disastrous and almost without remedy" quote.** The Confederate theater commander's own verdict on the loss, widely cited in secondary sources. A strong one-liner for the consequences section; gives the significance beat a Confederate voice.

5. **Nashville's industrial/rail significance.** Named as symbolic trophy only. The city was also the South's second-largest industrial center and a key rail hub — one phrase would give the Nashville beat material weight alongside the symbolic.

6. **Force sizes at the fort.** The draft gives Johnston's total line (26k split across two flanks) but not the forces actually inside the trap: ~16,000 Confederates vs. ~15–25,000 Union. One sentence on the encirclement math.

7. **Tilghman's deliberate sacrifice at Fort Henry.** Pre-battle context only; not blocking. But the "commander stays behind to buy his men time" detail is vivid and could earn one sentence in the Fort Henry passage.

8. **Bushrod Johnson as fourth command layer.** Adds depth to the command confusion section; not essential given how well the draft covers Floyd/Pillow/Buckner.

9. **European recognition blow.** Out of scope for a pure battle story but present in the factpack and worth one line in consequences if space allows.
