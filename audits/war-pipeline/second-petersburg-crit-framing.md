## VERDICT: **PASS — FAIR.** Name-the-cause gate satisfied in the section's own voice, concretely and repeatedly. No MUST-FIX. 2 SHOULD-FIX nuances.

This is, framing-wise, one of the strongest sections the pipeline could produce.
The cause is not bolted on — it IS the spine. And critically, the draft makes the
cause *physical and active*: enslaved people forced to build the wall; formerly
enslaved soldiers taking it. That is the opposite of Lost Cause evasion.

---

## FIRST CHECK — is slavery named as the cause, in the section's own prose?

**YES. Unambiguously, in the section's own voice, more than once.**

- §1 ¶3, direct and unhedged: *"The Confederacy had broken eleven states out of the
  United States to do one thing: preserve and extend slavery — the holding of roughly four
  million Black people as property."* This is the textbook-correct naming: slavery as THE
  cause, stated as the Confederacy's own purpose, not laundered through "states' rights" or
  "way of life."
- §2: the cause is made physical — the Dimmock Line "built by enslaved labor"; the USCT
  "many of them formerly enslaved" storming it; the explicit thesis *"The railroad was the
  object. Slavery was the reason. And the proof of the reason was right there on the
  parapet, in blue, on June 15."*
- §4 closing MEANWHILE reasserts it.

The §1 framing even pre-empts the Lost Cause move directly: *"a railroad town is the
*object* and not the *reason*."* This is the gate's central requirement met head-on. The
draft anticipated and named the exact evasion the gate exists to catch.

**NAME-THE-CAUSE GATE: PASS.**

## SECOND CHECK — is the USCT cause-hook accurate and non-tokenizing?

**Accurate** — see the fact gate (Dimmock Line enslaved-built: VERIFIED via EV; USCT
captured Batteries 6–11: VERIFIED via EV/ABT; Kiddoo quote exact). **Non-tokenizing** —
yes, and notably well-handled:

- The USCT are given **agency**, not decoration: they "tore open the wall," "carried a long
  stretch … at the point of the bayonet," "did their part." They are the subject of the
  active verbs, not objects of white rescue.
- The draft resists the white-savior frame and instead lands the inversion: *"the men the
  Confederacy had insisted could never be soldiers"* — putting the racist Confederate
  prediction on the page precisely so the USCT can refute it with action.
- The Kiddoo quote is framed correctly — *"the white officer corps registering, in real
  time and against its own prejudice, what the freedmen had proved."* The draft uses the
  white officer's praise as evidence of Black achievement overcoming white skepticism, NOT
  as a white officer conferring legitimacy. That is the right reading.
- Fleetwood is humanized (free Black man, Baltimore, enlisted 1863) rather than used as a
  generic symbol, and the draft is careful to keep his MoH at New Market Heights, not
  inflate Petersburg.

**One tokenization watch-point (SHOULD-FIX 1):** the §4 closing — *"The freedmen opened the
door. White hesitation let it close."* — is rhetorically powerful and substantially true,
but it flattens slightly toward a Black-valor/white-failure binary. It is defensible because
it is *factually* what happened (USCT carried the works; Smith/Hancock — both white — paused),
and the draft elsewhere gives the USCT full agency, so this is not tokenizing. But the line
should not be the section's ONLY structural statement about the USCT, and it isn't (§2 carries
the weight). Keep it; just ensure §2's substantive treatment stays primary. No change required.

## Lost Cause / soft-pedaling / euphemism sweep

- **No "states' rights" laundering.** Slavery is named as the purpose. PASS.
- **No romanticization of the Confederacy.** Beauregard's "finest hour" and the defenders'
  "fought fiercely" are tactical assessments, not moral admiration; the draft never ennobles
  the cause. The bluff is framed as a defense of "the men who had owned them." FAIR.
- **No false balance.** The draft does not "both-sides" the war's morality. The Confederate
  POV appears as military fact (Beauregard's bluff, his verdict) without moral equivalence.
- **No euphemism for slavery.** "Held as property," "enslaved," "formerly enslaved,"
  "forced to dig" — concrete, correct, non-evasive vocabulary. No "servants," no "peculiar
  institution" dodge. PASS.
- **Casualty framing is honest, not glorifying.** The 1st Maine and the "roughly three times"
  disproportion are presented as the *horror* of failed frontal assault, not as noble
  sacrifice. The "drunken general" (Ledlie) is named as incompetence, not softened.
- **Presentism check.** The draft applies a moral judgment (slavery as the cause/evil) that
  is historically grounded in the Confederacy's OWN stated purpose, not a retrofitted modern
  lens — so this is not presentism, it is accuracy. The Kiddoo "against its own prejudice"
  framing correctly locates the racism in its period, not as a modern accusation.

**SHOULD-FIX (2) — minor: the "MEANWHILE — the man holding the empty city" sidebar on
Beauregard.** It's neutral and tactical, which is correct, but pairing the phrase "his finest
hour" with no reminder of *what* he was defending could, in isolation, read as light
admiration. The section as a whole forecloses that (the very next section names the wall as
slaveholders' and the cause as slavery), so this is not a real Lost Cause leak — just keep
the cause-naming in §1–§2 ahead of any Confederate-competence beat, which the current order
already does. No change required.

---

## Summary

- **MUST-FIX:** none.
- **SHOULD-FIX (1):** keep §2's substantive USCT treatment primary so the punchy §4
  "freedmen opened the door / white hesitation closed it" line reads as summary, not as the
  whole frame. Already true in the current draft.
- **SHOULD-FIX (2):** the Beauregard "finest hour" beat is fine given section order; no edit
  needed.

**Framing gate PASSES (FAIR).** Slavery is named as the cause in the section's own
prose, the USCT hook is accurate and gives Black soldiers agency rather than
tokenizing them, and there is no Lost Cause soft-pedaling, false balance,
romanticization, euphemism, or presentism. This section is a model for the gate.
