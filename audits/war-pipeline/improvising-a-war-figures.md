## Marker 1 — line 9 (Lead: recruiting poster / raw volunteers drilling)
- **file:** `improvising-a-war-fig1-recruiting-poster.jpg`
- **caption:** An actual 1861 recruiting broadside for the "Scott Legion Regiment," one of fourteen called up from Pennsylvania, posted on the streets of Philadelphia. Notice the fine print: officers "who have served in Mexico," and a "three months enlistment." Everybody, the paperwork included, thought this would be a short war.
- **credit:** J. H. Jones & Co., Philadelphia · 1861 · Library Company of Philadelphia / Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- (1920x2977, tall poster)

## Marker 2 — line 63 (Anaconda beat → Winfield Scott portrait)
- **file:** `improvising-a-war-fig2-winfield-scott.jpg`
- **caption:** General-in-Chief Winfield Scott, the old hero of 1812 and Mexico, photographed in his uniform around the start of the war. By 1861 he was seventy-four, heavy, and could barely sit a horse, and the country laughed at his slow strangle-the-South plan. He turned out to be right.
- **credit:** Mathew Brady studio, New York · c. 1861 · Library of Congress / Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- (1920x3123, carte-de-visite; has a printed studio mount/border — crop if desired)

## Marker 3 — line 99 (1861 US map: free / Confederate / border states)
- **file:** `improvising-a-war-fig3-secession-map.jpg`
- **caption:** The country splits, 1860 to 1861. Blue is the free states; the hatched and shaded states are the slaveholding ones that stayed loyal (Kentucky, Maryland, Missouri, Delaware), with the dates each seceding state left the Union. The four border states sit right in the gap, which is exactly why Lincoln guarded them so carefully.
- **credit:** Charles O. Paullin (Atlas of the Historical Geography of the United States) · 1932 · Carnegie Institution / Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- (1789x1201, landscape; legend distinctly marks free states, loyal slave states, loyal slave states of divided allegiance, and the Confederate boundary)

## Marker 4 — line 125 (First Bull Run: the rout)
- **file:** `improvising-a-war-fig4-bull-run-rout.jpg`
- **caption:** "The Stampede from Bull Run," drawn on the spot by a special artist for the illustrated press. Soldiers, wagons, and the sightseers who had ridden out to watch all tangle together in a panicked scramble back toward Washington. This is the afternoon the dream of a short war died.
- **credit:** Frank Vizetelly · 1861 · New York Public Library / Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- (760x567 — the canonical period rout engraving; modest resolution but the best on-brief panic-on-the-road image)

## Marker 5a — line 143 (paired portraits: Abraham Lincoln)
- **file:** `improvising-a-war-fig5a-lincoln.jpg`
- **caption:** Abraham Lincoln in 1861, the self-taught prairie lawyer whose entire military record was a mosquito-bitten stint in the Black Hawk War. He would teach himself how to run a war out loud, and become the better commander-in-chief of the two.
- **credit:** Mathew Brady · 1861 · Library of Congress / Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- (1920x2453, portrait)

## Marker 5b — line 143 (paired portraits: Jefferson Davis)
- **file:** `improvising-a-war-fig5b-davis.jpg`
- **caption:** Jefferson Davis around 1861, the new Confederate president. On paper he was built for the job (West Point, a Mexican War wound, a turn as U.S. Secretary of War) and would have rather been a general. He raised an army for a republic founded to keep four million people enslaved.
- **credit:** Unknown photographer · c. 1861 · Library of Congress / Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- (1789x2720, carte-de-visite labeled "JEFFERSON DAVIS"; has a printed CDV mount/border — crop if desired)

## Hub hero — `/how-the-war-was-fought`
- **file:** `how-the-war-was-fought-hero.jpg`
- **caption:** Union officers on a ridge watch a battle unfold in the valley below, horses and signal flags around them and gunsmoke smudging the far hills. A grand chromolithograph print of the kind that taught a postwar generation what the fighting looked like.
- **credit:** Thure de Thulstrup (chromolithograph by L. Prang & Co., "Battle of Chattanooga") · 1880 · Library of Congress / Wikimedia Commons · public domain
- (1920x1410, landscape; distinct from the ACW home hero and the Anaconda map. Has a printed Prang mat/border — crop the mat for the hero band.)
