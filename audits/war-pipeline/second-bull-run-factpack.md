## 1. Dates / Location / Theatre

- **Dates:** August 28–30, 1862 (three days). [WP][ABT][NPS]
  - NOTE: NPS frames the whole *campaign* as Aug 25–30 (Jackson's flank march begins Aug 25; Manassas Junction seized Aug 26–27). Britannica narrows the *main battle* to Aug 29–30. - **Theatre:** Eastern Theatre. Fought on / overlapping the **First Bull Run (First Manassas) battlefield of July 1861** — same ground, second time. [WP]
- **Result:** Confederate victory. [WP][ABT][NPS][BRIT]

## 4. Geography (relative positions for map accuracy)

The battle runs along the **Warrenton Turnpike**, the main east–west road; Confederates (South) hold the NORTH side along an unfinished railroad, Union (North) attacks from the SOUTH/east; the action shifts EAST toward Bull Run and the Stone Bridge as the Union retreats. Cardinal anchors below. [WP][ABT][NPS]

- **Warrenton Turnpike** — primary east–west axis of the whole battle; connects Gainesville (west) → Groveton → Stone Bridge → Centreville (east). [WP][NPS]
- **Manassas Junction** — major rail junction ~6–7 mi SOUTH of the battlefield; site of the **huge Union supply depot** Jackson captured/burned Aug 26–27. The campaign's trigger. [WP][ABT][NPS]
- **Bristoe Station** — on the Orange & Alexandria RR, a few miles SOUTHWEST of Manassas Junction; Jackson's wing struck the railroad here Aug 26 en route to the Junction. [WP]
- **Thoroughfare Gap** — pass through Bull Run Mountains to the WEST; Jackson marched through it (Aug 25) and Longstreet came through it Aug 29 to reach the field. [WP]
- **The unfinished railroad grade / "railroad cut"** — an abandoned, never-finished rail line running roughly NE–SW just NORTH of and parallel to the Warrenton Turnpike, below **Stony Ridge**. Jackson's men used its cuts and fills as ready-made earthworks; this is the wall the Union threw itself against Aug 29–30. [WP][NPS]
- **Stony Ridge** — wooded high ground NORTH of the railroad grade; concealed Jackson's line and gave observation over the turnpike. [WP]
- **Brawner's Farm (a.k.a. Brawner Farm / Groveton)** — open farm fields just NORTH of the Warrenton Turnpike near **Groveton**; site of the Aug-28 opening clash (Gibbon's brigade vs. Jackson). [WP][ABT][NPS]
- **Groveton** — hamlet on the Warrenton Turnpike, CENTER of the battlefield, near Brawner's Farm. [WP][NPS]
- **Dogan Ridge** — Union artillery position; its guns repeatedly broke Confederate counterattacks Aug 29. [WP]
- **Chinn Ridge** — high ground SOUTH of the turnpike; the Union (North) rearguard stand here Aug 30 bought time and prevented encirclement during Longstreet's assault. [WP][ABT]
- **Henry House Hill (Henry Hill)** — dominant high ground EAST end of the field, commanding the Union line of retreat; the **same hill where "Stonewall" Jackson earned his nickname at First Manassas (1861)**; Union forces held it Aug 30 to cover the withdrawal. [WP][ABT]
- **Stone Bridge** — carries the Warrenton Turnpike over **Bull Run** at the EAST edge of the field; the Union retreat funneled across it (and nearby fords) toward Centreville. [WP]
- **Bull Run** — the creek (runs roughly N–S/NW–SE) east of the battlefield; the Union army withdrew across it after dark Aug 30. [WP][ABT]
- **Centreville** — town a few miles EAST/NE across Bull Run; Pope's army fell back here, then on toward the Washington defenses. [WP][ABT]
- **Washington, D.C.** — ~26 mi NE; the army's ultimate refuge after the rout. [WP]

**Rough map layout (W → E along the turnpike):** Thoroughfare Gap / Gainesville (W) → Brawner's Farm–Groveton & the unfinished railroad/Stony Ridge (N of road, CENTER) → Dogan Ridge / Chinn Ridge (S of road) → Henry House Hill → Stone Bridge over Bull Run (E) → Centreville → Washington (NE). Manassas Junction & Bristoe Station sit several miles SOUTH/SW of this line.

## 5. Sequence of Events

### Strategic setup (the "why we're here")
- After McClellan's (North) Peninsula Campaign collapsed in the **Seven Days Battles (late June 1862)**, the threat to Richmond eased. Lincoln (North) created a **new Army of Virginia** and gave it to **Pope (North)**, tasked with protecting Washington and the Shenandoah Valley and drawing Confederate strength away from McClellan. [WP][ABT]
- Lee (South), judging McClellan no longer an immediate threat, shifted forces north to "destroy Pope before returning his attention to McClellan." [WP]
- Aug 22–25: armies skirmished along the **Rappahannock River**. [WP]

### Jackson's flank march & the supply depot (Aug 25–27)
- **Aug 25:** Lee sent **Jackson (South)** on a wide flanking march (~50–55 miles, per NPS/ABT) around Pope's RIGHT via **Thoroughfare Gap**. [WP][ABT][NPS]
- **Aug 26:** Jackson's wing struck the **Orange & Alexandria Railroad at Bristoe Station**, severing Pope's rail line. [WP]
- **Aug 27 (before dawn):** Jackson **captured the vast Union supply depot at Manassas Junction**, in Pope's rear. After "a day of wild feasting," his hungry veterans **burned what they couldn't carry**. This forced Pope to abandon his Rappahannock line and turn back to find Jackson. [WP][NPS]
- Night Aug 27–28: Jackson pulled back to a concealed defensive line behind the **unfinished railroad grade below Stony Ridge**, on the old First Manassas ground. [WP]

### Day 1 — Aug 28: Brawner's Farm / Groveton
- Evening (~6:30 p.m.): Jackson (South) attacked a passing Union column (**Rufus King's division (North)**) on the Warrenton Turnpike to fix Pope's attention. The clash centered on **John Gibbon's brigade (North)** at **Brawner's Farm**. [WP][NPS]
- A stand-up, close-range firefight (~2+ hours) — roughly **6,200 Confederates vs. ~2,100 Union** — ended a bloody stalemate at dark. "One of every three men engaged was shot." Jackson achieved his aim: Pope now fixated on him. [WP]

### Day 2 — Aug 29: assaults on the railroad cut
- Pope (North), wrongly believing Jackson was *retreating*, concentrated to "bag" him. Jackson actually held ~20,000 men in a ~3,000-yard line along the railroad grade. [WP]
- From ~7 a.m., **Sigel (North)** then successive Union attacks (Grover, Kearny, others) hammered the line in **uncoordinated, piecemeal assaults**; the line bent but held. [WP][NPS]
- **~Noon: Longstreet (South) arrived through Thoroughfare Gap** and quietly deployed on **Jackson's RIGHT**, extending the Confederate line SOUTH across the turnpike. **Pope did not realize Longstreet was on the field** — the fatal misconception. [WP][NPS]
- **Porter (North)** controversy seeds here: Pope's orders expected Porter/McDowell to hit Jackson's right flank, but Longstreet's hidden corps was now in the way (see §8). [WP]

### Day 3 — Aug 30: Longstreet's flank attack & the Union rout
- Pope (North), still convinced the enemy was withdrawing (Hood had pulled back overnight), ordered a **pursuit/attack** — sending **Porter's (North)** men forward against Jackson's unbroken line, where they were shattered. [WP][NPS][BRIT]
- **~4:00 p.m.: Longstreet (South) launched the largest simultaneous mass assault of the war** — ~25,000–28,000 men in five divisions, ~1.5 miles wide — into the exposed Union LEFT. **Hood's division (South)** spearheaded it south of the turnpike. [WP][BRIT]
- The 5th New York Zouaves (North) were annihilated (~300 casualties/10 min). [WP]
- **McDowell's (North)** shift of **Reynolds's division (North)** off **Chinn Ridge** left ~2,200 Union troops facing "ten times their number" — WP calls it possibly "the worst tactical decision of the day." [WP]
- **Union rearguard stands on Chinn Ridge then Henry House Hill (North)** bought just enough time to prevent total encirclement. [WP][ABT]
- **After dark, Pope's army retreated EAST across Bull Run (Stone Bridge/fords) to Centreville, then toward the Washington defenses.** Unlike First Manassas, an organized rearguard kept it from becoming a total rout. [WP][ABT][NPS]
- (Coda — *not* part of the three days: a rearguard fight at **Chantilly/Ox Hill, Sept 1**, in a thunderstorm, killed Union generals **Kearny** and **Stevens** as Lee tried to cut off the retreat. Mention only as transition if needed. [VERIFY date Sept 1 if used.])

### Turning points (for the author)
1. Jackson's capture/burning of **Manassas Junction** — forced the whole battle by yanking Pope off the Rappahannock.
2. **Longstreet's hidden arrival (Aug 29 noon)** + Pope's belief the enemy was retreating — set the trap.
3. **Longstreet's 4 p.m. Aug-30 mass attack** — the hammer that broke the Union left and won the battle.

## 6. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (SHIP-BLOCKING — section must name slavery in its own prose)

The framing gate auto-fails if the word **slavery** never appears in the section's prose (omission = Lost Cause). Factual hooks the author MUST use in the section's OWN voice:

- **What the whole war was for.** The Confederacy (South) was fighting to preserve and extend **slavery**; the United States (North) was fighting to preserve the Union — and by the summer of 1862 the war was visibly turning into a war to **end slavery**. Second Bull Run sits exactly at that hinge. [CWM][HIST][BRIT]
- **The Emancipation Proclamation was already written and waiting.** By late summer 1862, **Lincoln (North) had drafted the Emancipation Proclamation but was holding it for a Union battlefield victory** — issuing it after defeats "would seem to smack of desperation." A Union defeat at Second Bull Run (Aug 30) is precisely the kind of disaster that kept emancipation locked in Lincoln's desk drawer. [CWM][HIST]
- **What a Confederate victory threatened.** The win at Second Bull Run emboldened Lee (South) to launch his **first invasion of the North** (the Maryland Campaign). A successful invasion could have won foreign recognition for the Confederacy and frozen emancipation — i.e., **a Confederate win here was, concretely, a threat to the survival of a slaveholding republic on the continent.** [HIST][CWM][BRIT]
- **The road to emancipation runs straight out of this defeat.** The Confederate victory → Lee crosses the Potomac → **Antietam, Sept 17, 1862** (the bloodiest single day in U.S. history) → that Union strategic success finally gave Lincoln his victory → **Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation, Sept 22, 1862**, redefining the war as a war against slavery. **Second Bull Run is the defeat that set up the battle that freed the slaves.** [CWM][HIST][BRIT]

**Author note:** The cleanest in-prose move is to state plainly that this Confederate victory propelled the invasion that led to Antietam, which gave Lincoln the moment to strike at **slavery** itself via the Emancipation Proclamation — and that slavery was the war's root cause throughout. Name slavery directly; do not euphemize ("states' rights," "the cause," etc.).

## 8. The Fitz John Porter affair (cast/aftermath detail — use carefully)

- **Maj. Gen. Fitz John Porter (North)** was made the scapegoat for the defeat. Pope (North) blamed his corps commanders broadly; **McDowell (North)** — himself facing a court of inquiry — helped pin the loss on Porter. [Emerging Civil War; WP "Court-martial of Fitz John Porter"]
- Porter was **arrested/court-martialed (proceedings from Nov 25, 1862), found guilty Jan 10, 1863** of disobedience of orders and misconduct, and **dismissed from the Army Jan 21, 1863.** [WP court-martial]
- **The conviction was later found unjust and overturned; Porter was eventually reinstated.** Modern scholarship largely views him as wrongly blamed — Longstreet's hidden corps stood where Porter was ordered to attack. [WP][BRIT]

## 10. Significance / What It Changed

- **Confederate (South) victory** drove the Union army out of eastern Virginia and back to the Washington defenses. [WP][ABT][BRIT]
- **It opened Lee's (South) first invasion of the North** — directly enabling the **Maryland Campaign** that climaxed at **Antietam (Sept 17, 1862)**. NPS: "Lee's bold and brilliant Second Manassas campaign opened the way for the south's first invasion of the north." [NPS][ABT][BRIT]
- **It cemented Lee's reputation** as a tactical commander at the height of his ascendancy. [HIST]
- **It is the defeat that set the stage for emancipation:** the Union loss kept Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation in the drawer; the invasion it triggered led to Antietam; Antietam gave Lincoln his victory and the Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation followed (Sept 22, 1862). [CWM][HIST][BRIT]
- **Historiographical note:** long overshadowed by Antietam/emancipation; reassessed as a major battle after **John Hennessy's 1992 study** *Return to Bull Run*. (Useful for a "why you've maybe never heard of it" beat.) [HIST]
