## ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX

### S1 — Self-emancipation quote is misattributed to the NPS (§emancipation-made-real)
The draft says: *The National Park Service … stresses that the enslaved … "more often already had taken steps to secure their own emancipation."*
- The QUOTE is **verbatim-exact and real** — but its source is the **Mississippi Encyclopedia** entry on **"Emancipation"** ("…those men and women **more often already had taken steps to secure their own emancipation**"), NOT the NPS. I checked the two NPS pages the fact pack credits (`african-americans-and-the-campaign.htm` and `july-4-1863-slavery-ends-in-vicksburg.htm`); neither contains the phrase.
- **Fix:** reattribute to the Mississippi Encyclopedia (or drop the named attribution and keep the quote with a generic "historians stress"). Source: https://mississippiencyclopedia.org/entries/emancipation/
- (Fact-pack §6 itself mis-credits this to "NPS 'African Americans and the campaign for Vicksburg'" — the error originates in the pack.)

### S2 — "~70,000 freedmen from the Mississippi Valley alone" is not cleanly sourced as worded (§emancipation-made-real)
The draft: *"from the Mississippi Valley alone something like 70,000 formerly enslaved men joined the United States forces."*
- I could not confirm a clean "70,000 from the Mississippi Valley" figure in the cited NPS/Miss. Encyclopedia sources. What IS attested: Lorenzo Thomas's Mississippi-Valley recruiting yielded **"as many as seventy Black regiments"** by war's end (≈70,000 at full strength, so the number is *plausible* but is a regiment-count extrapolation, not a sourced headcount); one source gives **"more than 17,000 enslaved Black Mississippians"** specifically. The NPS African-Americans page gives the national figure "**over 178,000** free men and former slaves … nearly 175 regiments."
- **Fix:** soften ("tens of thousands," or "as many as seventy regiments — on the order of 70,000 men"), or recite the firmer "~70 regiments" framing. Keep the existing "something like" hedge at minimum. Not ship-blocking, but the bare "70,000 from the Mississippi Valley alone" overstates the cleanliness of the sourcing.

### S3 — Loughborough "trapped … while visiting" / age 26 (§the-siege)
- The quote *"I shall never forget my extreme fear during the night, and my utter hopelessness of ever seeing the morning light"* is **VERBATIM-exact** (My Cave Life in Vicksburg, Ch. X). ✅
- But she was not merely a visitor caught out: she came to Vicksburg **with her husband, Major James M. Loughborough, a Confederate officer** (arrived April 15, 1863), and their young child. "Trapped in the city while visiting" is loose. Age **26** is consistent with a c.1836 birth (women's-history sources) but the memoir itself states no age. **Fix:** reword "visiting" to "had come to the city with her Confederate-officer husband," and either source the age or drop the precise "twenty-six."
