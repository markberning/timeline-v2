# Fact-Checker Gate — Glorieta Pass
## Independent verification against Wikipedia (Battle of Glorieta Pass), American Battlefield Trust, NPS Pecos/Shattered Dream, TSHAonline, New Mexico campaign Wikipedia, William Read Scurry Wikipedia, Sand Creek Massacre Wikipedia, NPS Chivington biography, Jornada del Muerto Wikipedia

---

## SPECIFICALLY TASKED CLAIMS (from gate brief)

### 1. Wagon count given as "60–80" ✅ CONFIRMED (with note)
The draft writes "roughly 60 to 80 wagons of it (the sources split on the exact count, with figures running from 60 to 73 to 80)" and cites Slough's own dispatch at "about 60 wagons." This is an accurate, honest handling of a genuinely contested figure. Slough dispatch (NPS Shattered Dream) = ~60; Wikipedia = 80; TSHAonline = 80; civilwarmonths.com = 73; NPS Shattered Dream teaching guide itself says "60–80." No single number is asserted. **PASS for this specific item.**

### 2. Raid credited to Chivington; Lewis-led claim noted as unverified, not asserted ✅ CONFIRMED
The draft credits Chivington with command per the mainstream record, then identifies the Rio Abajo Press account (1864) as unverified and not echoed by other sources. Wikipedia, NPS, ABT, and TSHAonline all credit Chivington with commanding the flanking force. The newspaper's claim is real (Wikipedia does note it) but the draft's treatment — mainstream-first, dispute flagged but not elevated — is correct. **PASS.**

### 3. Sibley simply absent; intoxication NOT asserted ✅ CONFIRMED
The draft states only "who had not even been present on the battlefield at Glorieta." ABT confirms "Sibley remained in Albuquerque." All sources agree he was not on the field; intoxication is disputed in primary sources and the draft correctly omits it. **PASS.**

### 4. Valverde + Santa Fe sequence ✅ CONFIRMED (dates correct)
- Valverde: February 21, 1862 ✅ (confirmed NPS, Wikipedia, New Mexico campaign Wikipedia)
- Albuquerque occupied March 2, 1862 ✅ (confirmed New Mexico campaign Wikipedia, ABT)
- Santa Fe occupied March 13, 1862 ✅ (confirmed New Mexico campaign Wikipedia, multiple searches; one Wikipedia Battle of Glorieta Pass article fetch returned "March 10" but this appears to be an artifact of the fetch/summary — the New Mexico Campaign article and the search synthesis both confirm March 13 as the consensus date for the Confederate *occupation* of Santa Fe)

### 5. "Gettysburg of the West" as a common nickname ✅ CONFIRMED (with important nuance)
The phrase is widely used and the CWSAC Priority I (Class A) rating placing it on par with Gettysburg/Antietam is confirmed. HOWEVER: the Wikipedia article on the battle explicitly notes that historians have cautioned the phrase "vastly inflates its importance and serves the novelist better than the historian," and more than one Civil War battle carries that nickname (Chickamauga, Westport). The draft writes "people would later call Glorieta Pass 'the Gettysburg of the West,' and the phrase, however worn it has gotten, points at something real" — this hedged phrasing is acceptable and accurate. **PASS** (the hedge is well-calibrated; no over-claim).

### 6. Chaves-guided mesa crossing ✅ CONFIRMED
Lt. Col. Manuel Chaves, 2nd New Mexico Mounted Volunteers, guided Chivington's flanking force over Glorieta Mesa to a vantage point above Johnson's Ranch. Wikipedia (Chaves article), NPS Shattered Dream, and ABT all confirm this. The draft's description of him as "a Hispanic New Mexican frontiersman" and "chosen by Canby and Chivington precisely because he knew this terrain" is confirmed. **PASS.**

### 7. Starving retreat ✅ CONFIRMED
The draft describes the retreat through the Jornada del Muerto as a "march through misery" with hungry soldiers stripping food from civilians; ~500 dead of fighting, starvation, or disease. New Mexico campaign Wikipedia confirms ~1,700 reached El Paso by May 4 (then continued to San Antonio), with significant losses. The draft's "only about 1,500 made it back to San Antonio by that summer" is consistent with the source range (1,500–1,700 at different checkpoints). The Jornada del Muerto is confirmed as the route. **PASS** (minor note below on Jornada distance).

### 8. Chivington → Sand Creek forward reference ✅ CONFIRMED AND ACCURATE
- Date: November 1864 ✅ (Sand Creek Wikipedia: November 29, 1864)
- Rank: "colonel commanding in Colorado" ✅ (he was Colonel commanding the Colorado Military District; he commanded the 3rd Colorado Cavalry at Sand Creek as a full colonel)
- Victims: "peaceful Cheyenne and Arapaho encampment" ✅ (Black Kettle's camp, which had complied with a peace agreement and relocated under military direction)
- "killed scores of people, most of them women and children" ✅ — "scores" is accurate and appropriately hedged given the wide source range (70+ to 150+); ~two-thirds were women and children per multiple sources
- "one of the worst perpetrators of anti-Native violence in the West" — the Joint Committee on the Conduct of War called it "a foul and dastardly massacre"; this characterization is defensible and not overstated
- **PASS. The forward reference is accurate and appropriately calibrated.**

### Confederate Territory of Arizona Proclamation

**"On January 18, 1862, Sibley proclaimed a Confederate Territory of Arizona out of the southern half of New Mexico Territory"** ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED AS WRITTEN — POTENTIALLY MISLEADING
January 18, 1862 is the date the **Confederate Congress** passed the Organic Act formally organizing the Confederate Territory of Arizona — it is not a Sibley proclamation. The original proclamation of a Confederate Arizona was made by **Col. John R. Baylor** on **August 1, 1861**, after the Battle of Mesilla. Sibley issued a separate proclamation on **December 20, 1861** to the people of New Mexico claiming the territory for the Confederacy, but this was not the founding act. January 18, 1862 was a Congressional, not a field commander's, action.

**SHOULD-FIX: The sentence attributes a Congressional act to Sibley personally on that date. Revise to clarify: "On January 18, 1862, the Confederate Congress formally organized the Confederate Territory of Arizona out of the southern half of New Mexico Territory" — or simplify to "The Confederacy had already carved a Confederate Territory of Arizona out of the southern half of New Mexico Territory" without attributing it to Sibley specifically on that specific date.**

### Pike's Peakers March

**"300 to 400 miles (480 to 640 km)"** ✅ CONFIRMED AS A RANGE (Essential Civil War Curriculum: "300 miles"; Wikipedia says "400 miles (600 km) in 14 days"; the range covers the source spread)

**"Roughly 67 miles in about 24 hours"** ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED — COMPETING FIGURE
The fact pack flags this as well-attested at 67 miles in 24 hours (citing Essential Civil War Curriculum, ABT, NPS Shattered Dream). However, one source (spiralspecs.com regimental history) gives "92 miles in 36 hours" for the forced-march leg. The 67/24-hour figure appears in the NPS and Essential Civil War Curriculum teaching materials and is widely used. The competing figure (92 miles/36 hours) may refer to a different segment or a different calculation. The draft hedges this correctly ("roughly 67 miles in about 24 hours") and the 67/24 figure is present in NPS materials. **This is a low-severity flag** — the draft's figure matches authoritative teaching materials but a competing figure exists. Not a must-fix since the draft's source is NPS, but worth noting.

### Retreat Details

**"Jornada del Muerto...more than 100 miles (160 km) of desert with no reliable water"** ⚠️ SLIGHT OVERSTATEMENT ON DISTANCE
Jornada del Muerto Wikipedia gives the waterless trail as approximately **90 miles (140 km)**, not "more than 100 miles." The basin extends 160 km north-to-south but the historic *trail* is 90 miles. One search result confirms "twelve days to cross the 100 miles of Jornada del Muerto" (Legends of America), so "more than 100 miles" is in use as a popular figure. The discrepancy between 90 and 100+ miles is small and within the range of how different sources measure it. **SHOULD-FIX: change to "roughly 90 to 100 miles (140–160 km)" to straddle the source range and avoid overclaiming.**

**"~2,500 marched up; ~1,500 made it back to San Antonio by that summer; ~500 killed/died/disease, ~500 captured; ~40% losses"** ✅ CONFIRMED (Essential Civil War Curriculum, Legends of America; one source gives 1,700 reached El Paso by May 4 before continuing to San Antonio, so 1,500 reaching San Antonio by summer is consistent)

## SUMMARY

### MUST-FIX (ship-blocking)

1. **"Lt. Col. Harold Raguet"** — ❌ Both the first name and rank are wrong. His name is **Henry Raguet** (full name Henry Wynkoop Raguet; confirmed TSHAonline biographical entry, Wikipedia). His rank is **Major**, not Lt. Colonel (Wikipedia Glorieta Pass article, TSHAonline). Fix: "Maj. Henry Raguet." This error is in the draft twice (narrative at §the-battle, and in the Fact Ledger).

2. **"Sibley proclaimed a Confederate Territory of Arizona"** — ❌ January 18, 1862 was when the **Confederate Congress** formally organized the territory (Organic Act), not a Sibley field proclamation. Baylor proclaimed it August 1, 1861; Sibley issued his own separate proclamation December 20, 1861; the Congress acted January 18, 1862. The draft as written makes Sibley the active agent of a Congressional act on that specific date. Fix: attribute to the Confederate Congress or rewrite to decouple Sibley from this specific date (e.g., "By early 1862, the Confederate Congress had formally organized a Confederate Territory of Arizona out of the southern half of New Mexico").

### SHOULD-FIX (non-blocking but accuracy improvements)

3. **Jornada del Muerto distance "more than 100 miles"** — The historic trail is ~90 miles per Wikipedia; "more than 100 miles" is used by some popular sources (Legends of America) but is slightly inflated vs. the primary geographic source. Revise to "roughly 90 to 100 miles" or simply "roughly 100 miles."

4. **"67 miles in about 24 hours"** — This NPS-sourced figure is defensible, but one regimental history gives 92 miles/36 hours. Consider adding "by one account" or keeping the NPS cite in mind if contested during review. Low severity; current hedge ("roughly...about") is appropriate.
