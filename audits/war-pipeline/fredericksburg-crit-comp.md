## MUST-ADD (genuinely essential — these block)

**MUST-ADD 1 — Name that the slaughter at the wall was a SECONDARY/diversionary attack
gone wrong, not the intended main blow.** The draft (§3) presents Marye's Heights and
the south front as two co-equal simultaneous battles, and tells the Franklin-held-back
story well — but it never tells the reader the structural irony that defines the day:
Burnside's *plan* made Franklin's southern wing (~60,000) the **main effort** and the
Marye's assault the **supporting/diversionary** one. His late, garbled orders (delivered
~6 a.m., near-identical "seize the heights" wording to both wings) gutted the 60,000-man
main attack down to Meade's single ~4,500-man division while feeding division after
division into the wall that was only ever meant to *pin* Longstreet. The reader currently
can't understand WHY Franklin committed so little (it reads as pure timidity) or WHY
Burnside kept throwing brigades at an impossible wall (it reads as pure stupidity); the
plan-vs-execution inversion is the missing connective tissue that makes both failures
intelligible and is squarely Burnside's responsibility. This is on the ABT "True Battle
for Fredericksburg" / "After the Crossing" pages and is standard in every serious account.
A few sentences in §1 (plan) and/or §3 (the south front) close it. **This is the one
real comprehensiveness gap.**

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (strengthens; not blocking)

**SHOULD-CONSIDER 1 — Make the emancipation throughline LOCAL, not just calendrical.**
The cause-naming floor is met, but the hook leans entirely on the Jan 1 date. The
strongest available fact is that Fredericksburg was *itself* one of the first
large-scale sites of self-emancipation: enslaved people crossing to Union lines as
"contraband" (legally seized property, not yet free), with John Washington as the
documented witness (NPS "Freedom's Eve," Discover Stafford "Trail to Freedom,"
ECW case study — all confirm). One verified sentence (Washington's dating/details
re-checked first, per pack §10) would turn an abstract calendar note into a concrete
local human throughline. Strongly recommended; the sourcing is now confirmed.

**SHOULD-CONSIDER 2 — A line on the political earthquake the defeat caused.** The
Dec 1862 cabinet crisis (Seward/Chase, Lincoln holding the cabinet together), the
~27% desertion figure, and Copperheads weaponizing "butchery" would deepen the
"lowest point of Northern morale" claim §5 already makes — turning an assertion into
something the reader feels the weight of. A sentence or two in §5. Optional.

**SHOULD-CONSIDER 3 — One clause on Longstreet's STRENGTH (numbers behind the wall).**
The draft conveys the wall was lethal but never gives the reader the lopsidedness of
the local match-up at the wall itself (Confederates ~ a few thousand sheltered infantry
firing on tens of thousands sent across open ground). The §5 6–8k vs ~1,200 line does
this for casualties; a force-ratio clause at §3 would let the impossibility land in the
moment rather than only in the box score. Minor.

## FACT-PACK BLIND SPOTS (gaps in the source pack itself, for the checker/owner)

1. **The diversion-vs-main-attack plan design + the garbled 6 a.m. orders.** The pack
   describes Sumner/Hooker driving Marye's and Franklin attacking Jackson, but never
   states that Marye's was the *secondary* effort and the south the *main* one, nor
   the order-confusion that inverted it. This is why the draft couldn't include it.
   The single most important thing to add to the pack. (ABT "True Battle…"; "After the
   Crossing"; Encyclopedia Virginia.)
2. **Henry Halleck's role in the pontoon delay** is unnamed in the pack ("bureau tangle"
   only). Web sources (Wikipedia, History.com) put the miscommunication squarely between
   Burnside and Halleck. Naming Halleck isn't essential but is the documented specific.
3. **The post-battle political crisis** (cabinet crisis, desertion rate, Copperheads) is
   absent from the pack entirely — a real omission for the "significance/aftermath" beat.
   (Library of America "Lincoln's Cabinet crisis"; Britannica; Encyclopedia Virginia.)
4. **The local self-emancipation throughline is under-developed in the pack** relative to
   how well it's sourced. Pack §6 hedges John Washington hard; my web check shows NPS +
   Discover Stafford + ECW give firm dating ("first of over 10,000… spring/summer 1862,"
   worked for Gen. Rufus King, wife Annie, Shiloh Baptist). The checker can likely lift
   the [VERIFY] and let the draft name him.
5. **Barksdale's delay bought Lee a concrete ~12 extra hours** (per ABT) — the pack
   frames the riverfront fight qualitatively; the quantified payoff is available and
   sharper. Minor.

## BOTTOM LINE
The draft is comprehensive, correctly structured around the two fronts, honest about the
human cost, properly hedges the contested legends (Kirkland, the Lee "war is terrible"
quote), and meets the slavery cause-naming floor. **One essential gap: it never tells the
reader Marye's Heights was a secondary/diversionary attack that Burnside's botched orders
turned into the main slaughter — the connective tissue that makes both Franklin's and
Burnside's failures intelligible and pins responsibility. Add that (MUST-ADD 1) and the
section passes the comprehensiveness gate.** Everything else is enhancement.
