### The Hudson Held at Lake George

**❌ MUST-FIX: Theyanoguin and the 1710 London visit**  
Draft: "He had sailed to London in 1710 as one of the 'Four Mohawk Kings' presented to Queen Anne."  
**This is wrong.** Modern scholarship has definitively established that the "Hendrick" who traveled to London in 1710 was Hendrick **Tejonihokarawa** (Wolf Clan, c. 1660–c. 1735) — a different person from Hendrick **Theyanoguin** (Bear Clan, c. 1691–1755, killed at Lake George). The two were long conflated by historians but are now understood to be separate individuals: their clan affiliations differ (Wolf vs. Bear), their portrait features differ, and the math doesn't work — if Theyanoguin had gone to London in 1710 he would have been approximately 95 years old when killed at Lake George, which no source claims. The History Cooperative's peer-reviewed article "Searching for Hendrick: Correction of a Historic Conflation" addresses this directly; so does the New York History Blog's "The Two Hendricks."  
Wikipedia's Hendrick Theyanoguin article makes no mention of a 1710 visit; the Four Mohawk Kings article identifies Tejonihokarawa, not Theyanoguin.  
**Required fix:** Remove or rewrite the 1710 London claim. Theyanoguin's biography does not include the Queen Anne visit. His significance should rest on his actual documented role: paramount Mohawk war chief, instrumental in maintaining the Six Nations' British alignment through his diplomatic relationship with Johnson and the Albany Congress (1754). The line "Had sailed to London in 1710…" must be cut.  
_Sources: Wikipedia/Four Mohawk Kings; Wikipedia/Hendrick Theyanoguin; Wikipedia/Hendrick Tejonihokarawa; History Cooperative "Searching for Hendrick"; New York History Blog "The Two Hendricks."_

### The War Goes Global, and Oswego Falls

**❌ MUST-FIX: Mercer killed August 13**  
Draft: "Mercer was killed on August 13 when a cannonball struck him while he inspected the walls."  
**This is wrong.** Multiple sources confirm Mercer was killed on **August 14**, when French artillery opened its final bombardment on Fort Oswego's walls. Littlehales surrendered on August 14 following Mercer's death, consistent with all sources.  
**Required fix:** Change "August 13" to "August 14."

**⚠️ SHOULD-FIX: "1,500" figure origin — imprecise attribution to Cooper**

The Francis Parkman mention later in the same sentence is accurate.  
**Fix:** Change to "The famous figure of 1,500 entered the record before Cooper — Jonathan Carver's popular 1778 account put it there first — but Cooper's 1826 novel *The Last of the Mohicans* and later historians like Francis Parkman burned it into the popular imagination through a lens of nineteenth-century racial hierarchy." Or simply remove the Cooper-as-origin implication and note the figure pre-dates him.  
_Sources: Fort William Henry Wikipedia; Penn Museum "The Massacre at Fort William Henry"; Fort William Henry Massacre Encyclopedia.com._
