# Storytelling Critic Report — "Regulars & Provincials" (French and Indian War, theme section)

**Draft:** `audits/war-pipeline/fi-regulars-provincials-draft.md`
**Fact pack:** `audits/war-pipeline/fi-regulars-provincials-factpack.md`
**Critic role:** STORYTELLING (hook & stakes, human dimension, pacing, voice, list-of-facts tedium) + house-voice enforcement.
**Verdict:** FAIL

---

## Summary

As a piece of storytelling this is genuinely strong work. It has a real thesis ("they were arguing about what a man was"), a beating human heart (Washington's pride, Clough's diary), a controlled build from definitions to the Louisbourg episode to the Revolution payoff, and it refuses to let the Native nations become scenery. On *story* grounds most headings are STRONG or GOOD.

It fails the gate on **house voice**, not on story. The locked rule "no meta-narrator" is broken repeatedly and structurally (it is the draft's main rhetorical engine), and Braddock is introduced without his full rank on first mention (full-rank-first). Those are ship-blocking under the gate's terms. Everything else is fixable with light touches.

No em-dashes in prose (the `—` only appears as quote-attribution separators, which is the established quote format, and on the eyebrow/cross-ref lines, not as sentence punctuation). No bold in body prose. Cross-refs are clean standalone pill lines. Those three rules pass.

## Per-heading grades

### Opening (untitled lede + the three definitions) — GOOD (story) / house-voice FAIL
The hook is good: "two armies took the field, and they could not stand each other... They wore the same king's cause on their coats and despised almost everything about how the other man soldiered." Strong, concrete, sets the central tension fast. The three-definition block (regulars / provincials / militia) is necessary load-bearing exposition and is handled about as gracefully as a glossary can be, ending on the line that earns its keep: "That word, contract, is the whole story."

The problem is the meta-narrator. These lines talk *about the telling* instead of telling:
- "is one of the quieter engines of everything that came after." (narrator editorializing about significance)
- "Start with the names, because the reader needs three of them and a war owns three armies, not two." (narrator instructing the reader / narrating its own structure)

**Rewrite notes:**
- Cut or rewrite "Start with the names, because the reader needs three of them..." Just give the names. Try: "Three armies took the field on the British side, not two. Their names are the first thing to get straight." (still slightly meta — better: open the definition run with the first definition directly, "The British regulars were...").
- "is one of the quieter engines of everything that came after" — replace the meta-framing with the concrete claim: e.g. "is where a generation of American soldiers learned both their resentment of the King's army and their confidence in their own."

### "The rawest wound was rank" — STRONG (story) / minor house-voice
Excellent heading and excellent storytelling. "A colonial colonel... was outranked in the field by a British captain young enough to be his son" is a vivid, instantly-graspable stakes line. Washington-as-unpaid-volunteer is the perfect human anchor: "He would soldier for nothing before he would soldier as an inferior." The Washington quote is used correctly and is on the fact pack's SAFE/verify list.

House-voice catch: the closing line "That choice tells you how raw the wound was" is mild meta-narrator (narrator telling the reader what to conclude). The scene already proved it; trust it.

**Rewrite note:** delete "That choice tells you how raw the wound was." End the paragraph on "He would soldier for nothing before he would soldier as an inferior." — it's a stronger button and it's not meta.

### "What the British thought of the provincials" — GOOD (story) / house-voice FAIL (rank) + 1 meta line
Good downhill structure (Braddock → Loudoun → Wolfe as escalating contempt). The Loudoun material is the highlight: "what they call Rights and Priviledges, totaly unknown in the Mother Country" and the "leading People" observation land the real point ("He was watching a political culture he did not recognize and calling it insolence" is a great line). The closing turn is excellent: "He was not wrong that the provincials would not behave like regulars. He was wrong about why."

**House-voice FAIL — full-rank-first:** Braddock's first mention here is "Edward Braddock set the tone in 1755" (no rank). His rank "Major General" doesn't appear until the third army section ("the expedition of Major General Edward Braddock" earlier at the rank section, line 29 — but that's a later section than this one in some reading orders; within *this* heading he is rank-less). The rule is full-rank-first on first mention. Fix: "Major General Edward Braddock set the tone in 1755."

**Meta-narrator catch:** "What that contempt bought him is its own story." — this is a narrator nudge/teaser toward the Monongahela. Cut it or make it a plain forward sentence. Try: "His contempt for both the colonials and the Native scouts cost him at the Monongahela." (then the cross-ref pill follows).

Note for the integrator (not a story issue): the Wolfe "worst soldiers in the universe" and the Braddock Franklin line both carry [VERIFY] flags in-text, correctly mirroring the fact pack. As story, the inline "[VERIFY: ...]" brackets read fine as draft scaffolding but must be resolved/removed before ship.

### "What the provincials thought of the British" — STRONG (story)
The best-built heading in the piece. Three-beat structure (the lash → the work → the contract) climbing to the thesis. The flogging detail is concrete and sourced to Clough. The covenant explanation is clear without being academic. And it lands the single best sentence in the draft: "They were not arguing about discipline. They were arguing about what a man was." The Clough "debarred Englishmen's liberty" quote is the fact pack's designated USE-THIS line and is deployed at exactly the right moment. No house-voice violations here. Keep as-is.

### "The mutiny that was really a lawsuit" — STRONG (story) / house-voice FAIL (1 meta line)
Terrific heading — it *is* the thesis in six words. The single-episode zoom is the right pacing choice after four headings of argument; the round-robin petition, the [CONTESTED] enlistment-length handled honestly ("Another account puts the term at eight months; the men themselves reckoned by Clough's six"), and the "God only knows who will see the end" closing quote are all strong. The payoff line is the keeper: "They were rehearsing, without knowing it, a habit of mind their grandsons would not have to be taught."

**Meta-narrator FAIL:** the opening line "You can watch the whole collision in one episode, on a cold headland in Nova Scotia." This is the narrator directing the reader's gaze ("You can watch..."). It's a *good* line, which is why it's tempting, but it's exactly the meta-narrator move the house voice bans. Rewrite to enter the scene directly: "The whole collision played out in one episode, on a cold headland in Nova Scotia in the autumn of 1759." (Then you can trim the redundant date from the next sentence.)

### "The third army, the one that actually worked in the woods" — STRONG (story) / house-voice FAIL (1 meta line)
This is the heading the fact pack's framing directive most demanded, and it delivers: the "French and Indian War... tells you the war was being seen from one porch" framing is sharp and fair; the Monongahela is rendered as a *story* (Beaujeu killed at once, the warriors fighting from cover, the British "a packed target that could see almost no one to shoot back at") rather than a casualty table, even while landing the numbers. Rogers' Rangers and Johnson/Hendrick both keep the Native nations as principals ("They were principals in this war, not auxiliaries to it"). The Saint-François line ("communities of families, not scenery") honors the framing duty.

**Meta-narrator FAIL:** "It is worth saying plainly what the British colonists were calling this conflict." — narrator announcing its own move. Cut "It is worth saying plainly" and just say it: "The British colonists named it the French and Indian War, after the enemies they pictured, which tells you the war was being seen from one porch."

Minor pacing note (not blocking): this is the longest section and the Monongahela paragraph is one very long block. Consider a paragraph break after "a slaughter." so the casualty figures and Washington's rear-guard breathe separately. Story-optional.

### "The bargain changes" — GOOD (story)
Clean turn ("Then the man running the war from London changed the terms"), good through-line to the debt. "Pitt had discovered that the colonists would not be driven but could be persuaded" is the right summary. The forward-looking button "It was a lesson London would forget with stunning speed" is plain narration of fact (not meta) and earns its place; the cross-ref pill to War Finance is correctly placed. The [VERIFY] on the rank-rule change correctly mirrors the fact pack's caution. No house-voice violations.

### "What they carried home" — STRONG (story)
A genuinely moving close. It does three hard things well: (1) refuses triumphalism by giving the Native dispossession its due as "the war's truest result"; (2) grounds scale in the human (the Anderson 30% stat, the disease-not-combat truth, handled as *pattern* per the fact pack rather than a fake ratio); (3) lands the two-convictions thesis cleanly and ties it to Washington and the Revolution. "The contempt and the confidence went home together, and they kept." is the right last line. No house-voice violations.

## House-voice ledger (the gate)

| Rule | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|
| No em-dashes (prose) | PASS | `—` appears only as quote-attribution separators and on eyebrow/cross-ref lines, not as prose punctuation |
| No meta-narrator | **FAIL** | lede ×2; rank section ("That choice tells you..."); British-contempt ("its own story"); Louisbourg ("You can watch..."); third-army ("It is worth saying plainly") |
| Little bold | PASS | no bold in body prose |
| Full-rank-first | **FAIL** | "Edward Braddock set the tone in 1755" — first mention in that heading lacks "Major General" |
| Cross-refs as standalone pill lines | PASS | Monongahela ×2 and War Finance pills are standalone lines |

---

## Verdict

**FAIL** — gated because two locked house-voice rules are violated (meta-narrator, repeatedly and structurally; full-rank-first, Braddock). No heading is graded NEEDS WORK or REWRITE on story grounds; the storytelling is strong-to-good throughout and the fixes are small. Resolve the meta-narrator lines and Braddock's rank and this clears the storytelling gate.
