## BLOCKERS

**B1 · Vigo debt year wrong — "1876, forty years" — appears in commander bio AND fact ledger**

> (commander bio, lines 49–50): "The debt Virginia owed him was paid to his heirs in 1876, forty years after he died poor."
>
> (fact ledger, line 263, §2): "debt paid to heirs 1876 · HIGH (§2, §3)"

**The fact:** Wikipedia's Francis Vigo article states the payment was "not until 1875" — one year earlier than the draft's "1876." Vigo died March 22, 1836; 1875 = 39 years, not 40. Both the year and the "forty years" arithmetic are wrong together. Wikipedia also says "his estate" received payment, not "his heirs" — the distinction is minor but the year is not.

**The pack:** The pack (§2 and §3) says "1876" — the draft faithfully copied the pack. The PACK IS WRONG and the draft inherited it.

**Fix:** Change "1876" to "1875" and "forty years" to "nearly forty years" (or "thirty-nine years") in both the bio and the fact ledger. Recommend flagging the pack error for correction in `rev-vincennes-factpack.md` §2 and §3. Optionally change "heirs" to "estate" to match Wikipedia's phrasing.

**B2 · Philip Dejean called "magistrate" — Wikipedia and secondary sources say "judge"**

> (S3 prose, line 223): "Helm led about 50 men in boats upriver and captured the British supply convoy coming down the Wabash from Detroit, seven boats and about 40 prisoners, including Detroit's notorious magistrate Philip Dejean, and a fortune in goods."
>
> (fact ledger, line 310): "~40 prisoners (incl. magistrate Philip Dejean)"

**The fact:** The Wikipedia article for the Illinois campaign / Siege of Fort Sackville calls Dejean "a British appointed judge, Philippe DeJean." No separate Wikipedia article exists for him. "Magistrate" and "judge" were sometimes used interchangeably for frontier civil officials, but the standard accessible secondary description is "judge," not "magistrate." The pack (§5d) also says "magistrate" — inherited error.

**Fix:** Change "magistrate" to "judge" in the S3 prose and fact ledger. One word; no source dispute.

## SHOULD-FIX

**SF1 · Clark's age at departure is described inconsistently between dossier and Section 1**

> (dossier note, line 21): "a 26-year-old Virginia colonel"
> (S1 prose, line 161): "Clark was a 25-year-old Virginia surveyor turned Kentucky militia leader"
> (S1 prose, line 172): (Clark departs Kaskaskia Feb 5; no re-age here)

**The fact:** Clark was born November 19, 1752. He was 25 when he took Kaskaskia (July 1778) and turned 26 in November 1778, making him 26 when he departed Kaskaskia on February 5, 1779. Both ages appear in the draft, describing different moments in the story — "25" in the S1 setup for the 1778 backstory, "26" in the dossier for the February 1779 march. The pack (§1, §3) uses "26" for the march itself and "25" for the background setup, which is correct. This is not an error — but the casual reader hitting "25" in S1 then "26" in the dossier will be confused.

**The pack:** The pack (§2) correctly says Clark was "a 25-year-old Virginia surveyor" in the background section and "a lieutenant colonel at 26" in the bios, both accurate for their respective moments. The draft mirrors this correctly but does not call out the year-change to the reader.

**Fix:** In S1 at first mention of Clark's age ("25-year-old"), add a brief parenthetical noting he turned 26 that November — something like "25 that summer, 26 by the time the march began." This makes the progression legible for a reader who sees both figures. No factual correction needed; clarity fix.

**No fix required.** Noting for the reviser: the 600-mile figure is the standard account and is pack-compliant. If a later reader challenge emerges, "about 600 miles" softens without losing meaning.

**One quote-delivery issue to flag (not a BLOCKER):**

> (S2 prose, line 209): Hamilton "told me with great satisfaction how he had been employed."

The pack (§9.7) has the verified form as Hamilton writing Clark came "all bloody and sweating… while he washed his hands and face still reeking from the human sacrifice in which he had acted as chief priest, he told me **with great exultation** how he had been employed." The draft renders this as "with great satisfaction." **"Great satisfaction" is not "great exultation."** These are different words with different valences; "exultation" (triumph/gloating) is the period word in Hamilton's journal. The broader passage surrounding it is paraphrased rather than quoted, so the "with great satisfaction" may be treated as paraphrase — but if the reviser intends it as near-verbatim it is wrong. Mark this a SHOULD-FIX if the passage is reader-facing as a quotation, a NIT if framed as paraphrase.
