# STORYTELLING CRITIC · Brandywine (rev-brandywine)

*Critic pass 2026-06-11, against `rev-brandywine-draft.md`, bar = `fi-carillon-draft.md`.
Storytelling lane only; facts are the fact-checker's lane. No edits made to the draft.*

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

This is a strong draft. The arc works: section 1 builds the wall ford by ford and the
"Above the forks, he thought, the wall had no more doors. / It did." turn is the best
single beat on the page; section 2's dramatic-irony structure (the reader knows about the
open doors while Washington drowns in cancelling reports) builds real dread; "Fooled by
the same man, with the same move, twice in thirteen months" lands as resonance, exactly as
briefed, because it is eleven words and stops. The Quaker texture is woven, not deposited:
the Old Kennett meeting that "did not stop," the displaced Birmingham Friends walking out
of a wheelwright shop into the sight of Cornwallis's army, and the common-grave MEANWHILE
is the emotional high point of the page. Knyphausen gets his due ("heard Cornwallis's guns
and stopped pretending" is a keeper), Howe's craft reads as genuine craft, Lafayette is
human. Mechanical voice scan clean: zero em-dashes in reader-facing surfaces, zero
drama-bold.

What keeps it from a clean PASS: the climax at Birmingham is narrated from the map, not
from inside the fight (the carillon "imagine being one of them" bar is unmet at the exact
spot the page most needs it), and four meta-narration instances, which are ship-blockers
by rule.

## BLOCKER

### B1 · No "you are there" beat at Birmingham — the climax is told, not felt
The carillon bar renders its climax from inside the abatis ("Imagine being one of them.
You hit a belt of felled trees too thick to run through..."). Brandywine's equivalent
peak, the hour-and-a-half meatgrinder around the meetinghouse, is delivered entirely at
operational altitude:

> "The ground changed hands again and again in roughly an hour and a half to an hour and
> three quarters of close fighting, some of the most intense of the entire war."

That sentence *asserts* intensity instead of producing it, and the double hedge
("roughly... to an hour and three quarters") deflates the one moment the page cannot
afford to deflate. The brief's instinct is right: Sullivan's men wheeling into line with
the hospital meetinghouse at their backs is the natural home for the interiority beat.

**Fix (sketch — author to rewrite from the pack, nothing here asserts new facts):** after
"Between three thirty and four, Cornwallis attacked off Osborne's Hill in parade-ground
order," drop to ground level for 3–5 sentences. Something like: *Imagine standing in
Stirling's line on that hill. You have been marching since the news broke; the field
hospital your army set up yesterday is the building behind you; and coming up the slope,
in step, with bayonets, is the army that was supposed to be seven miles away at Chadds
Ford. Your guns on the knoll fire over your head. The line holds, breaks, retakes the
ground, holds again, for the better part of two hours.* Then resume the existing
operational prose ("British grenadiers came on with the bayonet..."). Replace the
"roughly an hour and a half to an hour and three quarters" hedge in prose with "the
better part of two hours" (the precise range already lives in the dossier/stats, where
it belongs).

### B2 · Meta-narration (four instances) — ship-blocker by rule
1. > "is an argument historians have now been having for two and a half centuries, and it
   > will not be settled here."
   "It will not be settled here" points at the page itself. **Rewrite:** "...is an
   argument historians have been having for two and a half centuries. The fact pattern is
   not argued: Howe sailed away, the northern army went unsupported, and that fall it died
   at Saratoga." (Also trims the paragraph; see T2.)
2. > "falling on Howe's camp at Germantown on October 4 (that story has its own page)."
   Self-reference to the app's structure inside prose. **Rewrite:** cut the parenthetical
   cold; if/when a Germantown battle page exists, the cross-reference belongs in a PILL
   (`/war-revolution/battles/germantown | Germantown, 1777: Washington attacks back`),
   not in the sentence.
3. > "So tally the ledger honestly, both columns." ... "Now the other column."
   This narrates the shape of the analysis ("the shape is this" is the banned move). The
   paragraph underneath is excellent and needs no scaffolding. **Rewrite:** open directly
   on the credit: "Howe's campaign was executed about as well as any British operation of
   the war." For the pivot, replace "Now the other column." with a turn that does story
   work, e.g. "And then there is everything the masterpiece did not buy." The closing
   ledger-balance ("Howe had won a city. What Britain needed him to win was an army...")
   already lands the both-columns idea without announcing it.
4. > "Here is the logic of the creek."
   Announces the explanation instead of giving it. **Rewrite:** delete the sentence; the
   paragraph opens perfectly well on "The Brandywine had no bridges along this stretch
   in 1777." (Carillon never announces; it just says "Hold Carillon and you held the
   gate.")

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1 · The flank march disappears for nine hours — give the column one on-screen cut
Section 2's cutting is Knyphausen's show → Washington's confusion → and then the column
is suddenly *done*, delivered in pluperfect summary:

> "Cornwallis's column had crossed Trimble's Ford on the West Branch of the creek, then
> Jefferis's Ford on the East Branch, where the narrow defile beyond the crossing went
> unwatched, the wide-open pass Captain Ewald could not believe."

Section 1 planted the Jamaica-Pass analog and spent Ewald's quote on it; section 2 owes
that plant a *scene* and pays it off with a recap clause. One present-tense cut to the
column (placed between the Spear null report and the 2 p.m. confirmation, where it
maximizes dread) fixes it: the fog, nine hours of marching, the vanguard filing through a
defile a hundred men could have held, and nobody there. 2–4 sentences is enough; it also
makes "They were already across" land harder because the reader just watched it happen.

### S2 · Section 2's cinematic open stalls under glossing and the miles aside
The 5 a.m. split is the right opening shot, but the first paragraph carries three
parentheticals in one sentence ("(American)... (the rebels' regular army)... (shallow
wading places)") plus a 40-word distance reconciliation ("about 17 miles by the standard
modern measure, though Major John André of Howe's staff, who made it, wrote 'a circuit of
about fifteen miles' in his journal. Call it some fifteen to seventeen miles."). The
re-gloss discipline is mandatory, but it can be spread across the first two paragraphs
instead of stacked; and the André texture is good but should be one clause: "a march of
some fifteen to seventeen miles (Major John André of Howe's staff, who made it, called it
'a circuit of about fifteen miles')." Keep the fog sentence as the paragraph's exit — it
is the right last image.

### S3 · Plant Quaker country in section 1
The Old Kennett meeting in section 2 is wonderful but arrives unplanted; section 1
establishes Loyalist country ("this corner of Pennsylvania held plenty of Loyalists") and
never mentions that it is also Quaker country, which is the page's signature. One clause
in section 1 (provided the pack supports it; fact-checker to confirm) sets the table:
the war is about to march through a community whose conviction is not to fight it. Then
the meeting that "did not stop" pays off instead of introducing.

### S4 · Lafayette's boot fills with blood three times
Blurb ("a nineteen-year-old French volunteer with blood filling his boot"), bio ("staying
in action until blood filled his boot"), and prose, where the image trips over itself in
one sentence:

> "He stayed on the field until loss of blood forced him out, his boot filling with blood."

"Loss of blood" + "filling with blood" is redundant within the sentence; three deployments
across surfaces dulls a great detail. **Fix:** keep the image once at full strength in the
prose ("He stayed on the field until the blood filling his boot forced him out"), keep the
blurb's version (it is the section's hook), and vary the bio (e.g. "...and stayed in the
fight until he could no longer stand").

## TRIM

### T1 · Section 3 casualty paragraph duplicates the dossier
The paragraph does have a thesis ("including how hard it is to count") and Greene's own
estimate humanizes it, but "93 killed, 488 wounded, 6 missing, a total of 587, call it
around 580 to 600" re-renders what the stats block and casualties block already carry
twice. Trim the prose to the story-bearing parts: no American return ever issued, the
ranges, Greene's own count, eleven of fourteen guns, and the lovely "only about 40 of
them Hessians, a measure of how completely Knyphausen's morning had been a show." The
"American observers guessed... 2,000... around 600 is the defensible figure" sentence can
lose its second half.

### T2 · Section 1's opening paragraph carries a Burgoyne-blame digression
The two-armies frame is the right opening, but the blame adjudication ("Who deserves the
blame for that, Howe, London's sloppy coordination, or Burgoyne's own overconfidence...")
is a historiography beat before the story has started, on a page that is not about
Saratoga. Compress per the B2.1 rewrite; the PILL to `/war-revolution/two-armies` exists
precisely to hold that argument.

## NIT

### N1 · "beaten but not broken" / "beaten but unbroken"
The prose ("The army retreated east toward Chester... beaten but not broken") and
Washington's bio ("retreated beaten but unbroken") run the same near-cliché twice. Keep
one; the dossier note's "the army he needed to destroy marched away intact" is the
fresher rendering anyway.

### N2 · "almost perfectly" / "almost flawlessly" in adjacent bios
Knyphausen "played it almost perfectly"; Howe "executed both almost flawlessly." Same
construction two bios apart; vary one.

### N3 · Single QUOTE block in section 2 sits at the quiet beat, not the loud one
The Kennett Friends' "all was quiet and peaceful within" as the section's only pull-quote
is a defensible, even distinctive choice (the contrast IS a peak, and it serves the Quaker
signature). Noting it only so the choice is on record as deliberate; do not add a second
QUOTE block unless B1's rewrite surfaces a verbatim line that earns it.

### N4 · "If this is starting to sound familiar, it should have sounded familiar to the Americans too."
Reader-address, but judged in-bounds: it does story work (turns the reader's recognition
into the indictment) and the carillon bar's "Imagine being one of them" establishes
direct address as house-legal. Keep.
