## SPECIAL-SCRUTINY ITEM — the 23rd USCT location

**Draft (lines 118, 120, 124):** places the May 15, 1864 23rd USCT action "at the intersection
of the Catharpin Road and the Orange Plank Road."
**Fact pack §8 / the panel brief:** flagged this as a presumed mismatch vs. "Alrich Farm."

**RESOLUTION — the draft is CORRECT; there is NO mismatch. VERIFIED.**
The American Battlefield Trust "23rd USCT At The Alrich Farm" heritage-site page and its
historical-marker text place the engagement **near the Alrich Farm, AT the intersection of
Catharpin Road and Orange Plank (a.k.a. Old Plank) Road, in Chancellor, Virginia.** The
marker reads: *"the 23rd United States Colored Infantry hurried forward to the intersection of
Catharpin Road and Orange Plank Road. After skirmishing with the Confederates, the 23rd charged
and caused Rosser to withdraw."* The "Alrich Farm" and "the Catharpin/Orange Plank intersection"
are the **same place** — Alrich Farm is the farm sitting at that crossroads. The fact pack's own
§8 header even reads "23rd USCT at the Alrich Farm... At the intersection of the Catharpin Road
and the Orange Plank Road." So the draft did not contradict the pack; it used the road-intersection
descriptor and dropped the proper place-name.

- **NOT a MUST-FIX.** The location is right.
- **SHOULD-ADD (carried to the comprehensiveness gate, minor here):** the draft never prints the
  proper name **"Alrich Farm"** or the precise distinguishing qualifier **"north of the James
  River."** The well-attested superlative is specifically "first directed combat between USCT and
  the Army of Northern Virginia **north of the James River**." The draft says "the first time that
  United States Colored Troops fought Lee's Army of Northern Virginia in directed combat — the
  first such clash." That is true as written, but the precise marker claim is qualified by "north
  of the James"; without the qualifier a reader could over-read it as the first-ever USCT-vs-Lee
  combat anywhere. SHOULD add the qualifier for precision. Place-name "Alrich Farm" is optional color.

## Claim-by-claim

### `the-race` (May 7–8)

- **Wilderness "about ten miles to the northwest," burned the wounded, bloody stalemate** —
  VERIFIED. ~10 mi, May 5–7 1864, brush fires. Matches pack §1.
- **Grant turned southeast toward Spotsylvania instead of retreating north** — VERIFIED. Core,
  uncontested.
- **Crossroads "about ten miles below the Wilderness"** — VERIFIED (~10 mi SE, pack §1).
- **Lee sent First Corps under Anderson; Anderson inherited it because Longstreet was shot by his
  own men in the Wilderness two days earlier** — VERIFIED. Longstreet wounded by friendly fire
  May 6; Anderson took First Corps.
- **Anderson set off ~10 p.m. on a freshly cut forest road, marched all night** — VERIFIED (pack
  §1, [EV]/[WIKI]).
- **Union tangled on the Brock Road; Sheridan vs. Stuart friction jammed the column** — VERIFIED.
- **Laurel Hill, "a low rise... just south of the Brock Road," reached "minutes" ahead** —
  VERIFIED. Anderson won the race to Laurel Hill by minutes.
- **"Two weeks the pattern almost never varied" template** — VERIFIED, fair characterization.
- **MEANWHILE: Sheridan took "roughly ten thousand troopers" away on May 9 raid toward Richmond** —
  VERIFIED. Pack §2 gives ~10,000. (Note the figure later given for the same force at Yellow
  Tavern is ~12,000 — see below; both appear in sources, not an internal error but worth flagging.)

### `laurel-hill` (May 8–9)

- **~4 miles of works over May 8–9** — VERIFIED (pack §5, ~4 mi).
- **Warren / V Corps drew Laurel Hill, repulsed May 8; Sedgwick / VI Corps joined and failed** —
  VERIFIED.
- **Sedgwick "Uncle John," killed May 9, sniper at ~1,000 yards, struck below the left eye, caught
  by chief of staff Maj. Gen. Martin McMahon, never regained consciousness** — VERIFIED against
  Wikipedia "John Sedgwick": Whitworth rifle bullet "under the left eye," McMahon present.
  - **CAUTION — McMahon's rank.** Draft calls McMahon "Maj. Gen. Martin McMahon." At the time of
    Sedgwick's death (May 1864) McMahon was a **lieutenant colonel / colonel** serving as Sedgwick's
    chief of staff; he was brevetted/promoted to brigadier and later major general **later in / after
    the war**. The pack itself tags him only "Martin T. McMahon (North)" with no rank. Calling him
    "Maj. Gen." at this moment is an **anachronistic rank**. **SHOULD-FIX:** drop to "his chief of
    staff, Martin McMahon" or "Lt. Col. Martin McMahon." (Not ship-blocking — peripheral figure,
    role is correctly stated — but it is a rank error.)
- **"Elephant" quote** — VERIFIED verbatim-safe. Wikipedia: *"Why are you dodging like this? They
  couldn't hit an elephant at this distance."* Draft prints the canonical short form *"They couldn't
  hit an elephant at this distance."* CORRECT.
- **The "cut off mid-sentence" legend debunked in prose** — VERIFIED and exactly right. Wikipedia:
  "reports that Sedgwick never finished the sentence are apocryphal, although the sentence was
  certainly among his last words." The draft's debunk ("It's a good story and it isn't true... he
  spoke after it") matches the source. Good fact-checking by the author. The "highest-ranking Union
  officer killed" superlative was correctly **omitted** (it is real but qualified — "highest-ranking
  US Volunteers general officer killed"; McPherson outranked him in the Regular Army).
- **Wright took VI Corps** — VERIFIED.

### `the-mule-shoe` (May 10–11)

- **Ewell / Second Corps dug the salient night of May 8; bulged north over high ground; >½ mile out;
  ~250 acres; named "Mule Shoe"** — VERIFIED. Pack §4: >½ mi (up to ~1 mi), ~250 acres, ~1,160 yds
  wide. Draft's "more than half a mile" and "something like 250 acres" are in range.
- **Salient / enfilade / known death-trap; Lee knew it was weak; packed with ~30 cannon** —
  VERIFIED (pack §4, ~30 guns).
- **Upton, ~6 p.m. May 10, 12 hand-picked regiments ~5,000 men, four lines, ~150 yards, "do not stop
  to fire," broke in, took trench + battery, Mott's supporting division wilted, Upton driven back,
  promoted brigadier on the spot** — VERIFIED across the board (pack §5, [WIKI]). Wikipedia confirms
  "12 hand-picked regiments, about 5,000 men in four battle lines," ~6 p.m. Mott's failure, Upton's
  promotion.
- **May 11: Lee believed Grant was disengaging, ordered cannon — "around twenty-nine guns" — pulled
  from the Mule Shoe; Johnson asked for them back late night; order crawled; guns didn't start back
  until ~3:30 a.m. May 12; Hancock's assault ~30 min later** — VERIFIED. Wikipedia: the order to
  return "did not reach the artillery units until 3:30 a.m. on May 12, 30 minutes before Hancock's
  assault." The "~29 guns" figure is standard (Wikipedia did not state an exact number in the fetched
  body, but ~22 cannon were captured the next morning and ~29 withdrawn is the conventional figure;
  pack §5 gives ~29). VERIFIED within the pack's sourcing.
- **E. Porter Alexander judged pulling the guns the worst Confederate mistake of the campaign** —
  VERIFIED as a **paraphrase** (the draft does NOT quote it verbatim, per the [VERIFY] flag).
  Alexander's memoir does fault the artillery withdrawal; the exact wording "the one fatal Confederate
  blunder of this whole campaign" was correctly NOT printed inside quote marks. Handling is correct.
- **MEANWHILE "fight it out on this line":** dispatch ~8:30 a.m. May 11 to Halleck, chief of staff in
  Washington, with the Belle Plain wagons/provisions clause, paraphrased not quoted —
  - Recipient = **Halleck**: VERIFIED. (Relayed onward to Stanton/Lincoln; the dispatch itself was to
    Halleck.)
  - The Belle Plain provisions/ammunition clause: VERIFIED.
  - **SHOULD-FIX (variant note):** the draft paraphrases as *"he proposed to fight it out on this
    line."* The **original telegram text reads "purpose to fight it out on this line if it takes all
    summer"** (the word is "purpose," not "propose"). "Propose" is the universally popularized form
    and even CSS prints it that way, so this is not wrong in popular usage — and because the draft
    paraphrases without quotation marks it is not a verbatim error. But since the panel was told to
    scrutinize this dispatch: the verbatim original is "purpose." If the line is ever upgraded to a
    direct quote, it MUST be "**purpose**." As a paraphrase, leave-as-is is defensible; flagging
    SHOULD-FIX only so the variant is on record. NOT ship-blocking.
- **MEANWHILE Yellow Tavern:** May 11, six miles north of Richmond; Sheridan "roughly twelve thousand"
  vs. Stuart "roughly forty-five hundred"; Stuart shot at close range by dismounted trooper Pvt. John
  Huff; died in Richmond next day May 12; Fitzhugh Lee took the cavalry — ALL VERIFIED. Wikipedia
  "Battle of Yellow Tavern": May 11, 6 mi N of Richmond, Sheridan ~12,000, Stuart ~4,500, "John A.
  Huff" .44 revolver, died May 12, Fitzhugh Lee succeeded. Huff's rank as private: VERIFIED.

### `the-bloody-angle` (May 12)

- **~4:35 a.m. (delayed from 4:00 by darkness/mist/drizzle), Hancock's II Corps "between fifteen and
  twenty thousand"** — VERIFIED. Wikipedia: 4:35 a.m., "15,000 men" (ABT says ~20,000); the draft's
  range "fifteen and twenty thousand" correctly spans both sources.
- **Johnson's "roughly four thousand" defenders overwhelmed; Barlow's division overran the works;
  most of the division captured; Johnson and "Maryland" Steuart taken; guns captured** — VERIFIED.
  Pack: Johnson ~4,000; ~3,000+ prisoners (ABT: "more than 3,000 prisoners," 22 cannon, two general
  officers). Johnson + Steuart captured: VERIFIED.
- **Gordon's counterattack; McGowan (South Carolinians), Ramseur fed in; "Lee to the rear"** —
  VERIFIED. Wikipedia + ABT confirm Gordon's counterattack and the "Lee to the rear!" chant (a
  near-repeat of the Widow Tapp / Wilderness episode). McGowan = South Carolina brigade: VERIFIED.
  Ramseur engaged: VERIFIED. (Minor: ABT names Evans and Hoffman's brigades as Gordon's specific
  counterattack force; McGowan and Ramseur were among the broader brigades fed into the salient.
  The draft's "brigade after brigade thrown straight into the breach" is fair and not wrong.)
- **~20 hours ("many accounts say twenty to twenty-four") of hand-to-hand fighting in the rain;
  soaked powder; muzzle-to-muzzle over the parapet; ditch filled with mud/dead** — VERIFIED. ABT:
  "almost 24 hours of desperate hand-to-hand fighting." Pack: "20–24 hours." Draft's hedge is exact.
- **MEANWHILE Warren attacks Laurel Hill again; Meade ordered "at once at all hazards"; failed; drew
  off no reserves; some units' 4th/5th charge** — VERIFIED. The Meade order phrase "at once at all
  hazards" is paraphrased in the draft ("Meade told him to go in at once at all hazards") not staged
  as a hard quote; safe. Matches pack §5.
- **The Spotsylvania Stump:** oak "nearly two feet thick," cut down by rifle fire (not a cannonball),
  minié balls embedded, dug up and sent to the Smithsonian in 1888, on display at the National Museum
  of American History in "an exhibition about the price of American freedom" — VERIFIED. Smithsonian
  record + magazine: 22-inch oak trunk shot away by rifle bullets at the Bloody Angle May 12 1864,
  conical Minié balls embedded, transferred 1888, on display third floor NMAH in **"The Price of
  Freedom: Americans At War."** Draft's "nearly two feet thick" correctly sidesteps the ambiguous
  22-inch (original-diameter vs. surviving-piece) figure per pack guidance. The 24th Michigan / 6th
  Wisconsin credit was correctly **omitted** (flagged "believed"). EXCELLENT handling.
- **Withdrawal "around three or four in the morning on May 13" to a fresh line across the base of the
  salient, ½ to ¾ mile behind the apex; apex abandoned by dawn** — VERIFIED (pack §4/§5).
- **Horace Porter aftermath — Union dead scattered near the angle, Confederate dead "vastly more
  numerous," "piled on top of one another several layers deep"** — VERIFIED as a **paraphrase**. The
  draft does NOT print Porter's "four layers deep" inside quotation marks (it renders "several layers
  deep" in the author's voice), per the [VERIFY] flag. Correct handling. Lt. Col. Horace Porter's
  rank/role (Grant's aide) is correct.
- **Bloody Angle single-day "on the order of nine thousand Union and eight thousand Confederate...
  roughly three thousand... as prisoners"** — VERIFIED as approximate. Pack: ~9,000/~8,000, ~3,000
  captured; ABT cites ~17,000 combined casualties for the day, consistent. Draft correctly hedges
  ("on the order of"). No false precision.

### `the-23rd` (May 13–21)

- **May 13–16 eastward shifts in rain/mud, no soft spot** — VERIFIED.
- **May 18 Hancock attacked the old Mule Shoe ground again at dawn; Ewell's Second Corps held the
  improved works with massed artillery and repulsed it** — VERIFIED (pack §5).
- **May 19 Harris Farm: Lee sent Ewell on a reconnaissance that hit green "heavy artillery" units
  converted to infantry; cost the Confederates "around nine hundred"; Lee recalled them** — VERIFIED.
  Pack §5: ~900 Confederate loss, heavy-artillery-turned-infantry. Correct.
- **May 21 Grant disengaged southeast toward the North Anna, keeping continuous contact** — VERIFIED.
- **Strategic arithmetic: Grant could replace ~18,000; Lee could not replace ~12,000–13,000; tactical
  draw, both sides claimed it; Lee never again took the strategic offensive in the East; road ran
  through North Anna and Cold Harbor to Petersburg by mid-June** — VERIFIED. All consistent with pack
  §3/§9 and sources. Casualty figures (Union 18,399; Confederate 12,421–12,687) support the ranges.
- **The cause / 23rd USCT (May 15):**
  - "Eleven states had broken from the United States" — VERIFIED (11 CSA states).
  - Rosser (South) pushed back the 2nd Ohio Cavalry; 23rd USCT came up, skirmished, charged, drove
    Rosser back — VERIFIED verbatim against the ABT marker.
  - "23rd USCT... organized just months earlier, mustered beginning in late 1863" — VERIFIED.
    ABT: organized Nov 23, 1863 – June 30, 1864, at Camp Casey. "Mustered beginning in late 1863" is
    correct.
  - County roster "Spotsylvania, Stafford, Orange, Culpeper, Caroline" — VERIFIED but INCOMPLETE.
    ABT lists "Stafford, Spotsylvania, Orange, Culpeper, and Caroline counties **and the city of
    Fredericksburg**." **SHOULD-FIX:** the draft drops the city of Fredericksburg from the roster
    (it names Fredericksburg only as the regional anchor "the very counties clustered around
    Fredericksburg"). Adding "and Fredericksburg" to the roster would make it exact. Minor.
  - "Many of them came from... formerly enslaved and escaped people," "thousands escaped to Union
    lines" rendered without a hard figure — VERIFIED and correctly hedged. ABT: "over 10,000 enslaved
    people escaped from the various counties around Fredericksburg" when Union forces occupied
    Fredericksburg in April 1862. The draft's "many of them," "thousands... in the general flight"
    is conservative and safe; the precise 10,000+ figure was correctly not asserted as a hard number,
    though it COULD now be printed (it is sourced). Acceptable as is.
  - "First time USCT fought Lee's Army of Northern Virginia in directed combat — the first such clash"
    — VERIFIED but should carry the **"north of the James River"** qualifier (see SPECIAL-SCRUTINY
    item above). SHOULD-ADD.
  - "As the National Park material puts it, the fight proved to the white Union soldiers watching that
    Black soldiers would stand and fight the Confederate army" — VERIFIED as a **paraphrase**. ABT/NPS:
    "This battle action proved to the white troops that black soldiers would fight against the
    Confederate army." The draft attributes it to "National Park material"; the directly-quoted-ish
    sentiment actually traces to ABT's regiment article (drawing on NPS / local sources). Attribution
    "National Park material" is loose but defensible since the underlying NPS Mysteries & Conundrums
    blog carries the same claim. Rendered as paraphrase, not a staged quote — safe. SHOULD note the
    attribution could be tightened to "the Battlefield Trust / Park Service material."
