## Items Requiring Attention

### ⚠️ #1 — Corbett's shot wound location: "back of the head" vs. neck
**Draft text:** "a sergeant named Boston Corbett fired through a gap in the wall and hit Booth in the back of the head — by one much-noted account, almost exactly where Booth's own bullet had struck Lincoln."

**Finding:** This is anatomically imprecise in a way that could mislead. Autopsy records show the bullet entered "just behind the sterno-cleido muscle — 2½ inches above the clavicle," passing through the 4th and 5th cervical vertebrae and severing the spinal cord. The entry wound was at the neck/base of skull, not the top or rear of the head. Multiple authoritative sources (Wikipedia, Smithsonian, autopsy report) describe the wound as a "neck" wound. The comparison to Lincoln's wound (behind the left ear) is also inexact: Corbett's shot entered lower, at the neck/cervical vertebrae. The "almost exactly where Booth's own bullet had struck Lincoln" parallel is a popular dramatic claim but is not supported by the anatomical evidence.

**Recommendation (borderline MUST-FIX):** Change "hit Booth in the back of the head" to "hit Booth in the back of the neck" or "struck Booth at the base of the skull / neck." Either omit the "almost exactly where Booth's bullet struck Lincoln" parallel or flag it explicitly as a popular legend rather than an anatomical fact. This is the most significant imprecision in the draft.

Source: https://rogerjnorton.com/Lincoln83.html (autopsy); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Corbett

### ⚠️ #2 — "~400 communities" on the funeral train route
**Draft text:** "winding back through some 400 communities across seven states."

**Finding:** The most authoritative sources cite 180 cities with scheduled stops, across seven states. "400 communities" appears in some popular accounts (which count every whistle stop and passing town, not just scheduled stops), while 180 is the number used by the Library of Congress exhibit and Lincoln classroom resources. The figure is not wrong enough to be a hard error, but "180 cities" or "hundreds of communities" would be safer and better sourced.

**Recommendation (SHOULD-FIX):** Replace "some 400 communities" with "some 180 cities" or "hundreds of communities" — both are defensible and the latter avoids committing to a specific count.

Source: https://www.abrahamlincolnsclassroom.org/abraham-lincoln-in-depth/the-funeral-train-of-abraham-lincoln/index.html; https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/lincoln/interactives/long-journey-home/apr_21/index.html

### ⚠️ #3 — Booth's diary quote: missing opening clause
**Draft text:** "Our country owed all her troubles to him, and God simply made me the instrument of his punishment."

**Finding:** The verified full sentence begins: "I can never repent it, though we hated to kill. Our country owed all her troubles to him, and God simply made me the instrument of his punishment." The draft quotes only the second part. As presented, without quotation marks indicating a partial extract or an ellipsis, it implies this is the complete quoted passage. This is a minor issue (the portion quoted is accurate), but the lack of an ellipsis or "he wrote" framing that signals partial quotation could imply it's the full diary entry.

**Recommendation (SHOULD-FIX):** Either add an ellipsis at the start ("...Our country owed all her troubles to him...") or add a framing clause like "he wrote, among other things" to signal the partial quotation. The passage as printed is not wrong, but it presents a partial extract as if complete.

Source: https://lincolnconspirators.com/2013/10/13/the-text-of-john-wilkes-booths-diary/

### ⚠️ #4 — Lamon's publication timing: "roughly twenty years later" is slightly off
**Draft text:** "Lamon published it roughly twenty years later."

**Finding:** The book of Lamon's reminiscences was published in 1895, which is 30 years after the assassination — not 20. Lamon died in 1893 and the book was assembled by his daughter from his notes and unpublished manuscript, but the compilation/research was done in the 1880s (10-20 years after), which may be what the draft intends to convey. Still, the published date is 1895 = 30 years post-assassination. "Roughly twenty years" misdirects the reader.

**Recommendation (SHOULD-FIX):** Change to "roughly thirty years later" or "decades later" (which the draft in fact also uses elsewhere in the same sentence — "reconstructed from notes" — and the Fact Ledger entry already says "published ~20 yrs later," echoing the same error from the fact pack). The fact pack's own [VERIFY] flag may have been intended for exactly this.

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Hill_Lamon; https://teachinghistory.org/history-content/ask-a-historian/19423
