### Special-scrutiny items

WIKI-RR lists aim #3 verbatim as "Confiscate as much as 100,000 bales of cotton from the plantations along Red River"; confirms Banks "would allow cotton speculators to come along," Porter collecting cotton "as lucrative naval prizes" and realizing "a substantial sum of money." Ludwell Johnson's *Red River Campaign: Politics and Cotton in the Civil War* is a real title. The "grown by enslaved people / for Northern mills and the Treasury" framing is sound inference: Red River cotton plantations were worked by enslaved labor (general antebellum fact, not in dispute) — see Framing gate for the honesty assessment. NO false-precision; the "as much as 100,000 bales" is sourced as an objective figure, and the draft preserves "as much as" / range language. VERIFIED.

WIKI-RR lists "1st Brigade, Corps d'Afrique" (Col. W.H. Dickey) in the Union order of battle. The draft's MEANWHILE (engineer regiments, a cavalry brigade, fresh freedmen companies recruited in occupied Alexandria that April) is accurate. The draft does NOT over-claim a specific Mansfield-field combat role for the USCT — it says they "served in the Red River Campaign," which is exactly right (the 84th saw combat at Pleasant Hill the next day, not Mansfield). Clean.
