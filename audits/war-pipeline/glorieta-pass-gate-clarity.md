## MUST-FIX LIST (9 items)

These are hard failures — a newcomer reader will be **lost or materially misled** without the fix.

1. **"the Rio Grande"** — no orientation that it is a river or where it runs.
   - Phrase: `"march a force of Texans up the Rio Grande"`
   - Fix: `"up the Rio Grande (the great river that runs the length of New Mexico, from north to south)"`

2. **"Jornada del Muerto"** — translated but never explained as a physical thing.
   - Phrase: `"the Jornada del Muerto (the 'Journey of the Dead Man')"`
   - Fix: `"the Jornada del Muerto (the 'Journey of the Dead Man'), a 90-mile-plus waterless desert basin along the Rio Grande — one of the most lethal stretches of the old Spanish road through New Mexico"`

3. **"vanguard"** — military term with no gloss; first use in a fast-moving scene.
   - Phrase: `"ran straight into the Confederate vanguard"`
   - Fix: `"ran straight into the Confederate vanguard — the leading edge of the Texan force"`

4. **"flanked" / "flanked him again"** — used three times; never explained.
   - Phrase: `"The canyon walls were steep and close, so he split his force and sent infantry climbing up both sides... catching the Confederates in a crossfire... Pyron pulled back about a mile and tried to re-form; the Union troops flanked him again."`
   - Fix: After first use of "crossfire from the high ground," gloss flanking on second use: `"Pyron pulled back about a mile and tried to re-form; the Union troops flanked him again — moved around his sides before he could set a new line."`

5. **"supply train"** — appears in §3 as implied concept but never named/glossed until §5 where it does the most work.
   - Phrase (§5): `"What they destroyed was the Confederate campaign itself. The supply train held food, ammunition..."`
   - Fix: On first standalone use of "supply train" (somewhere in §3-§4 context), add a one-time gloss: `"supply train — the wagon convoy that hauled everything an army needed to survive: food, ammunition, medicine, spare gear"`

6. **"parole" (prisoners)** — Civil War parole has a specific technical meaning a newcomer will not know.
   - Phrase: `"took 17 prisoners, whom they paroled"`
   - Fix: `"took 17 prisoners and paroled them (released them on their word not to fight again until formally exchanged — standard Civil War practice)"`

7. **"Department of New Mexico"** — military bureaucracy unexplained.
   - Phrase: `"who headed the entire Department of New Mexico"`
   - Fix: `"who commanded all Union forces in the territory"`

8. **Lt. Col. Harold Raguet missing side-tag** — inconsistency with house style; named in a casualty moment where the reader needs instant orientation.
   - Phrase: `"Confederate Lt. Col. Harold Raguet was mortally wounded"`
   - Fix: `"Lt. Col. Harold Raguet (South) was mortally wounded"`

9. **Scurry missing scene-set / the "main Confederate column" is unexplained** — Scurry appears suddenly commanding "the main Confederate column" with no prior indication a larger force existed behind Pyron.
   - Phrase: `"Lt. Col. William R. Scurry (South) came up with the main Confederate column."`
   - Fix: `"Lt. Col. William R. Scurry (South) arrived with the bulk of the Texan force — the main body that had been marching up behind Pyron's advance guard all along."`

## SHOULD-FIX LIST (11 items)

These are clarity improvements; a determined reader can push through without them, but they reduce friction and keep the prose accessible.

1. **"column" (military)** — used as "Slough's column left Fort Union."
   - Fix: First use, gloss inline: `"Slough's column (his marching force)"` — thereafter the word works on its own.

2. **"hostelry"** — obscure word already self-glossed two lines later; just simplify.
   - Phrase: `"a stage stop and hostelry called Pigeon's Ranch"`
   - Fix: `"a stage stop and inn called Pigeon's Ranch"`

3. **"draft animals"** — means wagon-pulling horses/mules; already clarified one sentence later but order could be swapped.
   - Phrase: `"killed or drove off the draft animals, somewhere on the order of 500 to 600 horses and mules"`
   - Fix: `"killed or drove off the horses and mules — the draft animals that pulled the wagons and cannon — somewhere on the order of 500 to 600 of them"`

4. **"forage"** (in supply list) — means animal feed, not foraging.
   - Phrase: `"food, ammunition, medicine, forage, clothing"`
   - Fix: `"food, ammunition, medicine, forage (feed for the horses and mules), clothing"`

5. **Jefferson Davis missing explicit side-tag** — titled "Confederate President" which carries the implication, but house style says tag every named person on a side.
   - Phrase: `"Confederate President Jefferson Davis"`
   - Fix: `"Confederate President Jefferson Davis (South)"` — or simply: `"Jefferson Davis (South), the Confederate president"`

6. **Maj. John Shropshire missing "(South)" tag** — listed as "Confederate Maj." in prose but not tagged per house style.
   - Phrase: `"Confederate Maj. John Shropshire was killed"`
   - Fix: `"Maj. John Shropshire (South) was killed"`

7. **Capt. William Lewis missing side-tag** — briefly named, immediately dismissed, but house style.
   - Phrase: `"a Capt. William Lewis actually led the assault"`
   - Fix: `"a Capt. William Lewis (North) actually led the assault"`

8. **Sangre de Cristo Mountains** — named but unlocated.
   - Phrase: `"At the southern tip of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains"`
   - Fix: `"At the southern tip of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains (the long range running along New Mexico's eastern edge)"`

9. **Manuel Chaves / "2nd New Mexico Mounted Volunteers"** — regiment name drops without context; "local knowledge" point undersold.
   - Phrase: `"Lt. Col. Manuel Chaves (North), a Hispanic New Mexican frontiersman of the 2nd New Mexico Mounted Volunteers, chosen by Canby and Chivington precisely because he knew this terrain"`
   - Fix: `"Lt. Col. Manuel Chaves (North), a New Mexican frontiersman who'd spent his life in these mountains and now served in the territory's own Union militia — chosen by Canby and Chivington precisely because no Coloradan or Texan knew this ground the way he did"`

10. **The two-column plan — why Chivington's mesa detachment was supposed to help Slough** — implied but not explicit.
    - Phrase: `"send Chivington with 450 to 490 men south over the high ground to come down behind the enemy"`
    - Fix: After "come down behind the enemy," add: `"— a hammer-and-anvil move: Slough pushing the Confederates from the east, Chivington cutting off their retreat from the west."`

11. **Simultaneity of the mesa raid and Pigeon's Ranch fight** — the structure (Pigeon's Ranch section THEN wagons section) implies sequence rather than simultaneous action. The opening of the wagons section corrects this, but a bridge sentence before the section break would help.
    - Phrase (end of Pigeon's Ranch section, before `---`): The section ends with Slough retreating and Scurry on the field. The reader does not yet know Chivington was moving at the same time.
    - Fix: Add a one-sentence bridge before the section break: `"What neither army knew yet was that while they were bleeding at Pigeon's Ranch, the battle had already been decided somewhere else entirely."`

## Overall assessment

The draft is strong and largely newcomer-accessible — the author has done real work glossing the Santa Fe Trail, mesa, volunteer regiment, and spiking a cannon. The core failure pattern is **military vocabulary used without gloss in fast-moving battle prose** (vanguard, flanked, supply train, parole, column) and **one geographic term left as pure atmosphere** (Jornada del Muerto). None of the MUST-FIX items require more than a phrase; the draft skeleton is solid.
