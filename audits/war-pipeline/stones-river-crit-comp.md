# Stones River — COMPREHENSIVENESS critic gate (step 4)

Inverse of the fact-checker: this hunts what is ESSENTIAL and MISSING, not what is present-and-wrong.
Independent must-cover checklist built via web (American Battlefield Trust, Wikipedia, Britannica, HISTORY,
HistoryNet, Essential Civil War Curriculum, NPS) and compared against the draft + fact pack.

**Storytelling-first rule applied:** SELECTIVE is fine. Only genuinely essential gaps (a turning point, a key
decision, the human/emancipation dimension) are MUST-ADD/blocking. Nice-to-haves are SHOULD-CONSIDER.

## MUST-ADD (essential, blocking)

**1. Wheeler's cavalry "ride around the army" + the LaVergne supply-train strike — give it weight and a moment, not a glancing clause.**
This is the one genuinely under-told strategic thread. On Dec 29–30, BEFORE the main battle, Joseph Wheeler's
cavalry rode COMPLETELY AROUND Rosecrans's army and, at midday Dec 30, fell on the lightly-guarded supply train
near LaVergne — destroying/carrying off nearly $1 million in supplies and capturing/paroling ~800 prisoners
(ABT, HistoryNet, Essential Civil War Curriculum; the draft's fact pack §5/§9 mentions Wheeler raiding the pike
but never the ride-around or the LaVergne strike or its scale/date). This matters for the STORY, not as trivia:
it is *why the Nashville Pike lifeline is the whole game* — the reader is told the pike is the Union's one open
artery (§3) but is never shown the blow that nearly cut it, so the stakes of "hold the pike and you hold the
battle" are asserted rather than dramatized. It also explains Bragg's confidence (he believed he could strangle
Rosecrans's supply and force a retreat) and sharpens why the Jan 1 pause — Rosecrans resupplying through the
night despite the raids — was the quiet tide-turner. Recommended fix: one or two sentences in §1 or §3 giving
Wheeler's pre-battle ride-around and the LaVergne strike a concrete moment (date + the ~$1M / ~800 prisoners),
so the pike's importance is earned. This is the only blocking gap.

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (not blocking)

- **The pre-battle march / three-wing advance (Dec 26–30).** Rosecrans split into three wings (McCook/Thomas/
  Crittenden) and marched ~30 mi over four days through skirmishing weather to deploy ~2 mi NW of Murfreesboro.
  The draft compresses "marched out of Nashville… toward Murfreesboro" — fine for a storytelling-first telling;
  the wing structure pays off later (Thomas center / Crittenden left appear in §2's night council), but the
  reader meets those names cold there. A single clause naming the three wings at the march could earn them.
  Not essential.
- **Forrest & Morgan detached.** Part of why Rosecrans dared advance was that Bragg's best raiders (Forrest,
  Morgan) had been sent off to other departments, leaving only Wheeler. A nice causal grace note if §1 wants it;
  not required.
- **The freezing-rain/cold woven earlier.** The cold and rain appear only at the retreat (§5, the rising river).
  Both nights of the battle were bitterly cold; a touch earlier would deepen the "men among their dead" of §3.
  Atmosphere, not substance.
- **Britannica/ABT give slightly different round force numbers (34,700 / 38,000 CSA; 41,400–44,000 Union).** The
  draft's "~76,000 engaged" and range-handling is defensible and consistent with the fact pack. No change needed.

## Fact-pack blind spots (gaps in the pack itself, so the draft couldn't include them)

1. **Wheeler's ride-around + LaVergne strike under-specified.** Fact pack §5/§9 says only "Wheeler's cavalry kept
   raiding Union supply on the pike" and §2 lists Wheeler as "raided Union supply trains." It never captures the
   Dec 29–30 complete circuit of the Union army or the LaVergne haul (~$1M, ~800 prisoners). The draft therefore
   could only render the cavalry threat vaguely. **This is the root cause of MUST-ADD #1** — fix the pack and the
   draft gap closes. (Sources: ABT, HistoryNet, Essential Civil War Curriculum.)
2. **Forrest/Morgan detached** — absent from the pack; relevant to why Rosecrans advanced when he did. Minor.
3. **The pre-battle three-wing march detail (routes, LaVergne, Dec 26–30 timeline)** — the pack jumps from
   "marched out of Nashville" to the Dec 30 face-off; the four-day approach and wing routes aren't in it. Minor;
   only matters if the author wants to earn the wing names.

Otherwise the fact pack is unusually complete and the draft uses essentially all of it. No missing turning point,
no missing key decision, and the human/emancipation dimension is fully and correctly carried.
