# NEWCOMER-CLARITY CRITIC — The Battle of Quebec (1775) draft

Draft reviewed: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-quebec-draft.md`
Critic lens: reader has ZERO prior knowledge — no Continental Army, no map of Canada,
no military vocabulary, no person ever heard of. Every surface checked: dossier note,
stats, sides, casualties, commander bios, outcome, blurbs, captions, locator captions,
all three sections, MEANWHILE blocks.

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The draft's main-prose glossing discipline is genuinely good (grapeshot, palisade,
barricade, bateaux, feints, habitants, seigneurs, self-inoculation all defined in
parentheses on first use), all four required causal chains are explicit mechanisms,
the timeline never loses the reader, and the Upper Town / Lower Town / cliff
orientation at the top of section 2 is exemplary. The F&I 1759 beats (Wolfe's Cove,
the Montcalm MEANWHILE) stand alone. But there are six BLOCKERs, all clause-level
insertions, no structural rework: the war itself / Congress / "Continental" are never
established for a zero-knowledge reader; two of the user-listed terms of art
(battery, blockade) are undefined at first use; and sections 2 and 3 — which are read
independently — re-introduce nobody (Montgomery, Arnold, Carleton, Washington all
appear bare). The fixes below are mandatory before ship; with them applied this
passes.

---

## BLOCKER

**B1 — Which war? Section 1 opens mid-war with no war established (criterion 6: the
outbreak beat does not stand alone).**
Draft: "In the spring of 1775 the war was a few weeks old and already pointing north."
A newcomer does not know there is a war, between whom, or that it started in April
1775. The PILL link to the outbreak chapter is a pointer, not context.
Fix: "In the spring of 1775 the war between Britain and its thirteen rebellious
American colonies (it had broken out in Massachusetts that April) was a few weeks
old and already pointing north."

**B2 — "Congress" and "Continental" never defined anywhere on the page.** The reader
explicitly does not know what the Continental Army is; "Continental" appears four
times unglossed (Montgomery bio "Continental commission"; §1 "He accepted his
Continental commission reluctantly"; §3 "paper Continental dollars"; §3 "the entire
Continental Army"). "Congress" first appears §1: "By late June, Congress had
authorized an invasion."
Fix at §1 first use: "By late June, Congress (the Continental Congress, the thirteen
colonies' joint assembly in Philadelphia, acting as their rebel government) had
authorized an invasion." And: "He accepted a commission in the new Continental Army
(the joint army Congress had created out of the colonies' militias) reluctantly..."
Echo a compressed version in the Montgomery bio ("a commission in Congress's new
Continental Army").

**B3 — "battery" undefined at first use (user-listed term).**
Draft (§1): "a few light guns firing from batteries built of ice and snow, which the
walls shrugged off."
Fix: "a few light guns firing from batteries (emplacements where cannon are grouped
and mounted; theirs were built of packed ice and snow), which the walls shrugged
off." The §2 use ("a blockhouse with a battery of small cannon") then reads fine,
but it is a different section — add "(a cluster of cannon)" there too, since
sections are read independently.

**B4 — "blockade" never actually defined (user-listed term).**
Draft (§1): "they put Quebec under what was called a siege and was really a
blockade: a few light guns firing from batteries..." — this tells the reader what
the operation *wasn't*, never what a blockade *is*. The word then recurs undefined
in the Arnold bio ("kept the blockade going from a hospital bed"), §2, §3, and the
outcome text.
Fix (§1): "...was really a blockade (they could not break the walls, only seal the
roads and try to starve the city): a few light guns firing from batteries..."

**B5 — Section 2 re-introduces nobody (criterion 2; sections are read
independently).** First mentions in §2 are all bare:
- "Montgomery's plan aimed there." → "The plan of the American commander, Major
  General Richard Montgomery, aimed there."
- "...and Arnold from the north, through the streets called the Sault-au-Matelot."
  → "...and Colonel Benedict Arnold (commanding the American column that had marched
  overland through the Maine wilderness) from the north..."
- "...Carleton had been warned an attack would come on a stormy night." → "...Guy
  Carleton, the British governor commanding the defense, had been warned..."

**B6 — Section 3 re-introduces nobody (criterion 2).** First mentions in §3 are bare:
- "the defenders found Montgomery in the snow at Près-de-Ville" → "the defenders
  found the body of Richard Montgomery, the American general killed leading the
  southern attack column, in the snow at Près-de-Ville (the riverside strongpoint
  where his assault had died)."
- "Carleton's government gave the rebel general a decent, honorable burial" →
  "The government of Guy Carleton, the British governor who had held the city, gave
  the rebel general a decent, honorable burial."
- "Washington, who got the news from General Schuyler, put the loss plainly." →
  "George Washington, the Continental Army's commander-in-chief, who got the news
  from General Philip Schuyler (the American commander of the northern department),
  put the loss plainly."
- "Arnold, promoted brigadier general on January 10 and running the army from a
  hospital bed" → "Benedict Arnold, the American colonel shot in the leg during the
  assault, promoted brigadier general on January 10 and running the army from a
  hospital bed..."

## SHOULD-FIX

**S1 — Dossier side-u note: enlistment cliff stated without its mechanism.**
Draft: "The attack was forced by the calendar: most of the men's enlistments expired
at midnight on December 31."
Fix: "...most of the men's enlistments (their agreed terms of service) expired at
midnight on December 31, after which they could lawfully walk home." (The §1 prose
does this perfectly; the dossier is its own surface.)

**S2 — Montgomery bio: "grapeshot" unglossed in the bio surface.**
Draft: "died at 37 in the defenders' first blast of grapeshot."
Fix: "died at 37 in the defenders' first blast of grapeshot (a cannon charge of
small iron balls)" — or simplify to "the defenders' first cannon blast." Same bio:
"sold his commission" → "sold his officer's commission (British officer ranks were
bought and sold)".

**S3 — Arnold bio leans on unintroduced references.**
"co-captor of Ticonderoga" → "co-captor of Fort Ticonderoga (the British fort on
Lake Champlain seized in May 1775)"; "after the Kennebec march" → "after his
starving march to Quebec through the Maine wilderness"; "kept the blockade going" is
covered once B4's gloss exists in prose, but the bio is standalone — prefer "kept
the siege lines going from a hospital bed."

**S4 — Carleton bio terms of art.**
"Wolfe's quartermaster general" → "quartermaster general (chief supply officer) to
General Wolfe"; "wounded on the Plains of Abraham" → "wounded on the Plains of
Abraham (the battlefield just outside the city's walls)"; "architect of the Quebec
Act's conciliation of the French Canadians" → "architect of the Quebec Act (the 1774
law that protected French Canadians' Catholic Church and French civil law)".

**S5 — Braddock and Cowpens unexplained (Morgan bio + §2).**
Bio: "drove wagons on Braddock's 1755 campaign" and §2: "driven supply wagons for
Braddock's army twenty years before" → add "(a British expedition against the French
in the previous war)" at each first-per-surface use. Bio: "at Cowpens, he handed
Britain one of its worst small-battle defeats" → "at Cowpens, in South Carolina, he
handed Britain..."

**S6 — §1 Montgomery intro: "British regular" + previous-war anchors.**
Draft: "an Irish-born professional who had spent sixteen years as a British regular
(Louisbourg in 1758, the Lake Champlain front in 1759)..."
"Regular" is a term of art, and Louisbourg/1759 mean nothing without the
previous-war frame (which only arrives later via Carleton). Fix: "...sixteen years
as a British regular (a full-time professional soldier), fighting in Britain's
previous war against France in North America (Louisbourg in 1758, the Lake Champlain
front in 1759)..."

**S7 — §2: Livingston's "Canadian volunteers" can be read as defenders.**
Draft: "Colonel James Livingston with about 200 Canadian volunteers against St.
John's Gate." Since the garrison is also substantially Canadian, a newcomer can lose
which side these Canadians are on. Fix: "Colonel James Livingston with about 200
Canadian volunteers (Canadians who had enlisted on the American side)..."

**S8 — §2: "picket" used in two different senses four sentences apart.**
"Montgomery himself helped pull the pickets aside" (= fence stakes) is followed by
"a mixed picket of perhaps 30 to 50 men" (= guard detachment). Fix the second:
"a mixed picket (a small guard detachment) of perhaps 30 to 50 men..."

**S9 — §2: "General Howe" unintroduced.**
Draft: "Carleton, in his dispatch to General Howe..." Fix: "...in his dispatch to
General Howe (the British commander-in-chief in America)..."

**S10 — "effectives" unglossed (dossier stats, side-u, §1).**
Draft (§1): "Together they had perhaps 900 to 1,200 effectives." Fix: "...900 to
1,200 effectives (men actually fit for duty)." Apply once in the dossier too.

**S11 — "nor'easter" unglossed (§2 + blurb 2).**
Draft (§2): "a nor'easter delivered a full blizzard." Fix: "a nor'easter (a winter
storm that blows in from the northeast) delivered a full blizzard."

**S12 — Blurb 3 pronoun ambiguity.**
Draft: "Carleton buried his dead enemy with honors while Congress voted him the
first national monument." The nearest referent for "him" is Carleton. Fix: "...while
Congress voted Montgomery the first national monument."

**S13 — Montreal→Quebec spatial relation only implied.**
Draft (§1): "slipped out of the city... and went downriver... making for the one
place still worth holding: Quebec..." A reader with no map gets the direction only
from "downriver." Fix: "...went downriver (northeast, down the St. Lawrence toward
the sea) ... making for the one place still worth holding: Quebec, the walled
capital on the cliff above the St. Lawrence River." (Locator A helps, but the prose
should carry it.)

## NIT

**N1 — §3 MEANWHILE: "the fight at Valcour Island"** → "(a naval battle on Lake
Champlain)".

**N2 — §2: "a brass 6-pounder"** → "a brass 6-pounder (a light cannon)".

**N3 — §2: "a company of the 7th Foot"** → "(a regiment of British regulars)".

**N4 — §3: "Trois-Rivières"** unplaced in prose → "(a town on the river roughly
halfway back toward Montreal)". It is on Locator A, but the section should orient.

**N5 — §1: the Aaron Burr cameo lands flat for a zero-knowledge reader** — the name
carries no charge if you've never heard of anyone. Either accept the cameo as-is or
add the lightest forward whisper ("a name with a long future"); do not explain the
duel here.

**N6 — The St. Charles River appears only in Locator B's water note, never in
prose.** Arnold attacks "from the north" — one clause naming the second river would
complete the two-rivers geography: "from the north, along the shore where the
St. Charles River meets the St. Lawrence."

**N7 — "the cape" (§2 + Locator B caption)** → "(the high headland between the two
rivers)" once.

**N8 — §3: "paper Continental dollars"** → "(Congress's paper money)"; needed even
after B2, since §3 is read independently.

**N9 — §3: "General Wooster"** → give first name and side: "the American general
David Wooster" (confirm name against the fact pack before inserting).

## WHAT PASSES (so the reviser doesn't fix what isn't broken)

- **Causal chains (criterion 4): all four explicit and mechanical.** Why invade
  ("Britain's back door... close the door and the war stayed in the thirteen
  colonies" + the fourteenth-colony political hope); why Dec 31 ("at midnight on
  December 31 their enlistments expired and they could lawfully shoulder their packs
  and walk home. Attack before New Year's, or watch the army dissolve."); why
  Montgomery's death stopped the column ("the command group lay dead in the snow,"
  Campbell's withdrawal order, and §2's later "waiting on Montgomery's column, which
  was never coming"); why smallpox decided the siege (crowding + against-orders
  self-inoculation, glossed, + "Reinforcements trickled north, and smallpox killed
  them," + silver/requisition mechanism). Do not touch these.
- **Timeline (criterion 5): clear throughout.** Spring→May→June→Sept→Nov 13→Dec
  2-3→Dec 31 in §1; the night itself hour-by-hour in §2 (rockets 4–5 am, surrenders
  by 10 am); New Year's Day→Jan 4→Jan 25→April→May 6→June in §3, with the 1818
  reburial clearly marked as a coda.
- **Geography (criterion 3): mostly strong.** The Upper/Lower Town paragraph opening
  §2 ("Quebec was really two cities...") is the model; Près-de-Ville and
  Sault-au-Matelot are both introduced as holdable places; Arnold's route is fully
  drawn (Kennebec → Height of Land → Chaudière) in prose, figure, and Locator A.
  Remaining gaps are S13 (Montreal relation) and N6/N7 only.
- **F&I 1759 standalone (criterion 6):** Wolfe's Cove ("the same cove where General
  Wolfe's army had climbed the cliffs in September 1759 to take this city from
  France") and the Montcalm MEANWHILE both carry enough context on their own; the
  PILL to the F&I battle page is a bonus, not a crutch.
- **Glossing style:** parentheses used throughout, no em-dash glosses found on any
  surface. Miles-first observed ("about 20 miles (32 km)").
