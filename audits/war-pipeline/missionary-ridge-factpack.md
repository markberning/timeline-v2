## 1. Dates / Location / Theatre

- **Date:** **November 25, 1863** — the third and climactic day of the
  three-day **Battle of Chattanooga** (Nov 23–25). [WP, ABT-Chatt]
  - Nov 23 = Grant seizes **Orchard Knob** (the advanced hill that becomes his
    vantage point). Nov 24 = **Lookout Mountain (see Lookout Mountain)**. Nov 25 =
    **Missionary Ridge**, this section. [WP, ABT-Chatt]
- **Location:** **Missionary Ridge, just east of Chattanooga, Hamilton County,
  Tennessee** (battle coordinates given as 35°01′48″N 85°15′25″W). [WP]
- **Theatre:** **Western Theater** of the American Civil War. **Accent = blue**
  (Western lane). [WP; CLAUDE.md accent rule]
- **Result:** **decisive Union victory** — Bragg's army routed and driven out of
  Tennessee. [WP, ABT-Chatt]

## 2. Forces & Commanders (each tagged SIDE — first-mention side-tags MANDATORY)

### Union (North) — three armies fighting together under one man
- **Maj. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant (North)** — **overall commander** of the combined
  force (newly created Military Division of the Mississippi). Watched the assault
  from **Orchard Knob.** [WP, ABT-Chatt, NPS-Grant]
- **Maj. Gen. George H. Thomas (North)** — **Army of the Cumberland** (IV and XIV
  Corps); the army that had been beaten at Chickamauga and that makes the
  spontaneous charge here. ("The Rock of Chickamauga" — see Chickamauga.) [WP, ABT-Chatt]
- **Maj. Gen. William T. Sherman (North)** — **Army of the Tennessee**; assigned
  the **north end** of the ridge (**Tunnel Hill**) — and **stalls there all day.** [WP, ABT-Art]
- **Maj. Gen. Joseph Hooker (North)** — detachment from the **Army of the Potomac**
  (XI Corps + a XII Corps division); assigned the **south end**, coming over from
  **Lookout Mountain via Rossville Gap.** [WP, ABT-Chatt]
- Subordinates who matter to the charge: **Maj. Gen. Philip Sheridan (North)** and
  **Brig. Gen. Thomas J. Wood (North)** — their divisions lead the climb; **Brig.
  Gen. August Willich (North)** and **Brig. Gen. William B. Hazen (North)** roll up
  the cracked line north-to-south; **Maj. Gen. Gordon Granger (North)** — IV Corps,
  source of the famous line (see Quotes). [WP, AH] `[VERIFY]` Sheridan/Wood as the
  two charge divisions; Willich/Hazen flanking.

### Confederate (South) — Army of Tennessee, on the heights
- **Gen. Braxton Bragg (South)** — **overall commander**, Army of Tennessee; his
  army holds Missionary Ridge and is routed off it. (Same commander beaten-then-
  besieging from Chickamauga — see Chickamauga.) [WP, ABT-Chatt]
- **Lt. Gen. William J. Hardee (South)** — commands the **right** (north end,
  including Tunnel Hill against Sherman); his sector **holds** all day. [WP]
- **Maj. Gen. John C. Breckinridge (South)** — commands the **center/left** of the
  ridge (the sector that breaks); former U.S. Vice President. [WP] `[VERIFY]` exact
  sector boundary (center vs left) and that Breckinridge held the breaking center.
- **Maj. Gen. Patrick Cleburne (South)** — division on the north end; **repulses
  Sherman at Tunnel Hill** and then forms the **rear guard** of the retreat. [WP, ABT-Art]

### Sizes / strengths (label the ranges — ACW numbers are contested)

- **Whole three-day Chattanooga campaign (ABT):** **Union ~72,500 · Confederate

- **Local odds at the decisive point:** Thomas sent **~23,000–24,000** men against

## 4. Geography (relative positions — load-bearing for map accuracy)

North is up; east is right. **Chattanooga sits on the south bank of the Tennessee
River; Missionary Ridge runs north–south roughly 2–3 miles EAST of the city, and
the armies face roughly EAST↔WEST across the valley.** `[VERIFY]` exact ridge-to-
city distance.

- **Missionary Ridge** — a long, steep, narrow **high ridge** (~hundreds of feet
  of rise) running **north–south east of Chattanooga**, the main Confederate
  defensive line. Its narrowness is tactically decisive: too thin a crest to hold a
  proper reserve. [WP, ABT-Chatt]
- **The TWO Confederate lines (the flawed deployment — see §5):** **rifle pits /
  trenches at the BASE of the ridge** AND a second line **on the crest at the top**,
  with the troops **divided between them** — neither line strong. [WP, ABT-Art]
- **Tunnel Hill** — the **NORTH end** of the ridge (named for a railroad tunnel);
  **Sherman's objective and where he stalls** all day against Cleburne. [WP, ABT-Art]
- **Rossville Gap** — a pass at the **SOUTH end** of the ridge; **Hooker's avenue**
  of approach, coming over from Lookout Mountain. He is **delayed ~3 hours** by a
  burned bridge over **Chattanooga Creek** and reaches the gap mid-afternoon. [WP]
- **Orchard Knob** — a detached **hill on the valley floor between Chattanooga and
  the ridge**, taken by Thomas on Nov 23; **Grant's and Thomas's vantage point**
  from which they watch the charge (and where the "who ordered those men up?"
  exchange happens). [WP, NPS-Grant]
- **Chattanooga** — the Union-held city on the Tennessee River to the **WEST**; the
  Union lines ring out from it toward the ridge. [WP]
- **Chattanooga Creek** — runs between Lookout Mountain and the ridge's south end;
  its **burned bridge** is what delays Hooker. [WP]
- **Retreat axis:** Bragg's army falls back **EAST across Chickamauga Creek, then
  southeast toward Dalton, Georgia**; Cleburne's rear guard makes its stand at
  **Ringgold Gap** (Nov 27). [WP]
- **Relative layout for a strategic map (N→S along the ridge):** **Tunnel Hill
  (north, Sherman/Cleburne)** → **center (Thomas's charge, Bragg's HQ, the break)**
  → **Rossville Gap (south, Hooker)**. Orchard Knob sits in the valley west-of-center
  (Grant's vantage). Chattanooga is west of the whole line. [WP, ABT-Art]

## 5. Sequence (turning points — the heart of the story)

**The frame:** Grant's plan made **Sherman the hammer** (the north end) and
Thomas's center attack only a **diversion**. The plan failed in the most glorious
possible way — the diversion won the battle, by accident, against orders.

1. **The flawed Confederate deployment (the setup for everything).** Bragg put one
   line of infantry in **rifle pits at the BASE** of Missionary Ridge and a second
   line **on the crest**, splitting his force between them. Worse, his engineers
   laid the upper works on the **topographic (physical) crest rather than the
   "military crest"** — the result was **dead ground / blind spots** where defenders
   on top **could not see or shoot Union troops climbing the lower slope.** WP: the
   works were "Placed along the physical crest rather than what is termed the
   military crest…these works severely handicapped the defenders," and Bragg's
   "defenses were only a thin crust" with no real reserve on the narrow ridge.
   [WP, ABT-Art] **(Define "military crest" inline for the reader: the line a little
   below the true top, from which you can actually see and fire down the whole
   slope.)** `[VERIFY]` "military crest" phrasing and that engineer error is securely sourced.
2. **Morning — Sherman stalls at Tunnel Hill (north).** Sherman's Army of the
   Tennessee (~16,600) attacks the **north end** but is **repulsed all day** by
   **Cleburne's (~4,000)** division; piecemeal assaults, Confederate counterattacks,
   ~2,000 Union casualties, **no breakthrough.** Grant's intended main blow fails.
   [WP, ABT-Art] (Described as Sherman's worst day as a commander — `[VERIFY]` that characterization.)
3. **Afternoon — Hooker comes up from Lookout via Rossville Gap (south).** Hooker
   leaves Lookout Mountain (~10 a.m.), is **held up ~3 hours** by the burned bridge
   over Chattanooga Creek, and reaches **Rossville Gap (~3:30 p.m.)**, then presses
   up the **south end** of the ridge, rolling the Confederate left. [WP]
4. **~3:30–3:40 p.m. — Grant orders Thomas's men to take the BASE rifle pits as a
   DIVERSION.** With Sherman stuck, Grant orders the Army of the Cumberland forward
   to seize **only the first line — the rifle pits at the base** — to pull
   Confederate pressure off Sherman. (Orders on whether/where to halt were
   confused/contested.) `[VERIFY]` the exact orders-confusion: were the men told to
   stop at the base, or was it ambiguous? Sources differ; do not overstate certainty. [WP, ABT-Chatt, NPS-Grant]
5. **THE MOMENT — the unordered charge straight up the ridge.** Thomas's
   **~23,000–24,000** men overran the **~9,000** defenders in the base rifle pits,
   shouting **"Chickamauga! Chickamauga!"** (still stung by their September defeat —
   see Chickamauga). Then, **trapped at the base under murderous fire from the crest
   and unwilling to stay**, the soldiers — **on their own initiative, without
   orders** — **kept climbing**, Sheridan's and Wood's divisions leading. The blind
   spots in the badly-sited crest line (§5.1) let them get up the slope; the thin,
   reserveless Confederate center **cracked, then shattered and routed.** Willich
   and Hazen rolled the broken line up. **One of the most dramatic moments of the
   entire war — "the miracle on Missionary Ridge."** [WP, ABT-Chatt, AH]
6. **~5–6 p.m. — total collapse.** The Confederate center gave way (first crack
   cited near Bird's Mill Road ~5 p.m.); the whole line unraveled by ~6 p.m. Bragg's
   army **fled east across Chickamauga Creek and retreated toward Dalton, Georgia.**
   **Cleburne's division formed the rear guard** and later fought the successful
   **Ringgold Gap** stand (Nov 27) that saved Bragg's trains. [WP, ABT-Art]

## 6. The Cause — Slavery (SHIP-BLOCKING — MUST appear in the section's own prose)

> **NAME THE CAUSE rule (locked 2026-05-30).** The framing gate FAILS any Civil War
> section whose own prose never names slavery as the cause. Omission is the default
> Lost Cause failure. The author MUST state it plainly somewhere the section sets
> its stakes — not lean on Chickamauga or another section to carry it.

- The war these armies fought was, at root, **a war over slavery.** The Confederate
  states seceded to protect and extend the enslavement of Black people; the Union
  by late 1863 (post-Emancipation Proclamation) fought to destroy that system. The
  battle on the ridge is one engagement in that larger war. [standing rule; ABT-Art]
- **The specific hook here — the gateway to the slave heartland.** Chattanooga was
  the **rail gateway into the Deep South.** Winning it (NPS: it opened the
  **"Gateway to the Deep South" of Georgia and Alabama**; ABT: it "opened up the
  Deep South for a Union invasion") put the Union army on the road to **Atlanta** —
  the rail-and-supply core of the cotton-and-slavery economy at the heart of the
  rebellion. **The charge up Missionary Ridge cracked open the door to the slave
  South itself.** [NPS-Grant, ABT-Chatt]
- **Author hooks to choose from:** (a) the men screaming "Chickamauga!" were
  avenging a defeat in a war whose purpose was the end of slavery; (b) the ridge was
  the last barrier between the U.S. Army and the plantation interior; (c) Sherman's
  1864 march through Georgia — which broke slavery across the Deep South by force —
  *begins logistically here*, with Chattanooga as its supply base.

## 7. Human Cost (the toll, named where sourced)

- **~5,824 Union and ~6,667 Confederate casualties** at Missionary Ridge alone
  (§3); the campaign total runs higher. The Union charged **uphill into prepared
  positions** — most of its loss was **wounded** men shot on the slope. [WP]
- **The Confederate cost was an army's cohesion, not just bodies:** **4,146 men
  captured** and **~40 guns lost** — the wreckage of a force that broke. Whole
  regiments surrendered as the line dissolved (e.g., ~700 of Holtzclaw's men at the
  south end). [WP] `[VERIFY]` the Holtzclaw surrender figure if used.
- **Make the cost felt through the climb:** men advancing up a steep, open slope
  under rifle and artillery fire from above; the wounded sliding back down; the
  terror of being pinned at the base in the rifle pits (which is what *drove* the
  unordered charge — staying still was deadlier than going up). Keep specifics to
  what sources support; do not invent named individuals (route any new named witness
  through the fact pack + fact-checker FIRST). [WP, AH]

## 8. Key Quotes (exact attribution + source; shaky ones flagged)

- **"Thomas, who ordered those men up the ridge?"** — **Grant (North)**, watching
  from Orchard Knob, stunned by the unauthorized charge. **Thomas (North)** replied
  he did not; Grant then turned to **Granger (North)**. [AH, WP] `[VERIFY]` exact
  wording — multiple renderings exist ("Who ordered those men up the hill/ridge?").
- **"When those fellows get started, all hell can't stop them."** — **Maj. Gen.
  Gordon Granger (North)**, answering Grant about who ordered the charge. The
  signature quote of the battle. [AH, WP] `[VERIFY]` exact wording/attribution
  (sometimes rendered slightly differently; confirm it is Granger, not Thomas).
- **[VERIFY — IMPORTANT, the Grant temper] "I ordered your attack an hour ago. Why
  has it not been made?"** and **"I order you to attack."** — attributed to **Grant

- **"This…is the death-knell of the Confederacy."** — attributed to **a Confederate

## 9. Significance (for the Outcome card + "what it meant")

- **Shattered Bragg's Army of Tennessee and drove it out of the state** — routed it
  off the ridge and back into Georgia (Dalton). The Confederacy's main western army
  was wrecked as an offensive force; Bragg was **relieved of command** soon after.
  [WP, ABT-Art] `[VERIFY]` Bragg's relief/resignation date.
- **Lifted the siege of Chattanooga for good** — the supply crisis that began after
  Chickamauga (see Chickamauga) was ended permanently; the Union held the city and
  all of eastern Tennessee. [WP, ABT-Chatt]
- **Opened the road to Atlanta / the gateway to the Deep South.** Chattanooga became
  the **supply and logistics base for Sherman's 1864 Atlanta Campaign**; the victory
  "opened up the Deep South for a Union invasion." [WP, ABT-Chatt]
- **Made Grant's reputation → general-in-chief.** This was **Grant's last battle in
  the West**; a few months later (**March 1864**) he was promoted to **lieutenant
  general** and given **command of ALL Union armies** (general-in-chief). NPS: "A few
  months later, Grant was appointed General in Chief and given command of the entire
  Union army." [NPS-Grant, WP]
- **The signature historiographical note:** the result was so improbable — a
  *diversion* that, *against orders*, stormed a fortified ridge and routed a
  veteran army — that it entered legend as **"the miracle on Missionary Ridge."**
  Soldiers' initiative, not generals' plans, won it. [AH, WP]
- **Outcome-card verdict (draft):** *Decisive Union victory.* Grant meant Thomas's
  men only to feint at the ridge's base; stung by Chickamauga and pinned under fire,
  the Army of the Cumberland charged straight up the slope without orders and broke
  Bragg's center, routing his army out of Tennessee. The siege of Chattanooga was
  lifted for good, the gateway to Atlanta and the Deep South thrown open, and Grant —
  three months later — made general-in-chief.
