## MUST-ADD 1 — Hooker wounded, leaves the field
- Maj. Gen. Joseph Hooker (North), commanding I Corps, was shot **through the foot**
  around mid-morning (~9:30 a.m.) Sept 17 and had to leave the field.
- I Corps command passed temporarily to Brig. Gen. George Meade (North).
- Significance: the morning's dominant Union commander exits ~halfway through the fight —
  needs narrative closure in the Cornfield section. `[VERIFY exact time]`
- Sources: NPS Antietam overview; Wikipedia "Battle of Antietam".

## MUST-ADD 2 — Mansfield's XII Corps; Mansfield mortally wounded
- The morning fight was **three** Union corps, not one: I Corps (Hooker), then **XII Corps
  under Maj. Gen. Joseph K. F. Mansfield (North)** as the second wave, then later II Corps.
- Mansfield was **mortally wounded** early advancing his corps on Sept 17; **died Sept 18**.
- Significance: two senior Union generals (Hooker wounded, Mansfield killed) became
  casualties before noon — the morning's Union commitment is understated by ~50% without XII Corps.
- Sources: NPS; Wikipedia.

## MUST-ADD 3 — Hood's counterattack (the South's tactical voice in the Cornfield)
- Brig. Gen. **John Bell Hood's division (South)** launched the counterattack that drove
  Union troops back through the Cornfield — the engine behind the field changing hands ~15 times.
- Hood's division suffered **~60% casualties** doing it.
- Hood's reported reply when later asked where his division was: roughly **"Dead on the
  field."** `[VERIFY exact quote wording — confirm or cut; do not invent]`
- Significance: gives the South tactical agency in the morning section (dual-POV requirement);
  explains the back-and-forth rhythm the section currently describes without a cause.
- Sources: American Battlefield Trust; NPS; Wikipedia.
