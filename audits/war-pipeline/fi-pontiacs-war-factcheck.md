## ❌ WRONG (must fix)

### 1. Trent journal quote — wrong preposition ("for" should be "to")
**Draft (line 93, gated quote block):**
> "Out of our regard **for** them, we gave them two Blankets and an Handkerchief out of the Small Pox Hospital. I hope it will have the desired effect."

The verbatim primary text reads **"Out of our regard *to* them..."** — confirmed against both the UMass derrico transcription of Trent's journal and the Wikipedia *Siege of Fort Pitt* article (which quotes "Out of our regard to them we gave them two Blankets and an Handkerchief out the Small Pox Hospital"). The draft also inserts a comma after "them" that the source does not have, but the load-bearing error is **for → to**. This is a primary-document quotation on a gated surface; it must be verbatim.
**Correct:** `"Out of our regard to them, we gave them two Blankets and an Handkerchief out of the Small Pox Hospital. I hope it will have the desired effect."`
**Source:** Trent's journal, June 24, 1763 (UMass derrico archive; Wikipedia *Siege of Fort Pitt*).

## Resolved [VERIFY] flags raised by the author

### [VERIFY] (draft line 34) — Amherst "vilest race of beings... meritorious act" wording
**RESOLVED — author made the correct call.** The draft deliberately uses ONLY the well-attested "extirpate this execrable race" line (July 16, 1763 letter to Bouquet) and explicitly declines the more sweeping "vilest race" wording until locked. ✅ The "execrable race" line is confirmed verbatim in the July 16 Amherst→Bouquet letter ("try every other method that can serve to extirpate this execrable race"). The cautious choice stands; no "vilest race" quote ships, so nothing to lock. The "execrable race" attribution, date (July 16, 1763), and recipient (Bouquet) are all ✅ CONFIRMED.

## ✅ CONFIRMED (full list)

### Collapse of leverage / Treaty of Paris
- Quebec fell September 1759 (Plains of Abraham) ✅; Montreal capitulated September 1760 ✅; Treaty of Paris signed **February 10, 1763** transferring Canada + French claims east of the Mississippi ✅.

### Amherst's policy
- British commander-in-chief in North America ✅. Ended gift-giving beginning ~1761 (Wikipedia: decision February 1761) ✅. Restricted gunpowder/lead-shot sales ✅. August letter to **William Johnson**, Superintendent of Indian Affairs, to stop "purchasing the good behavior of the Indians" and limit ammunition ✅. Nations said they were treated "no better than slaves or dogs" ✅.
- **"extirpate this execrable race"** — Amherst → Bouquet, **July 16, 1763** ✅ (verbatim). The Amherst→Bouquet smallpox exchange is real: Amherst's "Could it not be contrived to send the small pox among the disaffected tribes...use every stratagem in our power to reduce them" (c. late June / "c. July 1763" as the draft hedges) ✅; Bouquet's reply about inoculating via blankets and not catching it himself ✅; Amherst's July 16 approval ✅.

### Neolin / the Master of Life
- Neolin, Lenape (Delaware) holy man, "the Enlightened One," vision ~1761 ✅. Message: reject European goods/rum, return to old ways, drive out the British; blended Christian + traditional threads; spread across Ohio + Great Lakes ✅. Recited by Pontiac at a 1763 Detroit war council; recorded by a French chronicler, translated ✅ (draft correctly frames it as relayed, not a transcript).
- **Master-of-Life quote** ("This land where ye dwell I have made for you and not for others. Whence comes it that ye permit the Whites upon your lands? ... did you not live by bow and arrow? You had no need of gun nor powder ... and yet you caught animals to live upon.") ✅ — this is an **attested published translation**. The "ye/Whence comes it" wording is reproduced verbatim in the Lumen Learning / SUNY *US History I* text. (A competing translation at americanyawp.com reads "you/How comes it"; both are legitimate published renderings of the same Pioneer Society of Michigan source. The draft's is real, not fabricated.) The draft also modernizes "come"→"came" in the second clause; trivial and within translation variance.

### Pontiac and the coalition
- Pontiac (Obwandiyag), war chief of the Odawa (Ottawa); prominent figure, NOT a supreme commander; broad decentralized coalition ✅. Nations named (Odawa, Ojibwe, Potawatomi, Wyandot/Huron, Lenape/Delaware, Shawnee, Seneca/Mingo, Miami, Wea, Kickapoo, Mascouten) ✅. Guyasuta (Seneca/Mingo) and Charlot Kaské (Shawnee, crossed the Mississippi rather than make peace) ✅.
- War council **April 27, 1763, Ecorse River, ~10 miles SW of Detroit** ✅. **Pontiac quote** "It is important for us, my brothers, that we exterminate from our lands this nation which seeks only to destroy us." ✅ (verbatim per the French chronicler's recorded summary; draft correctly labels it a translated summary).
- **May 1**: entered Fort Detroit with ~50 Odawa to scout ✅. **May 7**: surprise seizure failed; commander **Henry Gladwin** warned ✅. Siege began ~May 9 ✅.

### Fall of the forts
- "About eight of roughly twelve" framing ✅ (Wikipedia: 8 captured; sources describe "more than a dozen" British forts, with Detroit, Niagara, Pitt holding — the draft's hedge is correct).
- Sandusky May 16, ~15 soldiers killed, burned ✅. St. Joseph May 25, 15-man garrison mostly killed ✅. Fort Miami May 27, commander lured out by a woman, 9 men surrendered ✅. Ouiatenon June 1, ~20 men taken without bloodshed ✅. Venango mid-June, ~12 killed by Senecas ✅ (sources date it June 16 — "mid-June" is correct). Le Boeuf June 18, most of ~12 escaped to Fort Pitt ✅. Presque Isle held ~2 days vs ~250 warriors, surrendered June 19–20 ✅.
- **Michilimackinac, early June** (June 2/4 across sources — "early June" is correct ✅): Ojibwe baggataway/lacrosse ruse on the **king's birthday** ✅, women passed in weapons under blankets ✅, **~35-man garrison, roughly half killed** ✅ (sources span "~15 of 35" to "27 killed"; "roughly half the thirty-five-man garrison" is a fair, hedged statement).

### Detroit / Bloody Run
- Bloody Run **July 31, 1763** ✅ (the dedicated *Battle of Bloody Run* article, Detroit Historical Society, and Zinn Education Project all date it July 31; an arithmetic inference of "July 30" from a secondary phrasing is not the asserted date). Captain **James Dalyell**, ~250 men, ambushed at **Parent's Creek**, **~20 British killed incl. Dalyell** ✅ ("20 dead, 41 wounded" per the battle article — draft's "roughly twenty" ✅). Siege lifted **October 31, 1763** after word the French would not come ✅.

### Fort Pitt / the blankets
- Besieged from ~June 22 ✅; Captain **Simeon Ecuyer** ✅; **~550 people, 200+ women and children** ✅ (main *Pontiac's War* article + HISTORY.com; the narrower *Siege of Fort Pitt* article counts the 230-man garrison but notes settlers crowded in); smallpox already loose ✅.
- Blankets given at a parley **June 24, 1763** ✅. The **supply invoice** billed the items "taken from people in the Hospital to Convey the Smallpox to the Indians," and the fort reimbursed Trent ✅. Intent + act NOT contested; medical effectiveness IS — draft frames this exactly right (smallpox already circulating; material possibly too old; **Philip Ranlet** argues no evidence the scheme worked; others think it may have) ✅.

### Bushy Run / Devil's Hole
- Bushy Run **August 5–6, 1763** ✅; Bouquet ~500 men ✅; intercepted **~25 miles east** of Fort Pitt by Lenape/Shawnee/Mingo/Wyandot ✅; won by feigned retreat ✅; **~50 killed, ~60 wounded** ✅ (Bouquet's own report: 50 killed, 60 wounded, 5 missing ≈ 23% — "close to a quarter" ✅). Siege of Fort Pitt lifted **~August 20, 1763** ✅.
- Devil's Hole **September 14, 1763** ✅; Senecas at the Niagara portage ✅; **"more than seventy" killed** ✅ (sources give 70+/81); deadliest single British loss of the war ✅.

### Stalemate, reversal, and peace
- Amherst **recalled to London, autumn 1763** ✅ (Gage took over the New York command Nov 17, 1763; Amherst left N.A. in 1763 — "autumn 1763" is correct; the fact-pack's flagged "1863" was a typo, draft says 1763). Replaced by **General Thomas Gage** ✅. Sir William Johnson authorized to **restore gift-giving** ✅.
- Niagara congress **July–August 1764, ~2,000 Native people** ✅. Bouquet to the **Muskingum, October 1764**, Ohio nations returned **200+ captives** ✅. Pontiac's peace **July 25, 1766, Fort Ontario at Oswego** — **no land ceded, no prisoners surrendered, no hostages taken**, Johnson showed exceptional deference ✅.

### Royal Proclamation of 1763
- Issued by **George III, October 7, 1763** ✅. Drew a line along the Appalachian crest/watershed ✅; barred settlement west; created an "Indian Reserve" ✅; only the Crown could buy Native land ✅. Both verbatim quotes ("...reserved to them ... as their Hunting Grounds" / "forthwith to remove themselves") are from the Proclamation primary text ✅. George Washington promised **~20,000 acres** of bounty land, called the line a temporary expedient ✅. Later treaties (Fort Stanwix 1768) pushed the boundary west, opening Kentucky and West Virginia ✅.

### Aftermath / casualties
- **~400 British soldiers killed, ~50 captured/killed** ✅. George Croghan estimate: **~2,000 settlers killed or captured, ~4,000 displaced** (draft attributes it to Croghan and frames it as an estimate ✅; historians debate the figure, which the draft's "estimated" honors). **200+ Native warriors killed** ✅. First major multi-nation resistance not ending in outright defeat; precedent for Brant and Tecumseh ✅.
