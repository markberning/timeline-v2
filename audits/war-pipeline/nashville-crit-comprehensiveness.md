# Nashville — COMPREHENSIVENESS GATE (crit-comprehensiveness)

> War content pipeline, step 4 (critic, comprehensiveness gate). Adversarial. I built the
> must-cover checklist independently from the historical record (not from the draft's
> table of contents), then checked the draft against it. "Selective is correct" — a battle
> section is a STORY, not an encyclopedia; the test is whether any *essential* beat,
> perspective, or stake is missing or underweighted, not whether every detail is present.
>
> **VERDICT: FAIR (PASS).** No MUST-ADD. The draft covers every load-bearing beat of the
> battle and gives correct weight to the thing Nashville is *for* (the destruction of the
> last western Confederate army, with the USCT assault as the human center). A few
> SHOULD-ADDs and one fact-pack blind spot noted below.

## A. Independent must-cover checklist (built from the record)

A complete Nashville section must convey, at minimum:

1. **Strategic stakes** — this is the last Confederate field army in the West; Nashville
   destroys it. The 1864 collapse backdrop (Atlanta, Mobile Bay, Lincoln, Sherman).
2. **The standoff** — Hood too weak to attack, unwilling to retreat (desertion logic);
   parked before a fortress city held since 1862.
3. **Forrest's absence** — Hood's best weapon detached to Murfreesboro at the worst moment.
4. **The Thomas/Grant relief drama** — deliberate Thomas, ice storm, Grant's telegrams,
   the Logan order, Grant en route, Thomas vindicated.
5. **Thomas the soldier** — Rock of Chickamauga, Virginian who broke with family, the slow
   methodical character that is the whole story's engine.
6. **The plan** — feint (Steedman, east) + grand right wheel (Wilson cav + A.J. Smith) onto
   Hood's left.
7. **Dec 15** — the feint, the wheel, the five redoubts falling in sequence, the ~2-mile
   withdrawal to Shy's Hill / Overton Hill.
8. **Dec 16 — Overton/Peach Orchard Hill** — the USCT assault, repulsed but draining
   Confederate strength east; the 13th USCT's catastrophic loss; enemy + friendly
   testimony to their valor.
9. **Dec 16 — Shy's Hill** — the crest flaw, McArthur's assault, the break, Shy killed.
10. **The collapse / rout** — rolled up west to east, retreat down Granny White / Franklin.
11. **The cause — slavery** — named in the section's own voice (also a framing-gate item).
12. **Aftermath** — army destroyed as a force, Beauregard's count, Hood resigns, Forrest's
    rearguard saves the remnant, the West decided.
13. **Casualty shape** — ~3,000 Union / ~6,000 Confederate, the prisoner-heavy fingerprint
    of dissolution.

## C. Perspective check

- **Confederate POV** — present and not flat: Hood's logic given sympathetically-enough to
  be understood (the desertion trap), Holtzclaw's report used, Shy's death noted, Forrest's
  rearguard given its due. The Confederate experience is *defeat and dissolution*, and the
  draft conveys it as tragedy-of-an-army without softening why they were there. FAIR.
- **USCT POV** — central, not tokenized (see framing gate). FAIR.
- **Common soldier** — represented (the redoubt-racing regiments, the men on the parapet,
  the 13th's freshmen-in-first-action detail). FAIR.
- **The relief drama gives the Union high command POV** (Grant's anxiety, Thomas's calm).
  FAIR.

## D. SHOULD-ADD (non-blocking — would deepen, not required)

1. **Confederate corps commanders go unnamed in prose.** The fact pack names Stewart (left),
   Cheatham (right), Stephen D. Lee (center) — the three men whose corps actually held the
   line that broke. The draft names Hood, Forrest, Holtzclaw, Shy, Beauregard but never
   Stewart/Cheatham/Lee. Stewart held the LEFT — the very flank that collapsed both days —
   and Cheatham held the RIGHT at Overton Hill where the USCT charged. Naming at least
   Stewart and Cheatham would let the reader attach the collapse and the Overton defense to
   a Confederate command, and the side-tag convention already invites it. **SHOULD-ADD.**
2. **Wilson's pursuit / the scale of the cavalry role is thin.** Wilson's rebuilt cavalry is
   the hinge of the whole plan (it's why Thomas waited) and the engine of the pursuit, but
   after the wheel it mostly disappears until the Forrest-rearguard paragraph. One more line
   on the dismounted cavalry doing the actual flank work on Dec 15–16 would pay off the
   "that's what the horses were for" setup. **SHOULD-ADD (light).**
3. **The "Sledge of Nashville" nickname is unused.** Thomas earned a *new* nickname FROM
   this battle ("the Sledge of Nashville") — a tidy capstone to the "Old Slow Trot who was
   right" arc. Optional flourish. **SHOULD-ADD (optional).**

## E. Fact-pack blind spots (flagging for the record, per brief)

- **Third Battle of Murfreesboro / Battle of the Cedars (Dec 7).** The pack mentions Forrest
  was "driven off" at Murfreesboro on Dec 7; the draft drops it entirely (says only that
  Forrest was off "tearing up track"). This is correct selectivity — the Murfreesboro fight
  is a sideshow and including it would muddy the "Forrest absent" point. NOT a gap; noting
  that the draft made the right cut.
- **The First Colored Brigade's actual combat on Dec 15.** The pack and draft both keep the
  Dec-15 Colored Brigade to a clause ("took part in Steedman's diversion"). That brigade
  also took real losses on the 15th. The draft's choice to keep Dec-16 / 2nd Brigade as the
  centerpiece and Dec-15 / First Brigade as a distinguishing footnote is defensible and
  serves clarity (the two-brigade confusion is a known trap). NOT a required add, but if any
  USCT expansion happens, the Dec-15 brigade's fight is the natural place.
- **Hood's own post-battle framing / the controversy over his generalship.** The pack treats
  Hood's decisions as blunders (Franklin assault, Forrest detachment); the draft follows.
  Fair — this is the consensus and the section isn't a Hood-rehabilitation venue.

## F. Underweighting check

Nothing essential is underweighted. If anything the relief-drama (§2) is the *longest*
non-USCT beat and could feel like it competes with the battle proper — but it is also the
section's best narrative engine and resolves into the "slow man was right" payoff, so the
weight is earned. The USCT/Overton centerpiece (§4) correctly carries the most weight, which
is right both historically and for the cause-hook. Balance is sound.
