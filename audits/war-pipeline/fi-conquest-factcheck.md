# Fact-Check Report — "The Conquest of Canada"

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS**
**MUST-FIX (❌): 2 | SHOULD-FIX (⚠️): 5**

Checked against: Wikipedia (Battle of the Plains of Abraham, Battle of La Belle-Famille, Battle of Sainte-Foy, Montreal campaign, Fort Niagara, Louis-Joseph de Montcalm, Battle of Sainte-Foy), American Battlefield Trust, Canadian Encyclopedia, Parkman's *Montcalm and Wolfe*, Treaty of Paris (1763) sources.

## MUST-FIX ❌

### ❌ 1. Wolfe's wounds: "wrist, the body, and the chest" — "body" is the wrong word
**Draft:** *"Wolfe was hit several times, in the wrist, the body, and the chest"*
**Correct:** Multiple independent sources (Wikipedia, National Battlefields Commission) record Wolfe's wounds as: **wrist, groin (or stomach/abdomen), and chest**. The fact pack says "wrist, stomach, and chest." The draft's "body" is evasive and technically means nothing — it is not a recognized wound location and conflicts with the fact pack's own wording.
**Fix:** Change "in the wrist, the body, and the chest" → "in the wrist, the groin, and the chest" (Wikipedia/National Battlefields Commission wording) or "wrist, abdomen, and chest" — do not use the vague "body."
**Source:** Wikipedia, Battle of the Plains of Abraham; National Battlefields Commission, bataille.ccbn-nbc.gc.ca

---

### ❌ 2. Montcalm burial location precision: "floor of the Ursuline convent's chapel" — missing the crucial "choir" specification
**Draft:** *"buried in a hole that a bursting shell had torn in the floor of the Ursuline convent's chapel"*
**Fact pack:** "shell hole beneath the Ursuline church choir" (§6). Web sources confirm: "buried in a crater… beneath the choir of the Ursuline church." The choir and the chapel are different parts of the same building; "the floor of the chapel" is imprecise and potentially misleading.
**Fix:** Change "floor of the Ursuline convent's chapel" → "floor of the Ursuline chapel's choir" (or "beneath the choir of the Ursuline church").
**Source:** Wikipedia, Ursulines of Quebec; Find a Grave memorial; CBC History; multiple Canadian heritage sources.

## SHOULD-FIX ⚠️

### ⚠️ 3. Parkman book year: "his 1884 book *Montcalm and Wolfe*"
**Draft says:** 1884 — this is confirmed correct (Parkman's *Montcalm and Wolfe* was published in 1884 as volume 6 of *France and England in North America*). ✅ No fix needed — left as ⚠️ only because the draft attributes Wolfe's dying words to "a century later" (1884 is 125 years after 1759, not ~100). Minor rhetorical imprecision.
**Recommendation:** Change "a century later" → "more than a century later" or drop the time reference entirely.

---

### ⚠️ 4. Sainte-Foy artillery: draft says "twenty-two guns" — technically correct but worth a note
**Draft:** *"Murray marched out with about 3,800 men and twenty-two guns"*
**Fact pack:** "20 cannons and 2 howitzers" = 22 guns total. Wikipedia confirms this. The "22 guns" figure is mathematically correct. However, a howitzer is a distinct weapon class from a cannon; "twenty-two guns" is a defensible summary. ✅ No hard error; note that if space permits, "20 cannon and 2 howitzers" is more precise.

---

### ⚠️ 5. Treaty of Paris "meanwhile" block: "ejected France from mainland North America entirely"
**Draft:** *"handing Canada and the lands east of the Mississippi to Britain"*
**Verified:** Correct — Treaty of Paris (1763) gave Canada + all lands east of the Mississippi to Britain; France had already secretly ceded Louisiana (west of Mississippi) to Spain (Treaty of Fontainebleau, 1762). "Ejected France from mainland North America entirely" is accurate and supportable.
**Minor flag:** The "meanwhile" block also says the war debt and proclamation "lit the fuse to the American Revolution." This is a widely accepted framing and defensible as a forward-look editorial claim. No specific numbers cited, so nothing to verify beyond the framing. ✅ Acceptable as written.

---

### ⚠️ 6. Wolfe's dying words framing — confirmed correct, annotate for context
**Draft:** *"By tradition, told and retold a century later by the historian Francis Parkman in his 1884 book… he heard that the French were giving way… then said something close to 'Now, God be praised, I will die in peace.' These words come down through later reconstructions, not a contemporary transcript, and the exact phrasing varies in every telling."*
**Verified:** Correct. The phrase appears in Parkman and in numerous secondary sources. The draft properly marks it as tradition/reconstruction, not a verbatim quote. Gray's Elegy anecdote is also correctly marked as tradition via a named midshipman (John Robison), not a written record. ✅ No fix needed — framing is exactly right.

---

### ⚠️ 7. Montcalm's dying words framing — confirmed correct
**Draft:** *"By the traditional account, again from Parkman, he answered that he was glad of it, that he would not live to see the surrender of Quebec."*
**Verified:** This is a paraphrase (not a verbatim quote), correctly attributed to Parkman-era tradition. The exact wording varies ("So much the better" / "All the better" / "I am happy"). Draft handles this correctly with "by the traditional account" qualifier. ✅ No fix needed.
