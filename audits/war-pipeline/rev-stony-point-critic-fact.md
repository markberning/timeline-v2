## BLOCKERS

**B1 · Collier tribute truncated — the opening bravery clause is missing from the quoted text**

> (S3 prose, QUOTE 3 implicitly, lines 203–204):
> "the attackers had fought with a bravery they had never shown before, and 'showed at this
> moment a generosity and clemency which during the course of the Rebellion had no parallel.'"

The pack (§7.3) gives the full journal text: *"had made the attack with a bravery they never
before exhibited, and they showed at this moment a generosity and clemency which during the
course of the Rebellion had no parallel."* The draft correctly paraphrases the first clause in
its own voice ("had fought with a bravery they had never shown before") but then opens the
direct-quote block on only the second clause. Web-verified: the full sentence is a unit — the
bravery tribute and the clemency tribute are both in the same sentence; the opening clause is not
a separate remark.

Two problems with the current rendering:

1. The paraphrase of the first clause is subtly inaccurate: the original says they made "the
   attack with a bravery they never before exhibited" (i.e., specifically this assault was
   unprecedented, not that the rebels in general had never fought bravely). "They had never shown
   before" could be read to mean American troops never showed bravery before Stony Point, which
   is not what Collier wrote and would be a defamatory misread.

2. The quote block opens mid-sentence, so a reader seeing the quotation marks thinks
   that is the whole of what Collier said, missing the bravery half entirely.

**Fix:** Restore the full sentence as the quotation (the pack flags it MED-HIGH attributed to
his journal, which the draft does correctly). Simplest: open the quote at "had made the attack"
rather than at "showed":

> *"[They] had made the attack with a bravery they never before exhibited, and they showed at
> this moment a generosity and clemency which during the course of the Rebellion had no
> parallel."*

Or keep the paraphrase-then-quote structure but have the quote start at the full sentence
with a "[The rebels]" bracket. Either approach is correct; the truncated mid-sentence quote
block is not.

## SHOULD-FIX

**SF1 · "two years before" (Paoli) — works in context, but the Baylor/Old Tappan grief is
a second documented grievance the pack prescribes naming and the draft names only once, in
the ledger, not in the prose**

> (Section 2 prose, line 186):
> "Two years before, Wayne's own division had been surprised in the dark and bayoneted at
> Paoli, in Pennsylvania, an attack Americans called a massacre, and there had been a second
> such affair at Old Tappan since."

This is correct as written and the pack (§4 restraint beat) does call for both Paoli and
Old Tappan to be named. The prose does name both. However:

> (Section 3 prose, line 199):
> "The men of the Corps of Light Infantry had stormed a defended work in the dark, with Paoli
> to avenge, and had given the surrendering garrison quarter, and contemporaries on both sides
> said so plainly."

Old Tappan drops out of Section 3's framing of the restraint, even though §4 of the pack says
"Paoli (Wayne's own men bayoneted in the dark, September 1777) and the Baylor/Old Tappan affair
(September 1778)" together form the two grievances that make the restraint meaningful. Section 3
is the meaning-beat section — the omission of Old Tappan here slightly weakens the "with every
grievance in their hands" argument the whole section is built on.

**Fix:** In Section 3, one brief reference alongside Paoli: "with Paoli and Old Tappan to
avenge" (three extra words). The full explanation of both appears in Section 2; Section 3 just
needs the pointer.

**SF2 · Wayne's 2 a.m. dispatch — the dossier/stats block and the Wayne bio each drop the
"Stoney" dateline spelling that the pack flags as a key authenticating detail**

The pack (§7.1) specifies: *dateline "Stoney Point 16th July 1779 2 OClock A.M."* — and notes
both Wayne's spelling "Johnston" for Johnson and his spelling "Stoney" for the place. The draft
correctly flags "Johnston" in-prose when quoting the dispatch (Section 2, line 188: "('Johnston'
was his spelling for Johnson.")"). But the "Stoney" dateline spelling is in the pack
specifically because it authenticates the document and the precise 2 a.m. timing. The dossier
stats block (line 28) says "Wayne's victory dispatch is dated 2 o'clock in the morning" — good —
but neither the bio nor the inline quote annotation mentions "Stoney." The quote block itself
also does not carry the dateline.

This is not a factual error but the pack explicitly singles out the "Stoney" spelling alongside
"Johnston" as a byte-exact authenticating detail that the task brief specifically asked to
verify. The draft's treatment leaves half of that verified detail out of the reader-facing text.

**Fix:** When the dispatch quote appears in Section 2, the annotation after it currently reads
"('Johnston' was his spelling for Johnson.)". Expand to: "('Johnston' was his spelling for
Johnson; the dispatch is dateline 'Stoney Point, 16th July 1779, 2 OClock A.M.' — Wayne's
own hand.)" Or fold it into the sentence that introduces the quote. Either way, the "Stoney"
spelling belongs alongside "Johnston" in the reader-facing annotation.

**SF3 · Stewart medal — the outcome section identifies him as "Major John Stewart (American)"
but never names his role (north column van commander) or his state (Maryland), which the pack
prescribes as the extent of his biography**

> (Outcome section, line 52):
> "silver medals to Fleury and Stewart"
> (Section 3 prose, line 205):
> "Major John Stewart (American), who had led the north column's van, got the war's other
> silver medal for Stony Point."

The Section 3 prose is correct and matches the pack's biography floor ("led the north column's
van; voted the war's other silver medal for Stony Point"). The outcome block on line 52 just
says "silver for Fleury and Stewart" — no role, no state. That is understandable compression in
the outcome block, but since Stewart has no dedicated commander bio card (he appears in neither
the dossier commanders section nor the bio list in the ledger), this one Section 3 sentence is
his only biographical identification. The pack (§3) says "Maj. John Stewart (Maryland) [HIGH on
role]." The draft gives his rank and role correctly in Section 3, but nowhere says "Maryland."

**Fix:** In Section 3 line 205, change "Major John Stewart (American)" to "Major John Stewart
of Maryland (American)" — one word, matching the pack's material-level identification.

**N2 · "five hundred prisoners" in the dossier note (line 17) uses a round figure; the
stats block correctly gives "roughly 470–540"**

> (Dossier note, line 17): "They took some five hundred prisoners and gave quarter"

The dossier note is the short reader-facing introduction above the stats block; "some five
hundred" is idiomatic and defensible (it lands within the 470–540 range the pack authorizes,
and "some" signals approximation). The stats block immediately below gives the full range. No
factual error; the reviser should confirm they are comfortable with "some five hundred" as an
idiomatic shorthand that matches "roughly 470–540" in the stats block below it.

**N3 · "British (Brig. Gen. Thomas Stirling)" — pack spells it "Stirling"; web-confirmed
correct; the draft has "Stirling" on line 209 (Section 3) and in the ledger — both consistent
with pack. No error.**

The one note here: the pack spells the name "Stirling" (§6); the draft matches; web spot-check
confirmed Thomas Stirling, 7th baronet, commanding the reoccupying force. Flagged only because
the surname is close to "Sterling" and a copyediting eye might "correct" it erroneously.
