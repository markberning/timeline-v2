## Opening / why it met
- War already sparking on the Ohio in summer 1754 → §1 (war already underway).
- "land beyond the Appalachian Mountains... colonies, France, and Native nations all claimed" → §1 + §3 Hendrick land-quarrel quote (lands "which belong to us"); Appalachian framing is general geographic context consistent with §1's "Ohio" frontier.
- Lieutenant Colonel George Washington, marched west spring 1754 → §1 (Washington's frontier reverses). Marquee name, no side-tag (correct).
- Jumonville Glen skirmish, May 1754 → §1.
- Fort Necessity surrender, July 3, 1754 → §1.
- "first sparks of... war fought on three continents" → §1 ("opening clashes of what became the global Seven Years' War"). "Three continents" = popular shorthand for the global Seven Years' War; supported by "global."
- Seven colonies met at Albany, NY, that June → §2.
- "the meeting was not really their idea" → §1 (Board of Trade ordered it).

## Why London called the meeting
- Board of Trade in London ordered the meeting → §1.
- First order of business = repair Iroquois alliance, ahead of union talk → §0, §1, §3.
- Covenant Chain = metaphor/relationship linking Iroquois (and other nations) to British colonies, esp. New York → §3 (Covenant Chain definition).
- No formal treaty council at Albany in ~3 years → §1, §3 (Hendrick quote: "three years since we have been called to any public treaty here").
- Neglect pushed Iroquois toward neutrality or France → §1.
- "a frontier war could not be fought without them... precondition for everything else" → §1.
- Board of Trade specified proper presents → §1, §3 (Gifts).

## The colonies that came
- Seven colonies, 21 commissioners; NY, PA, MD, NH, MA, RI, CT → §2.
- Virginia, New Jersey, southern colonies did not attend; plan later set aside seats → §2.
- James DeLancey, Lieutenant Governor of NY, chairman → §2. Side-tag (British) applied (not a marquee name). "Lieutenant Governor" spelled out, defined as deputy to royal governor → consistent with §2.
- Thomas Hutchinson (MA), helped draft plan, later royal governor → §2, §5. Side-tag (British) applied.
- Stephen Hopkins (RI), later Declaration signer → §2. Side-tag (British) applied.
- William Johnson (NY), key Iroquois intermediary, later Superintendent of Indian Affairs → §2. Side-tag (British) applied.
- Benjamin Franklin of Pennsylvania, arrived with the plan → §2, §5. Marquee, no side-tag (correct).
- Met daily, city hall at Albany, June 19 – July 11, 1754 → §2.

- Council fire at Onondaga, central New York → §3.
- "not anyone's subjects... negotiated like a power... played empires against each other" → §3 NATIVE-FIRST framing + §0.

- Five Hendrick quotes used verbatim, all attributed "as recorded at Albany, 1754" → §3 (verbatim quote block). Provenance hedge ("interpreters and clerks... as recorded, not a recording") → §3 provenance note + §9.
  - "ancient place of treaty..." → §3.

  - "Look at the French, they are men... you are like women, bare and open..." → §3.

## The land taken in the next room
- Wyoming Valley deed, on/around July 11, 1754, Connecticut's Susquehannah Company, ~£2,000, called fraudulent; secret, liquor/bribes, land Mohawk did not rightfully control → §4.
- £2,000 glossed as British pounds → reasonable gloss of "£".
- Actual occupants = Delaware (Lenape), already displaced by Walking Purchase of 1737, and Shawnee; neither a party → §4.
- Exclusion pushed Delaware and Shawnee toward the French, with bloody consequences on the PA frontier → §4.
- Conrad Weiser (PA Indian agent), deed to unceded Iroquois land west of Susquehanna toward Ohio/Lake Erie, nominal sum + promise of more → §4. Side-tag (British) applied. "Cede/unceded" glossed (genuine jargon per voice rules).
- Overlapping claims → Pennamite-Yankee Wars from 1769 → §4.
- "held alongside the diplomacy" dual-truth → §4 author note + §9.

## Franklin's bigger idea
- Franklin argued colonies were dangerously divided while France acted as one → §6 (editorial on "present disunited state of the British Colonies") + §5 origin.
- "Join, or Die": woodcut by Franklin, Pennsylvania Gazette, May 9, 1754; first American political cartoon → §6.
- Snake cut into eight pieces, labeled by colony/region; "JOIN, or DIE." motto → §6. (Draft says "colony or region" — accurate because N.E. = one segment for four colonies, §6.)
- Folk superstition: snake rejoined before sunset could revive → §6.
- Timing: ran May 9, BEFORE the congress (June 19); argument for union, not product of Albany → §6 [TIMING].
- Vote June 24 to take up union; committee draft submitted June 28; adopted July 10, one day before close → §2 (pacing) + §5 (adopted July 10).
- Hutchinson among those shaping the draft → §5.
- President-General: single executive appointed and paid by the Crown; consent required for all council acts → §5.
- Grand Council: members chosen by colonial assemblies (elected); meets annually at Philadelphia; 3-year terms → §5.
- "assemblies = elected bodies that governed each colony" → gloss consistent with §5.
- Apportionment: 48 seats; MA 7, VA 7, PA 6, CT 5, ... NH 2, RI 2; adjusted after 3 years by financial contribution → §5.
- Powers: common defense (raise/pay soldiers, build forts, armed ships); Indian affairs (treaties, war/peace, regulate trade); western lands (buy from Native nations for Crown, manage settlements); taxes/duties → §5.
- "the three things... defense, Indian affairs, western lands" → §5 author note (all three explicitly in the plan).

## Adopted, then refused by everyone
- Congress adopted it, sent to assemblies and London; rejected both directions → §7.
- Assemblies: too much power taken from them (too much central/prerogative authority) → §7.
- Crown/Board of Trade: gave colonies too much union/autonomy; preferred direct military control from home; never enacted → §7.
- Two Franklin Autobiography quotes ("too much prerogative... too much of the democratic"; "true medium... happy for both sides the water") → §7. See [VERIFY] below.

## What the failure rehearsed
- First serious proposal of intercolonial union; failed 1754; became precedent → §8.
- Resurfaced at Stamp Act Congress (1765) and First Continental Congress (1774); union returned "in anger" ~20 years later → §8.
- Franklin "might-have-been" quote ("colonies, so united... bloody contest... avoided") → §8. See [VERIFY] below.
- Closing frame: name "French and Indian War" centers British-colonial POV, names enemies; Native nations fought on all sides and for themselves/land/sovereignty; played empires off each other; war ended as catastrophe for eastern-woodlands Native nations who lost the leverage of two competing empires and the land → F&I FRAMING brief + §3 NATIVE-FIRST + §4 dispossession. ("Eastern woodlands" is a standard regional descriptor for the nations named in the pack — Iroquois, Delaware, Shawnee; no new named nation introduced.)
- "Join, or Die" afterlife: reused in Stamp Act crisis / Revolution as resistance symbol, meaning Franklin did not intend → §6 (Afterlife). (Figure caption.)
