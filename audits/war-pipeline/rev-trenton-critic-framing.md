# FRAMING CRITIC — Battle of Trenton draft (rev-trenton)

Critic: framing (fairness, emphasis, interpretive honesty).
Inputs: `rev-trenton-draft.md` against `rev-trenton-factpack.md` (sole source).
Date: 2026-06-10

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

No blockers. The draft's framing work is largely excellent: the Hessians are people
throughout, all three myth-handlings land as story rather than gotcha, the exposure-deaths
honesty is prominently and correctly weighted, and Washington's gamble is told as
desperation-forced with the two failed crossings kept fully honest. The fixes are a small
number of unpack-backed superlatives/causal claims and one ledger/prose mismatch.

## Dimension-by-dimension assessment

### 1. Hessian humanity — STRONG, no findings
- Section 3 carries the subsidy story with full dignity: "the men in the column were not
  soldiers of fortune chasing loot contracts... conscripted subjects serving for their
  ordinary army pay, sent across an ocean by a prince balancing his books. They were good
  troops" — exactly the pack §7 picture. The Hesse-Kassel MEANWHILE ("The men captured at
  Trenton were entries in that ledger") lands the point that the soldiers were the
  commodity, not the customer.
- The BATTLE telling (section 2) also gives them dignity, which is the harder test: "the
  garrison turned out fast and fought," the three regiments named, Wiederholdt's alarm
  given in his own German with translation (a Hessian voice inside the American victory
  story), Rall re-forming in the orchard and leading the counterattack in which he was
  shot, the surrendering regiments "struck their colors" (defined, not mocked). The
  Wiederholdt sketch-map image ("the fight at Trenton as drawn by a man who was in it")
  gives a Hessian authorship of the battle's own record.
- Rall is humanized at every appearance: career credentials up front in section 1
  ("a hard-fighting career soldier who had led the decisive assault at Fort Washington"),
  his errors attributed to documented contempt and over-confidence rather than buffoonery,
  and the court-martial beat ("fixed the blame on Rall, who was dead and could not
  answer") is openly sympathetic. The dossier side note leads with "Exhausted by weeks of
  militia alarms, not drunk."

### 2. Myth-retirement framing — STRONG, no findings
- Drunk myth: "Generations of storytellers have the Hessians sleeping off a drunken
  Christmas; no eyewitness on either side describes drunkenness, and when the shooting
  started the garrison turned out fast and fought." Corrective without smugness; the
  exhaustion explanation immediately replaces what it removes (good story mechanics, not
  a debunk-shaped hole).
- Pocket note: "it is the story the Hessians told afterward... No note survives" — told
  inside the Hessians' own attribution exactly as pack §10.5 instructs, and it works AS
  a story (found on him as he lay dying).
- Paine read-aloud: "traces back to a biography written in 1809, a generation later, and
  no eyewitness mentions it. Maybe it happened. What is certain is the text itself, and
  the moment it was written for." This is the model badge — honest, generous, and it
  pivots to what IS solid.

### 3. Victory proportion + exposure honesty — GOOD, two SHOULD-FIX
- Scale honesty holds: "a reinforced brigade outpost" proportion is kept via concrete
  numbers everywhere (~1,500 garrison, ~2,400 attackers, 4–6 wounded), the dossier
  verdict is "Decisive American victory · the revolution survives its worst month" (no
  "turning point of the war" inflation), and the consequence claims stay inside the
  pack's own "flipped the psychology" / "saved the army-in-being" frame — except the
  closing sentence (SHOULD-FIX 2).
- The exposure-deaths honesty SURVIVES AND IS PROPERLY WEIGHTED — this is the draft's
  best framing move. It appears twice: the dossier casualties footnote, and a dedicated
  section-3 paragraph placed immediately after the "Our Loss is very trifling" pull
  quote, where it directly counters the triumphal document the reader has just read
  ("Honesty requires the next sentence... the weather was another army, and it collected
  its own casualties"). Phrasing matches the pack's required qualified form ("by some
  accounts more men than the battle had cost the Hessians in dead"). Any revision must
  preserve both the placement (right after the Hancock quote) and the qualifier.

### 4. Washington — GOOD, no hagiography findings
- The gamble is desperation-framed start to finish: the enlistment cliff as "the real
  enemy... the calendar," his own "game is pretty near up" and "necessity, dire
  necessity" quotes carrying the interpretation, "a gambler's plan, because only a
  gambler's plan fit the situation."
- The failed crossings are kept fully honest in all four surfaces: dossier side note
  ("The two supporting crossings, ~2,200–2,600 more men, never got over"), section 2
  ("The other two crossings failed" as a paragraph opener), Locator 1 caption ("Only
  Washington's column got over"), and the Bordentown MEANWHILE makes the open bridge —
  the plan's failure — "the one flaw."
- The rally beat is attributed and unvarnished: "by a sergeant's account rode along the
  line and asked again when no one stepped forward at first. About half of the eligible
  men stayed in the moment." No one stepping forward at first is exactly the
  anti-hagiography detail; it is kept.
- The Wilkinson "glorious day" line is badged hard ("written forty years later by a man
  with a famously high opinion of himself... take it as Wilkinson remembered it").

### 5. Emphasis vs. pack confidence — mostly clean
- Duration rendered exactly per pack instruction (no bare "45 minutes"); MED quotes all
  carry their attribution badges in prose; the deliberately-written-around list (no
  Frederick praise, no Cornwallis number, no parade date) checks out in the prose.
- Monroe is proportionate: one sentence in the battle, one commander card, the
  president beat used once each — not inflated into a co-star.
- Findings below: one unpack-backed superlative (SHOULD-FIX 1), one over-stacked causal
  close (SHOULD-FIX 2), small invented specifics (NITs).

### 6. Captions and dossier surfaces — clean
- Trumbull hero: "Composed decades after the battle and idealized; Washington and Rall
  never met mid-field like this." ✓ (both dossier and IMAGES block)
- Leutze: 1851/Düsseldorf/seventy-five years later + "deliberately heroic and wrong on
  the details... the image of the event, not a record of it." ✓
- Sullivan: "an imagined likeness... no from-life portrait of Sullivan exists," carried
  in BOTH the caption and the bio. ✓
- Glover: NONE with the rights reason stated; bio carries the story. ✓
- Rall: NONE with "No authentic portrait of him survives" on the card, plus the
  do-not-substitute-Trumbull-without-caveat instruction. ✓
- Winner line "America, decisively" — pack-backed, proportionate, no flourish. ✓

## SHOULD-FIX

1. **"The cheapest victory of the war" — unpack-backed superlative.**
   Draft (section 3): "The cheapest victory of the war was not free; the bill just came
   later, and quietly."
   The pack supports "famously tiny" losses (§5), not a war-wide ranking. The superlative
   is also self-undermining in a sentence whose whole point is that the victory was NOT
   cheap. FIX: "A victory this cheap was not free; the bill just came later, and quietly."
   (or "The nearly bloodless victory was not free..."). Keep the rest of the paragraph
   exactly as is.

2. **Closing sentence over-stacks causality and prints an out-of-pack figure.**
   Draft (section 3): "...and the war went on for six and a half more years because
   roughly 2,400 freezing men, about half of whom then agreed to stay for ten dollars,
   made it possible for there to be a war at all."
   Two problems: (a) "six and a half more years" appears nowhere in the pack — no war-end
   date or duration is in scope; (b) "the war went on... because... made it possible for
   there to be a war at all" doubles the causal claim past the pack's "saved the
   Revolution's army-in-being" (§1). FIX: "...and the war went on because roughly 2,400
   freezing men, about half of whom then agreed to stay for ten dollars, kept an army in
   the field for it to be fought with." (Drops the duration, single causal step, keeps
   the arithmetic and the ten-dollar callback.)

3. **Ledger claims a Stephen-fury rendering the prose does not carry.**
   FACT LEDGER (section 2 block): "Washington's fury rendered as 'by one account telling
   him he might have ruined all his plans' (wording MED, not quoted)" — but the section-2
   prose stops at "sent across by General Adam Stephen without Washington's knowledge,
   had hit an outpost and vanished." No fury beat exists in the prose. Either the ledger
   line is stale (then delete it) or the beat was cut (then the ledger must say so). The
   fury beat is also a fairness asset if restored — it shows the "accidental gift" was an
   indiscipline Washington condemned, not cleverness — but restoring it is the reviser's
   call; the mismatch itself is the defect.

## NIT

1. **"every British post in New Jersey would know by nightfall" — invented specificity.**
   (Bordentown MEANWHILE.) The pack supports the escapees carrying the news, not a
   by-nightfall timetable. FIX: "...and it meant the news of what had happened at Trenton
   was already running south to every British post in New Jersey."

2. **Rum→myth connection left on the table.** Pack Known Uncertainty 6 notes the ~40
   hogsheads of captured rum "likely helped the myth along." The draft mentions the rum
   (section 3) and retires the myth (section 2) but never connects them. One clause —
   e.g., in section 2: "(the forty barrels of rum the Americans found in the town the
   next morning probably helped the legend along)" — would make the myth-retirement even
   more generous: it explains how honest people came to believe it. Optional.

3. **Monroe image caption: "carried off the field with a severed artery."** The severed
   artery is pack-backed; "carried off the field" is not in the pack. FIX: "...he was an
   eighteen-year-old lieutenant, nearly killed by a ball that severed an artery in his
   shoulder."

4. **Outcome text: "fixed the blame on him alone."** The court-martial fixing blame on
   Rall is pack-backed (details MED), but "alone" overstates within a sentence that has
   no room for the pack's fuller reckoning (Howe's outpost chain condemned in London,
   Grant sharing criticism, §6). FIX: drop "alone" — "a Hessian court-martial later fixed
   the blame on him." (Section 3's "dead and could not answer" already carries the
   scapegoat note with the right weight.)

5. **"an otherwise total victory" (Bordentown MEANWHILE).** "Total" is a mild superlative
   the pack renders as "decisive"; with ~250–500 escaped, "near-total" or "an otherwise
   complete victory over the garrison" is tighter. Lowest priority.
