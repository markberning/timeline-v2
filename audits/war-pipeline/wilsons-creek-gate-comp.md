## MUST-ADD (Essential, blocking)

### MUST-ADD 1: Camp Jackson riot as Confederate recruiting tool — the neutral-Missourian conversion effect

**What is missing:** The draft says Camp Jackson "lit secessionist feeling on fire" but stops there. The specific, documented consequence — that Lyon's own action at Camp Jackson *converted thousands of previously neutral Missourians to the Confederate cause* — is absent from the draft. This is not a minor nuance; it is the mechanism that explains the scale of the guerrilla war the draft describes at the end. The draft cannot credibly say Missouri became the third-most-fought state while omitting the reason: Lyon's aggressive opening move in May 1861 gave Price and Jackson a moral recruiting argument that worked. Multiple sources (civilwaronthewesternborder.org, Camp Jackson Wikipedia, ABT) make this explicit.

**What to add (1–2 sentences in the Camp Jackson section):** Something like: "The violence did exactly what Jackson needed it to do. Tens of thousands of Missourians who had wanted nothing to do with the war — who had voted for neutrality back in March — now had a reason to hate the Union army. Camp Jackson gave the Confederate cause in Missouri more recruits than a dozen speeches would have."

**Fact pack status:** The pack mentions "energized secessionist sentiment" and "panicked the legislature" but does not fully develop the neutral-to-Confederate conversion. The author needs to add this point to the pack *or* can source it directly from civilwaronthewesternborder.org ("driving thousands of hitherto unaligned Missourians to the Confederate cause") and Camp Jackson Wikipedia ("forcing most Missourians to take a side").

---

### MUST-ADD 2: Lyon's contested legacy — the irony that the "Savior of Missouri" also inflamed the war he was saving it from

**What is missing:** The draft closes on a clean heroic beat: "Lyon lost the battle, lost his life, and somehow still won the thing he'd died for." That is a true statement and a good one. But it is the *only* frame the draft offers for Lyon's legacy. The emergingcivilwar.com article (already in the fact pack, §7/§10) explicitly names the interpretive dispute: "Historians debate whether he was a patriotic hero or a zealous radical who unnecessarily escalated Missouri's conflict." The draft's closing italic hands the reader a settled verdict that historians have not actually settled. A strong telling does not need to resolve the debate — it needs to *acknowledge* it, because the tension is the story.

**What to add:** One or two sentences in the closing section (death of Lyon or Missouri's fate) that surface the historical argument: Lyon's aggressiveness secured Missouri but also made the war in Missouri nastier than it had to be. The reader should leave knowing that the question is genuinely open.

**Fact pack status:** The information is in the fact pack (§7 Lyon's legacy, §10 interpretive dispute). This is a draft selection failure, not a pack gap.

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (Enriching, not blocking)

### SC-1: Price was also wounded during the battle

The fact pack records that Price suffered a "painful side wound" during the fighting (§2, §6). The draft mentions Lyon's multiple wounds in detail but never mentions that Price was hit too. This is a useful symmetry — the battle wounded the commanders of both forces, killed one. It takes one sentence and would land well in the Bloody Hill section alongside the McCulloch command-friction discussion.

**Fact pack status:** Present (§2, §6). Draft omission only.

### SC-2: The Texas cavalry rear-charge attempt

The fact pack mentions a Texas cavalry unit that attempted a circuitous rear charge against the Union position and was repulsed (§4). The draft omits this. It is a brief, colorful detail that slightly enriches the Bloody Hill section — it shows the Confederates were not just grinding up the slope but trying flanking moves that also failed. One sentence would cover it.

**Fact pack status:** Present (§4). Draft omission only.

### SC-3: Dug Springs skirmish (August 2) as Lyon's final intelligence moment

The fact pack references the August 2 Battle/Skirmish at Dug Springs (§3), where Lyon marched out to probe Confederate strength, won a minor engagement, and then confirmed he was outnumbered more than 2-to-1 before retreating to Springfield. The draft entirely skips from mid-July (Lyon at Springfield with ~7,000 men) to "by early August the math caught up with him." Dug Springs is the specific moment Lyon confirmed his odds and made his final calculus. It is not essential — the draft's "end of the tether" section reads fine without it — but adding one sentence ("A brief skirmish at Dug Springs on August 2 confirmed what Lyon already feared...") would sharpen the timeline from Springfield to the decision to attack.

**Fact pack status:** Briefly in §3. Could use one sentence of elaboration in the pack and draft.

### SC-4: Sigel's subsequent reputation damage from Wilson's Creek

The fact pack notes (§6) that Sigel "faced criticism after the battle." The draft ends Sigel's arc with the cornfield escape and does not note that Wilson's Creek damaged his professional standing. This is genuinely optional — the draft is not Sigel's biography — but a one-sentence coda in the aftermath section would close the loop for the reader who just watched Sigel's column collapse.

**Fact pack status:** Single note in §6. Present but thin; would need one additional sourced sentence.

### SC-5: McCulloch's own death at Pea Ridge, March 1862

The draft mentions Pea Ridge as where the organized Confederate threat was broken. The fact pack (§6) records that McCulloch himself was killed there on March 7, 1862. Adding this takes half a sentence and gives Pea Ridge a narrative resonance it currently lacks: the man who refused to chase after Wilson's Creek paid for the Trans-Mississippi war with his life the following spring.

**Fact pack status:** Present (§6). Draft omission only.

## Fact Pack Blind Spots

The pack is thorough and well-sourced. The following are genuine gaps that would need research if the corresponding points are added to the draft.

### Pack Gap 1: The neutral-to-Confederate conversion after Camp Jackson — needs expansion

The pack states the Camp Jackson Affair "energized secessionist sentiment" but does not quantify or characterize the conversion of neutral Missourians. The civilwaronthewesternborder.org source already in the pack's reference list contains stronger language ("driving thousands of hitherto unaligned Missourians to the Confederate cause"). The pack should quote or paraphrase this more explicitly so the author has it at hand for MUST-ADD 1.

### Pack Gap 2: The Dug Springs skirmish is mentioned but not developed

The pack mentions Dug Springs in §3 as a single-sentence item ("After the August 2 Battle/Skirmish at Dug Springs, McCulloch positioned his army..."). It does not give Lyon's reaction — specifically that after the skirmish Lyon confirmed his odds were worse than 2-to-1 and retreated to Springfield. This is the turning point from "cautious probe" to "decide whether to attack or retreat." If SC-3 is used, the pack needs a half-sentence addition.

### Pack Gap 3: Lyon's "Savior of Missouri" label and the counter-argument need fuller sourcing

The pack's §7 mentions the interpretive dispute but cites only emergingcivilwar.com for the "zealous radical" framing. The civilwaronthewesternborder.org entry on Lyon is a richer source for this debate and should be cross-referenced. Not a blocking gap, but if MUST-ADD 2 is written, the author should cite both.

### Pack Gap 4: Price's wound — source should be verified in the pack

§2 mentions Price's wound briefly ("Price also wounded (painful side wound) during the fighting (ABT article)"). The ABT article should be confirmed as the authoritative source here, as the draft currently does not use this detail at all. Low priority, but if SC-1 is added, the pack citation should be confirmed.

## Summary Verdict

**MUST-ADD count: 2**

Both are selection failures (information exists in the fact pack or in the pack's cited sources; the author simply did not include it) rather than fact-pack gaps requiring new research.

**Most important:** MUST-ADD 2 (Lyon's contested legacy). The draft currently closes with an unambiguous heroic verdict that historians dispute. A selective telling that ends with "he died for Missouri and Missouri stayed" without acknowledging the Camp Jackson irony — that Lyon's own aggression helped ignite the brutal guerrilla war the draft then describes — is incomplete as storytelling, not just incomplete as history. This is the central tension of Wilson's Creek; it belongs in the draft.

MUST-ADD 1 (the Camp Jackson neutral-to-Confederate conversion) is a close second: without it, the reader has no mechanism to explain why the guerrilla war that closes the draft was as savage and sustained as it was.

Both can be added with one to three sentences each. Neither requires new research beyond sources already in the pack's reference list.
