### Combined / the percentage record
- **Combined casualties: ~24,645** (ABT) — "nearly 24,000." (ABT; ABT 10-Facts)
- **Casualty rate: ~32.7% of all troops engaged** (WP), reported by ABT as **3.8% killed / 19.8% wounded / 7.9% missing-captured** → among the **greatest percentage of casualties of any major Civil War battle** — ABT states it was higher than Shiloh and Antietam. (WP; ABT)
- **Competing framing:** ABT "10 Facts" phrases it as **"~24,000 casualties out of ~81,000 engaged — a 29% rate, second only to Gettysburg's 31%"** among major battles. (ABT 10-Facts)
- **Reconcile for author:** Both framings are sourced and both say "one of the very highest of the war." The cleanest defensible claim: *the highest (or among the highest) percentage casualty rate of any major Civil War battle.* Avoid asserting a single hard rank without the qualifier. (WP; ABT)

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy — relative positions)

| Feature | Description / relative position | Source |
|---------|--------------------------------|--------|
| **Stones River (West Fork)** | Flows roughly south-to-north through the field; Murfreesboro sits on/just east; armies fought mostly on the WEST bank, with the Jan 2 action erupting on the EAST bank | WP; NPS |
| **Nashville Pike (Turnpike)** | Ran NW from Murfreesboro toward Nashville; the Union's **one open supply line** by midday Dec 31 — holding it = holding the battle | WP; NPS |
| **Nashville & Chattanooga Railroad** | Ran roughly parallel to the Nashville Pike; the Union fell back onto the pike-and-railroad line and anchored there | WP; NPS |
| **Round Forest ("Hell's Half Acre")** | A ~4-acre rocky cedar copse forming a salient on the Union LEFT-CENTER, near the pike/railroad; Hazen's brigade held it against repeated attacks Dec 31. Today's "Cotton Field Trail" runs through it | WP; NPS; ABT |
| **The cotton field** | Open ground in front of / adjoining the Round Forest, crossed under fire; gives the modern NPS "Cotton Field Trail" its name | NPS |
| **Cedar brakes / glades** | Dense eastern-red-cedar thickets over barren limestone (karst) flats and outcrops, broken by open "glades" — choked formations, hid movement, defined the Dec 31 fighting | WP; NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory |
| **The Slaughter Pen** | A particularly deadly cedar-brake sector where Sheridan's division fought a 4-hour holding action on the Union right-center | WP; ABT |
| **Wilkinson Pike (Turnpike)** | Ran roughly W from Murfreesboro; McCook's broken right wing fell back **behind the Wilkinson Turnpike** as the line jackknifed | NPS |
| **McFadden's Ford** | Crossing on Stones River N of the Nashville Pike unit; the point where Breckinridge's Jan 2 assault was destroyed by massed Union guns firing from the WEST bank across the river | WP; ABT; NPS |
| **Overall Creek** | Beyond McCook's right — the Union right flank was **not anchored** on it (hung in the air), enabling the wheel/envelopment | WP |
| **Tullahoma, TN** | ~36 mi south; Bragg's withdrawal destination | WP |

> **Map shape in one line:** Both armies camped facing each other astride Stones River W of Murfreesboro along the Nashville Pike/railroad axis; the fight pivoted on the Union RIGHT (W of the river) on Dec 31, hinged at the Round Forest near the pike, then jumped to the EAST bank at McFadden's Ford on Jan 2.

## 5. Sequence / Turning Points

### Pre-battle (night of Dec 30, 1862)
- Both generals independently planned to **attack the OTHER's right flank at dawn** — mirror-image battle plans. (WP; ABT 10-Facts)
- **Dueling bands / shared "Home! Sweet Home!":** the night before, regimental bands of both armies serenaded each other ("Yankee Doodle" answered by "Dixie," etc.); when one struck up *Home! Sweet Home!*, bands and soldiers on **both** sides joined in together. (Rutherford County Historical Society; House Divided / Dickinson College; widely documented. **[VERIFY] best to cite a specific eyewitness/regimental source for exact attribution if quoting a soldier**)

### December 31, 1862 — Bragg strikes first
- ~6:00 a.m.: **Hardee's corps (McCown's then Cleburne's divisions) hit the Union right first**, before Rosecrans could launch his own attack — catching many Union troops at breakfast. McCown's ~10,000 men attacked in a great wave. (WP; ABT)
- The Union right (McCook) was **wheeled/folded back like a jackknife** — driven ~3 miles back to the Nashville Pike & railroad by ~10:00 a.m.; McCook fell back behind the Wilkinson Turnpike. (WP; NPS)
- **Sheridan's stand** in the cedar brakes ("Slaughter Pen"): held ~4 hours at brutal cost — all three of his brigade commanders killed, over one-third casualties — buying time before pulling back ~11 a.m. when ammunition ran out. (WP; ABT)
- **Garesché killed** — Rosecrans's chief of staff decapitated by a cannonball riding beside him. (WP)
- **The Round Forest ("Hell's Half Acre"):** Hazen's brigade anchored the Union left-center near the pike and held the salient against as many as four separate Confederate attacks — the pivot that kept the line from collapsing. (WP; NPS)
- **Confederate self-inflicted friction:** false reports of Union forces on the Lebanon Pike + the Bragg–Breckinridge feud delayed/diverted Breckinridge's division from reinforcing the main blow — Bragg never landed the knockout. (WP; ABT 10-Facts)
- End of day: Bragg believed he'd won; wired Richmond essentially "the enemy is falling back." **Rosecrans's night council: some urged retreat; Rosecrans, backed by Thomas and Crittenden, chose to stay and fight.** (WP)

### January 1, 1863 — the pause
- Largely quiet; both armies rested on New Year's Day. Rosecrans pre-dawn ordered a division (Beatty's, after Van Cleve's wounding) to cross to the EAST bank and seize the high ground / artillery platform. Polk's minor probes; Wheeler's cavalry kept raiding Union supply on the pike. (WP)
- **Emancipation Proclamation takes effect Jan 1, 1863 — mid-battle** (see §6). (Lincoln signed/issued Jan 1, 1863.)

### January 2, 1863 — Breckinridge's doomed assault
- ~4:00 p.m.: Bragg ordered **Breckinridge to attack the Union division on the hill east of the river.** Breckinridge **protested it as suicidal**, then attacked. (WP; ABT)
- Initial Confederate success drove the Union back **across McFadden's Ford** — then ran into **Mendenhall's massed artillery on the west bank** (~45–58 guns; ABT "nearly 50 cannon hub-to-hub," WP details 45 on the ridge + 12 more enfilading): the guns shredded the assault in under an hour. (WP; ABT)
- **Over 1,800 Confederate casualties in roughly an hour** (~36% of ~5,000 attackers); Hanson's Kentucky "Orphan Brigade" nearly destroyed (Hanson mortally wounded). A Union counterattack (Negley's division) routed the survivors. (WP; ABT 10-Facts)

### January 3 → retreat
- Jan 3: minor actions; Rosecrans reinforced and resupplied. **Bragg, with ~20,000 effectives, fearing Rosecrans's buildup and a rising river from freezing rain, withdrew toward Tullahoma the night of Jan 3.** Rosecrans occupied Murfreesboro Jan 5; no pursuit. (WP)

## 6. The Cause — Slavery (SHIP-BLOCKING throughline)

- **The Emancipation Proclamation took effect on January 1, 1863 — literally during the battle's New Year's pause.** Lincoln issued it that morning; it declared enslaved people in the rebelling states "forever free" and authorized Black enlistment. This is the strongest possible factual hook tying this battle to slavery/emancipation. (Standard EP date; ECW.)
- **The war's purpose had just become explicitly about ending slavery** — Stones River is the first major battle fought *under* the Proclamation. A Union defeat here, days after the Fredericksburg disaster, would have undercut the Proclamation politically at the moment it took legal effect. (ECW)
- ECW frames it directly: Lincoln's victory "put some vitally needed military muscle behind the Emancipation Proclamation"; had Rosecrans withdrawn, "the story of the Emancipation Proclamation would have been written far differently." (ECW)
- **Author guidance (per the standing framing gate):** name slavery as the war's cause in the section's OWN prose — the Emancipation-takes-effect-mid-battle fact is the natural, documented vehicle. Do not treat this as a battle disconnected from why the war was fought. (Project framing rule; ECW)

## 7. Human Cost / Vivid Documented Detail

- **"Hell's Half Acre"** — the soldiers' own name for the Round Forest; close, hand-to-hand fighting in a rocky cedar copse, repeated assaults, the most intense combat of Dec 31. (WP; NPS; ABT)
- **"The Slaughter Pen"** — Sheridan's sector; all three of his brigade commanders killed; >1/3 of his division down in ~4 hours. (WP; ABT)
- **Col. Julius Garesché** — Rosecrans's chief of staff, decapitated by a cannonball at Rosecrans's side; a named, vivid, documented death. (WP)
- **The Orphan Brigade (Hanson's Kentuckians)** — nearly destroyed in Breckinridge's Jan 2 charge; Breckinridge reportedly anguished ("My poor Orphans!"-type laments are attributed but **[VERIFY] exact wording before quoting**). Hanson mortally wounded. (WP)
- **The shared "Home! Sweet Home!" the night before** — bands and men of both armies singing together hours before slaughtering each other; one of the war's most-cited human moments. (Rutherford County Historical Society; House Divided/Dickinson)
- **Scale of suffering:** ~24,000+ casualties from ~76,400–81,000 engaged in 3 days — the highest *percentage* of a major battle (or 2nd to Gettysburg, per framing). The toll is the headline. (WP; ABT)

### Lincoln's later thanks to Rosecrans — VERIFIED
- **Exact quote:** *"I can never forget, whilst I remember anything, that about the end of last year, and beginning of this, you gave us a hard earned victory, which, had there been a defeat instead, the nation could scarcely have lived over."*
- **Attribution / date:** Abraham Lincoln to Maj. Gen. William S. Rosecrans, **letter dated August 31, 1863** (written months after the battle, in the Collected Works of Abraham Lincoln). (ECW; multiple secondary sources)
- **Common short form** (used by ABT/popular sources): *"You gave us a hard-earned victory, which had there been a defeat instead, the nation could scarcely have lived over."* — same quote, lightly compressed; the long form above is the verifiable full text. (ECW)

## 9. Significance / What It Changed

- **A costly Union strategic victory** — tactically close to a draw, but Bragg retreated, so the Union held the field and the campaign. (WP; ABT; CWSAC)
- **Morale after Fredericksburg:** came days after the Union's bloody December disaster at Fredericksburg; Stones River gave the North a badly needed win at year's turn. (ABT; ECW)
- **Secured Middle Tennessee & Nashville** as a forward Union base; nullified the Confederate threat to Kentucky/Middle Tennessee for the moment. (WP; ABT)
- **Backstopped the Emancipation Proclamation** politically at the exact moment it took effect (see §6); helped discourage European recognition of the Confederacy. (WP; ECW)
- **Cost vs. result:** strategically valuable but bought at one of the highest casualty *rates* of the war — the win was real but Pyrrhic-adjacent, and Rosecrans did not pursue (a recurring critique). (WP; ABT)
- **Setup for the next campaign:** Bragg fell back to Tullahoma, setting up Rosecrans's 1863 Tullahoma Campaign and the road to Chattanooga/Chickamauga. (WP)
