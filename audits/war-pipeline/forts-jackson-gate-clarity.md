## UNDEFINED TERMS / JARGON — full inventory

### 1. "West Gulf Blockading Squadron" (Section 1, "Meanwhile in the Gulf")
> "Washington had handed him … the West Gulf Blockading Squadron"

**What it is:** the formal name for the U.S. Navy fleet assigned to blockade the Gulf Coast west of Florida. The word "squadron" is military jargon; the reader has already seen the plain word "fleet" used correctly elsewhere. The phrase is dropped with no gloss and sounds like a proper noun the reader is supposed to know.

**Verdict:** SHOULD-FIX — add a one-phrase gloss or just write "his fleet" on first use and name it parenthetically.

### 2. "mortar flotilla" / "mortar schooner" (Section 2, opening paragraph)
> "a mortar flotilla (a squadron of small sailing schooners, each carrying one enormous 13-inch seacoast mortar — a stubby cannon that lobs a heavy shell high in a steep arc, to drop it down inside fortress walls rather than batter them from the front)"

**Assessment:** The definition of "mortar" is given inline — that part is fine. But "flotilla" is used before the explanation: a 15-year-old reading the word "mortar flotilla" cold sees two words they don't know stacked together. The parenthetical explains what the schooners do but never defines "flotilla" itself (it just uses "squadron" in the gloss, which is also jargon). "Schooner" (a two-masted sailing ship) is also undefined.

**Verdict:** SHOULD-FIX — "flotilla" and "squadron" should both resolve to plain language (e.g., "a fleet of small sailing ships"). "Schooner" can be dropped in favor of just "sailing ship."

### 12. "spiking guns" (Section 3, "The flag and the forts")
> "around 300 of its soldiers mutinied — spiking guns and walking out"

**Assessment:** "Spiking" a gun means driving a metal spike into the touch-hole (or modern equivalent) to make a cannon permanently unable to fire — sabotage by the troops themselves to prevent the enemy using captured weapons. This is completely opaque to a 15-year-old. They might guess it's some kind of destruction, but not why, not how.

**Verdict:** MUST-FIX — add a brief gloss: "spiking guns (jamming them so they couldn't be fired)" or "smashing their own cannon so the enemy couldn't use them."

### 13. "hot-shot furnaces" (Section 2, mortar bombardment)
> "The shelling smashed Fort Jackson's drawbridge, wrecked its hot-shot furnaces and cisterns"

**Assessment:** "Hot-shot furnaces" are special ovens used to heat cannonballs red-hot before firing, so they could start fires in wooden ships on impact. A 15-year-old has no concept of this. "Cisterns" (water storage tanks) is also a slightly old-fashioned word but inferrable from context. "Hot-shot furnaces" is pure 19th-century military jargon.

**Verdict:** MUST-FIX — gloss it: "hot-shot furnaces (ovens used to heat cannonballs red-hot before firing, to set wooden ships ablaze)" or simplify to "its specialized cannon-heating furnaces."

### 14. "levee" (Section 3, "The Fall of New Orleans")
> "The crowds on the levee were furious and helpless at once."

**Assessment:** A "levee" here means the raised earthen embankment along the riverbank (the waterfront). In New Orleans specifically, the levee was the riverfront area where people gathered and goods were loaded. A 15-year-old may know a levee as a flood barrier but probably not as a busy city waterfront promenade. The sentence gives no context clue.

**Verdict:** SHOULD-FIX — "The crowds on the riverfront levee" or "The crowds lining the riverbank."

### 17. "Flag Officer" (Section 1, "Meanwhile in the Gulf")
> "Flag Officer David Glasgow Farragut (North)"

**Assessment:** "Flag Officer" is a naval rank title (roughly equivalent to a general; it specifically means an officer senior enough to fly a flag indicating their rank). It was the U.S. Navy's top command title before "Admiral" was created. A 15-year-old who hears "Captain" or "Admiral" gets it immediately; "Flag Officer" sounds like someone who manages flags. No gloss is given.

**Verdict:** MUST-FIX — add a brief parenthetical or just introduce him as "the U.S. Navy commander" on first mention. The rank title can follow: "Flag Officer — roughly, admiral — David Glasgow Farragut."

### 18. "ninety-day militia" (Section 1, "The prize | Why the city lay open")
