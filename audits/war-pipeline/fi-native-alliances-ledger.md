## Claim-by-claim

### Opening / framing
- Colonists named it "French and Indian War"; King George's War already used the monarch's name → §1.
- Name is a British point of view; enemy = French Empire + Indigenous allied nations → §1, FRAMING DIRECTIVE.
- Native nations the thing the war turned on / not a sideshow → §1.
- Map: British coastal, New France a St. Lawrence ribbon, interior (Great Lakes + Ohio Country) Native homeland with no colonial towns; neither empire could hold interior without Native allies → §1.
- Ohio Country defined (upper Ohio valley, W. Pennsylvania / E. Ohio / WV panhandle) → §1, §3.

### What France had built
- Century of fur trade; voyageurs and coureurs de bois lived in villages, learned languages, married Native women → §2 (fur trade + intermarriage).
- pays d'en haut = "upper country"; allies Ottawa/Odawa, Ojibwe/Chippewa, Potawatomi, Huron-Wyandot, Mississauga, Winnebago → §2.
- Seven Nations of Canada = seven Catholic mission villages on St. Lawrence near Montreal; Kahnawake ("near the rapids") most reliable, across from Montreal, Mohawk → §2.
- Kahnawake origin: French raids on Mohawk villages 1660s, conversion + resettlement, descendants France's most reliable allies; Mohawk fought both sides → §2 (Kahnawake origin note).
- Gift-giving = obligatory substance of alliance, not bribery; accepting a gift creates obligation; stopping gifts breaks the bond → §2 (gift-giving).
- Catholic/Jesuit missions converted St. Lawrence nations while preserving languages/clan systems → §2 (Catholic missions).
- Onontio = father figure who gives gifts and mediates, not commands; middle ground / mutual accommodation → §2 (Onontio / middle ground, Richard White).

### What Britain had
- Covenant Chain = alliance of British colonies (esp. New York) + Iroquois/Six Nations/Haudenosaunee; six nations named → §3, §5.
- Grew from Dutch trade ties; formalized after England took New Netherland 1664; renewed at Albany; silver chain "polished" metaphor → §3.
- Brother-not-son insistence → §3 (1692 quote).
- Fraying by 1750s over land seizures → §3.
- Ohio Company of Virginia: royal grant 200,000 acres 1749, +300,000 if 100 families settled in 7 years; speculators defined → §3.
- This threatened Ohio Delaware (Lenape) and Shawnee; France marched troops to block; collision started the war → §3.
- Sir William Johnson (British): Irish-born trader, Mohawk Valley NY, learned Mohawk language/custom; adopted ~1742 as honorary sachem, named Warraghiyagey ("a man who undertakes great things") → §3.
- Superintendent of Indian Affairs Northern District 1756, reported to London over colonial govts, charged to revive Covenant Chain → §3.
- Molly Brant (Konwatsi'tsiaienni), Mohawk clan mother, household partner, power broker, ran household, diplomacy/intelligence central → §3. Used "household partner," NOT "wife," per CAUTION.
- Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea), Molly's younger brother, ~13 at first battle 1755 with Johnson, fame came later → §3. Did not overstate his 1754–63 role.

### Iroquois playing their own game
- Confederacy strategy: not be conquered/dominated by either, keep trade from both; strategic neutrality/balancing to preserve leverage → §5.
- Mohawk (esp. Johnson's valley Mohawk) leaned British; divided (Kahnawake for France); aggrieved not loyal → §5.

### Hendrick at Albany
- Theyanoguin a.k.a. Hendrick, Bear Clan, based at Canajoharie → §4 IDENTITY.
- Distinguished from older same-name Mohawk who visited Queen Anne in London 1710 → §4 IDENTITY.
- Christian convert, foremost Mohawk orator/diplomat → §4.
- 1753 declared Covenant Chain broken before NY governor; ~800,000 acres lost to fraud (framed as his stated grievance, not audited) → §4, [CONTESTED].
- Albany Congress June 1754, lead speaker, the quote → §4.
- 1755: helped recruit Mohawk for Johnson's expedition toward Crown Point, rode at head of force, in his sixties (~64) → §4.
- Sept 8, 1755 near Lake George: Johnson sent detachment (~1,000 provincials + ~200 Mohawk under Hendrick) south; Dieskau (French) ambushed in a ravine → §4.
- Hendrick objected to splitting the force; the "too few/too many" saying → §4 (framed as remembered, per [CONTESTED]).
- Killed: horse shot from under him, elderly/couldn't get clear, killed → §4 (used the safe Wikipedia statement; bayonet detail folded into "killed there" to avoid over-specifying).
- Killers were Kahnawake Mohawk for France + Abenaki + Nipissing; Mohawk killed Mohawk → §4 (forces at Lake George).

### Monongahela
- Braddock marched toward Fort Duquesne (forks of the Ohio, present Pittsburgh) → §6.
- July 9, 1755, ~10 miles short, near the Monongahela → §6.
- Force ~900: ~250 French/Canadian, ~640 Native (great majority Native); nations Ottawa, Ojibwe, Potawatomi, Huron, Delaware, Shawnee, Mingo, Abenaki → §6 (used pack's recommended "around 250 / roughly 640" phrasing).
- French field commander killed almost at once; warriors carried the fight from forest cover vs. open formations → §6 (Beaujeu not named, just "field commander").
- Braddock's ~1,300–1,460 engaged, 900+ killed/wounded; French/allied losses a few dozen → §6.
- Braddock mortally wounded, died July 13; Washington (no side-tag, marquee) helped organize retreat → §6.

### Fort William Henry
- Aug 1757, Montcalm (no side-tag, marquee) besieged Fort William Henry, south end of Lake George; Lt. Col. George Monro (British), garrison ~2,300, many sick with smallpox → §7.
- Montcalm's army ~8,000: ~3,000 regulars, ~3,000 Canadian militia, nearly 2,000 Native warriors from both circles (Great Lakes pays d'en haut + St. Lawrence mission, Abenaki) → §7.
- Regulars defined (full-time professional soldiers) → gloss per pack jargon guidance.
- Surrender Aug 8: march out under French escort, honors of war, keep muskets (no ammo) + 1 symbolic cannon, no fighting for 18 months → §7.
- Warriors promised plunder, scalps, captives; terms gave war stores to French army, British kept personal effects, left warriors nothing; looked like French conspired with enemy → §7.
- Aug 9: warriors struck sick/wounded in camp, then column; killed, stripped, seized women, children, servants, enslaved as captives → §7.
- Legend up to ~1,500 + Cooper's Last of the Mohicans; modern ~69–184 killed/missing, under 8%; disease killed more of the dead than combat → §7, [CONTESTED]. Used the modern range per directive.
- Montcalm culpability contested (officers did what they could vs. saw it coming) → §7, [CONTESTED], presented as a debate.
- Smallpox carried home by Great Lakes warriors → epidemics in their own communities → §7.
- Damaged France's alliances; many western nations wouldn't march for France again → §7.

### Easton
- By 1758 Ohio Delaware + Shawnee reconsider; always fought for own land not France → §8, §9.
- Oct 1758, Easton PA, 500+ Native people; governors of PA and NJ; ~13 nations incl. Ohio Delaware, Shawnee, three Iroquois nations; Teedyuscung (Delaware) prominent → §8.
- Oct 26 terms: recognize Ohio hunting-ground rights; no settlement west of the Appalachians after the war; PA returned earlier-fraud lands; PA governor deals with Delaware directly, restoring standing → §8.
- Military effect: Delaware/Shawnee neutrality; Fort Duquesne lost its Native shield; Forbes (British) closed in that November; French blew up and abandoned Fort Duquesne; British built Fort Pitt (Pittsburgh) → §8.
- Ohio nations chose peace (land back + Iroquois off their backs), decided the war in the Ohio → §8.
- Fed into the 1763 Royal Proclamation line → §8.

### Allies not pawns / human cost
- Pattern of own-aims agency across episodes; Tanaghrisson (Mingo, "Half King"), Hendrick, Teedyuscung each pursued a Native aim → §9, §3.
- Protocols assumed independent powers; never "Britain's/France's Indians" → §9.
- Human cost: smallpox in garrisons + villages (exported from FWH); war/captivity emptied communities; land loss — Walking Purchase (Delaware fraud), 800,000 acres Mohawk, Ohio Company 200,000-acre grant → §9, §7, §8, §3.
- Contest for alliances was at bottom a contest over Native land; the people whose land it was paid regardless of winner → §9 FRAMING & human cost.

### Amherst / Pontiac
- France lost: Quebec 1759, Montreal 1760, Treaty of Paris 1763 handed New France to Britain → §10.
- Defeat removed the second empire to balance against; leverage evaporated → §10.
- Amherst (no side-tag, marquee): treated nations as conquered subjects; ended gift-giving early 1761; restricted gunpowder/ammunition (threat to hunting + fur economy); open contempt, "no better than slaves or dogs" → §10.
- May 1763 coalition under Odawa leader Pontiac (Obwandiyag) rose against British posts across Great Lakes/Ohio; trigger = Amherst's policy + news France ceded the region → §10.
- Fort Pitt siege summer 1763: British gave besieging Delaware two blankets + a handkerchief from the smallpox hospital; a trader recorded it (Trent) → §10, Quote Ledger #7. Henry Bouquet (British) named.
- Amherst's letters to Bouquet endorsing spreading smallpox by blankets, "extirpate this execrable race" → §10, Quote Ledger #6.
- Whether blankets caused an outbreak debated; intent documented in Amherst's hand → §10, [CONTESTED].
- Reciprocity (gifts, mediation, nations-as-nations) is what France built and Amherst discarded; Britain won the war, lost the peace → §10 takeaway.
