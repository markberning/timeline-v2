## MUST-ADD (genuinely essential — these block)

### 1. "The hill of death" is a REAL sourced quote from Hovey himself — stop hedging it away
This is a comprehensiveness AND accuracy finding. The draft (§2) and fact pack (§7, §11)
both insist the phrase is a *vague paraphrased attribution to "Hovey's men,"* not traceable
to a named source, and explicitly warn **not** to upgrade it to a direct quote. **That is
wrong, and it's costing the page its single most evocative human-cost beat.**

The phrase is a documented first-person quote from **Maj. Gen. Alvin P. Hovey** himself,
written after the battle:

> "Champion Hill was, after the battle, literally the hill of death. Men, horses, cannon,
> and the debris of an army lay scattered in wild confusion."
> — Brig. Gen. Alvin P. Hovey (sourced: WHN "Sealing Vicksburg's Fate"; hmdb "The Hill of
> Death" historical marker; ABT)

**Action:** the fact-checker should confirm the wording, then the author should attribute
it to **Hovey** (the division commander, already a named character in the draft) rather than
to anonymous "Hovey's men," and is free to quote the line directly. This converts the
weakest, most hand-wavy passage in §2 into the draft's strongest human beat. The current
ledger entry #1 ("do not upgrade to a direct first-person quote") should be reversed.

### 2. The "lost division" mechanism needs one more concrete sentence — Loring couldn't take the GOOD road
The draft says Loring "could not get across Baker's Creek… the usable crossing was cut off."
True but under-mechanized. The reason it's the campaign's defining irony (every source leads
with it): **Logan's division had moved onto the Jackson Road — the direct route to the
crossing — putting Union troops squarely across the only good road the Confederates could
retreat on.** That is why Loring's men had to peel off cross-country to the south. The draft
mentions Logan only as helping take the crest (§2); it never connects him to the blocked
road. One clause linking Logan's position to *why* Loring was trapped closes the causal loop
that the entire "lost division" payoff rests on. (See SHOULD-CONSIDER #1 for the nuance.)

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (strengthens; not blocking)

### 1. The Logan-blocked-the-road IRONY — handle carefully
The most repeated story-detail in the secondary literature: Logan's division unknowingly sat
astride the **only road** Pemberton could retreat on — "Logan was actually in command of the
only road over which the enemy could retreat" — and **neither Grant nor Logan realized it.**
Grant, not knowing, then shifted McPherson's command leftward to support Hovey, which
**uncovered the retreat road and let much of Pemberton's army escape** (Grant says as much in
his *Memoirs*). This is a richer and more honest version of "for a moment Pemberton's escape
route was shut." It also reframes the Union "missed opportunity" as TWO missed chances
(McClernand idle + the accidentally-uncovered road), not one. Worth a sentence or two in §2,
but the author should keep it accurate: it's an accidental, then-undone blockage — not a
deliberate Union encirclement.

### 2. Give the reader the alternate name "Baker's Creek" once
Low cost, real comprehensiveness gain — many readers/sources know the battle by that name.
Fact pack §1 supports it; the draft already builds the whole retreat around the creek.

### 3. The McClernand idleness has a hard number worth its weight
WHN: Hovey's division took **619** casualties under Grant's direct hand; McClernand's Smith
(10th Div.) lost **6 wounded**, Carr (14th Div.) **3**. That contrast makes "a whole corps
standing idle" land as fact, not assertion. Optional, but it's the most damning single stat
for §2's Meanwhile. (Confirm figures at fact-check.)
