### Section: The Land in the Middle

- **Ohio Company founded 1748** — ✅ CONFIRMED

- **"acting head" of Virginia** — The fact pack calls him "Lieutenant Governor of Virginia, Virginia's de facto governor, acting as royal agent." This is historically accurate — the formal Governor was absentee; Dinwiddie ran the colony. ✅ CONFIRMED

### Section: The Forts and the Ultimatum

- **"spring of 1753 the Marquis Duquesne...ordered roughly 2,000 troops"** — ⚠️ CONTESTED RANGE. The NPS prelude-to-war article also states "fifteen hundred troupes de la marine." Some secondary sources (a 2016 Erie history blog) use 2,000 as the initial mobilization figure; the DCB's 1,500 reflects the force that actually marched to the Ohio. The fact pack used "~2,000" and the draft followed it. This is at the edge of acceptable range — "roughly 2,000" is not a hard error (some sources do cite that number) but 1,500 is the more authoritative scholarly figure from the DCB. ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX: change to "roughly 1,500" or "between 1,500 and 2,000" with the caveat that the DCB gives 1,500.

- **"timber stockades with bastions and a handful of cannon"** — ✅ CONFIRMED as a fair general description (fact pack gives "Eight 6-lb cannon per bastion" at Le Boeuf; "handful" is a slight understatement but not a factual error in a general chapter)
- **Dinwiddie sent Washington in "late October 1753"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (commission dated October 30/31, 1753)
- **"twenty-one-year-old Major George Washington"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (born 1732, age 21 in Oct 1753; rank Major confirmed by the commission)
- **"£150 for expenses"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (Founders Online commission document)
- **Christopher Gist = frontiersman, Ohio Company guide** — ✅ CONFIRMED
- **Jacob Van Braam = Dutch-born veteran who taught Washington fencing and some French** — ✅ CONFIRMED (Wikipedia; fact pack; ABT all describe him as Dutch-born, French language teacher, former Dutch army officer, Washington's fencing instructor)
- **Departed Williamsburg October 31** — ✅ CONFIRMED
- **Logstown near modern Ambridge PA** — ✅ CONFIRMED
- **Joncaire's name: "Philippe-Thomas de Joncaire"** — ⚠️ MINOR IMPRECISION. The full name used in authoritative sources (ABT, Wikipedia) is "Philippe-Thomas **Chabert** de Joncaire." The draft drops "Chabert." - **Saint-Pierre quote: "As to the summons you send me to retire, I do not think myself obliged to obey it."** — ✅ CONFIRMED (ABT, Fort Le Boeuf sources, Founders Online; this is from Washington's own journal/published report)
- **Washington departed Le Boeuf mid-December; nearly drowned in Allegheny; reached Williamsburg January 1754** — ✅ CONFIRMED (departed Dec 16; Allegheny crossing Dec 29; Williamsburg Jan 16, 1754)
- **Dinwiddie published Washington's journal** — ✅ CONFIRMED

### Section: Fifteen Minutes in a Ravine

- **"word reached him from Tanaghrisson that a French party was camped in a rocky glen about 7 miles (11 km) off"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (fact pack gives "about 7 miles away"; NPS and ABT sources corroborate)
- **"On the night of May 27, 1754, Washington led roughly forty Virginians...joining a dozen Mingo warriors under Tanaghrisson"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (~40 Virginians, 12 warriors per fact pack; Wikipedia says "12 warriors, two of whom were boys" — the draft's "a dozen" is accurate)
- **"At dawn on May 28 they closed in on the French camp, which had posted no sentries"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (multiple sources state no sentries were posted)
- **French numbered "perhaps thirty-five, under...Ensign Joseph Coulon de Villiers, Sieur de Jumonville"** — ⚠️ RANK PRECISION. The draft calls him "Ensign." His precise rank was **"second ensign" (enseigne en second)** — confirmed by NPS, Wikipedia ("Joseph Coulon de Jumonville"), and the Dictionary of Canadian Biography. "Ensign" alone is not wrong in general usage (it's the rank family), but the most precise designation is "Second Ensign." Force size: the NPS page gives 33 men; Wikipedia and fact pack give 35 (some sources 29–35). "Perhaps thirty-five" with the range note is acceptable. ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX (minor): use "Second Ensign" for precision at first mention, or add a parenthetical.
- **"fight lasted about fifteen minutes"** — ✅ CONFIRMED
- **"around ten French were killed and twenty-one taken prisoner. One man escaped"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (Wikipedia: 10 killed + 21 captured + 1 escaped; this is the standard figure)
- **"Washington's side, the cost was light, one killed and a few wounded"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (1 killed, 2–3 wounded per all sources)
- **"Tu n'es pas encore mort, mon père" / "Thou art not yet dead, my father"** — ✅ CONFIRMED as attested in the Denis Kaninguen account; the draft correctly flags it as "by one French-derived account." Accurate.
- **"Tanaghrisson was born likely Catawba, captured young and adopted by the Seneca, had risen to influence among the Mingo"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (NPS, Wikipedia, DCB all give this compound identity)
- **"Half-King" probably a British invention; Iroquois council regarded Ohio Mingos as warriors, not delegated viceroys** — ✅ CONFIRMED (DCB, ABT, Wikipedia note the title was "obviously of English origin" and not a formal Iroquois designation)
- **"By 1754 Tanaghrisson's own standing had crumbled...living near the Forks as something close to a refugee"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (ABT, Smithsonian, Wikipedia all describe his eroded influence by 1754)
- **"he claimed French soldiers had killed and eaten his father"** — ✅ CONFIRMED (NPS, ABT, Wikipedia — the "boiled and eaten" version is the fuller account; "killed and eaten" is accurate)
- **Washington quote: "I heard Bulletts whistle and believe me there was something charming in the sound."** — ✅ CONFIRMED. The primary source at Founders Online reads: "I can with truth assure you, I heard Bulletts whistle and believe me there was something charming in the sound." The draft reproduces this accurately, including the archaic "Bulletts" spelling. Note: many secondary sources modernize it to "bullets" and "there is" — the draft's version matches the Founders Online original. ✅ CONFIRMED
- **Letter to "his brother John Augustine Washington, May 31, 1754"** — ✅ CONFIRMED

### Section: A Union That Wasn't

- **Albany Congress June 19–July 11, 1754, Albany NY, seven colonies, "chaired by New York's governor James DeLancey"** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Note: DeLancey's actual role: Wikipedia and Founders Online confirm he was host governor and **chairman**. The draft calls him "New York's governor" — he was Lieutenant Governor (acting governor) not the full Governor. This is the same technical distinction that applies to Dinwiddie. ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX: DeLancey held the title of **Lieutenant Governor** of New York (acting as governor in the absence of a formal governor). Calling him "New York's governor" is inaccurate in the strict sense, though he functioned as governor. Same applies to Dinwiddie — both draft and fact pack use "governor" loosely for both men.
- **Covenant Chain declared broken by Mohawk in 1753 after "roughly 800,000 acres of Mohawk land slip away in fraudulent deals"** — ⚠️ SOFT UNSUPPORTED. The specific "800,000 acres" figure: the fact pack cites this, but independent verification could not pin it to a specific authoritative primary source. The Kayaderosseras Tract land dispute (which predated 1753) involved roughly 700,000 acres per one secondary source; there may be a conflation. The general charge of massive fraudulent land seizure is historically solid; the exact 800,000-acre figure is plausible but not independently verified here. ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX: change to "hundreds of thousands of acres" or verify with a primary source.

## SHOULD-FIX (⚠️)

1. **⚠️ French expedition force size: "roughly 2,000 troops"** — The Dictionary of Canadian Biography (gold standard for French colonial officers) gives **1,500 men**; the NPS prelude-to-war article also says "fifteen hundred." Some secondary sources cite 2,000 as the initial mobilization figure. The more authoritative scholarly number is 1,500. Recommend changing to "roughly 1,500" or "some 1,500 to 2,000." *(Draft: "spring of 1753...roughly 2,000 troops south from Fort Niagara")*

2. **⚠️ Joncaire's name is incomplete:** The draft uses "Philippe-Thomas de Joncaire." His full name in all authoritative sources is "Philippe-Thomas **Chabert** de Joncaire." Minor but worth correcting at first mention.

3. **⚠️ Jumonville's rank: "Ensign"** — His precise rank was **Second Ensign** (enseigne en second), per NPS, Wikipedia, and DCB. "Ensign" alone is not wrong but is imprecise. Consider "Second Ensign" at first mention for accuracy.

4. **⚠️ James DeLancey's title: "New York's governor"** — DeLancey was the **Lieutenant Governor** acting as governor, not the full governor. Technically inaccurate. Same applies to Dinwiddie ("lieutenant governor" is correct in the draft — that usage is fine). Fix DeLancey's title or add "acting" — e.g., "New York's acting governor James DeLancey."

5. **⚠️ "roughly 800,000 acres of Mohawk land"** — The Covenant Chain breaking in 1753 is confirmed; the exact 800,000-acre figure for the fraudulent deals is plausible but not independently confirmed here. A secondary source uses 700,000 for one specific tract. Consider softening to "hundreds of thousands of acres" unless a primary source can be nailed down.
