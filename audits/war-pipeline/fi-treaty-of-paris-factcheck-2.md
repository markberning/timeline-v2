# Focused Fact-Check #2 — "The Treaty of Paris & the Proclamation Line"

Scope: re-verify ONLY the two corrected facts (A, B) plus the softened causation
claim (C) in `fi-treaty-of-paris-final.md`. All other facts passed in the prior
fact-check and were not re-examined. Web-verified independently.

## Overall: PASS

---

### A. New Orleans clause — ✅ PASS

Draft (line 31) reads:
> "the town of New Orleans and the island in which it is situated, which shall remain to France"

Verified verbatim against Treaty of Paris (1763) Article VII via the Avalon Project
(Yale Law School). The treaty text reads exactly "the town of New Orleans and the
island in which it is situated, which shall remain to France." The phrase "and port"
does NOT appear in connection with New Orleans (Avalon confirms the only "port" in
the vicinity is "the river and port of the Mobile," which goes to Britain). The
restored "which" is correct and the spurious "and port" is gone.

Source: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/paris763.asp

### B. "Nine British forts" — ✅ PASS

Both prior "roughly ten" references now read "nine":
- Line 85: "a war that would seize nine British forts across the region"
- Line 119: "That rising, which took nine British forts"

No "ten" / "roughly ten" remains anywhere in the draft (confirmed by full read).

"Nine" is the sourced figure. Wikipedia (Pontiac's War) states verbatim: "Nine forts
were destroyed." The standard accounting is eight posts captured outright in the
May–June 1763 wave (Sandusky, St. Joseph, Miami, Ouiatenon, Michilimackinac, Venango,
Le Boeuf, Presque Isle) with the ninth (Fort Edward Augustus / Green Bay) abandoned —
yielding nine destroyed; Detroit, Pitt, and Niagara held out. The draft's "nine" and
its surrounding "eight captured" framing (not present here but consistent) are correct.

Note (not a defect): the draft says forts were "seize[d]" / "took"; sources phrase it
as "destroyed" (nine) vs. "captured" (eight). "Nine" is the correct headline number
the draft uses, so this passes. If the authors want maximum precision they could say
"nine forts fell," but "nine" is sound as written.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_War ; American Battlefield Trust;
EBSCO Research Starters.

### C. Softened causation — ✅ PASS

The draft no longer asserts Pontiac's War as the *cause* of the Proclamation's boundary
policy. It correctly frames the policy as already in development (post-treaty imperial
reorganization) and the war as having hastened/accelerated it:
- Line 85: "London had already been working toward a western boundary as part of
  reorganizing its enlarged empire after the treaty; news of the uprising hastened the
  Proclamation out the door."
- Line 119: "The Crown had already been moving toward a western boundary as it
  reorganized its swollen empire; the uprising accelerated that policy into print."

This matches the scholarly/source consensus. Wikipedia (Pontiac's War): "The
Proclamation, already in the works when Pontiac's War erupted, was hurriedly issued
after news of the uprising reached London." The boundary/Indian-reserve plan predated
the war; the war accelerated issuance. The draft's framing is accurate and no longer
overstates causation.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_War ;
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Proclamation_of_1763
