# NEWCOMER-CLARITY CRITIC · Battle of Trenton draft (rev-trenton)

Draft reviewed: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-trenton-draft.md`
Critic lens: reader with ZERO prior knowledge, may land on any single surface first
(dossier, one section, a MEANWHILE, a caption) without having read anything else.

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The three body sections are, on the whole, exemplary newcomer writing: the enlistment
mechanism ("fixed-term contracts... a scheduled, legal dissolution") is the best gloss in
the draft; "Hessians" is parenthetically defined at first use in all three sections;
Assunpink Creek is glossed identically in both sections that need it; powder, Durham
boats, jägers, hogsheads, and "struck their colors" are all glossed inline with
parentheses (not em-dashes) exactly per house rules; the why-it-mattered causal chain
(Section 3) is complete and explicit. No restructuring is needed.

What fails is the EDGES: the dossier note (the very first surface a reader sees) uses
"Hessian," "brigade," and "nor'easter" bare; two of the three MEANWHILE blocks do not
stand alone (bare "Rall," "Ewing," "jägers," "dragoons," "Hessians"); one term of art
("Loyalist") is never defined anywhere; and the single most important tactical mechanism
— why the Hessians lost — leans on the undefined term "form," which a newcomer cannot
unpack. All fixes are local one-clause glosses.

---

## BLOCKERS

### B1 · Dossier `note:` — "Hessian" (and "brigade," "nor'easter") bare on the first surface the reader sees
Draft (line 15):
> "Eight days after Washington wrote that the game was pretty near up, his dissolving army crossed an ice-choked river through a nor'easter and took a Hessian brigade in a morning."

The dossier note is the topmost reader-facing surface of the page; for a reader who taps
this battle first, this is the FIRST sentence of the whole experience, and three terms in
it are undefined. The brief's standing rule — Hessians glossed at first use in EVERY
surface that uses the word — fails here. Proposed:
> "Eight days after Washington wrote that the game was pretty near up, his dissolving army crossed an ice-choked river through a nor'easter (a violent winter coastal storm) and took a brigade of Hessians (German soldiers fighting for Britain) in a morning."
(If length is tight, "a Hessian brigade" → "about 1,500 Hessians (German soldiers fighting for Britain)" — the gloss is the non-negotiable part; "brigade" can be dropped rather than defined.)

### B2 · MEANWHILE: New Jersey ("The rising") does not stand alone
Draft (line 173):
> "Every alarm dragged Rall's garrison out of bed and into the cold. The Hessians Washington was coming for had been worn down for weeks by neighbors with muskets."

MEANWHILEs are spec'd to stand alone, and this one assumes the reader already knows who
Rall is and what a Hessian is. Proposed:
> "Every alarm dragged Colonel Johann Rall's garrison (the Hessians — German troops in British service — holding Trenton) out of bed and into the cold. The Hessians Washington was coming for had been worn down for weeks by neighbors with muskets."

### B3 · MEANWHILE: Bordentown ("The open back door") does not stand alone
Draft (line 196):
> "The bridge Ewing was supposed to seal stayed open, and several hundred men used it: dragoons, jägers, and Hessians from the outposts and the lower town, running south to the next garrison with the news."

Four undefined references for a cold reader: Ewing (who?), dragoons, jägers, Hessians.
(jägers is glossed in Section 2 prose, but a MEANWHILE must not depend on the prose.)
Proposed:
> "The bridge that James Ewing's American militia was supposed to seal — his crossing failed in the ice — stayed open, and several hundred men used it: British dragoons (mounted troops), Hessian jägers (light riflemen), and Hessian infantry from the outposts and the lower town, running south to the next garrison with the news."
(Rephrase to parentheses-only per house rules if the em-dash clause survives; e.g. "The bridge James Ewing's American militia was supposed to seal (his crossing had failed in the ice) stayed open...")

### B4 · "Loyalist" never defined anywhere in the draft
Draft (Section 2, line 190):
> "There is also the story of the note: a Loyalist farmer, the Hessians said afterward, brought Rall a written warning at the merchant Abraham Hunt's supper table that night..."

Sole use, no gloss, and a zero-knowledge reader has no way to know which side a
"Loyalist" is on — which is the entire point of the anecdote. Proposed:
> "...a Loyalist farmer (an American who sided with the king), the Hessians said afterward, brought Rall a written warning..."

### B5 · The why-the-Hessians-lost mechanism hangs on the undefined term "form"
Draft (Section 2, line 194):
> "The three Hessian regiments, Rall's own grenadiers and the von Lossberg and von Knyphausen fusiliers, tumbled out and tried to form in the streets under artillery fire."

The brief explicitly requires this causal chain to be explicit: the guns commanded the
streets, so the regiments could not form, so they could not fight. The draft has the
guns-command-the-streets half ("swept them end to end") but the second half depends on
the reader knowing that "form" means lining up in ranks and that an 18th-century
regiment that cannot form cannot fight at all. Two undefined unit-type terms ride along
(grenadiers, fusiliers — also bare in the dossier sides note, line 32). Proposed:
> "The three Hessian regiments, Rall's own grenadiers and the von Lossberg and von Knyphausen fusiliers (grenadiers and fusiliers: old distinctions of infantry type, by now mostly regimental titles), tumbled out and tried to form (line up shoulder to shoulder in ranks — an eighteenth-century regiment fought as a formed line or it could not fight at all) in the streets, and the streets were exactly where Knox's cannon were pointed."
(Any phrasing works; the load-bearing additions are the "form" gloss and the line that
ties can't-form to the guns.)

## SHOULD-FIX

### People — missing role/side at first appearance per section

S-1 · **Horatio Gates, Section 1** (line 161): "and regiments under Horatio Gates came in too." — only person in Section 1 with no rank, role, or side. Proposed: "and regiments under Major General Horatio Gates (American) came in too."

S-2 · **Joseph Reed, Section 1** (line 171): "On December 23 he wrote to Colonel Joseph Reed:" — who is Reed? Proposed: "he wrote to Colonel Joseph Reed (his adjutant general, an American officer he trusted with the secret):" (verify exact role against the fact pack; "a trusted aide" is the minimum).

S-3 · **Adam Stephen, Section 2** (line 190): "sent across by General Adam Stephen without Washington's knowledge" — side never stated. Proposed: "sent across by General Adam Stephen (American) without Washington's knowledge."

S-4 · **Monroe + William Washington, Section 3** (line 203): "Lieutenant James Monroe and Captain William Washington among them." First appearance IN THIS SECTION with no identity; a reader who opened only Section 3 gets two bare names. Proposed: "Lieutenant James Monroe (the future fifth president, eighteen that morning) and Captain William Washington (the general's cousin) among them."

S-5 · **Nathanael Greene, Section 3** (line 209): "(with Nathanael Greene, by most tellings)" — unidentified in this section. Proposed: "(with Major General Nathanael Greene, one of his column commanders, by most tellings)."

S-6 · **James Wilkinson, Section 3** (line 209): "Major James Wilkinson's memoirs, written forty years later..." — no side/role. Proposed: "Major James Wilkinson, a young American officer on the campaign, in memoirs written forty years later..."

### Terms of art — undefined at first use on a surface

S-7 · **"nor'easter," Section 3 + section blurbs** (lines 53–55, 207): Section 2 effectively defines it by expansion ("rain turning to sleet, snow, and hail, a high wind"), but Section 3 first use is bare ("across the Delaware in a nor'easter the night before") and the dossier blurbs use it bare. Fix the dossier note (B1) and Section 3: "in a nor'easter (a violent winter storm) the night before."

S-8 · **"bayonet"** — used in the dossier sides note (line 32 "trusting the bayonet"), Rall's bio (line 46), and twice in Section 2 (lines 188, 190), never defined. The Rall characterization ("trusting the bayonet") is opaque without it. Proposed, at Section 2 first use (line 188): "to use the bayonet (the long blade fixed on the musket's muzzle for close fighting), for the town must be taken." And in the sides note: "trusting the bayonet (close assault with fixed blades) over earthworks."

S-9 · **"picket," Section 2 + caption** (line 192 "the Hessian picket post at a cooper's shop"; image 4 caption "the Hessian officer whose picket took the first shots"): undefined. Proposed prose: "the Hessian picket post (a small guard detachment posted outside town to give warning) at a cooper's shop"; caption: "...whose picket (outlying guard post) took the first shots."

S-10 · **"vanguard," Section 2** (line 192): "Greene's vanguard came out of the sleet" → "Greene's vanguard (the lead troops of his column) came out of the sleet."

S-11 · **"colors" (flags)** — glossed beautifully in Section 2 ("struck their colors (lowered their flags in surrender)") but bare on three other surfaces: dossier casualties (line 35 "the regimental colors"), Section 3 (line 203 "the regiments' colors"), and the Peale Princeton caption (line 84 "captured Hessian colors"). Each is a standalone surface; add "(battle flags)" at each: "the regimental colors (battle flags)," "the regiments' colors (their battle flags)," "captured Hessian colors (battle flags)."

S-12 · **"court-martial," outcome + Section 3** (lines 50, 209): "a Hessian court-martial later fixed the blame on him alone" / "In 1778 a Hessian court-martial, ordered by the prince back home..." Proposed at both: "a Hessian court-martial (a military court)."

S-13 · **"Landgrave," Section 3 + MEANWHILE: Hesse-Kassel** (lines 211, 219): "the Landgrave Friedrich II of Hesse-Kassel collected the payments" — Landgrave is a German princely title no newcomer knows. Proposed: "the Landgrave (the ruling prince) Friedrich II of Hesse-Kassel." Same fix in the MEANWHILE, which must stand alone: "Landgrave Friedrich II (the state's ruling prince)."

S-14 · **George III unidentified, Section 3 + MEANWHILE** (lines 211, 219): "Their princes signed subsidy treaties with George III" — say who he is once per surface: "with George III (Britain's king)." The MEANWHILE needs it too.

S-15 · **Continental Congress, Section 3** (line 203): "John Hancock, the president of the Continental Congress" — Hancock is identified but Congress isn't. Proposed: "the Continental Congress (the assembly governing the rebelling colonies)." (Sullivan's dossier bio, line 43, also uses "Continental Congress" bare — same gloss or trim.)

### Causal chains — present but incomplete

S-16 · **WHY dawn / why "one hour before day."** The draft quotes the plan ("one hour before day is the time fixed") and notes the delay "meant the assault would come in broad daylight," but never says why darkness was wanted: surprise — hit the garrison in its beds with the approach march hidden. One clause at the plan paragraph (Section 1, line 171): "a dawn attack (the columns would make the whole nine-mile approach in darkness and hit the garrison before it was out of bed)."

S-17 · **WHY the storm helped as much as it hurt.** The draft gives the hurt (failed crossings, three hours late) and one help ("the storm blew straight into the Hessians' faces"), but the bigger help — the storm concealed the approach and made an attack unthinkable, which is why no patrols were out — is never stated. Proposed, Section 2 around line 188: "The same storm that had nearly wrecked the crossing was now cover: it muffled and hid nine miles of marching army, and no one in Trenton imagined an attack could move in it."

### Geography — buildable from scratch, with gaps

S-18 · **The Delaware as the line between the armies is implied, never stated.** Section 1 (line 157) has the boats clause ("took every boat he could find with him, so that the British could not follow at once across a river with no bridges") — good — but never the one orienting sentence a map-less newcomer needs. Proposed, end of that sentence: "...across a river with no bridges. The Delaware was now the front line: the British and their Hessians in New Jersey on the east bank, what was left of the American army in Pennsylvania on the west."

S-19 · **The outpost chain has names but no shape** (line 161): "strung a chain of outposts across New Jersey (Perth Amboy, New Brunswick, Princeton, Trenton, Bordentown)" — a newcomer can't tell these run from near New York down to the river, or that Trenton is the end of the string. Proposed: "strung a chain of outposts across New Jersey, running from the posts nearest New York down to the Delaware itself (Perth Amboy, New Brunswick, Princeton, Trenton, Bordentown)." This is also what makes "the most exposed link" land.

S-20 · **Princeton has no distance.** Section 3 (line 217): "Lord Cornwallis marched down from Princeton" and "smashed his rearguard at Princeton on the morning of January 3" — Princeton is never placed relative to Trenton in prose (locator only). Proposed: "marched down from Princeton, the next post up the chain about a dozen miles to the northeast" (take the exact figure from the fact pack; do not assert distance from memory).

S-21 · **Philadelphia never identified.** It appears twice (printing of The Crisis, line 165; the prisoner parade, line 211) with no identity or position. One gloss at the parade, where it carries weight: "paraded through the streets of Philadelphia (the rebels' capital and the largest city in America, thirty-odd miles down the river) to enormous crowds" — verify the distance figure against the pack; the identity clause alone is the minimum.

S-22 · **Bordentown unplaced.** "the enemy post at Bordentown" (lines 171, 186) — south of Trenton, downriver, ~6 miles; prose gives no placement beyond "downstream near Bristol." Proposed at Section 1 first use: "the enemy post at Bordentown (the next garrison downriver, a few miles south of Trenton)."

### Captions and dossier surfaces standing alone

S-23 · **Hero caption** (lines 19/67): "the mortally wounded Colonel Rall surrendering to Washington" — Rall's side and the word Hessians both unglossed on a surface many readers see first. Proposed: "John Trumbull's 'The Capture of the Hessians at Trenton': the mortally wounded Hessian commander, Colonel Rall, surrendering to Washington. (The Hessians were German troops fighting for Britain.) Composed decades after the battle and idealized; Washington and Rall never met mid-field like this." (Or fold the gloss into the first clause to keep it one sentence shorter.)

S-24 · **Knox bio, "Ticonderoga guns"** (line 41): "Knox had hauled the Ticonderoga guns to Boston the winter before" — a newcomer has never heard of Ticonderoga. Proposed: "had hauled the captured cannon of Fort Ticonderoga three hundred miles of winter road to Boston the year before" (distance per pack; "captured cannon... to Boston" is the minimum).

S-25 · **Greene bio, "the Fort Washington disaster"** (line 42): a cold reader can confuse Fort Washington with George Washington, and the bio never says what the disaster was. Proposed: "...even though he bore blame for the loss of Fort Washington (the Manhattan fort whose fall in November cost about 2,800 prisoners) a month earlier."

S-26 · **Knox rank inconsistency (clarity casualty).** Section 2 (line 182) styles him "Brigadier General Henry Knox" on Christmas night; his own bio (line 41) says the promotion "carries the date December 27, 1776, the day after the battle." A careful newcomer reads a contradiction. Use "Colonel Henry Knox" in Section 2 (or "Knox, the artillery chief") and keep the promotion beat in the bio — flag to the fact critic, but the prose styling should change.

S-27 · **"ferry" as a place-name concept** (Section 1, line 171; locators): McConkey's Ferry, Trenton Ferry, Dunk's Ferry — a newcomer may not parse "Ferry" as "a boat-crossing point on the river." One gloss at first use: "cross at McConkey's Ferry (a boat-crossing point on the river), nine miles upstream of Trenton."

## NITS

N-1 · "the Hudson" (Section 1, line 157): "Fort Lee, across the Hudson, was abandoned" — could carry "(the river on Manhattan's west side)"; the New York paragraph generally assumes light NYC geography, acceptable since it exits the frame immediately.

N-2 · "garrison" is used many times before any implicit definition; it is mostly inferable from context, but a single "(the troops stationed to hold the town)" at Section 1 first use (line 161) would cost nothing.

N-3 · "regiment" never glossed; context ("a regiment of Massachusetts fishermen and sailors") mostly carries it. If desired: "(a unit of a few hundred soldiers)" once.

N-4 · "pig iron" (Durham boats gloss, line 182): "built to haul pig iron" → "built to haul raw iron" reads cleaner for a newcomer; optional.

N-5 · "marched to Newtown" (Section 3, line 211): say "back in Pennsylvania" so the prisoner route reads as over-the-river: "ferried over the river, marched to Newtown in Pennsylvania, and then paraded..."

N-6 · Dossier stats "~1,000 Hessian (most captured)" (line 27): reads fine once B1 fixes the note above it; no separate gloss needed if the note carries the definition first.

N-7 · Section blurb line 53 "an army melting away to expiring enlistments" — the blurb teases the mechanism the section then explains; acceptable as a tease, but "expiring enlistments (soldiers' contracts running out)" would make the blurb self-sufficient.

N-8 · Stacy Potts (Section 3, line 209): "carried to his headquarters in Stacy Potts's house" — adequate ("his headquarters" does the work); could add "a Trenton merchant" if the pack supports it.

N-9 · Timeline is otherwise excellent: Christmas night → loading "most of the night" → last guns ~3:00 a.m. → nine-mile march → split at ~4.5 miles → first shots ~8:00 a.m. → surrenders ~9:30–10:00 → "by mid-morning... over" → recrossing "by noon." A reader always knows what time it is. The only soft spot: the march start time is implied, not stated; if the pack carries ~4:00 a.m., one clause ("the march stepped off around 4:00 a.m.") closes it.

N-10 · MEANWHILE: Hesse-Kassel is the one MEANWHILE that nearly stands alone already; with S-13/S-14 (Landgrave, George III) it fully passes.
