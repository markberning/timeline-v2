## §1 Pitt
- Pitt = politician, never fought/crossed ocean → §1 (political figure, not combatant).
- Pitt-Newcastle ministry June 1757; Newcastle nominal head (Lords, Treasury, patronage); Pitt = Secretary of State for the Southern Department, war minister in all but name → §1 Timeline of Power; explicit author note "NOT Prime Minister."
- Quote "My Lord, I am sure I can save this country, and no one else can." → §1, attributed to Pitt on assuming coalition (Britannica). Used verbatim, attributed to Pitt (factpack says told to Duke of Devonshire; draft attributes to Pitt generally — accurate).
- Loudoun (Lt. Gen. John Campbell, Earl of Loudoun): high-handed demands, "outright mutiny," recalled late Dec 1757 → §1 Why Strategy Failed.
- 1754 order: provincial officers below lowest regular w/ king's commission → §1.
- Pitt quote "won America on the banks of the Rhine" → **PARAPHRASED, not quoted.** Factpack ⚠️#8 flags exact verbatim [UNVERIFIED]. Draft renders as paraphrase ("boasted … that he had won America on the banks of the Rhine") — NOT in quote marks, NOT as verbatim. Note: factpack says "Canada"; draft says "America" — **[VERIFY] minor wording; recommend changing to "Canada" to mirror factpack phrasing exactly even as paraphrase.**
- Prussia subsidy £670,000–£700,000/yr to Frederick the Great → §1 #1; range used per ⚠️#1.
- "almost unlimited funds" from Parliament → §1 #2.
- Reimbursement of colonial war expenses; 1757 warrant lifting provincial colonels to near-parity → §1 #2.
- Royal Navy = lever: blockade ports, cut sea lanes, carry armies → §1 #3.
- Target = St. Lawrence/Quebec; conquered Canada makes European peace terms moot → §1 #4.
- ~50,000 British troops in N. America by summer 1758 ≈ entire white pop. of New France → §1 #5.
- "debts of a near-bankrupt monarchy" vs "resources of an empire" → §8 Why Tide Turned.

## §2 Three-Pronged Offensive
- Three simultaneous thrusts: Louisbourg, Carillon, Fort Duquesne → §2 table.
- Logic: France too stretched to be strong everywhere → §2, §8.
- Two succeed, one = bloodiest disaster; fourth (Frontenac) not in original design → §2 note ("Plus: Fort Frontenac … not originally the primary third prong").

## §3 Louisbourg
- Île-Royale / Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia; guards mouth of St. Lawrence; key to Quebec → §3 Location.
- 11,000–14,000 British under Amherst → §3, range per ⚠️#2.
- 40 warships, 150 transports, Adm. Boscawen → §3.
- Drucour (Chevalier Augustin de Boschenry de Drucour), governor/commander, ~7,000 defenders → §3. Draft uses "Chevalier de Drucour" — acceptable short form.
- Harbor squadron present (7 ships of line etc.) → §3; draft says "squadron of warships."
- Mi'kmaq allies fought alongside garrison throughout → §3 (explicit author note: first-class).
- Landing June 8, 1758, Wolfe, Kennington Cove; first boats repulsed; drifting light-infantry boats found undefended spot, held it, rest followed; French pulled back, gave up outer defenses → §3 The Landing.
- Seven-week siege; July 21 Célèbre struck by mortar, fire spread to two more ships; July 23 King's Bastion burned; July 25 one ship captured, one (aground) burned, harbor cleared → §3 Siege Unfolds.
- Drucour requested honors of war; Amherst refused → §3 Surrender Terms.
- Amherst quote "We give your Excellency an hour…surrender yourselves prisoners of war." → §3, verified verbatim. Used verbatim.
- Articles signed midnight July 26, 1758 → §3.
- Casualties: 172 British killed, 102 French killed → §3 table.
- ~6,600 French captured, shipped to France as POWs → §3 table + Surrender Terms.
- St. Lawrence open / road to Quebec → §3 Strategic Significance.

## §4 Carillon
- Largest army Britain sent into the interior → consistent with §4 (~15,000) vs prior forces.
- Fort Carillon, narrows between Lake George & Lake Champlain, present-day Ticonderoga NY; gate to Champlain corridor into heart of New France → §4 Location.
- ~15,000 (≈6,400 regulars + 9,000 provincials) → §4, exact 6,367+9,034; range per ⚠️#3. Draft rounds to "about 6,400" / "9,000" / "roughly 15,000."
- Abercromby: 52, politically connected, limited combat experience; managed logistics capably → §4 + ⚠️ author note (balanced framing).
- Lord Howe (George Augustus, Viscount Howe), 2nd-in-command, beloved by regulars+provincials; learned wilderness war from Maj. Robert Rogers & rangers; cropped coats/shortened muskets/no wigs → §4 Death of Lord Howe.
- Killed July 6, two days before assault, at Trout Brook, musket ball, died instantly → §4.
- Howe quote "In Lord Howe, the soul of General Abercromby's army seemed to expire." → §4. Factpack ⚠️#9: attribution loose ("a British officer," sometimes Capt. James Abercrombie). Draft attributes to "A British officer" — **matches factpack's cautious attribution.** Trimmed to first sentence only (verbatim of that sentence). [VERIFY] attribution loose per ⚠️#9 — flagged, not asserted precisely.
- Montcalm ~3,500 regulars+militia (fewer than 1/4 of British), ~250 Indigenous warriors → §4, range per ⚠️#4.
- Breastwork built ~24 hrs (July 6–7), three-quarters mile in front of fort, ≥8 ft high, zigzag for enfilade, abatis 30 yards deep, sharpened branches outward, "like barbed wire" → §4 Breastwork.
- Lt. Matthew Clerk scouted, reported breastwork stormable by infantry w/o siege artillery; Abercromby believed him → §4 Why No Artillery. (Draft omits Rattlesnake Mtn name + Clerk's later death/blame dispute — acceptable compression; nothing asserted falsely.)
- Assault July 8, 1758 afternoon; no artillery, no flanking, no siege; planned coordinated assault dissolved into isolated charges; abatis broke formations; French fire from behind 8-ft wall; "slaughterhouse" observer; repeated charges for hours; retreat by evening, withdrawal July 9 down Lake George → §4 The Assault.
- Casualties: 1,600–2,000 British (500+ dead) vs <400 French → §4 table + ⚠️#5 (range used).
- "bloodiest battle of the war in N. America … until the Civil War" → §4 Aftermath (verbatim claim paraphrased).
- Montcalm celebrated across New France → §4 Aftermath.
- Abercromby recalled Sept 1758, replaced by Amherst; political connections spared disgrace; promoted by seniority; never field command again → §4 Aftermath.
- Fort taken by British 1759, renamed Ticonderoga → §4 Aftermath.
- Montcalm gambled near-entire field force; can't be everywhere → §4 Aftermath + §8.

## §5 Fort Frontenac
- Not in original 3-prong plan; possibly more damage than Carillon cost → §2 note + §5 Significance (author synthesis, supported).
- Bradstreet (Lt. Col. John Bradstreet), half-Acadian, knew waterways, not a marquee general but most efficient officer of 1758 → §5 Commander + author note.
- ~2,600 men (mostly provincials) → §5 Forces (2,635; ⚠️ desertion to ~2,035). Draft "around 2,600" — uses the higher cited figure.
- Fort Frontenac where Lake Ontario drains into St. Lawrence, present-day Kingston, Ontario; hub of French supply to all western posts (Duquesne, Niagara, Great Lakes) → §5 Location + Significance.
- Garrison ~100–150 under Pierre-Jacques Payen de Noyan, elderly → §5 Forces.
- Bradstreet landed Aug 25; surrendered Aug 26 or 27, 1758 → §5, per ⚠️#7.
- Casualties: 2 French killed, 11 British wounded → §5 Casualties.
- Haul: 60+ cannon; hundreds of barrels of provisions; furs/goods ~800,000 livres (season's western surplus); 2 warships taken; rest of French Lake Ontario presence destroyed; fort demolished → §5 What Was Taken.
- Western forts cut off; Duquesne isolated/starving before any army arrived → §5 Significance.
- Boost to morale after Carillon → §5 Significance.

## §6 Forbes / Duquesne
- Forks of the Ohio = Allegheny + Monongahela meet; most valuable spot; French seized 1754 to start war → §6 + prior chapter (Ohio Spark).
- Forbes (Brig. Gen. John Forbes), Scottish, methodical, respected, not glory-seeker → §6 Background.
- Dysentery throughout; carried in litter; died Philadelphia March 1759 → §6 Background.
- Bouquet (Lt. Col. Henry Bouquet), Swiss, operational deputy, most day-to-day decisions → §6 Forces.
- George Washington commanded Virginia troops; argued for Braddock's southern road; Forbes overruled, built new road → §6 Forces note.
- Forbes Road: new road W across PA mountains; depots Fort Bedford, Fort Ligonier at each stage; contrast w/ Braddock's fast advance; couldn't be cut off → §6 Forbes Road.
- Grant's defeat: Maj. James Grant (Highlanders), ~850 men, scout Duquesne, divided force into pieces unable to support each other; Sept 14, 1758; French + ~500 Native warriors sortied, destroyed fragments; ~342 British casualties (incl. Grant captured); ~16 French/Native losses → §6 Grant's Defeat, date per ⚠️#6. Draft: "around sixteen" matches "8 killed, 8 wounded."
- Native nations fighting for France; as long as they did, French held country → §6/§7 (synthesis, supported by §7 military effect).
- Nov 24, 1758 French blew magazine, burned buildings, retreated → §6 Fort Duquesne Abandoned.
- French commander recognized indefensible: Bradstreet cut supply, Easton removed allies, Forbes road bypassed wilderness defenses → §6.
- Washington led three scouting parties into ruins Nov 24 → §6.
- Forbes quote "I have used the freedom of giving your name to Fort Duquesne…makes us masters of the place." Nov 27, 1758 → §6, verified verbatim. Used verbatim.
- Fort Pitt: five-sided/pentagonal bastioned, largest/most elaborate British fortress in America; Pittsburgh grew around it, named for fort → for Pitt → §6 Fort Pitt. (Draft omits architect Gordon, 20 acres, 1759–61 dates — acceptable compression.)

## §7 Easton (the hinge)
- French power rested on Native allies (Lenape/Delaware, Shawnee, Mingo, others); French trade/garrison vs British clear-and-stay; "fort = trading partner w/ cannon; farm = leading edge of dispossession" → §7 + prior-chapter framing (Ohio Spark establishes this distinction). Consistent.
- Oct 1758; 500+ Native people; open-air council; Easton, PA → §7. Draft says "October 1758" (factpack: Oct 26, 1758) — date generalized to month, acceptable.
- 13 nations: Six Nations (Mohawk, Onondaga, Oneida, Cayuga, Seneca, Tuscarora), E+W Lenape, Shawnee, Mingo, + smaller nations → §7 Native Nations.
- Teedyuscung, Lenape, "King of the Delawares," principal negotiator, grievances since 1756 → §7 Key Figures. Tagged Lenape (Native, no Brit/Fr tag) per cast rules.
- Charles Thomson, advisor, later Continental Congress secretary → §7. Tagged "British colonial" per cast table (Colonial PA). [VERIFY] tag: factpack cast lists him "Colonial (Pennsylvania)" — draft uses "British colonial"; acceptable but confirm preferred tag form.
- William Denny, Lt. Gov. of PA, principal British negotiator → §7 British Signatories.
- Promises: Native hunting rights in Ohio valley; bar settlement W of Alleghenies after war; PA return ceded land blocks; ~1,000 Spanish dollars → §7 What the Nations Were Promised.
- Military provision: would not fight on the French side in this war → §7 Military Effect.
- Without allies, Duquesne operationally dead; "road = hammer, Easton cleared the path" → §7 Military Effect (factpack's own phrasing adapted).
- Nations chose to step aside, own reasons/terms; worth more than a battle → §7 + author note (Native agency mandate).
- Betrayal: settlement bar → Royal Proclamation of 1763 (reserved land), not enforced; colonists poured west; promises broken within years; Lenape/Shawnee took up arms 1763 in Pontiac's War → §7 The Betrayal.

## §8 Turning Year
- Ledger: Louisbourg/St. Lawrence open; Frontenac/supply cut; Duquesne/Ohio cleared; Easton/allies detached vs French only Carillon → §8 Balance Sheet.
- Carillon cost more blood than any other day, changed nothing strategically → §4 + §8.
- Navy strangled resupply; reimbursement+rank reforms brought ~50,000 men; Easton detached nations → §8 Why Tide Turned.
- Montcalm wins battles, can't be two places, Britain coming from three → §8.
- 1759 setup: Louisbourg→St. Lawrence to Quebec; Frontenac→Niagara next; Duquesne+Easton→Ohio British → §8 Setting Up 1759.

### Easton — WHY the nations left France (Fix 6) + Walking Purchase (round-2 §A/§B)
- Walking Purchase 1737: Penn proprietors (sons of William Penn) took ~1.2 million acres of Lenape land; likely-forged 1686 "deed"; runners covered ~70 miles in a day along a pre-cleared path; far more than the Lenape understood → factpack §A round-2 (lines 466–473): ~1,200,932 acres → "roughly 1.2 million"; ~70 miles; forgery; Penn sons.
- Teedyuscung pressed the Walking Purchase as fraud at every Easton conference since 1756 → factpack §B round-2 (lines 481–483).
- Ohio nations' own read that France was losing (Louisbourg/Frontenac gone, supplies failing) → factpack §B/§8 synthesis (a defeated ally is no protection); supported by the year's documented losses.
- Treaty did NOT void the Walking Purchase or grant restitution; grievance left unresolved → factpack §B round-2 (line 490): "1758 treaty did NOT formally void or compensate the Walking Purchase."

### Easton → Duquesne causal chain (Fix 11)
- French could hold the remote fort only because Native allies did the scouting/raiding that kept the road deadly (annihilated Braddock, bloodied Grant); Easton pulled them out → garrison isolated against Forbes's far larger army; they blew the fort and withdrew → factpack §7 Military Effect (lines 336–340) + §6 (lines 284–286).

### Shadow on the triumphal close (Fix 8)
