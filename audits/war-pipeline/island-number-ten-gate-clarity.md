## MUST-FIX LIST (9 items — these break comprehension for a newcomer)

1. **"running the gauntlet"** — First use (¶5, batteries section): undefined idiom. Add gloss: "(sailing straight past a line of enemy guns all firing at you at once)."

2. **"spiking some of his cannon"** — Undefined verb. Add: "(driving an iron spike into the cannon's firing hole so the enemy could not fire it)."

3. **"garrison"** — First use is in the impasse section ("trap the garrison"), but the definition arrives two sections later. Move the gloss to first use.

4. **"Flag Officer"** — An archaic rank zero readers will know. Add: "(the rank used by senior Union Navy commanders before 'admiral' was formally created — effectively the fleet commander)."

5. **"its bow — to the guns"** — "Bow" is identified by dash but the logic (bow = thinnest/least armored) is not stated. Add: "its bow (front end, where ships are narrowest and most vulnerable)."

6. **"struck its colors"** — Naval surrender idiom, completely opaque. Add: "(lowered the Confederate flag — the traditional signal of surrender)."

7. **"David Dixon Porter at Vicksburg"** — No side tag. Add "Union" or "(North)."

8. **"Pope needed gunboats below the island to cover the move"** — "Cover" in military sense unexplained. Add: "to cover the crossing (keep Confederate shore guns suppressed with their own fire while Pope's men crossed in the open)."

9. **"Brig. Gen." / "Cmdr." / "Lt."** — Military abbreviations should be written out on first use: Brigadier General, Commander, Lieutenant. (Counts as one fix — a consistent style rule.)

## SHOULD-FIX LIST (10 items — improve clarity but don't block comprehension)

1. **"blockade"** — First use has no gloss. Add: "(seal off Southern ports so no ships could enter or leave)."

2. **"general-in-chief"** — Add: "(the top commander of all Union armies at the war's start)."

3. **"Beauregard"** — Named once and gone. Either add a role gloss or drop the name and just say "The Confederate commander."

4. **"flotilla"** — Used twice without definition. Add gloss on first use: "(a fleet of smaller warships)."

5. **"snubbing hawsers around the stumps"** — "Snubbing" is nautical jargon. Replace with "looping" or "wrapping."

6. **"pilothouse"** — Add: "(the enclosed cockpit where the ship's pilot steers — the most exposed spot on deck)."

7. **"cutlasses for boarders"** — Add: "cutlasses (short curved swords) in case Confederate soldiers tried to climb aboard."

8. **William R. Hoel** — No side tag. He is Union. Add "(North)."

9. **"called east by Lincoln"** — Lincoln has no side tag. Add "(President Lincoln, North)" — or "President Lincoln" is sufficient given context.

10. **Antietam comparison** — Reader may not have read the Antietam section. Add a one-clause anchor: "Antietam (a single battle in September 1862 that killed or wounded more men in one day than any other in American history)."

**Worst single offender: "running the gauntlet"** — used four times in the draft (including the setup phrase "designed to make running the gauntlet downriver a form of suicide" and "ran the gauntlet" twice more in the Carondelet run section), never defined. It is the central kinetic image of the entire second section, and a newcomer reading it cold gets nothing from it.
