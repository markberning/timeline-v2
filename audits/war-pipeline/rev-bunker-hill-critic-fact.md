## BLOCKER

**B1. Dossier section-3 blurb claims Bunker Hill "put Washington in command of a war" — false causation, not in the pack.**
> blurb: "The shock ended one general's career, haunted another, fooled the winners' enemies into overconfidence, **and put Washington in command of a war**."

The Continental Congress appointed Washington commander-in-chief on **June 15, 1775 — two days BEFORE the battle** (he accepted June 16, took command at Cambridge July 3). The battle cannot have put him in command. The pack never makes this claim (§6 says only "Washington, taking command at Cambridge July 3"); the section-3 prose itself handles it correctly ("Two weeks after the battle, on July 3, George Washington took command of the army at Cambridge" — no causal claim). The blurb is a reader-facing dossier surface and is flat wrong as written.
**Fix:** replace the final clause, e.g. "...fooled the winners' enemies into overconfidence, and handed Washington an army convinced of itself and almost out of powder."
**Web check:** [History.com — June 15, 1775, Congress votes Washington to lead the Continental Army](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/june-15/george-washington-assigned-to-lead-the-continental-army); [LOC Continental Congress timeline 1775](https://www.loc.gov/collections/continental-congress-and-constitutional-convention-from-1774-to-1789/articles-and-essays/timeline/1775/).

**B2. The Winslow Homer work is a WOOD ENGRAVING, not an "oil painting" — medium invented, and wrong.**
> Image 5 caption: "Winslow Homer's 1875 **painting** of Bostonians watching the battle from Copp's Hill..."
> Image 5 credit: "Winslow Homer · **oil painting** · 1875 · Cleveland Museum of Art..."
> Section 2 MEANWHILE: "A century later Winslow Homer was still **painting** the watchers on the hill."

The pack (§9.10) names the work, date, and museum but gives **no medium**; the draft asserted "oil painting." Verified directly against the Cleveland Museum of Art's record for accession **1942.1327**: the work is a **wood engraving**, 1875 (published in Harper's Weekly, June 26, 1875).
**Fix:** caption → "Winslow Homer's 1875 wood engraving, published in Harper's Weekly, of Bostonians watching the battle from Copp's Hill..."; credit → "Winslow Homer · wood engraving · 1875 · Cleveland Museum of Art / Wikimedia Commons · public domain"; MEANWHILE → "...a century later Winslow Homer was still drawing the watchers on the hill" (or "picturing"). Also worth a pack addendum so the medium is on record.
**Web check:** [Cleveland Museum of Art, 1942.1327](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1942.1327) — "Medium: Wood engraving"; corroborated by [the Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/392163) and [Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/158343/the-battle-of-bunker-hill-watching-the-fight-from-copp-s-hill-in-boston) copies of the same engraving.

## SHOULD-FIX

**S1. Prescott "A Pepperell farmer" — "farmer" is not in the pack.**
> Prescott bio: "**A Pepperell farmer** and veteran of the 1745 Louisbourg expedition..."

Pack §3/§21 gives "of Pepperell MA; veteran of the 1745 Louisbourg expedition" — no occupation. Web-verifiable as true (Prescott was a Pepperell farmer), so the fix is either add it to the pack or write "A Pepperell man / of Pepperell." Out-of-pack assertions are the exact failure class the pipeline exists to stop, even when they happen to be right.

**S2. "his coat and waistcoat run through" — the pack says banyan, not coat.**
> Section 2: "Prescott, parrying bayonet thrusts with his sword, **his coat and waistcoat run through**, got out untouched..."

Pack §3: "his **banyan** and waistcoat pierced through." The draft itself established the banyan in Section 1; "coat" both drifts from the pack and quietly contradicts the earlier costume detail. Fix: "his banyan and waistcoat run through" (or the dossier bio's safe "his clothes pierced through").

**S3. Salem Poor petition addressee "the Massachusetts legislature" — not in the pack.**
> Section 2: "...moved fourteen officers to **petition the Massachusetts legislature** that he 'behaved like an experienced officer.'"

Pack §4/§8.6 says only "14 officers petitioned." The addressee (historically the Massachusetts General Court) is a draft addition. Fix: drop the addressee ("moved fourteen officers to petition that he...") or add it to the pack with a source.

**S4. Peter Salem "in the Massachusetts ranks" — not in the pack.**
> Section 2: "an 1818 account credits the shot to Peter Salem, a Black soldier **in the Massachusetts ranks**..."

Pack says "the Black soldier Peter Salem" with no unit/colony. True historically (Framingham, Mass. company), but out-of-pack. Fix: cut "in the Massachusetts ranks" or pack-add.

**S5. MEANWHILE (Section 2): Burgoyne and Clinton "close enough to direct the incendiary fire" — the pack doesn't say they directed it.**
> "Burgoyne and Clinton watched from the Copp's Hill battery..., **close enough to direct the incendiary fire** that burned Charlestown..."

Pack puts them at the battery (§3, §10) and the battery as a *source* of the incendiary fire (§4, §10), but never says either general directed it. Fix: "...watched from the Copp's Hill battery in Boston's North End, whose guns helped set Charlestown alight..."

**S6. The three generals "sent to put some aggression into the war" — purpose not in the pack.**
> Section 1: "...three newly arrived major generals..., **sent to put some aggression into the war**."

Pack §2 says only that Gage was "pushed by" the newly arrived three. The reason London sent them is a draft gloss. Fix: cut, or "whose arrival put new push behind Gage."

**S7. MEANWHILE (Section 1): "The southern heights stayed empty that summer" — not in the pack.**
> "Gage's June 12 plan had aimed at Dorchester Heights first... **The southern heights stayed empty that summer.**"

True (Dorchester Heights was not occupied until the Americans took it in March 1776), but the pack nowhere says it. Fix: cut the sentence (the MEANWHILE reads fine without it) or pack-add. Note the draft correctly avoids claiming Dorchester caused the evacuation, per its own ledger.

## NIT

**N1.** Section 1: garrison "penned in and **fed by sea**" — "fed by sea" not in the pack (true of besieged Boston, but an addition).
**N2.** Clinton bio: "Watching from the Copp's Hill battery **as the second attack failed**, he crossed the water" — the pack says only "crossed late on his own initiative to rally the reformed third assault"; the second-repulse timing is an (historically supportable) inference. Hedge or leave.
**N3.** Pyle caption: "it catches what the day's survivors **all** reported" — "all" is an overclaim flourish; the pack supports the substance (came on in order, three times, worst fire of the era) but not unanimity. Suggest "what the day's survivors reported."
**N4.** Section 3: "**Two weeks after** the battle, on July 3" — June 17 → July 3 is 16 days; harmless since the exact date is given, but "a little over two weeks" would be exact.
