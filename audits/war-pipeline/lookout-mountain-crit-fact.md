## ❌ MUST-FIX (factual error — correction + source)

**None.** No date, number, name, rank, place, or quote in the draft is factually wrong.
Every load-bearing claim the brief named was checked and stands:

- **LIGHT casualties** — ✅ confirmed. Union ~671 (89k / 471w / 111 c-or-m); Confederate
  ~1,251 of which **~1,064 captured/missing** (not killed). [Wikipedia infobox; ABT]
- **Summit NOT stormed — ABANDONED overnight** — ✅ confirmed. Bragg held a council that
  night and pulled the Lookout force off to reinforce Missionary Ridge; the summit troops
  escaped via the **Summertown Road**; the palisade was never assaulted. [Wikipedia:
  "Pettus and Holtzclaw received orders at 2 a.m. to march off the mountain"; ABT map page]
- **8th Kentucky raised the flag at dawn** — ✅ confirmed. Capt. John C. Wilson + 5 men of
  the 8th Kentucky Volunteer Infantry scaled the bluff at first light Nov 25 and unfurled the
  colors on the point of an already-abandoned summit, in view of both armies. [Kentucky Civil
  War Author; Kentucky Historical Society marker; NKyTribune]
- **Cravens House bench fight** — ✅ confirmed as the focal stand (Ireland front + Cobham
  flanking fire; Moore's ~1 p.m. counterattack stopped by the crossfire). [Wikipedia; ABT]
- **Grant's deflation ("all poetry")** — ✅ verbatim match to *Personal Memoirs*: "The battle
  of Lookout Mountain is one of the romances of the war. There was no such battle and no
  action even worthy to be called the battle on Lookout Mountain. It is all poetry."
  [Wikipedia, quoting Grant]
- **Meigs coined "Battle Above the Clouds"** — ✅ confirmed. "Brig. Gen. Montgomery C. Meigs,
  quartermaster general … observing the fog-shrouded action from Orchard Knob, was the first
  writer to name it the 'Battle Above the Clouds'." Both originator and Orchard Knob vantage
  are correct. [Wikipedia]
- **Date: November 24, 1863** — ✅ confirmed. [Wikipedia, ABT, HoW]

## ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX (precision / nuance — not a hard error)

1. **Geary "inaccessible heights" quote — partial quotation, verify it reads as a paraphrase.**
   The draft renders: Geary *"stormed what was considered the inaccessible heights."* The full
   sourced line (letter to his wife) is: *"I stormed what was considered the … inaccessible
   heights of Lookout Mountain. I captured it. This feat will be celebrated until time shall be
   no more."* The draft's clause is accurate as far as it goes and correctly attributed to the
   wife letter, but it silently telescopes across an ellipsis ("the … inaccessible heights").
   It is presented inside quotation marks. **Recommend:** either quote it exactly with the
   ellipsis, or recast as reported speech (no quote marks around the partial). Low severity —
   no word is invented — but it is a quotation-fidelity nit. [Wikipedia]

2. **Union casualty variant — draft handles it correctly; just confirming.** The draft pins
   the WP set (89k / 471w / 111 c-or-m / 671) and notes the "81 killed / ~629 total" variant.
   ✅ Both verified: Wikipedia infobox = 89/671; HoW = "81 killed out of 629 total." No fix
   needed — flagging only that the stat card must keep the variant note, not drop it.

3. **Confederate engaged-strength framing — verified, keep the hedge.** "~8,700 on paper
   (8,726 WP), ~3,000 in three brigades at the point of contact" is exactly right: HoW gives
   "three brigades (probably around 3,000 men)" on the western/northern slopes, with ~1,000 on
   the picket line. ✅ The draft's refusal to state a clean ratio is the correct call. No fix.

4. **Union strength ~10,000 vs ~12,000 (infobox).** ✅ Verified both: WP infobox ~12,000; HoW
   ~10,000. Draft uses ~10,000 in prose and flags the ~12,000 infobox figure — correct. The
   stat card should carry the ~12,000 WP figure for cross-dossier consistency with a note, as
   the factpack recommends. No prose fix.

5. **Osterhaus attachment reason — verified precisely.** Draft says his XV Corps division was
   "cut off on the wrong side of the river … and simply attached to Hooker." ✅ Confirmed: the
   Brown's Ferry pontoon bridge tore apart, stranding Osterhaus's division in Lookout Valley,
   so Grant reassigned it to Hooker. Accurate. No fix.

6. **"Drawn battle" minority framing — verified as a real sourced view, kept minority.** ✅
   HoW explicitly says Hooker's men "arrived at Missionary Ridge far too late to play an
   important part," supporting the "effectively a drawn battle" reading. The draft presents it
   as a defensible minority assessment, not consensus — correct handling. No fix.

7. **96th Illinois "roughest battle field of the war" — verified.** ✅ Wikipedia attributes it
   to a sergeant major of the 96th Illinois: "Much of the ground over which we advanced was
   rough beyond conception … undoubtedly the roughest battle field of the war." Draft quotes
   the operative phrase correctly. No fix.

8. **Brown / Pettus reinforcing brigades — verified.** ✅ Both named in Wikipedia as
   Stevenson's-division brigades brought into the fog-bound fight; coordination wrecked by mist.
   Accurate.

9. **Minor: Moore's brigade composition.** Draft calls Moore's counterattack force an
   "Alabama-and-Mississippi brigade." Sources describe Moore's command as Alabama regiments
   (Cheatham's Division). The "Mississippi" element is loose — Walthall's Mississippians were
   the forward/adjacent troops. **Recommend** trimming to "Alabama brigade" or "Alabama and
   Mississippi troops" generically. Very low severity. [Wikipedia, ABT]
