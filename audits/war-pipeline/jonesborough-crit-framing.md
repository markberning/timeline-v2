# Jonesborough — GATE 3: LOST CAUSE / FRAMING

**Verdict: PASS (FAIR). No MUST-FIX. Two SHOULD-FIX (tone only).**
This is among the strongest cause-framings in the war corpus. Slavery is named, in
the section's own prose, three separate times, structurally — not tokenized into one
ritual sentence and abandoned. Both cause-hooks are honest and load-bearing, and the
Ebenezer Creek inclusion is handled as moral complexity, not exploited.

---

## FIRST CHECK — is slavery named as the cause, in the section's OWN voice? **YES.**

Three independent, non-redundant placements:

1. **§1, before the shooting** — "The Confederacy existed to preserve and extend
   slavery: eleven states had broken from the United States rather than accept any
   limit on the enslavement of Black people, and they built their armies to defend
   that." This is the cause stated flatly, in the app's own voice, up front. It also
   pre-frames Atlanta as "the place where the war matériel of a slaveholding republic
   was forged." Passes the locked NAME-THE-CAUSE gate on its own.

2. **§4, the election** — slavery named as the *stake* of the 1864 vote: a negotiated
   Democratic peace "would have meant leaving the Confederacy in existence and
   slavery intact"; Lincoln's win meant "the war would be fought through to the end
   of slavery rather than negotiated to preserve it"; "the Thirteenth Amendment,
   abolishing slavery, followed"; "whether four million people would be freed or left
   in bondage." Honest, specific, non-euphemistic.

3. **§5, the meaning** — slavery as the *reason* the war was fought, made concrete:
   "people turning the war into their own freedom, in real time, by the thousands";
   closing MEANWHILE — "a republic founded to keep four million people in bondage."

The gate's auto-fail trigger (the word "slavery" never appearing / "a railroad town"
framing standing alone) is nowhere near tripped. The draft explicitly *anticipates*
and *refuses* that failure: §1 — "a rail junction is a thing you capture, not a thing
you fight a war for." That is the Lost-Cause reflex named and rejected in-text.

---

## Hook A (self-emancipating thousands) — honest, non-tokenizing? **YES.**

- Self-emancipation kept as the *verb*: "enslaved people freed themselves by the
  thousands," "a caravan of self-emancipated people," "people turning the war into
  their own freedom." The enslaved are agents, not passive recipients or scenery —
  the single most important framing test, and it passes.
- Their material contribution is credited (guides, intelligence, hidden stores) — not
  a sentimental backdrop but active participation. Faithful to Parten/[GPB].
- NOT tokenizing: the thread is the section's declared thesis ("the deepest meaning
  of the battle is… on the roads south of Atlanta"), not a closing genuflection.

## Hook B (the election about slavery) — honest? **YES.**

- Refuses the "a Union victory secured Lincoln's reelection" sanitized version and
  names what the reelection was *for*: the end of slavery vs. its preservation. The
  Democratic peace platform is correctly framed as, in practice, leaving slavery
  intact — historically accurate, not strawmanned (the draft notes McClellan
  personally repudiated the peace plank, so it does not caricature the party).

---

## Ebenezer Creek — handled as moral complexity, not exploited? **YES.**

This was the gate's sharpest test and the draft handles it with real care:
- It is framed as **a Union general's atrocity** — "one of the cruelest things any
  Union officer did," "one of the march's most monstrous acts." The draft refuses to
  let the Union wear a clean white hat. That is the opposite of Lost-Cause framing
  (which inverts to whitewash the Confederacy); here the discomfort is aimed
  *inward*, at the winning side, which is honest history.
- It is explicitly NOT smoothed: "That is not a contradiction to be smoothed away. It
  is the war itself, unsanitized." The draft does the moral work out loud.
- It does NOT exploit the victims for shock or false balance — the refugees remain
  the people the war was freeing, and Davis's act is named cruelty, not "the tragic
  cost of war." No euphemism, no both-sidesism.
- Timeline is honest (Dec 9, 1864, three months later, during the March) — so it is
  never used to imply Union savagery *at* Jonesborough; see the fact gate.
- **One guardrail to watch (SHOULD-FIX, tone):** the §3 tease — "for reasons that
  turn the stomach" — and §4 subhead language risk a faint *true-crime* relish around
  Davis. The §5 payoff earns it by insisting on the moral knot rather than the gore,
  so it stays on the right side. Keep an eye that revision doesn't sharpen the tease
  into ghoulishness; the victims, not Davis's villainy, are the point.

---

## Soft-pedaling / false-balance / romanticization scan

- **No romanticization of the Confederate cause.** Hood's army defends "a slaveholding
  republic." No "heroic Southern stand," no gallantry gloss. Cleburne is called "the
  most respected fighting general" — a fact about his reputation, immediately
  complicated (his own death at Franklin), not a valorization of his cause.
- **No "states' rights" / "way of life" euphemism.** Slavery is named as slavery.
- **The one real false-balance risk — the §4 MEANWHILE caveat** ("Hardee's defense
  bought the Confederate escape… narrowly a Confederate tactical success"). This is
  the Castel reading and it is handled correctly: presented as a "tactical footnote
  under an uncontested strategic verdict," not as genuine equivalence. It humanizes
  without exonerating. FAIR.
- **Presentism check:** the draft applies modern moral judgment (slavery = the cause;
  Ebenezer Creek = atrocity) but these are the *historically correct* judgments, not
  anachronism — abolitionists and the enslaved made exactly these judgments in 1864.
  No presentism problem.

---

## SHOULD-FIX (tone only — neither blocks ship)

1. **"the four words that turned a war" / "for reasons that turn the stomach."** Two
   bits of melodrama in the subheads. Not Lost-Cause and not false — but the section's
   power is in its plain statements; the teasers slightly cheapen them. Optional trim.

2. **Watch the Davis tease doesn't tip into relish on revision** (see Ebenezer Creek
   above). Currently fine.

**No MUST-FIX. Slavery is named, honestly and structurally; both hooks are
load-bearing; Ebenezer Creek is moral complexity, not exploitation. FRAMING: FAIR.**
