## OMISSIONS / underweighting flags

- **SHOULD-ADD (1) — Benjamin Butler & the Army of the James are invisible.** The pack
  names Butler (Army of the James, from which XVIII Corps was drawn, already at Bermuda
  Hundred). The draft never tells the reader that Smith's corps came from Butler's army,
  not Meade's, and that Butler was already operating near Petersburg. This matters lightly
  for the geography of *why Smith was the one in position* on June 15 — but it is genuinely
  selective-trim territory, not a wrong picture. Worth one clause.
- **SHOULD-ADD (2) — the First Battle of Petersburg (June 9, "Old Men and Young Boys")
  goes unmentioned.** A reader may not know this is the *Second* battle and that a militia
  defense had already happened a week earlier. One sentence would orient the reader and
  reinforce the "scratch defenders" theme. Not essential.
- **SHOULD-ADD (3) — Kautz's cavalry.** The pack lists Kautz (Union cavalry). Omitting an
  individual cavalry commander is fine and correct selectivity; flagging only because the
  cavalry's role in the approach is otherwise absent. Low priority.
- **SHOULD-ADD (4) — explicit "this is the OPENING of the siege, distinct from the siege
  itself" framing.** The draft does land this in §4 ("the Siege of Petersburg began"), but a
  reader could conflate the four-day assault with the 9½-month siege. The draft mostly
  handles it; a touch more separation at the top would help. Minor.

### Perspective / fact-pack blind spots
- **Confederate interiority is thin (acceptable).** We get Beauregard's bluff and his
  verdict, but the defenders are mostly a thin gray line. For a battle whose whole drama is
  "2,200 men bluffing an army," a beat more from the defenders' side (the home guard, old
  men and boys, the terror of holding a 10-foot-spaced line) would deepen it — but this is
  enrichment, not a gap. SHOULD-ADD at most.
- **The enslaved laborers as people, not just a fact.** The draft states the construction
  fact powerfully and lands the irony, which is the right move. The fact pack itself is a
  blind spot here: it has the *numbers* (264 + 1,000) but no individual voice or condition-
  of-labor detail (EV notes 8 a.m.–4 p.m. labor, 60-day limits, impressment quotas). The
  draft is right not to invent; one concrete labor detail from EV (the impressment mechanism)
  could be added if the author wants more texture. Not required.
- **No blind spot creating a factual error** — the draft never fills a pack gap with
  invention. Where the pack flagged [VERIFY], the draft trimmed. That is the correct posture.
