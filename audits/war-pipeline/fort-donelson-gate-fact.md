## MUST-FIX

### ❌ 1. Timberclad count: draft says TWO, sources say THREE

**Draft (the-gunboats section):**
> "four ironclads (the *St. Louis*, *Carondelet*, *Louisville*, and *Pittsburg*) and **two timberclads** behind them ... the *Tyler* and *Conestoga*"

**What the sources say:** The Western Gunboat Flotilla at Fort Donelson had **three** timberclads: USS *Tyler*, USS *Conestoga*, and USS *Lexington*. Wikipedia's infobox (Naval force: "4 ironclad gunboats + **3** timberclad gunboats under Foote") and multiple secondary sources confirm this. During the February 14 attack itself, Tyler and Conestoga are consistently named as the ones that "lobbed shells from a distance"; the Lexington's precise role on that day is less uniformly specified across sources, but the flotilla composition is three wooden gunboats, not two.

**Correct text:** "four ironclads (the *St. Louis*, *Carondelet*, *Louisville*, and *Pittsburg*) and **three timberclads** behind them (the *Tyler*, *Conestoga*, and *Lexington*)"

**Sources:** Wikipedia infobox; U.S. Naval History and Heritage Command; multiple secondary sources.

---

### ❌ 2. Nashville fall date: draft says February 23; formal surrender was February 25

**Draft (unconditional-surrender section):**
> "Nashville, the capital of Tennessee, was abandoned almost at once and fell on **February 23**, the first Confederate state capital to fall in the war."

**What the sources say:** The formal surrender occurred on **February 25, 1862**, when Mayor R.B. Cheatham surrendered the city to General Buell. February 23 was when a Union cavalry regiment (4th Ohio) crossed the river and set up pickets on the east bank — an advance element, not the formal occupation. The definitive date in most authoritative sources (Nashville Sites, Late Unpleasantness blog, History.com) is February 25. Some popular sources (Emerging Civil War anniversary post, some quick-reference pages) use February 23; that date likely reflects Confederate evacuation or the first cavalry contact rather than the Union occupation proper. The "first Confederate state capital to fall" designation is confirmed across all sources.

**Correct text:** "fell on **February 25**" (or, if hedging is preferred: "was abandoned within days and formally occupied on February 25")

**Sources:** Nashville Sites "Surrender of Nashville"; multiple secondary accounts.

## SHOULD-FIX

### ⚠️ 3. Foote's wound described as "ankle" — primary sources say "foot" (from wood splinters, not a direct projectile)

**Draft (the-gunboats section):**
> "Foote himself was wounded, hit in the ankle (accounts differ on whether it was a rifle shot or a shell fragment)."

**What the sources say:** Multiple sources (Wikipedia, ABT, Naval History) describe the wound as being in the **foot** (not ankle), caused by **wood splinters** from a shot that tore into the pilot house of the flagship *St. Louis*. Wikipedia even notes it as "coincidentally in his foot." The factpack correctly flags "ankle wound from a rifle shot or shell fragment; accounts vary" — but the dominant sourced description is splinter-to-foot, not ankle-from-direct-projectile. The draft's "rifle shot or shell fragment" framing is plausible (accounts do vary in secondary literature) but omits the most commonly cited mechanism: wood splinters.

**Recommended fix:** "Foote himself was wounded, struck in the foot by wood splinters when a Confederate shot tore through the pilot house of his flagship (some accounts vary on the exact wound)."

**Sources:** Wikipedia; ABT fact sheet; Naval History.

---

### ⚠️ 4. Grant's "attacks first" paraphrase matches *Personal Memoirs* substance but slightly mischaracterizes the knapsack detail

**Draft (the-breakout section):**
> "the captured Confederate soldiers were carrying full knapsacks, three days' rations packed for a march"

**What *Personal Memoirs* says (Ch. XXII):** Grant writes that he heard men say the enemy "had come out with knapsacks, and haversacks filled with rations." The draft renders this as "full knapsacks, three days' rations" — the "three days' rations" specificity is not in the Grant Memoirs passage. "Three days' rations" appears in the factpack as an editorial inference/synthesis. It is plausible given standard Civil War marching practice but is not directly sourced to the knapsack observation Grant recorded.

**Recommended fix:** Drop "three days'" or tag it as approximate: "full knapsacks and haversacks — rations for a march, not a sustained fight."

**Sources:** Grant, *Personal Memoirs*, Vol. I, Ch. XXII (Wikisource).

---

### ⚠️ 5. Grant's surrender quote: minor article variant

**Draft:**
> "No terms except **an** unconditional and immediate surrender can be accepted."

**The original letter (Smithsonian/Gilder Lehrman):** "No terms except **unconditional and immediate** surrender can be accepted." (no article "an"). Some transcriptions add "an"; the Smithsonian object page uses no article. This is minor and widely reproduced both ways, but the most document-faithful version omits "an." The current draft wording is in wide circulation and not a hard error; noting it here for the record.

**Assessment:** Not worth changing given how ubiquitous both versions are. But if the draft is ever revised, the no-article form ("No terms except unconditional and immediate surrender") is closer to the original letter.

**Sources:** Smithsonian Institution (nmah_439659); Gilder Lehrman Institute.

### ✅ CONFIRMED — Specifically Requested Checks

**Captured-count range (12,000–13,500):** ✅ CONFIRMED as the defensible editorial range. Wikipedia infobox gives 12,392 captured/missing; ABT uses ~13,000; Britannica goes high at ~15,000 (likely reflects Grant's field report). The draft's phrasing "roughly 12,000–13,500 ... up to 15,000 in some sources; ~12,400 most precise" is accurate and appropriately hedged.

**Feb 14 gunboat repulse:** ✅ CONFIRMED in all particulars (hit counts per ship, ~3:00 p.m. start, ~1.5 hours, closed to ~400 yards, two ironclads drifted unmanageable, 8 killed/44 wounded Union, zero Confederate casualties in the water batteries).

**Pillow's pull-back order (disputed):** ✅ CONFIRMED as genuinely contested, and the draft handles it correctly. Pillow's stated reason, Floyd accepting it, Buckner protesting and being overruled, and Pillow's "conscious of the commission of no errors" quote are all sourced and the draft accurately presents this as disputed rather than resolving it.

**Grant's "unconditional and immediate surrender" quote:** ✅ CONFIRMED — wording is correct and consistent across all sources (with only the minor "an" article variant noted above under SHOULD-FIX #5). The quote is well-documented.

**C.F. Smith coining the phrase — correctly omitted:** ✅ CONFIRMED omission is correct. The draft does not assert Smith coined the phrase or reproduce his alleged "No terms to the damned Rebels!" line. The factpack correctly flags this as a single-ABT-article claim. The draft's omission is the right editorial call.

**Grant's "attacks first" line as paraphrase, not hard quote:** ✅ CONFIRMED handled correctly. The draft explicitly notes "several slightly different versions" and does not present it as a verbatim quote. The *Personal Memoirs* text ("the one who attacks first now will be victorious and the enemy will have to be in a hurry if he gets ahead of me") matches the substance the draft conveys. The "three days' rations" specificity is flagged as SHOULD-FIX above.

**Forrest's escape — icy backwater of Lick Creek:** ✅ CONFIRMED in substance. Forrest led ~700 cavalrymen through the flooded, icy Lick Creek, encountered no enemy, and the escape is documented as proof the route was open. The draft calls it "waist-deep backwater" — the sourced accounts describe it as flooded and icy (cavalry scouts reportedly found it 3 feet deep or more in some accounts); "waist-deep" is consistent with that range for cavalry horses and troopers. This is acceptable.

**Nashville — first Confederate state capital to fall:** ✅ CONFIRMED as a claim (Nashville was the first Confederate state capital to fall to Union forces). The DATE (February 23 vs. February 25) is the error flagged in MUST-FIX #2 above.

**Western line collapse:** ✅ CONFIRMED. Columbus evacuated March 2 (consistent with the draft's "early March"); the entire western cordon collapsed; rivers opened. All confirmed across Wikipedia and ABT.

**Grant's promotion:** ✅ CONFIRMED: Major General of Volunteers, second in seniority in the West to Halleck. Confirmed in Wikipedia and ABT.

**Buckner–Grant personal history (West Point 1840s, Winfield Scott/Mexico, Popocatépetl, 1854 Astor House loan):** ✅ ALL CONFIRMED. The Popocatépetl expedition is confirmed (April 1848 per Buckner's Wikipedia entry; Grant's own memoirs say "I was with him on a visit to the top of Popocatépetl"). The draft says "1847" — the expedition was April 1848 (after the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, while troops awaited withdrawal). This is a marginal date question; Grant and Buckner both served under Scott in 1847 and the post-war period extended into 1848. The factpack does not give a year for the Popocatépetl climb and the draft does not either (the draft says "including a climb up the volcano Popocatépetl in 1847" — citing the year of the Mexico campaign, not necessarily the climb itself; this is borderline). Worth flagging but not blocking.

**Buckner pallbearer at Grant's 1885 funeral:** ✅ CONFIRMED (Grant Cottage NHS; Wikipedia on Buckner).

**Buckner accepting/declining Grant's money at Dover Tavern:** ✅ CONFIRMED as genuinely uncertain; the draft correctly frames it as "accounts differ." (Grant Cottage says declined; ABT says accounts differ; the ABT "Myth" article cites Buckner's 1904 claim that he struck the purse from Grant's hand.)

**"Why should we go through vain forms?" quote:** ✅ Sourced to NPS "Grant at Fort Donelson" article. Confirmed present in that source per factpack; NPS text as fetched did not return it in this pass but factpack's provenance is solid. Acceptable.
