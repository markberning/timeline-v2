## ❌ MUST-FIX

**1. The "roughly an eighth" Union casualty-share claim is only true against the *largest*
Union strength number — and the draft's whole "far heavier share" contrast collapses under
the other sourced figure.**
- Draft §hooker-quits: "something like a fifth of his entire force [South], against roughly
  an eighth of Hooker's [North]."
- Confederate share: 12,764/60,298 = **21.2%** (Wiki); 13,460/57,352 = **23.5%** (ABT). So
  "a fifth" is a slight *under*-statement but defensible.
- Union share: 17,287/**133,868** = **12.9%** ("an eighth," true) — BUT 17,304/**97,382** =
  **17.8%** (ABT). Against ABT's strength the Union also lost ~⅙–⅕, nearly the same
  proportion, which guts the "far heavier share of a much smaller body" point.
- **Correction:** either (a) anchor the proportional contrast explicitly to the high-count
  framing the draft already uses elsewhere (~130k Union → ~13% vs ~60k CSA → ~21%), or
  (b) drop the precise "a fifth vs an eighth" and keep only the directional, source-robust
  claim ("a far larger share of a much smaller army"). Do NOT leave the bare "fifth vs
  eighth" — it is fragile to which source the reader trusts.
- Source: [Wikipedia infobox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chancellorsville)
  (133,868 / 60,298; 17,287 / 12,764); [American Battlefield Trust](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/civil-war/battles/chancellorsville)
  (97,382 / 57,352; 17,304 / 13,460).

**2. Name Couch as the "whipped man" speaker — the source is on the record, so the vague
"a subordinate reportedly came away convinced" understates a known, citable fact.**
- Draft §lee-divides: "A subordinate reportedly came away convinced that 'my commanding
  general was a whipped man'."
- The speaker is **Maj. Gen. Darius N. Couch (North)**, in his own published recollection
  (*Battles and Leaders*): "I retired from his presence with the belief that my commanding
  general was a whipped man." This is the exact, attributed wording — not anonymous.
- **Correction:** attribute to Couch by name (he is already a named, side-tagged figure in
  §jackson-falls, so this also strengthens the cast). The "reportedly" hedge is unnecessary
  for a first-person published quote; "Couch later wrote that he came away believing…" is
  accurate. Keeping "a subordinate" is not *wrong*, but it is a needless loss of a verified,
  on-the-record attribution.
- Source: Couch, "The Chancellorsville Campaign," *Battles and Leaders of the Civil War*
  (via [historycentral.com transcript](https://www.historycentral.com/CivilWar/chance/Couch.html)).

## ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX

**A. Hooker's boast is a documented written order, not a "reportedly" rumor — the hedge is
over-cautious and slightly mis-frames it.**
- Draft §the-plan: "Hooker reportedly boasted in an order to his troops that Lee must now
  'ingloriously fly'…"
- This is **General Orders No. 47, Army of the Potomac, April 30, 1863**, verbatim: "our
  enemy must either ingloriously fly, or come out from behind his defenses and give us
  battle on our own ground, where certain destruction awaits him." It is a real published
  order, not an attributed-as-traditional anecdote.
- **Suggestion:** the draft may safely state it as fact ("in General Orders No. 47 on
  April 30, Hooker told the army that Lee must 'ingloriously fly'…"). "Reportedly" is not an
  error (it is conservative) but it undersells a hard primary source. Either is acceptable;
  factually the quote and date are CONFIRMED.
- Source: General Orders No. 47, Apr 30 1863 (text confirmed via multiple transcriptions,
  e.g. [the-american-catholic.com](https://the-american-catholic.com/2025/04/30/april-30-1863-fighting-joe-boasts/)).

**B. Wildlife detail — the standard accounts say "turkeys, rabbits, and deer"; the draft's
"deer and rabbits" omits the turkeys.** Not wrong, but if naming the animals, the attested
trio is deer/rabbits/turkeys (and game generally). The draft's [VERIFY]/"according to
accounts" softening is satisfied — the detail IS attested at the preserved flank-attack
landmark. Keep the hedge or cite it; either is fine.
- Source: [Central Virginia Battlefields Trust, "Jackson's Flank Attack"](https://cvbt.org/battlefields/flank-attack/)
  ("sudden eruption of wild game — turkeys, rabbits, and deer").

**C. Jackson's resting place — "Chandler plantation" is correct but the building was the
plantation *office* at the Chandler (Fairfield) plantation, Guinea Station; the 27-mile
distance is confirmed (draft's "~27 to 30 miles" is fine, lean to 27).** Detail-level only.
- Source: [NPS / History Hit Jackson Shrine](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/jackson-shrine/),
  [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/may-10/thomas-j-stonewall-jackson-dies)
  (age 39, died May 10, pneumonia — all CONFIRMED).

**D. ~14,000 holding-force figure (§lee-divides [I] beat) — derived, not sourced; keep the
caveat or convert to a clearly-approximate statement.** The arithmetic (60k − ~11k Early −
~28k Jackson ≈ 21k, or with the ~14k figure the pack used a tighter accounting) is a
back-of-envelope estimate. Sources confirm the *structure* (Early ~11,000; Jackson ~28,000;
Lee left "fewer than ~14,000–17,000" facing Hooker) but no single source prints "14,000."
**Suggestion:** render as "perhaps 14,000 — a sliver" / "barely 14,000" framing, NOT a hard
count, exactly as the draft already flags. The *point* (Lee left a fraction in front of the
bulk of Hooker's army) is solid and well-sourced. Acceptable as written if kept approximate.

**E. "70,000-odd" Union directly facing Lee (§lee-divides) — approximate, unverified to a
single source.** Low stakes; keep "-odd"/approximate. Not a ship-blocker.

## ✅ CONFIRMED (independently verified, ships as-is)

- **Strengths:** Union ~97,382 (ABT) – 133,868 (Wiki/Sears); Confederate ~57,352 (ABT) –
  60,298 (Wiki). Draft's "~130,000 vs ~60,000, better than two to one" and the inline
  source-vary caveat are CORRECT. ✓
- **Casualties:** Union 17,287 (Wiki) / 17,304 (ABT); Confederate 12,764 (Wiki) / 13,460
  (ABT); combined ~30,000–30,800. Draft's "~17,300 / ~13,000 / ~30,000" CORRECT. ✓
- **XI Corps loss:** ~2,500 (259 k / 1,173 w / 994 m). CORRECT. ✓
- **Jackson:** three balls, **left** arm, fired on by the **18th North Carolina**,
  amputated May 3, moved 27 mi to the Chandler plantation, Guinea Station, pneumonia, died
  **May 10, 1863, age 39**. ALL CONFIRMED. ✓
- **Lee "lost my right arm":** correctly rendered indirect ("Lee… is said to have answered
  that…") — widely attributed, no clean single primary; hedge is correct. ✓
- **Jackson's last words ("cross over the river… shade of the trees"):** correctly hedged
  "reported." Confirmed that the source (Dr. Hunter McGuire) admitted he was not present at
  the moment, and Anna Jackson recalled "*pass* over the river." Draft's "reportedly" is the
  right call. ✓ ([Emerging Civil War](https://emergingcivilwar.com/2016/12/21/stonewall-jacksons-last-words/))
- **Rodes's division led the assault and "rolled… over" the XI Corps.** CONFIRMED. ✓
- **Jackson flank march ~28,000–30,000 men, ~12 miles, ~7–8 a.m.** CONFIRMED (Wiki: 28,000,
  12 miles). ✓ XI Corps struck ~5:30 p.m. ✓
- **May 3 timings:** Fairview fell ~9:30 a.m.; Hooker concussed ~9:15 a.m. on the Chancellor
  house porch/pillar. CONFIRMED. ✓
- **Stuart's first infantry command;** took over Second Corps after Jackson (and A.P. Hill)
  fell, renewed assault May 3, seized Hazel Grove. CONFIRMED. ✓
- **Wilderness brush fires burned wounded of both sides** — documented recurring horror;
  draft prints no specific toll and keeps the [VERIFY] soften. CONFIRMED as a real,
  attested phenomenon. ✓ ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/228hpr-18a088bfebccbc9/))
- **Emancipation Proclamation took effect Jan 1, 1863** (four months prior). CONFIRMED. ✓
- **Salem Church / Second Fredericksburg / Marye's Heights / Early / McLaws / Sedgwick
  withdrawal across Banks' Ford** — consistent with sources; "Banks' Ford" is the standard
  label (Scott's Ford nearby), draft's inline map caveat is appropriate. ✓
- **Names/ranks:** Hooker (Maj. Gen.), Lee (Gen.), Jackson (Lt. Gen.), Stuart, Howard,
  Sedgwick, Sickles, Couch, Schurz, Early, McLaws, Rodes (Brig. Gen.), Alexander (Col.),
