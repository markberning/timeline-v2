## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name — clarify this for the reader (it's the headline naming oddity):** the battle's
  **official War Department name is the Battle of Opequon** (or "Opequon," after **Opequon
  Creek**, which the Union army crossed to reach the field). But it is **universally known as
  the Third Battle of Winchester**, because the town of **Winchester, Virginia** changed
  hands so often in the war that there were *three* named battles fought there (First
  Winchester, 1862; Second Winchester, 1863; this one, the third and largest). Author should
  name both up front and explain the "third" (the town's serial battles), and note "Opequon"
  is the formal name almost nobody uses. **[WIKI] [ABT-FACTS]**
  - **Pronunciation note (for the reader, [VERIFY] before stating):** "Opequon" is commonly
    rendered roughly **oh-PECK-on**. *Flag [VERIFY] — do not assert a pronunciation guide
    unless the fact-checker confirms a sourced rendering; safe to simply call it "Opequon
    Creek" without phonetics.*
- **Date:** **September 19, 1864** (a single day; before dawn cavalry probing through to
  dusk). **[WIKI] [ABT-FACTS] [NPS]**
- **Location:** **near Winchester, Frederick County, Virginia**, in the **lower (northern)
  Shenandoah Valley**. Coordinates ~**39°11′44″N 78°07′57″W**. **[WIKI]**
- **Theatre:** **Eastern Theater.** Part of **Sheridan's Shenandoah Valley Campaign of 1864**
  (Aug–Oct 1864), the campaign Grant ordered to take the Valley out of the war. **[WIKI] [NPS]**
- **Result:** **Union victory.** **[WIKI] [ABT-FACTS] [NPS]**
- **Scale:** the **largest and one of the bloodiest (most costly) battles fought in the
  Shenandoah Valley** — more than **54,000 men engaged, with over 8,600 casualties.** **[NPS]
  [ABT-FACTS]**

### Why the Valley mattered — the strategic spine
- The **Shenandoah Valley** was **"the breadbasket of the Confederacy"** — a corridor of
  prosperous farms whose grain, livestock, and forage fed **Lee's Army of Northern Virginia**
  at Petersburg/Richmond. It also ran like a **covered highway pointed at the North**: a
  Confederate army marching *down* the Valley (north — the Valley drains north, so "down the
  Valley" = northward) emerged near Washington, while a Union army in it threatened nothing
  vital. That asymmetry made the Valley a recurring Confederate invasion route (Jackson 1862;
  Early's raid that reached the gates of Washington in July 1864). **[SOSPACES] [WIKI]**
- **Grant's 1864 strategy:** end that. He sent **Sheridan (North)** with orders not just to
  beat Early's army but to **strip the Valley of its ability to feed and shelter an army** —
  the campaign that became known as **"The Burning"** (see §9). **[WIKI] [SOSPACES]**
- **Immediate prelude:** **Early (South)** had spent the summer raiding north (he had reached
  the outskirts of Washington in July 1864 and burned Chambersburg, Pennsylvania, in retaliation
  earlier in the campaign — *prelude context, [VERIFY] dates if used in prose*). By
  mid-September his **Army of the Valley was dispersed/over-extended** around Winchester, and
  he had just **detached a division (Kershaw's) back toward Lee** — the weakness Sheridan
  learned of and pounced on (see §8, the Rebecca Wright / Thomas Laws intelligence). **[WIKI]
  [SVBNHD]**

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (**north = up**; note the Valley convention — "down the Valley" means
**north**, "up the Valley" means **south**):

- **Winchester** sits at the center, a **road hub** in the lower Valley. **[WIKI] [NPS]**
- **Opequon Creek** runs **roughly north–south, east of Winchester** — the natural barrier
  Sheridan's army crossed coming from the **east**. Named fords/crossings include **Spout
  Spring ford, Locke's Ford, and Burns Ford.** **[WIKI] [NPS]**
- **The Berryville Pike** runs **west from Berryville (east) into Winchester**, crossing the
  Opequon and then threading the **"Berryville Canyon"** — a **narrow, ~2-mile defile/ravine**
  between the creek and the open ground east of town. **This canyon is the battle's defining
  terrain problem** (see §5): it was the **only practical approach axis for Sheridan's infantry
  from the east**, and it choked. **[WIKI] [NPS] [ABT-ART]**
- **The Valley Pike** (Valley Turnpike) runs **north–south through Winchester** — the macadam
  highway that was **Early's line of retreat south** (toward Newtown / Strasburg / Fisher's
  Hill). **[WIKI] [NPS]**
- **Other roads radiating from Winchester:** **Martinsburg Pike (north)**, **Front Royal Pike
  (southeast)** — the spokes that made the town a hub and the cavalry's avenues of approach.
  **[WIKI]**
- **Confederate line:** Early deployed **east/northeast of Winchester** on the high ground
  between the town and the Opequon, with **fortifications north of town — Fort Collier, Star
  Fort, Fort Jackson, and the "Smithfield Redoubt."** His **left flank lay to the north**,
  which is exactly where the Union cavalry massed. **[WIKI] [NPS]**
- **The Union attack came from the east** (infantry up the Berryville Pike out of the canyon),
  while **the cavalry swept down from the north/northeast** (Merritt, Averell, Custer) onto
  Early's **left flank** — the **hammer-and-anvil** that broke the Confederate line. **[WIKI]
  [NPS]**
- **Map orientation summary (north up):** Winchester center; **Opequon Creek and the Berryville
  Canyon to the east** (Union infantry attack arrow comes **from the east**, pinched through
  the canyon); **Early's line arcing east/north of town** with the **forts (Collier/Star/
  Jackson) to the north**; **Union cavalry arrows sweeping in from the north/northeast** onto
  Early's left; **Confederate retreat arrow exiting south down the Valley Pike** through
  Winchester toward Fisher's Hill.
- **Map labels should match the prose's named places:** Winchester, Opequon Creek, Berryville
  Pike / Berryville Canyon, Valley Pike, Martinsburg Pike, Fort Collier / Star Fort, Newtown
  (south). **[per war-map doctrine — many maps, big legible labels matched to named places.]**

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup:** Acting on intelligence that **Early (South) had detached Kershaw's division** and
was over-extended (see §8), **Sheridan (North) attacked** to destroy the Army of the Valley
before it could reconcentrate. **[WIKI] [SVBNHD]**

### Before dawn — the cavalry seizes the crossing
- **~2:00 a.m.: Wilson's cavalry (North) advanced from the east**, secured the **Opequon
  crossing / Spout Spring ford** and pushed up the Berryville Pike to seize **Limestone
  Ridge**, opening the approach to Winchester. **[WIKI]**

### The near-disaster — the Berryville Canyon traffic jam
- The infantry had to follow Wilson **through the narrow Berryville Canyon**. **Wright's VI
  Corps (North) reached the canyon first (~5:00 a.m.) but pushed its wagon train and artillery
  into the defile ahead of the infantry**, and **ambulances carrying McIntosh's wounded back
  out** met the **wagons going in** — a **massive traffic jam.** **[WIKI] [ABT-ART]**
- The result: **Emory's XIX Corps (North) could not even enter the canyon until ~9:00 a.m.**,
  and **Sheridan's attack was delayed by over two hours.** **[WIKI] [NPS]** That delay let
  **Early (South) — who had been dangerously dispersed — pull his divisions together** in
  front of Winchester. The "easy" surprise blow became a stand-up fight against a
  concentrated enemy. **The canyon is the section's "it nearly went wrong" beat.** **[NPS]
  [ABT-ART]**

### Late morning — the main assault and the gap
- **~11:40 a.m.: Wright's VI Corps and Emory's XIX Corps (North) attacked simultaneously**
  east of Winchester. **[WIKI]**
- Early initially gave ground (**Ramseur (South)** pushed back), but **a gap opened between
  the two Union corps** as they advanced over uneven ground. **Rodes (South) and Gordon
  (South) counterattacked into that gap**, throwing the Union center back in disorder. **[WIKI]
  [NPS]**
- **Turning point #1 — Russell's counter and death:** **Brig. Gen. David A. Russell (North)**
  brought up reserves (Edwards's brigade + regiments from Ricketts's division) to plug the
  gap. **As he ordered the counterattack, Russell was killed** (struck by a shell/shell
  fragment). **Emory Upton (North)** and Edwards restored the line — **"the turning point in
  the conflict."** **[WIKI]** In the same fighting, on the Confederate side, **Rodes (South)
  was killed** leading his counterattack (~12:30 p.m., see §6). **[WIKI] [NPS]**

### Afternoon — the flank attack breaks Early's army
- **~3:00 p.m.: Crook's Army of West Virginia (North) — the reserve — went in against Gordon's
  left flank (the Confederate north end)**, while **Merritt's and Averell's cavalry (North)
  drove in from the north.** The Confederate left was rolled up. **[WIKI] [NPS]**
- **~late afternoon (~5:00 p.m.): Custer (North) led a massed cavalry charge against the
  Confederate works (the Smithfield Redoubt / forts north of town).** With infantry pressing
  from the east and cavalry overrunning the left, **Early's line collapsed.** **[WIKI] [NPS]**

### The rout — "whirling through Winchester"
- **Early's army broke and retreated south, in disorder, through the streets of Winchester**
  and out the **Valley Pike** toward Newtown. **[WIKI] [NPS]** Sources call it a near-rout:
  one soldier described **"the most disorderly retreat I ever saw… the road was filled with
  fugitives from all commands."** **[NPS]** This is the origin of the famous phrase that
  Early's army was sent **"whirling through Winchester"** (see §7 for the contested
  attribution).
- **Early escaped to fight again at Fisher's Hill three days later (Sept 22).** **[WIKI] [NPS]**

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the local hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and each side — was ultimately fought over. Omission is
> the default Lost Cause failure; "the Valley's farms / a strategic corridor" framing alone
> fails the gate. The Shenandoah makes this concrete two ways: the *breadbasket* was a
> **slave-worked** agricultural economy feeding the Confederate army, and the battle itself was
> shaped by **an enslaved man who risked his life to help the Union win it.**

### The documented throughline
- The Confederacy fought to preserve **slavery**; the **Shenandoah Valley's value to that war
  effort was agricultural** — and that agriculture rested on enslaved labor. The Valley's
  **enslaved population was ~20.4% of the region in 1860** (down from a peak of ~26.1% in 1830),
  lower than the slaveholding counties east of the Blue Ridge but still a slave society;
  enslaved and free Black laborers worked the farms whose grain and livestock fed **Lee's
  army.** **[SOSPACES]** Sheridan's mission — to **burn that breadbasket** (see §9) — was an
  attack on the *material engine* of a slaveholding rebellion.
- **In Winchester specifically:** the **1860 census recorded 706 enslaved and 665 free Black
  people** in a town of ~4,400. When Union forces occupied the town, **"refugee slaves from
  the countryside entered Winchester in droves,"** freeing themselves; a Confederate diarist
  (Mary G. Lee) complained that **"[t]he freedom of the servants is one of the most irritating
  circumstances"** of Union presence — and when Confederates retook the town (1862, 1863,
  1864) they **"captured many local slaves and returned them to their owners."** Winchester
  changing hands wasn't an abstraction; for enslaved people it was the difference between
  freedom and re-enslavement. **[ENCVA]**

### STRONG LOCAL HOOK — Thomas Laws, the enslaved man who shaped this battle **[WGAZ] [SVBNHD] [ENCVA]**
The single best human anchor on this battlefield is that **the intelligence behind Sheridan's
attack was carried by an enslaved man at the risk of his life:**
- **Thomas (Tom) Laws** was an **enslaved Black man, age ~47, of Clarke County, Virginia**, who
  held **a pass to cross between the lines** (selling produce). Union scouts recruited him.
  **[WGAZ] [SVBNHD]**
- At Sheridan's request, Laws carried a message **to Rebecca Wright (North/Unionist)** — a
  **Quaker schoolteacher in Winchester** whose loyalty George Crook (North) vouched for —
  **concealed in tin foil and held in his mouth** so it couldn't be found if he was searched.
  **[WGAZ] [SVBNHD]**
- **Wright's reply, carried back by Laws, told Sheridan that a Confederate division (Kershaw's)
  and artillery had been sent away from Early toward Lee** — i.e., **Early was weaker than
  Sheridan thought.** That intelligence is what convinced Sheridan to attack on **September 19,
  1864.** **[SVBNHD] [WGAZ]**
  - *([VERIFY] the precise content — "Kershaw's division detached toward Richmond/Lee" [SVBNHD]
    vs. "forces redirected toward Petersburg" [WGAZ] — sources phrase the destination
    differently; the *fact that Early had been weakened by a detachment* is the hard claim.
    Date of delivery reported as ~Sept 16. [VERIFY].)*
- **The point for the section:** an enslaved man — legally property of the society Early's army
  was fighting to defend — **risked his life to help the army that would free him win the
  battle.** The Valley was a slave-worked breadbasket; the man who helped break Early was
  enslaved. That is the war's cause made flesh on this exact field.

### Reinforcing context (use lightly)
- The Valley's Black community supported the Union war effort throughout; **enslaved people
  fled to Union lines** whenever the army arrived, and Valley men enlisted in the **U.S.
  Colored Troops (USCT)** (e.g., **Edward Hall**, an enslaved man who fled Winchester in late
  1863, joined the **30th USCT**, rose to sergeant, and fought at the Crater and Fort Fisher).
  **[ENCVA]** *(Color/context — Hall is illustrative of the USCT/emancipation throughline;
  [VERIFY] specifics before printing.)*

**object** (a breadbasket worth burning), **slavery was the reason** it was a breadbasket worth

## 9. Significance

- **The first of Sheridan's three decisive Valley victories** — **Third Winchester / Opequon
  (Sept 19) → Fisher's Hill (Sept 22) → Cedar Creek (Oct 19)** — the run that **destroyed
  Early's Army of the Valley as a fighting force.** This battle **"began a series of losses for
  Early's army in the Valley from which it would not recover."** **[ABT-FACTS] [NPS] [WIKI]**
- **Cleared the lower Valley and enabled "The Burning":** after driving Early south, Sheridan
  carried out **Grant's order to strip the Valley** — systematically **burning barns, mills,
  and crops and driving off livestock** so it could never again feed or shelter a Confederate
  army. Sheridan's own final report tallied the destruction: **"3,772 horses, 10,918 cattle,
  12,000 sheep, 15,000 hogs, 20,397 tons of hay, 435,802 bushels of wheat, 77,176 bushels of
  corn, 71 flour mills, and 1,200 barns"** destroyed or captured. **[SOSPACES]** *(Those are
  campaign-wide totals, not from this one battle — author must frame them as the Burning that
  *followed* the victory; [VERIFY] the figures if quoted to the digit.)*
- **Political effect — the 1864 election:** the victory landed **seven weeks before the
  presidential election**, reinforcing the lift from **Sherman's capture of Atlanta (Sept 2)**.
  Together they turned a war Northern voters had feared was stalemated into one visibly being
  won — **helping secure Lincoln's reelection.** **[NPS] [ABT-ART]**
- **Made Sheridan (North) a national figure** and effectively **removed the Shenandoah Valley
  — and Early's army — from the war.** **[NPS] [WIKI]**
