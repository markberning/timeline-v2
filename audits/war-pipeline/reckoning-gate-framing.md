## MUST-FIX (1 item — factual error)

### 1. Revels / Jefferson Davis seat — confirmed myth repeated as fact

**The draft says:**
> "the seat he filled had once belonged, with a bitter perfection nobody missed, to Jefferson Davis himself"

**This is historically incorrect.** Revels filled Albert Brown's former seat (expiring 1871). Davis's former seat (expiring 1875) was filled by Union General Adelbert Ames — a white candidate the Mississippi legislature deliberately chose for that seat. The NPS, U.S. Capitol Historical Society, and Smithsonian Magazine all confirm this. The "Davis seat" story is itself a known myth generated by the symbolic power of the moment — the enthusiasm of Radical Republicans and press coverage inspired the error, which then circulated for generations. It is exactly the kind of popular-history myth this section is built to correct.

**Why this matters for this specific section:** The "Reckoning" section explicitly confronts myths and corrects the historical record. Repeating a well-documented factual error — especially one the draft itself frames as "with a bitter perfection nobody missed" (rhetorical amplification of the false claim) — is self-undermining. If this section ships with a confirmable myth inside it, it undercuts the credibility of everything else.

**Fix:** The poetic framing can be preserved with a small adjustment. Revels did replace a senator from Mississippi — the state whose secession was led by Jefferson Davis, who became Confederate president. The symbolism is real and earned; the specific seat claim is not. A corrected version:

> "the seat he filled had once been held by a Mississippi Democrat who vacated it when the state seceded — the state whose departure from the Union had been led by none other than Jefferson Davis, now a prisoner at Fort Monroe"

Or more directly:

> "He represented Mississippi — the same state whose senator Jefferson Davis had abandoned when he became president of the Confederacy — and the Senate gallery packed in to watch."

The draft's fact ledger does not flag this claim as verified or contested, suggesting it entered as assumed common knowledge. It is the draft's only unverified concrete factual claim.

## SHOULD-FIX (2 items — accuracy refinements, not disqualifying)

### 2. McPherson endorsement slightly over-stated

**The draft says:** McPherson "judged that it should and would gain acceptance among most scholars of the war"

**What McPherson actually said:** He called the new estimate "plausible," said he'd "always been convinced that 620,000 is too low," and expressed he'd been "waiting more than twenty-five years for an article like this." He did not predict it "should and would gain acceptance among most scholars" in those terms — that phrasing puts stronger predictive certainty in his mouth than the record shows. He endorsed the direction and methodology, not the specific number.

**Fix:** Soften to what the record supports: "McPherson, the Pulitzer-winning historian of the war, called the new figure plausible and said he'd long been convinced the old count was too low" — which is accurate and still carries the weight the draft needs.

### 3. "130 years before the country impeached another president" — the draft itself flags this

The draft's own [VERIFY] note correctly flags that Clinton 1998 is not in the fact pack. This is uncontroversial but outside the pack. The suggested softening ("well over a century") is the right call. Implement it.
