## A. The new Mi'kmaq-resolution passage — ✅ PASS

Draft (¶53) now reads: organized resistance was *broken* (not ended) at Beauséjour in summer
1755; "Acadian and Mi'kmaq partisans fought on for years, in a guerrilla and privateer war led
by … Charles Deschamps de Boishébert … and … Joseph Broussard … known as Beausoleil, alongside
the Mi'kmaq, the Maliseet, and the Abenaki, into about 1758"; the Anglo-Mi'kmaq War "ended not
on a battlefield but at a negotiating table, with the Peace and Friendship Treaties signed at
Halifax in 1760 and 1761 and ceremonially sealed at the 'Burying the Hatchet' ceremony of 25
June 1761 … Those treaties ceded no land … the Supreme Court of Canada upheld their hunting and
fishing rights as never extinguished in its 1999 Marshall decision."

Verified independently:
- ✅ Resistance CONTINUED after Fort Beauséjour fell. Broussard led armed resistance "between
  1755 and 1758" within Boishébert's forces; raids continued (e.g. April 1757 near Fort Edward;
  altercations through Jan 1759 at Bloody Bridge). Wikipedia + Parks Canada + DCB all corroborate.
- ✅ Led by **Boishébert** (French officer) and **Joseph Broussard "Beausoleil"** (Acadian militia
  leader), alongside Mi'kmaq, Maliseet (Wolastoqiyik), Abenaki (W8banakiak). Confirmed.
- ✅ "into about 1758" — defensible. Broussard's heaviest assaults span 1755–1758; his ship was
  seized Nov 1758, then fallback to Miramichi with scattered actions into 1759/1762. The draft's
  hedged "into about 1758" is within the sourced range.
- ✅ War ended with **Peace and Friendship Treaties of 1760–1761**, signed at Halifax. Confirmed.
- ✅ **"Burying the Hatchet" ceremony, 25 June 1761** — confirmed (Belcher's garden, Spring Garden
  Road; chiefs washed war paint as token hostilities ended). 25 June 1761 is the standard date.
- ✅ **Ceded NO land** — confirmed; non-cession treaties, no land-transfer language.
- ✅ **R v. Marshall (1999)** — confirmed; SCC held the 1760–61 treaty hunting/fishing rights were
  never terminated.
- ✅ The draft NO LONGER says the war "wound down only when the fort fell in 1755." The flagged
  overstatement is gone: ¶53 explicitly says the fall of the fort "did not end the Mi'kmaq war,"
  and ¶59 says only that it "broke the organized … resistance in one stroke." Correction applied.

⚠️ Minor: draft says treaties "signed at Halifax in 1760 and 1761." Accurate for the headline
treaties, though a few signings (e.g. Pictou/Malogomich, 12 Oct 1761) trailed slightly later and
elsewhere. The "1760 and 1761" framing is standard and fine — not a correction.

## B. Port-Royal on the Annapolis Basin (inlet off the Bay of Fundy), not the Atlantic — ✅ PASS

Draft (¶17) "founded the village of Port-Royal the next year, on the Annapolis Basin, an inlet
off the Bay of Fundy in what would become Nova Scotia."

Verified: ✅ Geographically correct. The Annapolis Basin is a sheltered bay on the south shore of
the Bay of Fundy (a finger-like inlet on the Nova Scotian side); Port-Royal sat on its north
shore near the mouth of the Annapolis River. It is on the Bay of Fundy side, NOT the Atlantic
coast proper. (Founding date 1605 also consistent — "the next year" after the 1604 first
settlement, ¶17.)

## C. December 1758 shipwreck passage — ✅ PASS

Draft (¶105): "The Duke William foundered in the North Atlantic around 13 December … roughly 97
km (about 20 leagues) off the French coast … The Violet went down a day earlier with more than
280 Acadians, lost with all of them. The Ruby, carrying around 310, ran aground at Pico Island
in the Azores, where about 213 of those aboard died. Across the three ships, close to 1,000
Acadians died in a single week."

Verified independently against Wikipedia, Duke William (ship):
- ✅ **Duke William** — sank ~13 Dec 1758, ~20 leagues (97 km) off the coast of France; over 360
  aboard. North Atlantic. Confirmed. (Draft's "around 13 December," "over 360," "97 km (about 20
  leagues)" all match.)
- ✅ **Violet** — sank night of 10–11 Dec (i.e. before the Duke William, so "a day earlier" relative
  to ~13 Dec is fair), >280 aboard, lost with all. North Atlantic. Confirmed.
- ✅ **Ruby** — ~310 Acadians; "ran aground in a storm on Pico Island in the Azores, which caused
  the death of 213 of the Acadians on board." Draft's "around 310 … about 213 … died" matches
  exactly. The Azores grounding (vs. the open-ocean foundering of the other two) is correctly
  distinguished.
- ✅ **Aggregate ~1,000** — NOT overstated. The standard figure is "almost 1,000 died when the
  transport ships Duke William, Violet, and Ruby sank." Draft's "close to 1,000 … in a single
  week" is faithful and conservative. (Note: the wider ~2,000 figure on Acadian Remembrance Day
  covers ALL Île Saint-Jean deportees' deaths from hunger/disease/drowning, not just these three
  ships — the draft correctly does not conflate the two.)
- ✅ **Noël Doiron** — wife Marie, five children with spouses, over thirty grandchildren, ~120
  family members total, all lost on Duke William. Confirmed verbatim.

⚠️ Minor: the Violet's exact loss count is a range in sources (280–400); the draft's "more than
280" sits at the low/conservative end and is safe. No change needed.
⚠️ Minor: "a day earlier" — the Violet sank the night of 10–11 Dec, the Duke William ~4 p.m. on
13 Dec, so strictly ~2 days earlier. "A day earlier" is a slight loosening but within casual
narrative tolerance; flag only, not a correction.
