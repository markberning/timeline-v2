## BLOCKERS

**B1 · "three weeks before Freeman's Farm" — arithmetic error; appears three times**

> (bio, line 53): "He took over the Northern Department on August 19, 1777, three weeks
> before Freeman's Farm…"
>
> (image caption #11, line 124): "…who took command three weeks before Freeman's Farm."
>
> (fact ledger, line 314): "Northern Dept. Aug 19, 1777 (three weeks before Freeman's
> Farm)…"

**The math:** August 19 to September 19 is exactly 31 days — four weeks and three
days. Three weeks would be August 19 to September 9. This is not debatable.

**Source of the error:** The fact pack itself (§3 bio, line 32) carries the same
wrong phrase — "Took the Northern Department from Philip Schuyler on Aug 19, 1777,
three weeks before Freeman's Farm." The draft faithfully copied it. The PACK IS
WRONG; the draft inherited it. Both need correction.

**Fix:** Change to "a month before Freeman's Farm" or "four weeks before Freeman's
Farm" in all three locations (bio, caption #11, fact ledger). Recommend also flagging
this as a pack error to be corrected in `rev-saratoga-factpack.md` §3.

**B2 · "45 miles short of Albany" for Kingston — understated by ~7–10 statute miles;
appears in three locations**

> (locator 2 inset note, line 197): "…burned Kingston, still ~45 miles short of Albany."
>
> (S1 MEANWHILE, line 231): "…it would burn its way to a town still 45 miles short of
> Albany before turning back."
>
> (S2 prose, line 248): "sent raiders far enough upriver to burn Kingston, still 45 miles
> short of Albany."

**The fact:** Kingston, NY to Albany, NY is approximately 52–55 statute miles by
straight line / river distance; Wikipedia's Kingston, NY article gives "59 miles south
of Albany" (highway). The ~45 figure appears to derive from nautical miles on the
Hudson (~45 nm ≈ 52 statute miles). In plain English without unit qualification, "45
miles" understates the gap significantly; the two cities are commonly cited at roughly
50–55 miles apart.

**Source check:** The fact pack (§4 and §6) says only "~45 miles short of Albany"
without specifying nautical vs. statute — the pack carries the same number. Both draft
and pack inherit what is likely a nautical-miles figure stated as if statute. Wikipedia
web-verified: ~52 statute miles straight line, ~59 by road.

**Fix:** Change to "about 50 miles short of Albany" in all three locations (safer in
any unit system, still conveys "nowhere near"). If nautical miles are intended, add
"by river." Also note the pack error for correction.

## SHOULD-FIX

**SF1 · Ticonderoga "four days" vs. pack's "July 5–6" — inconsistency that could
mislead**

> (S1 prose, line 205): "Fort Ticonderoga, the most famous fortress in America, had
> fallen to Burgoyne in four days, almost without a shot."
>
> (fact ledger, line 324): "Ticonderoga fell July 5–6 in four days, almost without a shot"

The fact pack (§2) says "Ticonderoga taken without a fight July 5-6, 1777." July 5–6
is two days. The Saratoga draft's fact ledger writes "July 5–6 in four days" —
internally contradictory. The gated Ticonderoga page resolves this: the full episode
ran July 2–8, with the fort's guns-on-Defiance endgame on July 5 and evacuation
night July 5–6. The gated dossier says "the fort itself fell in four days" counting
from July 2 to July 5. So "four days" is defensible as inherited cross-page framing —
but the fact ledger's "July 5–6 in four days" is a clear self-contradiction that
could confuse the reviser.

**Fix:** Correct the fact ledger entry to: "Ticonderoga fell July 2–6 (the fort in
four days from the army's approach; evacuation night July 5–6), almost without a shot
· HIGH (§2; framing consistent with the gated ticonderoga-1777 page)." The prose
sentence "had fallen to Burgoyne in four days" is acceptable as-is per the gated
Ticonderoga precedent.

**SF2 · Arnold's commander portrait caption drops the "Valcour Island" name from the
fact pack; "Lake Champlain fleet" is vaguer but acceptable — flag for the reviser**

> (commander bio, line 54): "…builder of the Lake Champlain fleet that bought 1776's
> survival."

The fact pack (§3 bio, line 33) says "builder of the Valcour Island fleet that bought
1776's survival." The draft substitutes "Lake Champlain fleet" — not wrong (the fleet
operated on Lake Champlain and the action at Valcour Island was the decisive engagement
of that campaign), but it drops the specific name the pack prescribed. "Valcour Island
fleet" is more precise and historically standard.

**Fix:** Change "Lake Champlain fleet" to "Valcour Island fleet" to match the pack's
exact phrasing. (One word change; no source dispute.)

**SF3 · Gates's Gates-to-Rush quote: draft has a minor wording divergence from the
pack's verified form**

> (Kościuszko commander bio, line 56): "Gates later told Benjamin Rush that the
> campaign's 'great tacticians' were 'hills and forests, which a young Polish engineer
> was skillful enough to select.'"
>
> (image caption #12, line 129): "Gates said the campaign's great tacticians were
> 'hills and forests, which a young Polish engineer was skillful enough to select.'"

The fact pack (§8.6) gives the verified form as: "The great tacticians of the campaign
were hills and forests, which a young Polish engineer was skillful enough to select for
my encampment." The draft omits "for my encampment" and changes the opening "The great
tacticians" to "the campaign's 'great tacticians'" (splitting the phrase). The pack
explicitly tags this quote as MED with the instruction "Frame as 'Gates later told
Benjamin Rush.'" The bio correctly frames it, but the trimmed quote arguably
misrepresents the structure of the original.

**Fix:** Keep the "Gates later told Benjamin Rush" framing but restore the closing
phrase, or treat as a close paraphrase without quotation marks on the full sentence.
The caption (#12) attributes it without the Rush frame, which the pack requires;
add "as Gates told Benjamin Rush" or close the quote before "hills and forests."

**N3 · "sent raiders far enough upriver to burn Kingston" — passive attribution
appropriate, but Kingston's Oct 16 burn date is suppressed throughout the prose**

The fact pack notes Kingston was "burned Oct 16" (HIGH). The draft's fact ledger
acknowledges this (line 354: "Kingston's Oct 16 burn-date moved to dossier-level
compression"). The draft chose to omit the date entirely from prose — this is
a defensible compression decision. The date does appear in the locator inset note
("Oct 3–16"). No factual error, but the reviser may want to verify that the locator
inset's date range ("Oct 3–16") accurately reflects the operation's span (it does:
Clinton sailed Oct 3, Kingston burned Oct 16).

**N4 · Frederika's portrait caption year arithmetic: "fifteen years before the
campaign, when she was sixteen" — verify birth year**

> (image caption #8, line 111): "…painted by Johann Tischbein in 1762, fifteen years
> before the campaign, when she was sixteen."

The fact pack (§9, line 147) says "she's 16 in it." Frederika von Riedesel was born
March 11, 1746. In 1762 she would have been 15 (before March 11) or 16 (after).
If the portrait was done after her March birthday, "sixteen" is correct; if before,
she was 15. The pack asserts 16, so the draft is pack-compliant. Flagging only because
the pack itself doesn't document the birth year — if precision is needed, 1746 birth
is verifiable. No change required against the pack.
