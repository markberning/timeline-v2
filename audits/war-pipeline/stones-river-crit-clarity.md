# Stones River — NEWCOMER / CLARITY critic gate

Step 4 of the war content pipeline. Read cold as a sharp 15-year-old with ZERO prior
Civil War knowledge. Every term inline-defined on first use, scene set before new
people/places/concepts, every named person on a side tagged on first mention per section.

Verdict legend: **CLEAR** / **NEEDS-GLOSS** (define it) / **LOST** (a first-timer is
genuinely lost or overwhelmed here).

# MUST-FIX (genuine comprehension breakers)

1. **The unit hierarchy is never laid out — brigade / division / corps / wing.**
   These four nesting units carry the entire battle narrative, and none of them is
   ever defined. "Corps" in particular appears with zero gloss (§the-dawn-blow,
   Polk). A newcomer cannot tell which is bigger or how they fit together, so the
   maneuvers ("a division spearheaded … a corps threw four attacks … all three
   brigade commanders killed … the whole right wing folded") become an
   undifferentiated blur of unfamiliar nouns. **Fix:** one short orienting gloss at
   first use in §the-armies-face-off (where "brigades" first appears) — e.g. armies
   break into corps, which split into divisions, which split into brigades; a wing
   is the right/center/left third of the army's line. This single fix retires the
   bulk of the jargon load.

2. **The rank ladder is never explained.** Major General / Lieutenant General /
   Brigadier General / Colonel / Captain are used to signal seniority (and the prose
   relies on it), but a newcomer cannot order them and will misread who outranks whom.
   **Fix:** one brief gloss of the pecking order on first use (it can ride along with
   the unit-hierarchy gloss, since rank maps onto the unit a man commands).

# SHOULD-FIX (real friction, not fatal)

3. **"Pike" / "turnpike" never glossed** (Nashville Pike, Wilkinson Turnpike, Lebanon
   Pike) — gloss "pike (a turnpike road)" once at first use. Recurs ~6 times.
4. **"Supply trains"** reads as railroad trains to a modern teen — gloss as "wagon
   convoys carrying food and ammunition" (§the-new-years-pause).
5. **Name-collision pairs** that a cold reader will mix up: **McCown (South) /
   McCook (North)** in the same paragraph; **George H. Thomas / Thomas L. Crittenden**
   (two "Thomas"es) in one sentence. Lean harder on the side-tags or add a
   differentiator.
6. **"Effectives"** (§the-cost) → "men still fit to fight."
7. **"Rout"** (§the-dawn-blow) → one-clause gloss; it's load-bearing.
8. **One orienting clause up top** naming the war / era / sides before the prose dives
   in ("In the second winter of America's Civil War…"). Para 2 self-rescues, but the
   first paragraph currently assumes the frame.
9. **Europe "recognizing the Confederacy"** (§the-cost) — add a half-sentence of stakes
   or cut; dropped with no setup.
10. **Mendenhall gun-count hedging** ("around fifty … near 45 … another dozen") reads
    like sourcing notes in the prose and dulls the moment — simplify to "around fifty
    cannon, wheel to wheel"; keep the breakdown in the ledger.
11. **"Center and left wing"** assumes the reader already pictures the army as a
    three-part line — state that mental model once (folds into MUST-FIX #1's "wing").
12. Minor undefined roles: **"chief of staff"** (Garesché). Optional gloss.
