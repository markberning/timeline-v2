## 1. SLAVERY-NAMED CHECK (first, ship-blocking) — **PASS / YES**

Slavery is named as the cause in the section's **own prose** (not just the VERIFY ledger),
in three separate places, plainly and without hedging:

- **the-siege ¶1:** "The Confederacy had seceded and built its armies for one overriding reason,
  to protect and extend the **enslavement of four million Black people**" … "the rail-and-supply
  heart of that **slaveholding** economy" … "a doorway into the **slave South** itself."
  This is the strongest possible compliance: it names slavery, quantifies the enslaved, and
  states the Confederate war aim in the Confederacy's own terms.
- **the-charge ¶6 (the-meaning, "the road south"):** "a war whose purpose, by late 1863, was the
  **destruction of slavery itself**."
- **the-meaning:** "the campaign that **broke slavery** across the Deep South by sheer force,
  **freeing the enslaved** as the army passed" … "the heart of the **slaveholding South**."

The hook ("Chattanooga was the rail gateway to the Deep South — Atlanta and the
cotton-and-slavery heartland") is delivered in-prose and tied causally to slavery, not left as
scenery. **No automatic MUST-FIX.** The cause is named, named early, named in the section's
own voice, and named as the Confederacy's reason for fighting.

Web check: characterizing the Union war's purpose "by late 1863" as including the destruction
of slavery is consistent with mainstream consensus — the Emancipation Proclamation (Jan 1863)
made freedom a Union war aim and every federal advance thereafter expanded freedom. The draft's
framing does **not** overstate. ([Emancipation Proclamation — National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/emancipation-proclamation))

## 2. FINDINGS — distortion hunt

### Slavery soft-pedaling — **FAIR**
No soft-pedaling. The draft does the opposite of the recurring failure mode: it names slavery
as *the* cause, anchors the battle's strategic meaning to it, and closes by tying the victory to
emancipation. The "gateway" geography is never allowed to float free of why the gateway mattered.

### False both-sides balance — **FAIR**
The dual-POV structure (Union plan / Confederate defense, Washington / Richmond) is *operational*
balance, not *moral* balance. The Confederacy's cause is stated as the defense of slavery; the
Union's is stated as its destruction. There is no "two noble sides" equivalence. The
"Meanwhile — Washington and Richmond" close explicitly frames Richmond's loss as "the gateway to
the heart of the slaveholding South now stood open" — moral framing intact.

### Confederate romanticization — **FAIR (one item to WATCH, not fix)**
- Cleburne is given genuine admiration ("one of the finest division commanders," "the best in the
  western Confederate army," "the man who couldn't be moved," "the only good news"). This is
  **tactical** praise of a soldier's competence, which is legitimate military history, and it is
  *not* moral praise of his cause — the draft never lets Cleburne's skill stand in for Confederate
  virtue, and the surrounding prose keeps the cause (slavery) explicit. This reads as **FAIR**, not
  romanticization. WATCH only: the phrase "It was the only good news the Confederates carried out
  of Chattanooga" is sympathetic in register; it is earned by the operational facts and is balanced
  by the unambiguous "Everything else was wreckage… a state lost, and the door to Georgia swinging
  open." No change required.
- "impregnable" / "the ridge they had been told was impregnable" is in quotes / reported as the
  defenders' belief, not asserted by the narrator. FAIR.
- Breckinridge "watching his line evaporate under him" — neutral, not pitying. FAIR.

### Euphemism — **FAIR**
No euphemism for slavery ("enslavement," "the enslaved," "slaveholding," "slave South" — direct
nouns throughout; no "servants," "the peculiar institution" as a dodge, no "states' rights"
substitution). No euphemism for combat death either — "shot to pieces," "shredded against the
slope," "shot on the slope," "an army ground down." Human cost is named plainly.

### Presentism — **FAIR**
No anachronistic moral lecturing. The slavery framing is historical, not imposed-from-2026 —
it states the Confederacy's documented war aim and the Union's documented late-war aim, both
period-grounded. "four million Black people" is a factual census-scale figure, not a presentist
gloss.

### Unfair sympathy — **FAIR**
Sympathy is distributed by *experience under fire*, not by *side*. The Union soldiers pinned at
the base ("Staying put meant being shot to pieces") and the Confederate line dissolving are both
rendered as men in a terrible spot. No side is granted victim status its cause doesn't warrant.

### Human reality — **FAIR (neither sanitized nor gratuitous)**
The casualty arithmetic is used analytically (the 4,146 captured = "the signature of an army that
broke and ran"), not for gore. Death is named but not lingered on. The enslaved appear as people
freed by the campaign, not as abstractions or scenery. Calibrated correctly.

### Quote-handling note (framing-adjacent, not a framing defect)
Several quotes carry [VERIFY] flags with indirect-render fallbacks. That is correct fact-pipeline
hygiene and is **out of scope for this gate** — flagging only that none of the quotes, as drafted,
introduce a *framing* distortion (no quote romanticizes the cause or soft-pedals slavery). The
"death-knell of the Confederacy" line, if it survives the fact gate, is a Confederate's
*self*-assessment of defeat — it does not tilt sympathy.

## 3. VERDICT

**Overall: FAIR.** This draft is, if anything, a model of the framing the gate exists to enforce —
it names slavery as the cause in its own prose three times, ties the battle's strategic stakes
directly to the slave economy, refuses moral both-sides-ism while keeping operational dual-POV,
and keeps human cost honest. Tactical admiration for Cleburne is the only thing in the
romanticization-adjacent zone, and it stays on the right side of the line (skill, not cause).
