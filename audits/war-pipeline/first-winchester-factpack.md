## 3. WHAT CAME BEFORE — STRATEGIC CONTEXT

### The Valley as strategic terrain
- The Shenandoah Valley ran ~150 miles southwest–northeast through western Virginia, opening at its northern end toward the Potomac River and beyond it, Washington, D.C. — an invasion corridor pointing like a rifle barrel at the Union capital. [NPS; encyclopediavirginia.org]
- The Valley was also an agricultural cornucopia, producing approximately **19% of Virginia's wheat crop** plus substantial livestock needed to provision Confederate armies and Richmond. [encyclopediavirginia.org; warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- The Valley Turnpike ran north–south through its length, passing through Winchester — the strategic junction at the Valley's northern end. [NPS; Shenandoah at War]

### Jackson's mission
- Confederate Gen. Joseph E. Johnston (commanding the main Confederate army facing McClellan) directed Jackson to operate in the Valley to **tie down Union forces and prevent them from reinforcing Maj. Gen. George B. McClellan's Peninsula Campaign drive on Richmond**. [NPS; encyclopediavirginia.org; warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- Jackson's specific instruction was to keep Banks's force in the Valley and away from McDowell's corps at Fredericksburg (which Lincoln planned to send south to join McClellan). [NPS; encyclopediavirginia.org]
- Jackson started the campaign with approximately **4,000–6,000 troops**, growing to **~17,000 by late May** through Ewell's division and other reinforcements. [NPS; encyclopediavirginia.org; Wikipedia Valley Campaign article]

### Jackson's "foot cavalry"
- Jackson's troops earned the nickname **"foot cavalry"** (or "Jackson's foot cavalry") through extraordinary forced marches. The entire Valley Campaign covered **646 miles in 48 days** (~13.5 miles/day average). [Wikipedia Valley Campaign; americanhistorycentral.com]
- During the push preceding Winchester, the army marched **140 miles in one week** (~20 miles/day). [warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- The Stonewall Brigade once marched **42 miles in a single day** through rain and mud without food (June 1, near the campaign's end). [warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- Jackson enforced strict march discipline: no straggling, hard starts, rare breaks, columns moved fast or suffered his wrath. [NPS; general Civil War scholarship — [VERIFY specific anecdote source]]

### Earlier campaign moves that drove Banks toward Winchester
1. **March 23, 1862 — First Battle of Kernstown:** Jackson attacked what he thought was a small Union rear guard; it was actually a full division. Tactical Union victory, but Jackson's aggression alarmed Lincoln into reinforcing Valley forces with troops intended for the Peninsula — a strategic Confederate win. [Wikipedia Valley Campaign; NPS]
2. **May 8, 1862 — Battle of McDowell:** Jackson defeated Union Brig. Gen. Robert H. Milroy's ~6,000-man force with ~10,000 Confederates, preventing Milroy from uniting with Maj. Gen. John C. Frémont's army. [Wikipedia Valley Campaign; americanhistorycentral.com]
3. **May 23, 1862 — Battle of Front Royal:** Jackson and Ewell launched a coordinated assault on the small Union garrison at Front Royal, capturing it and inflicting **773 Union casualties (mostly captured) against only 36 Confederate losses**. Front Royal sat on Banks's flank between Strasburg and Union reinforcements at Fredericksburg, threatening his supply line (the Manassas Gap Railroad). This maneuver placed Jackson on Banks's left flank and cut off his line of retreat southward. [Wikipedia Valley Campaign; Shenandoah at War — Front Royal & Winchester article]
4. **May 24, 1862 — Retreat from Strasburg / running fight:** Banks, learning his Front Royal garrison had been captured, ordered retreat north from Strasburg along the Valley Turnpike. Confederate cavalry and infantry harassed his columns throughout the day at **Middletown** and **Stephens City (Newtown)**. Jackson captured vast Union stores during this pursuit — it is from this phase that the "Commissary Banks" nickname began to crystallize. Banks reached Winchester by evening of May 24. [Wikipedia; Shenandoah at War; americanhistorycentral.com]

## 4. THE BATTLE — MAY 25, 1862

### Banks's defensive position
- Banks deployed approximately 5,000–6,500 exhausted troops in a line stretching roughly **2.5 miles** south and southwest of Winchester. [ABT; NPS]
- **Right flank (west):** Col. George Gordon's 3rd Brigade anchored on **Bowers Hill**, a rise **southwest of Winchester**, with two artillery sections and approximately seven infantry regiments. Gordon's position covered the Valley Turnpike approach. [NPS; Shenandoah at War; ABT]
- **Left flank (east/southeast):** Col. Dudley Donnelly's brigade near **Camp Hill**, on the southeast edge of town, covering the **Front Royal Pike**. [NPS; americanhistorycentral.com]
- Banks had 16 guns. [Wikipedia]
- Banks reportedly knew "defeat was certain" but fought to allow his **550 wagons** to escape toward Williamsport. [encyclopediavirginia.org]

### Jackson's dawn attack
- Jackson attacked at **dawn on May 25** through dense morning fog. [NPS; Shenandoah at War]
- He split his command: **Ewell's division** attacked on the Union left (east) along the **Front Royal Pike**; Jackson himself advanced on the Valley Pike in the center and right. [Wikipedia; ABT; NPS]
- Initial Confederate assaults on the Union left (near Camp Hill) achieved limited results. [NPS; americanhistorycentral.com]
- Confederate artillery exchanged fire with Union guns on Bowers Hill along the Valley Pike — described as a close-range duel. [Wikipedia]

### Taylor's Louisiana Brigade flanking attack — the decisive blow
- Jackson asked Brig. Gen. Charles S. Winder how to proceed; Winder suggested attacking the Union right flank (Bowers Hill). Jackson replied: **"I will send you up Taylor."** [Shenandoah Civil War History blog — [VERIFY exact wording; likely from Henry Kyd Douglas or Taylor memoir]]
- Brig. Gen. Richard Taylor — son of President Zachary Taylor, commanding the Louisiana Brigade — deployed his ~2,000-man brigade under cover of morning fog around **7:00–7:30 a.m.**, moving to the west to overlap and envelop the Union right flank on Bowers Hill. [ABT; NPS; Shenandoah Civil War History blog]
- As Taylor's men marched into position, Federal artillery fired on them. Some men ducked or sought cover. [ABT]
- At approximately **7:30 a.m.**, Taylor rode in front of his brigade and led them forward **"in a steady walk"** — disciplined, no premature firing — up the slopes of Bowers Hill. [ABT; Shenandoah Civil War History blog]
- Confederate observer **Henry Kyd Douglas** described Taylor's advance: *"a more beautiful charge… a line of glistening bayonets bright in that morning sun, its formation straight and compact."* [Shenandoah Civil War History blog — sourced to Douglas's "I Rode with Stonewall" — [VERIFY exact page]]
- Taylor's men drove through **Federal musket fire and canister**, closed the gaps, and broke the 27th Indiana and 29th Pennsylvania regiments on the Union right; the 2nd Massachusetts was forced to withdraw. [Shenandoah Civil War History blog]
- Gordon's brigade collapsed; the entire Union line disintegrated within minutes of Taylor's breakthrough. [americanhistorycentral.com; ABT]
- A Confederate sergeant called Taylor's charge: *"the grandest I saw during the war: … every man was in his proper place."* [warfarehistorynetwork.com; Shenandoah at War — [VERIFY: attributed to an unnamed sergeant; source is likely Shenandoah Valley battlefield literature]]

### Jackson's reaction
- On seeing the Union line break, Jackson reportedly exclaimed to his staff: **"Order forward the whole line, the battle's won,"** then called to the advancing troops: **"Very Good! Now let's holler!"** [Shenandoah Civil War History blog — [VERIFY source; likely Douglas memoir]]
- Jackson later approached Taylor and **"gratefully shook his hand in a silent gesture."** [Shenandoah Civil War History blog — [VERIFY]]

### The rout through Winchester
- With their line broken, Banks's troops retreated **north through the streets of Winchester** in disorder. [Wikipedia; ABT; NPS]
- Winchester was a strongly pro-Confederate town. As Union soldiers fled through the streets, **civilians reportedly shot at retreating Union soldiers** from buildings. [Shenandoah at War; this claim is common in accounts — [VERIFY: "fired upon" vs. "jeered at" — some sources say civilians lined streets cheering Confederates; the "shooting" claim is strong and should be checked against primary sources]]
- One Federal soldier, reportedly responding to Banks's attempts to rally the troops, answered: **"I am trying to get to it as fast as I can."** [NPS — [VERIFY source attribution]]
- The civilian response described in Confederate sources: Winchester's residents lined streets **"in jubilant excitement,"** with church bells ringing and residents greeting Confederate troops. [Shenandoah Civil War History blog]

### The pursuit
- Jackson ordered pursuit but Confederate coordination partially failed north of town. [encyclopediavirginia.org; Shenandoah at War]
- Pursuit halted at **Stephenson's Depot**, a few miles north of Winchester, due to exhausted infantry and insufficient cavalry to maintain pressure. [Shenandoah at War]
- Banks's retreating army was harassed but reached **Martinsburg** by early afternoon May 25. [americanhistorycentral.com]
- Jackson reportedly said **"It was all a question of legs"** in reflecting on the pursuit phase — though Lincoln used this same phrase. [CONTESTED — this phrase is attributed to Lincoln (encyclopediavirginia.org), not Jackson; [VERIFY who said it]]

## 5. AFTERMATH — "COMMISSARY BANKS"

### Banks crosses the Potomac
- Banks reached **Martinsburg** (now West Virginia, then Virginia) by **early afternoon, May 25**; the rest of his troops arrived by approximately **5:00 p.m.** [americanhistorycentral.com]
- That evening — still **May 25** — Banks crossed the Potomac River at **Williamsport, Maryland**, taking his shattered command into Maryland. [americanhistorycentral.com; Wikipedia]
- Distance from Winchester to Williamsport: approximately **35 miles** covered in one day. [encyclopediavirginia.org]
- Banks lost **nearly one-third of his force** (2,019 of ~6,500) but the army as an organized body survived the retreat — escape attributed partly to poor Confederate cavalry pursuit. [encyclopediavirginia.org; ABT]

### Captured Union stores
The supplies Banks abandoned became legendary. Documented captures:

| Item | Quantity | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ammunition rounds | ~500,000 | ABT; Shenandoah at War; NPS |
| Small arms (muskets, pistols) | 9,300+ | NPS; americanhistorycentral.com |
| Commissary stores / food (general) | 34,000 lbs | NPS; americanhistorycentral.com |
| Bacon specifically | 15,000 lbs | ABT; Shenandoah at War |
| Cattle | 100+ head | ABT; Shenandoah at War |
| Quartermaster supplies | clothing, blankets, etc. | NPS |

> **"Commissary Banks" nickname:** Confederate soldiers derisively called Banks their "Commissary" — as in, the Union general who kept them well-supplied — based on these repeated abandonments of stores throughout the retreat (May 23–25). The nickname is referenced across ABT, NPS, Wikipedia, and Shenandoah at War as standard. [Multiple sources — exact first use of the term in print [VERIFY: likely Confederate soldier letters or newspapers May–June 1862]]

### Lincoln's reaction and the Washington alarm
- Lincoln and Secretary of War Edwin Stanton were alarmed by Jackson's rapid advance toward the Potomac — fearing not just Banks's defeat but a possible Confederate strike toward Washington itself. [warfarehistorynetwork.com: "Lincoln and Stanton descended to their deepest pessimism"]
- On **May 24, 1862** (triggered by Front Royal news, confirmed after Winchester) Lincoln telegraphed McClellan: **"In consequence of General Banks's critical position I have been compelled to suspend General McDowell's movements to you."** He added: "The enemy are making a desperate push upon Harper's Ferry, and we are trying to throw Frémont's force and part of McDowell's in their rear." [Lincoln telegram, May 24, 1862 — cited in americanhistorycentral.com/shenandoah-valley-campaign; [VERIFY: exact date of telegram is May 24 (day after Front Royal, day before Winchester)]]
- Lincoln ordered a three-army pincer to trap Jackson: **Frémont** marching from Franklin toward Harrisonburg; **20,000 troops from McDowell's corps** redirected to the Valley; **Banks** repositioned. [Wikipedia Valley Campaign; americanhistorycentral.com]

### McDowell diversion — the strategic payoff
- Maj. Gen. Irvin McDowell commanded approximately **30,000–35,000 troops** at Fredericksburg, originally ordered to march overland toward Richmond to join McClellan's right flank in the Peninsula Campaign. [warfarehistorynetwork.com; americanhistorycentral.com]
- The diversion of **~20,000 of McDowell's troops** to the Valley directly denied McClellan the corps reinforcement he needed for his push on Richmond. [Wikipedia Valley Campaign; americanhistorycentral.com]
- Across the full campaign, Jackson with ~17,000 men tied down elements of **three separate Union armies totaling more than 60,000 men** (Banks, McDowell's detachment, Frémont) that would otherwise have been available against Richmond. [warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- Lincoln's pincer attempt ultimately failed — Jackson slipped out of the trap at Strasburg and escaped south after victories at Cross Keys (June 8) and Port Republic (June 9). [Wikipedia Valley Campaign; NPS]

## 6. KEY PEOPLE (SIDE-TAGGED)

### Maj. Gen. Thomas J. "Stonewall" Jackson (CS)
- Commanded the Army of the Valley / Valley District; overall Confederate commander at Winchester. [Wikipedia; ABT]
- West Point graduate (class of 1846); Mexican War veteran; former VMI professor. [Wikipedia]
- Earned "Stonewall" sobriquet at First Bull Run (July 1861) — holding his brigade like a "stone wall." [Wikipedia]
- Known for secretive, demanding command style; kept subordinates and enemies guessing. Deeply religious (Calvinist Presbyterian). [NPS; Wikipedia]
- Winchester was the high point of his Valley Campaign — his most famous operation. [ABT; multiple sources]
- Quote attributed to him about the Valley: **"If this Valley is lost, Virginia is lost."** [NPS Valley Campaign article — [VERIFY original source; likely a letter or dispatch]]

### Brig. Gen. Richard Taylor (CS)
- Commanded the Louisiana Brigade (including the "Louisiana Tigers" battalion). [ABT; Wikipedia]
- Son of President Zachary Taylor; brother-in-law of Confederate President Jefferson Davis. [Wikipedia — Richard Taylor]
- His flanking attack on Bowers Hill is widely described as **"a model brigade maneuver"** in military histories. [ABT]
- Later promoted to Lt. Gen.; commanded the Trans-Mississippi Department. [Wikipedia — Richard Taylor]
- Wrote the memoir *Destruction and Reconstruction* (1879), which covers his Valley Campaign service. [Project Gutenberg; docsouth.unc.edu]
- Taylor was known for demanding discipline in march and battle; the "steady walk" advance under fire was characteristic of his command style. [ABT; Shenandoah Civil War History blog]

### Maj. Gen. Richard S. Ewell (CS)
- Commanded a division under Jackson; attacked on the Confederate left (Union right–left, i.e., the Front Royal Pike axis). [Wikipedia; ABT]
- Ewell initially had a rocky relationship with Jackson due to Jackson's extreme secrecy about plans. [Wikipedia — Ewell]
- Provided critical mass to Jackson's command through his division reinforcement earlier in the campaign. [NPS]

### Maj. Gen. Nathaniel P. Banks (US)
- Pre-war Massachusetts politician (Speaker of the House, Governor); commissioned as a political general at war's start. [Wikipedia — Banks]
- His military inexperience and lack of aggressiveness were repeatedly exploited by Jackson throughout the Valley Campaign. [encyclopediavirginia.org; warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- Nickname "Commissary Banks" became a permanent part of his Civil War legacy. [ABT; NPS; Wikipedia]
- Despite the rout, Banks successfully extracted most of his army intact across the Potomac — a limited operational credit. [encyclopediavirginia.org]
- Later commanded Union forces at Port Hudson (1863) and in the disastrous Red River Campaign (1864). [Wikipedia — Banks]

## 7. WHAT IT MEANT

### The campaign's strategic achievement
- The First Battle of Winchester was **the climactic battle and emotional high point** of Jackson's Valley Campaign. [ABT; warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- With ~17,000 troops, Jackson (1) drove Banks's larger force entirely out of Virginia and across the Potomac; (2) so alarmed Lincoln that McDowell's corps reinforcement to McClellan was canceled; and (3) kept **three Federal armies totaling 60,000+ troops** tied down in the Valley and Washington defense for weeks. [warfarehistorynetwork.com; Wikipedia Valley Campaign]
- The diversion of McDowell's corps is considered a decisive factor in Confederate success in the Seven Days Battles (June 25–July 1, 1862), during which Gen. Robert E. Lee saved Richmond. McClellan's Peninsula Campaign collapsed without the northern-wing reinforcement Lincoln had promised. [encyclopediavirginia.org; warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- **Lincoln's reaction** — diverting troops back to the Valley — itself inadvertently further frustrated Union strategy by further dispersing forces. [americanhistorycentral.com]

### Jackson's legend
- The Valley Campaign made Jackson the **most celebrated Confederate commander** in the Confederacy and a household name in the North as well. [NPS; warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- The campaign became a **textbook case study** in use of interior lines, strategic deception, forced marching, and economy of force — studied at military academies for generations. [warfarehistorynetwork.com]
- Winchester in particular, with Taylor's picture-perfect brigade assault and Banks's rout, became the campaign's signature engagement. [ABT; Shenandoah at War]

## 9. GEOGRAPHY FOR A MAP

### Cardinal orientation
- **North = up.** The Shenandoah Valley runs **southwest–northeast**; north in the Valley context means toward the Potomac/Maryland.

### Town of Winchester
- Winchester sits at the **lower (northern) end** of the Shenandoah Valley, ~22 miles southwest of Harpers Ferry (on the Potomac). [geography; encyclopediavirginia.org]
- In 1862, the **Valley Turnpike** (now largely US Route 11) ran directly through Winchester on its north–south axis — the main artery of the Valley. [NPS; Shenandoah at War]
- The **Front Royal Pike** entered Winchester from the southeast — the secondary approach axis used by Ewell. [Wikipedia; ABT; NPS]

### Defensive terrain
- **Bowers Hill:** A ridge/rise **southwest of Winchester**, anchoring Banks's right flank (the western end of his line). The Union right, including Gordon's brigade with artillery, was positioned here. Taylor's flanking attack struck this position from the west. [ABT; NPS; Shenandoah Civil War History blog]
- **Camp Hill:** On the **southeast edge of Winchester**, anchoring Banks's left flank, covering the Front Royal Pike approach. Donnelly's brigade was here; Ewell attacked on this axis. [NPS; americanhistorycentral.com]
- Banks's line stretched **~2.5 miles** between these two anchor points, south and southwest of town. [ABT]

### Axes of advance and retreat
- **Confederate main advance:** Jackson came from the **south** along the Valley Turnpike; Ewell from the **southeast** along the Front Royal Pike.
- **Taylor's flanking maneuver:** Taylor moved from south/southwest, then swung **west** to overlap the Union right flank on Bowers Hill — described as striking the "western flank of Bowers Hill." [ABT; NPS]
- **Union retreat:** Banks's broken army fled **north** through Winchester's streets, then north-northeast along the Valley Turnpike toward **Martinsburg** (~22 miles north), then across the **Potomac at Williamsport** (another ~13 miles north of Martinsburg). [Wikipedia; americanhistorycentral.com]
- Pursuit halted at **Stephenson's Depot**, a few miles north of Winchester on the Valley Turnpike. [Shenandoah at War]

### Map elements
- Town: Winchester, Frederick County, Virginia
- Roads: Valley Turnpike (north–south spine), Front Royal Pike (southeast axis)
- Terrain: Bowers Hill (southwest of town), Camp Hill (southeast of town)
- Attack direction: Confederate from south and southeast; Taylor from southwest/west
- Retreat: Union north through town, to Martinsburg, then Potomac at Williamsport
- Pursuit halt: Stephenson's Depot (north of Winchester)
- Shenandoah River runs east of town (flows northeast); North Fork of the Shenandoah converges nearby [geography — [VERIFY exact river positions relative to the battle positions]]
