**A naval-battle note for the author:** this is a fleet action, not an infantry assault.
"Forces & Commanders" (§2) is fleets and ships; "Geography" (§4) is the bay mouth, the

in 1864.

## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name:** Battle of Mobile Bay. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Date:** **August 5, 1864** (the naval engagement). The broader operation ran **August 2–23,
  1864** — the fleet's passage and fleet fight were all on **Aug 5**; the fort sieges/surrenders
  ran out to Aug 23. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Location:** the **mouth of Mobile Bay, Alabama** — the channel between **Fort Morgan**
  (on Mobile Point, the eastern shore) and **Fort Gaines** (on Dauphin Island, the western
  shore). Coordinates ~30°14′N 88°03′W. **[WIKI]**
- **Theatre:** **Naval** — part of the Union blockade of the Gulf coast; Farragut's command was
  the **West Gulf Blockading Squadron.** **[WIKI] [NARA]**
- **Result:** **Union victory.** The Union fleet forced the bay; the Confederate squadron was
  destroyed or captured; the three guarding forts fell within weeks; the **port of Mobile was
  closed** to blockade-runners. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### Why Mobile Bay mattered — the last Gulf port (strategic spine)
- By August 1864, **Mobile was the last important port on the Gulf of Mexico east of the
  Mississippi River still in Confederate hands** — the Gulf's surviving **blockade-running
  hub.** Closing it tightened the noose around the Confederate economy. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **What "closing the port" actually means (be precise):** Aug 5 sealed the **bay mouth** and
  shut the harbor to blockade-runners. The **city of Mobile**, miles up the bay, was **not
  attacked until March–April 1865** and **fell only in the war's last days.** **[WIKI]** The
  1864 victory **corked the bottle**; it did not take the town.
- Pair this with **Charleston/Wilmington framing**: after Mobile Bay, **Wilmington, North
  Carolina (Fort Fisher, Jan 1865)** was the last major Atlantic blockade port to fall —
  Mobile Bay was the **Gulf** half of shutting the Confederacy's last doors. *(Wilmington/
  Fort Fisher is context; full facts out of scope of this pack.)*

## 2. Forces & Commanders (SIDE-TAGGED) — fleets, not regiments

> Side-tag rule: every named person tagged **(North)** = Union / **(South)** = Confederate
> on first mention per section.

### Union (North) — the attacking fleet
- **Rear Adm. David G. Farragut (North)** — fleet commander, flying his flag in **USS Hartford**;
  commander of the West Gulf Blockading Squadron. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Maj. Gen. Gordon Granger (North)** — commanded the **~1,500–2,000 army troops** that landed
  to invest the forts from the land side. **[WIKI]**
- **Capt. Percival Drayton (North)** — Farragut's flag captain, commanding **Hartford**; the
  officer Farragut addressed in the famous order ("Four bells, Captain Drayton"). **[WIKI] [USNI]**
- **Capt. James Alden (North)** — commanded **USS Brooklyn**, the lead wooden ship; **stopped/
  signaled for instructions** when Tecumseh sank, the hesitation that prompted Farragut to
  push Hartford into the lead. **[WIKI]**
- **Cdr. Tunis A. M. Craven (North)** — commanded the monitor **USS Tecumseh** (lead monitor);
  **went down with his ship** (see §6). **[WIKI-TEC]**
- **Lt. Cdr. James E. Jouett (North)** — commanded **USS Metacomet**, the gunboat lashed to
  Hartford; the officer in the "Go ahead, Jouett, full speed" version of the order. **[USNI]**

**Union fleet — composition (the lashed pairs + monitors):** **[WIKI]**
- **Four monitors (ironclads), in a single column to STARBOARD (the Fort Morgan / east side),
  leading:** **USS Tecumseh, USS Manhattan, USS Winnebago, USS Chickasaw** (in that order).
- **The wooden steam sloops/frigates, each lashed on its disengaged (port) side to a smaller
  gunboat** so the big ship shielded the small one and either could tow the other if disabled:
  - **Brooklyn — Octorara** *(leading the wooden column)*
  - **Hartford — Metacomet** *(Farragut's flagship pair)*
  - **Richmond — Port Royal**
  - **Lackawanna — Seminole**
  - **Monongahela — Kennebec**
  - **Ossipee — Itasca**
  - **Oneida — Galena**
  *(Author may name a representative few; the full list is here for map/diagram accuracy.)*
- **Land force:** Granger's troops, landed to besiege Forts Gaines and Morgan. **[WIKI]**

### Confederate (South) — the defending squadron + the forts
- **Adm. Franklin Buchanan (South)** — Confederate naval commander, flag in the ironclad ram
  **CSS Tennessee**; **wounded (a badly broken leg)** in the fight (see §5–6). Buchanan was the
  senior Confederate naval officer — he had commanded **CSS Virginia (the *Merrimack*)** at
  Hampton Roads in 1862 *(context, [VERIFY] if used)*. **[WIKI]**
- **Cdr. James D. Johnston (South)** — flag captain commanding **CSS Tennessee**; **surrendered
  the ironclad** with Buchanan's permission after Buchanan was wounded. **[WIKI]**
- **Brig. Gen. Richard L. Page (South)** — commanded **Fort Morgan** (and the district);
  held out until **Aug 23.** *(Page was a cousin of Robert E. Lee and a former U.S./Confederate
  naval officer — context, [VERIFY] before using.)* **[WIKI]**
- **Col. Charles D. Anderson (South)** — commanded **Fort Gaines** (in Page's absence);
  **surrendered Aug 8** — controversially, against Page's wishes (see §5). **[WIKI]**
- **Lt. Col. James M. Williams (South)** — commanded **Fort Powell**; **spiked his guns, blew
  the magazine, and abandoned the fort** the night of Aug 5–6. **[WIKI]**

**Confederate squadron — composition:** **[WIKI]**
- **CSS Tennessee** — ironclad ram, **6 guns**, Buchanan's flagship — the strongest ironclad
  afloat in Confederate service, but **slow** (weak engines).
- **Three wooden gunboats:** **CSS Selma (4 guns)**, **CSS Morgan (6 guns)**, **CSS Gaines (6 guns).**

### Strengths (give as approximations)
- **Union (North):** **~7,000** men across the fleet (4 monitors + more than a dozen wooden
  ships), plus Granger's land troops. **[ABT]**
- **Confederate (South):** **~5,500** total, **including ~1,500 army troops** in the three forts;
  the floating force was just **Tennessee + 3 small gunboats** against the whole Union fleet. **[ABT]**
  *(The mismatch afloat is the point — Buchanan fought one ironclad against a fleet.)*

## 3. Casualties & losses (both sides)

**Union (North) — fleet:**
- **~150 killed · ~170 wounded** in the naval action (total ≈ 320). **[WIKI]** ABT gives a
  combined **322 killed/wounded/missing** for the fleet — the same order of magnitude. **[ABT]**
- **The single bloodiest stroke was the loss of USS Tecumseh:** of her crew (complement
  variously given as ~100–114), **about 90+ men went down with her** — roughly **94 lost, ~21
  saved.** **[WIKI-TEC]** *(That one mine kill accounts for the bulk of the Union dead — flag
  for the author: most of the "150 killed" died in 25 seconds aboard one monitor. [VERIFY] the
  exact crew complement — sources give 100 vs 114; use "of her ~100-plus crew, only 21 were
  saved.")*
- **Army (land siege of the forts):** only **~1 killed, ~7 wounded** — the forts gave up
  rather than be stormed. **[WIKI]**

**Confederate (South):**
- **Squadron (the fleet fight):** very light — **~12 killed, ~19 wounded.** **[WIKI]**
- **Total captured ≈ 1,500–1,587** — overwhelmingly the **fort garrisons that surrendered**
  (Gaines, Powell, Morgan), plus Tennessee's crew. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

**Author guidance:** the casualty story here is **lopsided and counter-intuitive**: the fleet
action itself was cheap in blood on both sides *except* for the Tecumseh, whose single mine
strike killed more Union sailors than everything else combined. The big Confederate "loss"
number is **prisoners** (surrendered forts), not battle dead. State Union loss ≈ 320 (most of
it the Tecumseh), Confederate naval dead ~12, and ~1,500 Confederates captured with the forts.

**Decorations (documented, striking):** **115 men received the Medal of Honor** for Mobile
Bay — making it one of the most-decorated single battles in U.S. history; **four of them were
African American sailors** (see §8). **[WIKI] [MIL]** *([VERIFY] the "second-most decorated
battle" superlative before printing it as a ranked claim — the 115 figure is solid; the ranking

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (north = up; the fleet enters from the **south**, the Gulf, steaming **north**
into the bay):

- **The bay mouth** is a channel between two shores. **Fort Morgan sits on Mobile Point, the
  EAST side** (right hand as you steam in); **Fort Gaines sits on Dauphin Island, the WEST side**
  (left hand). **[WIKI]** Fort Morgan is the strong one (**46 guns**); Fort Gaines is weaker
  (**26 guns**). **[WIKI]**
- **Fort Powell** sat to the **NW**, covering **Grant's Pass** (a back channel west of Dauphin
  Island), not the main ship channel. **[WIKI]** It was abandoned the night after the battle.
- **The deep ship channel hugs the EAST shore — right under Fort Morgan's guns.** Ships had to
  pass **close to Fort Morgan** because that was where the water was deep and unmined. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **The minefield ("torpedoes"):** the Confederates planted **~67 "torpedoes" (naval mines —
  floating/anchored kegs of powder) across the channel**, leaving a **~500-yard gap on the
  EASTERN side** (the Fort Morgan side) so their own blockade-runners could slip in and out.
  **The mine field was marked by buoys.** **[WIKI]** **The Union ships had to thread the gap
  between the buoy line (west) and Fort Morgan's guns (east)** — a narrow lane with mines on one
  hand and the fort on the other. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **The fatal geometry:** the monitor **Tecumseh, leading on the starboard (Fort Morgan) side,
  veered WEST of the buoy — into the mined water — to get at CSS Tennessee**, and struck a mine.
  **[WIKI-TEC]** This is the spot that made the battle: the gap was deliberately tight, and the
  lead ship crossed the wrong side of the buoy line.
- **Behind/inside the forts:** the Confederate squadron (Tennessee + 3 gunboats) waited **inside
  the bay, north of the mine line**, to fight the fleet as it emerged into open water. **[WIKI]**
- **Map orientation summary:** Gulf at bottom (south); fleet steams **north** into the channel.
  **Fort Morgan on the EAST (right) bank**, **Fort Gaines on the WEST (left, Dauphin Island)**,
  **Fort Powell off to the NW** (Grant's Pass). **Mine field stretched across the channel with a
  buoyed gap on the EAST (Morgan) side**; the safe lane runs **between the buoy line and Fort
  Morgan.** Union arrow: monitors leading to starboard (east), wooden pairs to port (west),
  all **northbound**; the Confederate squadron waits **north of the mines, inside the bay.**

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup:** Farragut **(North)** meant to run his fleet **past** Forts Morgan and Gaines and the
mine field, into the bay, destroy Buchanan's **(South)** squadron, and seal the port. The forts
would then be reduced from inside the bay and by Granger's land troops. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### Dawn, Aug 5 — the run past the forts
- **Just before dawn**, Farragut formed his fleet: **four monitors in a column to STARBOARD
  (the Fort Morgan side)**, the **wooden ships lashed in pairs** in a second column to port. The
  big wooden ships took the fort's fire on their starboard side; the lashed gunboats sheltered to
  port. **[WIKI]**
- **The fleet stood in around 6–7 a.m.**, the monitors leading, **Brooklyn (Capt. Alden) at the
  head of the wooden column**, **Hartford (Farragut) second.** Fort Morgan opened fire. **[WIKI]**

### ~7:30 a.m. — USS Tecumseh sinks (the crisis)
- The lead monitor **USS Tecumseh (Cdr. Craven, North)**, trying to close on **CSS Tennessee**,
  **steered WEST of the buoy into the mined water** and **struck a torpedo.** She **sank in
  about 25–30 seconds**, bow first — **most of her crew went down with her** (~90+ lost; see §6).
  **[WIKI-TEC] [WIKI]**
- **The advance stalls:** with the lead monitor gone and mines clearly in the channel, **Brooklyn
  stopped (and began to back), signaling Farragut for instructions** — fouling the whole column
  under Fort Morgan's guns. **This is the moment of decision.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### "Damn the torpedoes" — Farragut takes the lead
- Farragut **ordered Hartford forward, swinging around the stalled Brooklyn into the lead**, and
  drove **straight through the mined water.** **[WIKI] [ABT]** The mines the fleet passed over
  **failed to detonate** (long-submerged, fouled, or defective). **[WIKI]** *(The famous order
  itself is a [VERIFY] — see §7. The ACTION — Farragut pushing Hartford past Brooklyn through the
  mines — is solid; the verbatim words are the part in doubt.)*
- **The whole fleet followed Hartford through.** The fleet was now **inside the bay, north of the
  mines.** **[WIKI]**

### The fleet fight — and CSS Tennessee alone
- Inside the bay, the Union ships engaged Buchanan's squadron. **CSS Selma, Morgan, and Gaines
  (South)** were quickly overmatched — **Gaines was beached/destroyed, Selma was captured,
  Morgan escaped** up toward the city. **[WIKI]** *(Gunboat fates: [VERIFY] exact Gaines/Selma/
  Morgan dispositions before printing specifics.)*
- **Then the lone stand:** **CSS Tennessee (Buchanan, South) turned and steamed back into the
  whole Union fleet — single-handed.** **[ABT]** Union ships **rammed her repeatedly** (the big
  sloops trying to run her down) and pounded her at **point-blank, arm's-length range**; the
  monitors **Manhattan and Chickasaw** closed and battered her. **[WIKI]**
- **Tennessee was crippled:** **smokestack shot away, steering chains parted so she could not
  steer, almost motionless.** **Buchanan suffered a badly broken leg.** With the ship helpless,
  **Cdr. Johnston (South) asked the wounded Buchanan's permission and surrendered** — raising the
  white flag after roughly an hour of being swarmed. **[WIKI]** *(ABT frames the whole Tennessee
  fight as ~3 hours; [WIKI] details the final ~hour of the swarming. Author: "fought the entire
  fleet alone until battered into surrender" is safe.)*
- **By ~10 a.m. the bay was won.** **[WIKI]**

### The forts fall (over the following weeks — NOT the city)
- **Fort Powell (South, Lt. Col. Williams):** with the fleet now inside the bay behind it,
  Williams **spiked his guns, blew the magazine, and abandoned it the night of Aug 5–6.** **[WIKI]**
- **Fort Gaines (South, Col. Anderson):** **surrendered Aug 8** — Anderson capitulated (over
  Page's objections from Fort Morgan), a surrender the Confederate command considered premature.
  **[WIKI]** *([VERIFY] the Page-vs-Anderson surrender dispute detail before dramatizing it.)*
- **Fort Morgan (South, Brig. Gen. Page):** held out under bombardment by fleet and Granger's land
  guns; **surrendered Aug 23, 1864**, white flag raised that morning. **[WIKI]**
- **The city of Mobile held until April 1865** — see §1, §9. **[WIKI]**

## 6. Human cost — named people / vivid documented detail

- **Cdr. Tunis A. M. Craven (North), USS Tecumseh** — went down with his monitor.
  - **The exchange at the escape scuttle** (the battle's most-told human moment): as Tecumseh
    flooded, **Craven and the pilot, John Collins, reached the narrow escape ladder/scuttle at the
    same instant**, with only time for one to get out. **Craven stepped back — "After you,
    pilot" — and let Collins climb out first. Craven did not make it; the ship dropped from under
    them.** **[TEC-NET] [WIKI-TEC]**
  - **Collins's own account:** *"There was nothing after me. When I reached the topmost round of
    the ladder, the vessel seemed to drop from under me."* — attributed to pilot **John H.
    Collins. [TEC-NET]. [VERIFY]** exact wording/source before printing inside quotation marks.
  - **Treat "After you, pilot" as venerated naval lore, not a confirmed verbatim.** It is ranked
    alongside "Don't give up the ship" and "Damn the torpedoes" as a Navy touchstone — which is
    exactly why it should be attributed carefully (Collins survived to tell it; Craven did not).
    **[TEC-NET]. [VERIFY] / attribute as "Collins later said Craven told him…," not as gospel.**
  - **The toll:** **~90+ of Tecumseh's crew died in ~25 seconds** — most of the battle's Union
    dead, in one ship, in less than half a minute. **[WIKI-TEC]**
- **Adm. Franklin Buchanan (South)** — the senior Confederate sailor, fought his one ironclad
  against the fleet and was **wounded with a badly broken leg** before Tennessee surrendered.
  **[WIKI]**
- **Landsman John Lawson (North), USS Hartford** — **free-born Black sailor from Philadelphia**
  (b. 1837); on Hartford's **shell-whip / ammunition crew. Wounded in the leg and slammed against
  the ship's side by an enemy shell**, he refused to go below and stayed at his post. **Medal of
  Honor.** **[MIL] [NARA]** (citation in §8). *(One of the war's iconic images of a Black sailor
  in action — documented, not lore.)*
- **Landsman Wilson Brown (North), USS Hartford** — **born enslaved (~1841) on Botany Bay
  Plantation, Natchez, Mississippi**; also on the **shell-whip crew. Knocked unconscious into the
  hold when a shellburst killed a man on the ladder above him**, he came to, **returned to his
  post, and kept the ammunition moving though 4 of the 6 men at his station were dead or
  wounded. Medal of Honor.** **[NHHC] [MIL]** (citation in §8).
- **Cdr. James D. Johnston (South), CSS Tennessee** — the man who actually struck Tennessee's
  colors, with the wounded Buchanan's leave. **[WIKI]**

## 7. Key quotes (exact attribution + source) — HEAVY [VERIFY] ZONE

> **This battle's fame rests on three quotes that are all, to varying degrees, romanticized or
> reconstructed after the fact. Handle with the shaky-quote discipline below.**

1. **Farragut (North) — "Damn the torpedoes":**
   - **Popular form:** *"Damn the torpedoes! Full speed ahead!"* — the version on monuments and
     in textbooks. **[ABT] [WIKI]**
   - **THE CAVEAT (load-bearing, must be conveyed):** the verbatim words are **uncertain and
     contested.** The line **"did not appear in print until years after the battle"** (first
     attributed to Farragut roughly **14 years later**), and **some historians doubt it was said
     at all.** **[WIKI] [USNI]** A signal officer (**Lt. John C. Kinney**) close enough to have
     heard it wrote a detailed 1881 account that **never mentions the exclamation**; men present
     doubted any such order could be heard over the guns. **[USNI]**
   - **More-plausible reconstructions** (if anything was said): *"Damn the torpedoes. Four bells,
     Captain Drayton"* (to his flag captain), then *"Go ahead, Jouett, full speed"* (to Metacomet's
     commander). **[USNI]** A separately surfaced version: *"Damn the torpedoes! Go on! Put the helm
     a-starboard, Captain Drayton!"* (from a Brownell pamphlet inscription). **[USNI]**
   - **Author directive:** give the famous line in quotes, then **explicitly tell the reader it's
     a compression/reconstruction** — "the words we remember were stitched together and polished
     later; what's certain is that Farragut drove straight through the mines." **The ACTION is
     verified; the QUOTE is [VERIFY].**
2. **Craven (North) — "After you, pilot":**
   - *"After you, pilot."* — Cdr. Craven to pilot John Collins at the escape scuttle of the
     sinking Tecumseh. **[TEC-NET] [WIKI-TEC]. [VERIFY]** — celebrated naval lore, survivor-
     reported (Collins lived, Craven didn't); attribute as reported, not gospel (see §6).
3. **Collins (North), survivor's account:** *"There was nothing after me. When I reached the
   topmost round of the ladder, the vessel seemed to drop from under me."* — pilot **John H.
   Collins. [TEC-NET]. [VERIFY]** exact wording.
4. **Farragut lashed to the rigging (the iconic image, also softened by fact):**

     Drayton, worried Farragut might fall to the deck if hit, had a sailor secure the admiral with
     a line** where he stood in the rigging/shrouds; **Farragut waved it off** (*"Never mind, I am
     all right"*) but the sailor obeyed orders. **[WIKI] [ABT]** *([VERIFY] the "Never mind, I am
     all right" wording. Author: he was lashed for safety at Drayton's insistence — not a
     swashbuckling self-tie — say so.)*

   lethal one-two punch"** that dispersed Northern war-weariness and helped assure Lincoln's
   re-election. **[WIKI]. [VERIFY]** exact McPherson wording before quoting verbatim; safe to

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the local hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and each side — was ultimately fought over. Omission is the
> default Lost Cause failure; "the last Gulf port" / "a daring fleet action" framing alone fails
> the gate. Mobile Bay gives TWO concrete, documented hooks — the blockade as the instrument
> strangling the slave economy, and the **Black sailors who manned the guns of the flagship.**

### The documented throughline — the blockade strangled a slave economy
- The Confederacy fought to **preserve and extend slavery.** Mobile was the Gulf's last open
  door for the **blockade-runners** that kept that slaveholding war effort supplied — cotton out,
  arms and goods in. **[WIKI] [ABT]** The Union blockade existed to **choke off that trade**, and
  Mobile Bay slammed the door. The fleet action was, concretely, **the instrument shutting down
  the economy of a society built on enslaved labor.** The blockade and emancipation were the same
  policy: as Union ships took the coast, **"vessels in all the squadrons began taking fugitive
  slaves on board, enlisting the men as needed."** **[NARA]**

### STRONG LOCAL HOOK — the integrated crews at Mobile Bay **[NARA] [MIL] [NHHC]**
The single best illustration on this battlefield of what the war was *for* is **who was firing
Farragut's guns.** The U.S. Navy was, by 1864, a **substantially integrated force**, and the men
who kept the flagship fighting at Mobile Bay included **formerly enslaved men shooting their way
toward their own freedom:**
- **Nearly 18,000 men of African descent served in the U.S. Navy during the Civil War — about
  20 percent of the Navy's enlisted force**, nearly double the Black proportion in the Army.
  **[NARA]** In Farragut's own West Gulf Blockading Squadron, **roughly 1 in 4** of the enlisted
  men aboard ship by mid-1864 were Black. **[NARA]**
- **On Hartford's gun decks at Mobile Bay**, two of those men earned the **Medal of Honor** —
  and their stories are the cause made flesh:
  - **Landsman Wilson Brown (North)** — **born enslaved on Botany Bay Plantation near Natchez,
    Mississippi.** Citation: *"…Knocked unconscious into the hold of the ship when an enemy
    shellburst fatally wounded a man on the ladder above him, Brown, upon regaining
    consciousness, promptly returned to the shell whip on the berth deck and zealously continued
    to perform his duties although 4 of the 6 men at this station had been either killed or
    wounded by the enemy's terrific fire."* **[NHHC]**
  - **Landsman John Lawson (North)** — **free-born Black sailor from Philadelphia.** Citation:
    *"…Wounded in the leg and thrown violently against the side of the ship when an enemy shell
    [struck] on the berth deck, Lawson, upon regaining his composure, promptly returned to his
    station and, although urged to go below for treatment, steadfastly continued his duties
    throughout the remainder of the action."* **[NHHC/Wikipedia citation text]**

## 9. Significance

- **Closed the last major Gulf blockade-running port.** With Mobile Bay sealed, the Confederacy's
  Gulf coast east of the Mississippi was effectively shut; **Wilmington (Jan 1865)** would close
  the last Atlantic one. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Be precise: it corked the bay, it did not take the city.** The **port** was closed Aug 5 and
  the **forts** fell by Aug 23; **Mobile the city held until April 1865.** **[WIKI]**
- **Destroyed the last significant Confederate squadron on the Gulf** — CSS Tennessee captured,
  the gunboats destroyed or taken, Buchanan a prisoner. **[WIKI]**
- **THE POLITICAL PAYOFF (give this full weight):** Mobile Bay came **three months before the
  November 1864 election**, with the North war-weary and Lincoln's re-election in doubt. Paired
  with **Sherman's capture of Atlanta (Sept 2, 1864)** — and Sheridan's Shenandoah victories —
  it turned Northern gloom into momentum. Historian **James M. McPherson** called it the **"first
  blow of a lethal one-two punch"** (with Atlanta) that helped **assure Lincoln's re-election** —
  and thus the war's continuation to total Union victory and the end of slavery. **[WIKI]** The
  military victory was also, decisively, a **political** one.
- **Made Farragut the Union's preeminent naval hero** — the "Damn the torpedoes" legend, true or
  burnished, sealed his fame; he became the U.S. Navy's first **admiral.** *(Promotion-to-admiral
