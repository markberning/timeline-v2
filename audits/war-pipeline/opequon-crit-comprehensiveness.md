## Correctly-omitted (selective is right)

- Color biographies — Hayes, McKinley, Fitzhugh Lee, Patton Sr., Edward Hall/30th USCT.
  Cutting these avoided wrong-rank/date risk and kept the throughline tight. Breckinridge
  (ex-VP) was kept because it's a one-line, low-risk, thematically apt aside. **Correct
  editorial call** — not a gap.
- Exact ford names (Spout Spring/Locke's/Burns), individual brigade/regiment names,
  Ramseur's name. Appropriate compression for a reading app; the prose names the places
  that carry the story (Opequon Creek, Berryville Pike/Canyon, Valley Pike, the forts).

## SHOULD-ADD (enrichment, not required)

1. **Ramseur (South) absorbing the first blow.** The pack and NPS/ABT note Ramseur's
   division was the first to receive the Union assault and held against long odds early.
   The draft jumps from "the assault went in" to "a gap tore open" without naming who
   stood in the door first. One clause ("Ramseur's division (South) took the first shock
   and bent without breaking") would add texture and explain why the morning fight was a
   grind before the gap appeared. Low priority — the story works without it.

2. **The forts as named places on the north flank.** §4 says "the Confederate forts north
   of town"; the pack names Fort Collier / Star Fort / Smithfield Redoubt, and the map
   plan calls for them. If the section ships with maps labeling those forts, a single
   place-name in the prose (e.g., "the works around Fort Collier and Star Fort") would tie
   prose to map per war-map doctrine. Optional.

## Fact-pack blind spots (for the record, not draft faults)

- The pack's strength section is genuinely ambiguous (engaged vs aggregate); the author
  navigated it well, but a future pack should lead with the engaged pair and footnote the
  aggregate, not present them as co-equal.
- The pack offers the Laws destination two ways (Lee/Richmond vs Petersburg) without
  resolving it; comprehensiveness-wise this is fine since the draft stayed on the hard
  claim, but it's a known soft spot (see the fact gate).
