## Claim-by-claim

### The stolen march / the James crossing

- **"~24 miles south of Richmond," Petersburg on the south bank of the Appomattox** —
  **VERIFIED** (Wikipedia, ABT both state 24 miles).
- **Pontoon bridge "2,200 feet long" at Windmill Point** — **VERIFIED (with a precision note).**
  ABT's published figure is "2,200-foot-long pontoon bridge at Windmill Point" — the
  draft matches ABT verbatim. The *measured* engineering figure is **2,170 feet, 101
  pontoon boats, built in ~7 hours** (LOC / civilwarmonths / civil-war-center). 2,200 is
  ABT's round number, not an error. **SHOULD-FIX (optional):** could note "about 2,200
  feet (2,170 measured)" but the current text is defensible as-is since it cites the ABT
  framing.
- **Five railroads converged at Petersburg** — **VERIFIED.** ABT: "the junction point of
  five railroads that supplied the entire upper James River region." The draft's hedge
  ("by the common count, five") is appropriately cautious.
- **"Lee did not know where Grant's army was" for ~two days** — **VERIFIED** (Wikipedia,
  ABT, LOC summary: "Grant then crossed his army over the James during the next two days
  with Lee still unsure as to his intentions").
- **Cold Harbor June 3, the Overland sequence (Wilderness → Spotsylvania → Cold Harbor)** —
  **VERIFIED.**

### Beauregard / Wise — the empty city

### June 15 — the Dimmock Line, USCT, the captured batteries

- **(a) SPECIAL SCRUTINY — Dimmock Line built with FORCED ENSLAVED LABOR. VERIFIED — accurate
  and sourced.** Encyclopedia Virginia confirms verbatim: "Some 264 slaves from Virginia's
  Eastern Shore and more than 1,000 from North Carolina turned over the soil." EV also
  documents the impressment mechanism (General Assembly authorized impressment; Capt.
  Dimmock requested "200 negroes"; 60-day labor limit). The draft's "Over a thousand
  enslaved people brought from North Carolina, and 264 more from Virginia's Eastern Shore,
  had been forced to dig it" is an exact, defensible rendering. **This thematic spine is
  factually sound.** No fix.
- **Dimmock Line = ~10-mile arc, 55 numbered batteries, both ends anchored on the
  Appomattox** — **VERIFIED.** EV: "fifty-five artillery batteries and connected infantry
  earthworks" in "a ten-mile arc around Petersburg." Wikipedia: "for over 10 miles."
- **(b) SPECIAL SCRUTINY — USCT capturing the Dimmock batteries June 15. VERIFIED;
  battery-number claim is the best-attested one and correctly stated.** EV states plainly:
  U.S. Colored Troops "captured Batteries Six through Eleven by nine o'clock"; white Union
  soldiers took "Batteries Four and Five"; "One through Three were evacuated." ABT confirms
  Hinks's USCT "captured Batteries 6 through 11." The draft credits the USCT with **Batteries
  6–11** and the overall assault with the **3.5-mile front** — exactly the sources' split, and
  it deliberately does NOT print the contested wider lists ("3 and 5–11," "3–12"). This is
  the correct call given sources split on the numbers. **Regiment/credit claims are
  defensible:** the draft asserts only the Kiddoo/22nd USCT credit (sourced) and does NOT
  assert the popular-source per-battery regiment list (Cook/22nd at Battery 7, etc.). Good
  discipline. VERIFIED.
- **~3,700 Black soldiers in Hinks's division** — **VERIFIED-WITHIN-RANGE / mild
  UNSUPPORTED-as-precise.** I could not independently pin "3,700" to a primary count in
  live search (Duncan's brigade alone ~2,200; the full division is multi-brigade). The pack
  carries "3,700–5,000" and the draft uses the firm low end. The figure is plausible and
  cited to the pack; not wrong. **SHOULD-FIX:** "roughly 3,700" is fine as a floor; if a
  scholarly division-strength figure (Hess) is later available, pin it. Not ship-blocking.
- **Baylor's farm roadblock, two USCT attacks, captured a cannon, delayed advance to early
  afternoon** — **VERIFIED.** Suderow/Beyond the Crater: XVIII Corps "ran into 850 troopers
  and two cannon, commanded by Dennis D. Ferebee"; "the 22nd USCT managed to press ahead and
  capture a cannon." NYPL period print: "Hinks's division … bringing in the guns captured
  from the Confederates at Baylor's farm." The draft correctly does NOT name Ferebee or the
  850 figure (kept as popular-source). Good.
- **Transport vessels landed Smith's divisions "almost at random"** — **VERIFIED** (pack
  [WIKI]; this is Wikipedia's wording). Not independently in ECW, but sourced.
- **Assault ~7 p.m.; eastern works in Union hands ~9 p.m.; 3.5-mile front** — **VERIFIED.**
  EV: assault began at 7 p.m., USCT captured 6–11 "by nine o'clock." 3.5-mile front is
  Wikipedia's figure (pack).

### The hesitation — June 15–16

- **(d) SPECIAL SCRUTINY — Smith's hesitation. VERIFIED and fairly stated.** Wikipedia/ECW:
  Smith "decided to wait until dawn"; ECW: Smith "did not believe a night attack was sound,
  even with a full moon." The draft's "His caution is understandable. It was also … the
  costliest hesitation of the campaign" is interpretive but squarely supported by the
  consensus "grand opening squandered" reading. No fix.
- **(d) SPECIAL SCRUTINY — Hancock's "lost order." VERIFIED.** This is well-documented:
  Suderow (Beyond the Crater) — "Grant failed to inform Hancock that Petersburg was the
  target"; faulty map telling him to halt at a creek "that isn't there"; poor maps forcing
  reliance on local Black guides. Wikipedia: Hancock "was uncertain of his orders and the
  disposition of forces, and uncharacteristically deferred to Smith's judgment to wait." The
  draft's framing — orders/maps miscarried, Hancock not told Petersburg was the objective,
  deferred to Smith — is accurate. **SHOULD-FIX (nuance, not error):** strictly, the failure
  was as much that Hancock was never *told the objective* (and a rations/map snarl) as a
  single literal "lost order" document; the draft's phrase "the 'lost order' that haunts every
  account" is the common shorthand and is hedged ("administrative failures"). Defensible.
- **June 16: Beauregard ~14,000 (Hoke + Johnson) vs ~50,000 Federals; Grant arrives with
  Burnside's IX Corps** — **VERIFIED** (pack [WIKI]).
- **Hancock's assault ~5:30 p.m.; Barlow took Redans 13–15, driven back; Patrick Kelly,
  Irish Brigade, killed** — **VERIFIED.** Kelly's death is **June 16, 1864** (Wikipedia
  "Patrick Kelly": "June 16, 1864 … shot through the head while leading the Irish Brigade").
  The draft places Kelly's death "in the same fighting" of the June 16 assault — correct.

### The door slams — June 17–18

- **(c) SPECIAL SCRUTINY — Fleetwood's Medal of Honor at New Market Heights, NOT Petersburg.
  VERIFIED — the draft gets this exactly right.** ABT: Fleetwood, 4th USCT, free Black man
  from Baltimore, enlisted August 1863, earned the MoH at **Chaffin's Farm / New Market
  Heights, Sept 29, 1864**, "He seized the colors, after 2 color bearers had been shot down."
  The draft's MEANWHILE explicitly flags "(That came later, at a different battle — not at
  Petersburg on June 15.)" Perfect. The Fleetwood "swept like a tornado" quote is correctly
  NOT printed. No fix.
- **June 17: Potter's IX Corps captured ~a mile of works + ~600 prisoners, stalled under
  enfilade; Hartranft flanked ~2 p.m.; Ledlie's evening attack failed; Ledlie observed
  drunk; Crater foreshadow** — **VERIFIED** (pack [WIKI]; Ledlie's drunkenness at the Crater
  is independently notorious). The light Crater foreshadow is within bounds the pack permits.
- **Beauregard withdrew ~a mile west to Taylor's Branch the night of June 17** — **VERIFIED**
  (pack [WIKI]).
- **Rooney Lee confirmed the crossings; Lee dispatched two divisions starting ~3 a.m. June 18**
  — **VERIFIED** (pack [WIKI]).
- **June 18: Kershaw + Field arrive ~noon → Beauregard over 20,000; Grant ~67,000 with
  Warren's V Corps; Hancock incapacitated by Gettysburg wound, Birney in command; troops rush
  into empty Dimmock works, halt at Taylor's Branch** — **VERIFIED** (pack [WIKI]).
- **Willcox emerged with ~1,000 men; Warren halted by murderous fire from Rives's Salient
  (Battery 27, Jerusalem Plank Road); Chamberlain severely wounded there, field-promoted
  brigadier by Grant** — **VERIFIED.** Wikipedia confirms Chamberlain "was severely wounded"
  at Rives's Salient and the battlefield promotion.
- **1st Maine Heavy Artillery, ~900 garrison troops, lost 632 men in minutes — heaviest
  single-battle regimental loss of the war** — **VERIFIED.** Wikipedia: "lost 632 men in the
  assault"; ABT: "Within ten minutes, 632 men lay dead or wounded … the largest regimental
  loss of the entire Civil War." The draft's "632 men in a matter of minutes" is exact.

- **Union ~11,386 (killed ~1,688, near 1,700 in draft); Confederate ~3,200–4,000; "roughly

- **Beauregard verbatim core: "Petersburg at that hour was clearly at the mercy of the
