## Findings sorted

### MUST-ADD
*(none)*

### SHOULD-ADD
1. **Burnside / IX Corps is absent from the prose.** The fact pack names Maj. Gen. Ambrose
   E. Burnside (North), IX Corps, reporting directly to Grant (not Meade), arriving piecemeal
   and fighting in the gap between the two roads on May 6. The draft never mentions him.
   Two reasons this is worth one clause: (a) Burnside's IX Corps is the parent corps of the
   USCT 4th Division the draft leans on for its entire cause-hook — naming Burnside ties the
   Black division into the order of battle instead of leaving it free-floating; (b) the
   Grant-rode-with-Meade / Burnside-reported-to-Grant split is a genuine command quirk of
   this army. One sentence in the May-6 section ("between the two roads, Burnside's (North)
   IX Corps fed in piecemeal") would close the only real order-of-battle gap. **SHOULD-ADD.**

2. **The "why couldn't the IX Corps / reinforcements decide it" beat (optional).** Part of
   why May 6 stayed a draw despite the dawn near-breakthrough is that Union reinforcements
   (Burnside) arrived slowly through the same impossible woods that hurt everyone. The draft
   implies this via terrain but never says the Union's own delay cost it the morning. Minor;
   the terrain theme already carries most of it. **SHOULD-ADD (optional).**

### Perspective / weighting check
- **Confederate POV** (Lee's gamble, Longstreet's rescue, the flank masterstroke) and
  **Union POV** (Grant's relentlessness, the soldiers' night-march cheer) are both given
  full weight — the dual-POV signature is intact and balanced.
- **The enslaved POV** is present and, correctly, given the *closing* weight via the 23rd
  USCT hook. This is the right call: it is the perspective with the most literal stake and
  the draft makes it the answer to "what was it for."
- **The human cost** is neither sanitized nor gratuitous — the fires passage is documented,
  attributed, and unflinching without inventing named victims.

### Fact-pack blind spots (independent of the draft)
- The pack omits **Wilson's / Sheridan's cavalry** action and the broader cavalry screen;
  the draft's single "Union cavalry spotted him coming" is enough for a storytelling-first
  cut — not a gap, but noting the pack never gave the cavalry layer.
- The pack flagged the **iron-furnace cut-over-woodland** origin of the thicket as
  unverified; the draft correctly OMITTED it (NPS does not state it). Confirmed the omission
  was the right call — NPS describes only "tangled/impenetrable/bewildering forest," no
  furnace claim. No gap.

**Bottom line:** comprehensive. Add one Burnside/IX Corps clause and the order of battle is
complete; everything else essential is already here. PASS.
