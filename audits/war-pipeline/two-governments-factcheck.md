## MUST-FIX FINDINGS

---

### MUST-FIX 1 — Lyon's Rank is Wrong (Camp Jackson, Missouri)

**Draft phrase:**
> "a Union officer named **Brigadier General Nathaniel Lyon**, commanding regiments largely made up of pro-Union German immigrants, surrounded and captured a state militia encampment..."

**Finding:** ❌ WRONG

Nathaniel Lyon held the rank of **Captain** on May 10, 1861, the date of the Camp Jackson affair. He was not promoted to Brigadier General until May 30, 1861 — twenty days after the event. Calling him "Brigadier General" at Camp Jackson is anachronistic and factually incorrect.

**Source:** Wikipedia, "Camp Jackson affair" (accessed 2026-05-30); Wikipedia, "Nathaniel Lyon" — "Captain Nathaniel Lyon" at the April 23 appointment; promoted to BG on May 30, 1861.

**Correct fact:** Captain Nathaniel Lyon (at the time of Camp Jackson).

**Fix:** Change "Brigadier General Nathaniel Lyon" to "Captain Nathaniel Lyon" (or simply "Union commander Nathaniel Lyon, then a captain").

---

### MUST-FIX 2 — Lincoln's "Hold, Occupy, and Possess" Quote Is Truncated Without Ellipsis

**Draft phrase:**
> "The power confided to me will be used to hold, occupy, and possess the property and places belonging to the Government."

**Finding:** ❌ WRONG (incomplete quotation presented as complete)

The actual sentence in Lincoln's First Inaugural does not end there. The complete sentence reads:

> "The power confided to me will be used to hold, occupy, and possess the property and places belonging to the Government **and to collect the duties and imposts**; but beyond what may be necessary for these objects, there will be no invasion, no using of force against or among the people anywhere."

The draft ends the quote with a period after "Government" — as if that were the end of the sentence — omitting "and to collect the duties and imposts." This is an inaccurate transcription of a locked primary-source quote. The omission is not signaled by an ellipsis. Since this is presented as a verbatim block-quote, it must either reproduce the full sentence or use an ellipsis to signal the cut.

**Source:** Avalon Project, Yale Law School, Lincoln First Inaugural (verified 2026-05-30); multiple secondary sources confirm the full sentence.

**Fix:** Either extend the quote to include the full sentence, or insert "[…]" after "Government" before the closing period.

---

### MUST-FIX 3 — Baltimore Riot: Pennsylvania Troops Were Not Attacked

**Draft phrase:**
> "soldiers from Massachusetts and Pennsylvania, marching through Baltimore on their way to defend Washington, were set upon by a pro-Confederate mob."

**Finding:** ❌ WRONG

Only the **6th Massachusetts Militia Regiment** was attacked on April 19, 1861. Pennsylvania troops had passed through Baltimore the day before (April 18) without incident and were not part of the riot. The Pennsylvania regiments were traveling separately; the attack specifically targeted the Massachusetts soldiers. Identifying Pennsylvania as co-victims of the riot is factually incorrect.

**Source:** Wikipedia, "Baltimore riot of 1861" — "The Pennsylvania troops arrived in Baltimore on April 18, before the riot, and passed through without incident. On April 19, the Massachusetts regiment was attacked."

**Fix:** Change "soldiers from Massachusetts and Pennsylvania" to "soldiers from Massachusetts" (specifically the 6th Massachusetts Militia). A reference to Pennsylvania troops arriving separately without incident may be added for completeness but is not required.

## SHOULD-FIX FINDINGS

---

### SHOULD-FIX A — Georgia Declaration Quote Omits Tail of the Sentence Without Indicating a Cut

**Draft phrase:**
> "Because their avowed purpose is to subvert our society and subject us not only to the loss of our property but the destruction of ourselves, our wives, and our children."

**Finding:** ⚠️ UNSUPPORTED AS RENDERED (truncation without ellipsis)

The actual Georgia declaration passage continues beyond what the draft quotes:

> "Because their avowed purpose is to subvert our society, and subject us, not only to the loss of our property but the destruction of ourselves, our wives and our children, **and the desolation of our homes, our altars, and our firesides.**"

The draft stops mid-sentence — "our children" is not the end of the original sentence. It also omits the comma before "and subject us" and the comma before "not only." Presented in quotation marks without ellipsis, this is a cut quotation misrepresented as complete. The missing tail ("and the desolation of our homes, our altars, and our firesides") is not negligible — it is the rhetorical climax of the sentence.

**Source:** Avalon Project, Yale Law School, Georgia Declaration of the Causes of Secession (verified 2026-05-30).

**Fix:** Either extend the quote to include the full sentence, or insert "[…]" before the closing period. Recommend restoring the full sentence — it's stronger.

---

### SHOULD-FIX B — Baltimore Soldier Death Count: "4" vs. "5" Needs Clarification

**Draft phrase:**
> "four Union soldiers and twelve civilians lay dead in the streets of Baltimore"

**Finding:** ⚠️ CONTESTED — likely accurate as rendered but source-dependent

The figure of four soldiers is widely reported and is not wrong exactly, but Wikipedia's detailed account identifies five individuals killed or mortally wounded (the fifth, Corporal Sumner Henry Needham, died eight days later, on April 27). Sources that count deaths at the scene or close to it say four; sources that include all who died of wounds from the riot say five. The American Battlefield Trust uses four. The draft figure of four is defensible and commonly cited, but the author should be aware that the number is contested and may be worth a brief hedge ("at least four").

**Source:** Wikipedia, "Baltimore riot of 1861" — "5 killed" in infobox but acknowledges "several sources state four soldiers"; American Battlefield Trust also uses four.

## FINAL VERDICT

**FAIL — 3 MUST-FIXES**

1. **MUST-FIX 1 (Most Serious):** Lyon's rank at Camp Jackson is wrong — he was a **Captain**, not a Brigadier General, on May 10, 1861.

2. **MUST-FIX 2:** Lincoln's "hold, occupy, and possess" block quote is presented as complete when it is truncated mid-sentence. Add "and to collect the duties and imposts" or use an ellipsis.

3. **MUST-FIX 3:** Baltimore riot: Pennsylvania troops were not attacked. Only the **6th Massachusetts Militia** was set upon. Remove "and Pennsylvania."

**Most serious error overall:** Lyon's rank (MUST-FIX 1) — calling a captain a brigadier general at the named event.

**Locked primary-source quote status:** The Cornerstone Speech, Confederate Constitution clauses, secession declaration passages, Lincoln's First Inaugural closing ("better angels" — complete), and the Browning letter are all verbatim-accurate against the primary sources. The one locked quote with an error is the "hold, occupy, and possess" truncation (MUST-FIX 2). The Georgia declaration quote is verbatim-accurate for what it includes, but it is cut mid-sentence without ellipsis (SHOULD-FIX A).

After correcting the three must-fixes and the two should-fixes, the draft is ready to pass the fact gate.
