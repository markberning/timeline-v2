## FINDINGS

### MUST-FIX
**None.**

The slavery-named requirement is met affirmatively, no surface romanticizes the
Confederacy, there is no euphemism for the cause, no false-balance "both sides" framing
of WHY the war was fought, and the human reality (the column of prisoners, the
exhaustion, the fog-blind surrender) is neither sanitized nor gratuitous. The draft
does not soft-pedal — its whole thesis is anti-legend.

### SHOULD-FIX (framing nudges; none ship-blocking)

1. **Confederate sympathy register — "impossible morning" / "too few rifles" (§2).**
   The draft's repeated, sympathetic emphasis on the Confederate defenders being
   outnumbered and overmatched ("an impossible morning," "too few men," "with too few
   rifles," "an outnumbered enemy who mostly surrendered") is accurate and is doing
   legitimate work (it supports the deflation thesis — the mountain wasn't really
   fought for). It is NOT Lost Cause valorization. But because the sympathy runs only
   one direction at the human level (we feel the overmatched defenders' plight vividly;
   we never feel a comparable human cost on the climbing Union side beyond "exhaustion
   and falls"), a reader could drift toward the gallant-outnumbered-Confederate trope by
   accident. **Suggested nudge:** keep the analysis (they WERE too few), but make sure
   the reason they were too few stays foregrounded — these were men defending the door to
   the slaveholding heartland, thinned by Bragg's own miscalculation, not noble martyrs.
   The draft already gestures at this; one clause tying Walthall's thin line back to what
   it was defending would close the gap. **TILTED-watch, not TILTED.**

2. **"the men beaten/humiliated at Chickamauga ... revenge" motif (§1, §4 sidebars).**
   The recurring "the army humiliated at Chickamauga, climbing to take its revenge"
   framing is rousing and is about the Union side, so it carries no Lost Cause risk. Flag
   only as a tone note: it is the one spot where the draft reaches for the very kind of
   stirring-spectacle register it spends the rest of its length deflating. Not a
   distortion; just verify it doesn't undercut the "this fight was overrated" thesis when
   the Missionary Ridge section is eventually written. **FAIR.**

3. **"four million Black people" / present capitalization & framing (§1).** Correct and
   appropriate; no presentism problem. Noted only to confirm it was checked: invoking the
   Emancipation Proclamation and the Union war aim "including slavery's destruction" is
   period-accurate (Proclamation in force Jan 1, 1863), not anachronistic moralizing
   imported backward. **FAIR.**

## DISTORTION SWEEP — explicit negatives (all clear)

- **Soft-pedaling the cause:** none. Cause is stated outright, twice.
- **Euphemism** ("states' rights," "way of life," "the conflict"): none. The draft says
  "enslavement of four million Black people" and "the slaveholding South" plainly.
- **False balance** on why the war happened: none. There is "hold both truths" balance,
  but it is about the BATTLE'S legend vs. reality, not about the war's cause — legitimate.
- **Confederate romanticization:** none. The Confederacy here is the side holding the door

- **Presentism:** none. Moral framing is grounded in 1863 facts (Emancipation, war aims).
- **Unfair sympathy:** borderline one-directional human sympathy for the defenders
  (SHOULD-FIX #1), but not unfair and not ship-blocking.
- **Sanitized human reality:** no — the prisoner column, exhaustion, fog-blind surrender
  are carried honestly. **Gratuitous** human reality: no — light casualties are not
  splashed into false carnage.
