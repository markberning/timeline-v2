# NEWCOMER-CLARITY CRITIC — Battle of Bunker Hill draft

Draft: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-bunker-hill-draft.md`
Critic brief: zero-prior-knowledge reader; every term/person/place defined on first
use PER SECTION (sections read independently); parenthetical glosses, not em-dashes;
causal mechanisms explicit; timeline always legible; cross-chapter beats stand alone.

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The storytelling spine is strong and most of the hard clarity work is already done
well: Section 1's geography setup (the two heights, the two hills with elevations,
the Neck, the cannon-on-hills mechanism) is exemplary, the Mystic-beach explanation
in Section 2 is a model gloss, and the timeline is clean end to end. But the
per-section reset rule is violated repeatedly — Section 2 opens cold with no
stand-alone setup, and several load-bearing terms (redoubt, the Neck, regulars,
parapet) and people (Gage, Warren in Section 3; Pitcairn's "Lexington fame") lean on
Section 1 or on outside knowledge. All fixes are local glosses or one added setup
sentence; none require structural rewrites. BLOCKERs are mandatory for the reviser.

---

## BLOCKER

### B1. Section 2 opens with zero stand-alone context (criterion 6 + 3)
> "From about one in the afternoon the longboats began putting Major General William
> Howe's (British) first wave ashore, some 1,500 men, at Moulton's Point on the
> eastern corner of the peninsula, unopposed and out of musket range."

A reader landing on this section knows nothing: which peninsula, why there is an
"American position," what the redoubt is, why the British are attacking at all. The
first paragraph references "the redoubt," "the Neck," and "the peninsula" as known
quantities — all defined only in Section 1. Sections are read independently.
**Fix:** open with 1–2 setup sentences, e.g.: "The night before, the American militia
army besieging the British garrison in Boston had dug a small dirt fort (a redoubt)
on Breed's Hill, on the Charlestown peninsula just across the water from the city,
within cannon range of the town. The British were crossing the harbor to take it
back. From about one in the afternoon the longboats began putting..."

### B2. "Redoubt" undefined in Section 2, Section 3 is fine, but also undefined on the DOSSIER surfaces (criterion 1)
First Section 2 use:
> "...a rail fence now extended the line from the redoubt down toward the Mystic River..."

Defined only in Section 1 ("a redoubt (a small enclosed dirt fort)"). The dossier
also uses it raw in two places read before any section:
> sides: "force: The redoubt and the rail fence"
> casualties footnote: "Most American casualties came in the retreat across the Neck, not in the redoubt."

**Fix:** Section 2 — covered by the B1 setup sentence ("a small dirt fort (a
redoubt)"). Dossier sides — "force: The redoubt (their overnight-dug dirt fort) and
the rail fence." Footnote — "...not in the redoubt (the dirt fort)." Keep glosses
parenthetical per house style.

### B3. "The Neck" undefined in Section 2 and on the dossier casualties footnote (criterion 1 + 3)
Section 2 first use:
> "...fresh men were streaming across the Neck to fill it."

and again: "walked his big New Hampshire regiment across the shot-swept Neck."
Defined only in Section 1. The retreat beat ("back across Bunker Hill and over the
Neck under naval fire") is the section's casualty engine — a newcomer who doesn't
know the Neck is the ONLY way off the peninsula misses why the retreat was a
shooting gallery.
**Fix:** first Section 2 use → "streaming across the Neck (the narrow strip of land
that was the only way onto the peninsula) to fill it." Dossier footnote → "across
the Neck (the one land exit from the peninsula)."

### B4. Section 3 introduces Gage by surname only, with no role or side (criterion 2)
> "Gage's official return came to 1,054 casualties..."

First appearance in Section 3; a section-only reader has never heard of him. He is
also load-bearing two paragraphs later ("Gage's report and private letters ... ended
his command").
**Fix:** "General Thomas Gage's (the British commander at Boston) official return
came to 1,054 casualties, 226 killed and 828 wounded..." Also gloss "return" — see S7.

### B5. "Pitcairn ... of Lexington fame" is meaningless to a zero-knowledge reader (criterion 6)
> "Major John Pitcairn (British), of Lexington fame, fell mortally wounded at the parapet..."

The reader has not been told what happened at Lexington or who Pitcairn was there.
"Of Lexington fame" reads as an in-joke.
**Fix:** "Major John Pitcairn (British), the Marine officer who had commanded the
troops at Lexington in April, where the war's first shots were fired, fell mortally
wounded at the parapet..."

### B6. Section 3 introduces Warren with no role or side (criterion 2)
> "Joseph Warren was among the dead."

His full identity arrives only four paragraphs later ("Warren's story had one more
chapter") and even that assumes the reader knows him. Section 2 introduces him
properly, but Section 3 must stand alone.
**Fix:** "Joseph Warren, the Boston physician and patriot leader who had fought in
the redoubt as a volunteer, was among the dead."

### B7. "Regulars" undefined in Sections 2 and 3 (criterion 1)
Section 2 first use:
> "...regulars struggling uphill through thick unmown grass..."

Section 3 first use:
> "...militia behind earthworks could maul the best regulars in the world."

Defined only in Section 1 and the dossier sides note. Section 3's closing lesson —
the battle's whole meaning — turns on the militia-vs-regulars contrast, and Section 3
defines neither term.
**Fix:** Section 2 → "regulars (Britain's professional full-time soldiers)
struggling uphill..." Section 3 → "militia (part-time citizen soldiers) behind
earthworks (walls of fresh-dug dirt) could maul the best regulars (professional
full-time troops) in the world."

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1. "Parapet" never defined anywhere (criterion 1)
Section 1: "Prescott climbed onto the parapet and walked it" — Section 2: "the
regulars came over the parapet." The Prescott parapet walk is a signature beat and a
newcomer can't picture it.
**Fix (Section 1 first use):** "climbed onto the parapet (the top of the redoubt's
earth wall) and walked it." Section 2 inherits via the B1 setup or gets "(the fort's
wall top)" at "came over the parapet."

### S2. Military "demonstrate / demonstration" undefined (criterion 1)
> "Brigadier General Robert Pigot (British) would demonstrate against the redoubt on
> the left while the grenadiers ... struck the rail fence..."

A newcomer reads "demonstrate" as protest. Same word raw in Locator 2's caption ("a
demonstration against the redoubt").
**Fix:** "would demonstrate (make a threatening show of attack, to pin the defenders
in place) against the redoubt..." Locator caption: "a pinning show of force against
the redoubt while the light infantry charged up the narrow Mystic beach."

### S3. "Rail fence" never explained as an object (criterion 1)
> "Captain Thomas Knowlton's (American) Connecticut men held the rail fence, stuffed with hay."

What is a rail fence, and why hay? It also appears raw on the dossier sides line and
in the Section 1 Page-plan caption (a Section 1 surface, where the fence hasn't been
mentioned in prose at all).
**Fix:** "held the rail fence (an ordinary farm fence of stacked wooden rails, its
gaps stuffed with hay so it read as solid cover)." Page-plan caption: "...the
Americans' rail-fence line running toward the Mystic..."

### S4. "Rotating volleys" undefined — and it's the mechanism that wins the beach (criterion 1 + 4)
> "...Stark's men held their fire to about fifty paces and then delivered rotating
> volleys that wrecked the head of the column."

Why "rotating" matters (no pause to reload = continuous fire into a column that
can't spread out) is the whole reason 96 men died on a strip of sand.
**Fix:** "delivered rotating volleys (ranks firing in turn, so someone was always
loaded and the fire never paused) that wrecked the head of the column."

### S5. Powder mechanism one clause short (criterion 4)
> "The powder was nearly gone. Colonel William Prescott's (American) men had perhaps
> one volley left."

Good — but a newcomer may not know a musket is a single-shot weapon loaded with
loose gunpowder, so "out of powder" = "the guns are clubs." The bayonet contrast
lands harder with the link explicit.
**Fix:** "The powder (the loose gunpowder each man poured down his barrel for every
single shot — no powder, and a musket was a club) was nearly gone."

### S6. "Lexington and Concord" carries no context in Section 1 (criterion 6)
> "Two months after the shooting at Lexington and Concord, a New England militia army ... had gathered around Boston..."

The PILL to /war-revolution/outbreak helps but the prose must stand alone.
**Fix:** "Two months after the shooting at Lexington and Concord (the April skirmishes
outside Boston where the war began), a New England militia army..."

### S7. "Official return" undefined (criterion 1)
Section 3: "Gage's official return came to 1,054 casualties" and dossier casualties:
"the firm figure, from Gage's official return."
**Fix:** "official casualty return (the army's formal report of killed and wounded)"
at the Section 3 use; dossier → "from Gage's official casualty report."

### S8. "Committee of Safety" and "Provincial Congress" undefined (criterion 1)
> "On June 13 the New Hampshire Committee of Safety passed word to Massachusetts..."
> "...Major General Joseph Warren (American), the president of the Massachusetts Provincial Congress..."

Rebel-government machinery a newcomer has never met (Committee of Safety also
appears twice more in Section 1; Provincial Congress also in Warren's dossier bio).
**Fix:** "the New Hampshire Committee of Safety (the emergency rebel body running
each colony's war effort) passed word..."; "...president of the Massachusetts
Provincial Congress (the rebels' stand-in legislature for the colony)..."

### S9. Copp's Hill unoriented at first prose use (criterion 3)
Section 2 first use:
> "...the British batteries on Copp's Hill and the ships set the town alight..."

Its location ("in Boston's North End") arrives only in the MEANWHILE block at the
section's end. Also raw in Clinton's dossier bio ("Watching from the Copp's Hill
battery") and gloss "battery" itself — cannon, not electricity.
**Fix:** first prose use → "the British batteries (clusters of heavy cannon) on
Copp's Hill, across the water in Boston, and the ships set the town alight..."
Clinton bio → "from the Copp's Hill battery (a British cannon position in Boston,
facing the battlefield across the water)."

### S10. Section 2 first-use name-list precedes the introductions (criterion 2)
> "...it was attributed after the fact to Putnam, Prescott, Stark, and Gridley..."

In Section 2, this legend sentence is the FIRST appearance of Putnam, Prescott, and
Gridley (Stark alone is already introduced). Putnam's intro comes two paragraphs
later, Prescott's in the next paragraph, Gridley's never.
**Fix (lightest touch):** "...attributed after the fact to half the American
officers on the field — Putnam, Prescott, Stark, even Gridley the engineer..." →
better: keep parenthetical style: "it was attributed after the fact to four
different American officers (Putnam, Prescott, Stark, and Gridley), and it was an
old European army formula..." The "(four different American officers)" framing
covers role+side for the whole list at once.

### S11. Section 3's Washington beat doesn't orient Cambridge or the army (criterion 6 + 3)
> "Two weeks after the battle, on July 3, George Washington took command of the army at Cambridge..."

Which army? Where is Cambridge? Section 3 readers haven't met either.
**Fix:** "George Washington took command of the American army besieging Boston,
camped at Cambridge just west of the city, and inherited both halves of the
inheritance..."

### S12. "Long Island and Kip's Bay" undefined (criterion 6)
> "...a faith in untrained men and fresh dirt that the campaigns of 1776, at Long
> Island and Kip's Bay, would punish brutally."

**Fix:** "...the campaigns of 1776 around New York, at Long Island and Kip's Bay
(American routs where militia broke and ran), would punish brutally."

### S13. "Proclamation of Rebellion" undefined (criterion 1)
> "...the news from Charlestown helped feed the Proclamation of Rebellion that August."

**Fix:** "...helped feed the Proclamation of Rebellion that August (the king's
formal declaration that the colonies were in open revolt, closing the door on
reconciliation)."

### S14. Paul Revere unintroduced (criterion 2)
> "Before the war, Paul Revere had made Warren a dental prosthesis..."

Zero-knowledge readers don't know Revere.
**Fix:** "Before the war, Paul Revere (a Boston silversmith and fellow patriot) had
made Warren a dental prosthesis..."

### S15. Dossier bios: "Rogers' Rangers" and "the 1745 Louisbourg expedition" undefined (criterion 1)
Stark bio: "A New Hampshire veteran of Rogers' Rangers..." — Prescott bio: "a veteran
of the 1745 Louisbourg expedition..."
**Fix:** "...veteran of Rogers' Rangers (a famous frontier scouting force from the
French and Indian War)"; "...veteran of the 1745 Louisbourg expedition (a colonial
assault on a French fortress in Canada)." Putnam's bio could likewise gloss the
French and Indian War as "(the previous war, against France)."

### S16. Homer caption (a Section 3 surface) names Burgoyne with no introduction (criterion 2)
> "Winslow Homer's 1875 painting of Bostonians watching the battle from Copp's Hill,
> where Burgoyne and Clinton stood at the battery."

Burgoyne never appears in Section 3 prose at all.
**Fix:** "...where the British generals Burgoyne and Clinton stood at the battery
(cannon position)."

### S17. Section 3 opening line could carry the battle frame (criterion 6)
> "The British army held the hill, and then it counted."

Great line — but a section-only reader doesn't know which hill or what just
happened. One clause preserves the line's snap.
**Fix:** "By five o'clock on June 17, 1775, the British army held the hill above
Charlestown, and then it counted."

## NIT

### N1. "47th Foot" (Section 2)
> "...reinforced by about four hundred fresh men of the 47th Foot and the Marines..."
"Foot" = infantry regiment. Fix: "the 47th Foot (an infantry regiment) and the Marines."

### N2. "Welch Fusiliers" (Section 2)
> "...the light company of the Welch Fusiliers leading..."
Fix: "the light company of the Welch Fusiliers (a storied British regiment) leading."

### N3. "isthmus" (Section 2)
> "...HMS Glasgow and gun-barges raking the isthmus."
A newcomer may not connect "isthmus" back to "the Neck," and "raking" is jargon.
Fix: "...HMS Glasgow and gun-barges firing down the length of the Neck."

### N4. "heated shot" (Section 2)
> "...set the town alight with heated shot and carcasses (incendiary shells)..."
Carcasses are glossed; heated shot isn't. Fix: "heated shot (cannonballs fired
red-hot to start fires) and carcasses (incendiary shells)."

### N5. Rawdon's side is implied, not stated (Section 2)
> "Lieutenant Lord Rawdon, in the assault, wrote home three days later."
Context makes him British; one word makes it airtight: "Lieutenant Lord Rawdon
(British), in the assault, wrote home three days later."

### N6. Mystic River unoriented in Section 2 prose (criterion 3)
> "...a rail fence now extended the line from the redoubt down toward the Mystic River..."
Locator 2 covers it, but prose could carry it: "down toward the Mystic River (the
water on the peninsula's north side)."

### N7. Charlestown village position at the burning beat (Section 2)
> "American sniper fire from the village had been galling Pigot's flank..."
"Beside the battlefield" arrives a sentence later; could front-load: "from the
village (at the foot of the hill, on the British left)."

### N8. "mezzotint" in Howe's caption and "aide-de-camp" in the Page-plan caption
Fix: "a 1777 mezzotint (a type of engraved print)"; "Howe's own engineer
aide-de-camp (personal staff officer)."

### N9. "Winter and Prospect Hills" direction (Section 2 close)
> "The Americans dug in a mile away on Winter and Prospect Hills that same evening..."
"A mile away" in which direction — back on the mainland? Fix: "a mile away on the
mainland, on Winter and Prospect Hills."

### N10. Gap between assaults 2 and 3 untimed (criterion 5)
First assault ~3:00, second "within the half hour," third — no time cue; the reader
re-acquires the clock only at "By about five o'clock." A light "late in the
afternoon" on the third assault would keep the clock continuous. (Only if the fact
pack supports it; otherwise leave as is — the §1 stat "~2 hours" bounds it.)

## WHAT ALREADY WORKS (no action — do not regress in revision)

- Section 1's geography is the gold standard: Boston besieged and "fed by sea," the
  two sets of heights with compass orientation, the cannon-on-hills mechanism
  ("Whoever fortified them first, and put cannon on them, would dominate the town"),
  both hills with elevations and positions, the Neck defined at first use.
- WHY the British had to attack is explicit ("a knife held to Boston's throat, and
  the British could not ignore it") and WHY a frontal assault was chosen is explained
  (contempt for militia + the era's regulars-beat-irregulars conviction).
- The Mystic beach is perfectly set up: "a strip of open beach along the Mystic,
  below the riverbank, where a column could walk right around the American line at
  the water's edge" + "about as wide as a wagon. The column had no room to spread
  out." A newcomer fully understands why 96 men died there.
- Timeline is legible throughout: evening march June 16 → midnight staking → ~4 a.m.
  discovery → morning council → ~1 p.m. landing → two-hour pause → ~3 p.m. first
  assault → "within the half hour" second → ~5 p.m. peninsula taken.
- militia, grenadiers, light infantry, breastwork, banyan, carcasses all glossed
  parenthetically at first use; no em-dash definitions anywhere.
- Persons in Section 1 are uniformly introduced with rank + side; Section 2's Stark,
  Knowlton, Pigot, Clinton, Burgoyne, Warren intros are model.
- The Trumbull caption's not-how-he-died caveat and the Prescott no-life-portrait
  disclosure are exactly the right newcomer honesty.
