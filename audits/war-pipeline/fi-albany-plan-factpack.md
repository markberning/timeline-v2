## 0. ONE-LINE FRAME (for the author's spine)
In summer 1754, with war already breaking out on the Ohio, seven British colonies met at Albany for two ranked purposes: **first**, to repair the fraying Covenant Chain alliance with the Iroquois Confederacy (the reason London ordered the congress); **second**, to debate colonial union. The Mohawk leader Theyanoguin (Hendrick) publicly rebuked British neglect. Franklin's Plan of Union was adopted by the Congress and then rejected by everyone who mattered — colonies (too much central power) and Crown (too much colonial autonomy). The plan died; the idea did not.

---

## 1. WHY IT MET — the trigger and the order from London

- **War already sparking on the Ohio.** The congress convened weeks after George Washington's frontier reverses: the May 1754 skirmish at Jumonville Glen and (just after the congress closed) his July 3, 1754 surrender at Fort Necessity. The opening clashes of what became the global Seven Years' War were already underway.
  *Source:* [Wikipedia — Albany Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Congress); [State Dept Office of the Historian — Albany Plan](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/albany-plan).
- **London's Board of Trade ORDERED the congress — chiefly to fix the Iroquois alliance.** The British Board of Trade mandated the meeting, and it directed that **renewing the Covenant Chain alliance with the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) be the congress's FIRST order of business**, ahead of any union talk. Governor Clinton's successor / NY authorities followed those instructions; "proper presents" for the Indians were a specified diplomatic necessity.
  *Source:* [New York Almanack — The Albany Congress of 1754](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2022/01/the-albany-congress-of-1754-native-people-colonists-the-monarchy/); [Britannica — Albany Congress](https://www.britannica.com/event/Albany-Congress).
- **The strategic logic:** British neglect (no public treaty at Albany for ~3 years) had pushed the Iroquois toward neutrality or France; a frontier war could not be fought without them. Fixing the alliance was the precondition for everything else.
  *Source:* New York Almanack (above); Hendrick's own speech (§3).

## 2. WHO AND WHEN — delegates, place, dates

- **Dates:** **June 19 – July 11, 1754.** Met daily, generally at the City Hall / courthouse in Albany, New York.
  *Source:* [Wikipedia — Albany Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Congress); American History Central.
- **Seven colonies sent delegates** (21 commissioners total): **New York, Pennsylvania, Maryland, and the four New England colonies — New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut.** (Virginia, New Jersey, and the southern colonies did NOT attend, though the plan later apportioned seats to them.)
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Albany Congress; State Dept Office of the Historian.
- **Notable delegates:**
  - **Benjamin Franklin** — Pennsylvania; architect of the Plan of Union.
  - **Thomas Hutchinson** — Massachusetts; helped draft the plan (later royal Governor of Massachusetts).
  - **James DeLancey** — Lieutenant Governor of New York; **chairman of the congress**.
  - **Stephen Hopkins** — Rhode Island (later a Declaration signer).
  - **William Johnson** — New York; the colonies' key Iroquois intermediary (later Superintendent of Indian Affairs).
  - **Peter Wraxall** — secretary.
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Albany Congress.
- **Pacing of the union debate (from the proceedings):** congress opened June 19; commissioners voted **June 24** to consider a union; a committee submitted a draft **June 28**; final plan adopted **July 10** (one day before the congress closed July 11).
  *Source:* American History Central — Albany Congress; [Founders Online — Proceedings of the Albany Congress](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-05-02-0096).

## 3. THE IROQUOIS PURPOSE — FIRST ORDER OF BUSINESS (keep this first-class)

> **NATIVE-FIRST FRAMING (author must lead with this, not footnote it):** The Covenant Chain alliance was the congress's PRIMARY business by London's own order. The Iroquois negotiated as a power protecting its own interests — not as subjects. They left only partly satisfied; their core grievances (encroachment, land loss) went unresolved in the colonists' favor.

### Who: the Iroquois Confederacy / Haudenosaunee
- The **Iroquois Confederacy** (Haudenosaunee, "People of the Longhouse") — by 1754 the **Six Nations**: Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, and (since c.1722) Tuscarora. Their council fire was at **Onondaga**. The **Covenant Chain** was the metaphor for the alliance network linking the Iroquois (and through them other Native nations) to the British colonies, especially New York.
  *Source:* [Wikipedia — Covenant Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_Chain); New York Almanack.
- **Roughly 150 Iroquois delegates** attended; **~200 Native people** present in Albany overall.
  *Source:* New York Almanack — Albany Congress of 1754.
- **Gifts:** The Board of Trade specified that "proper presents be furnished to the Indians"; substantial presents were sent from England as a diplomatic necessity. *(The "30 wagonloads" figure could NOT be confirmed in these sources — do NOT use a wagon count.)*
  *Source:* New York Almanack.

### The speaker: Theyanoguin (Hendrick) — get the identity RIGHT
- **Theyanoguin** (also rendered Tiyanoga, Teoniahigarawe; baptized "Hendrick," called **"King Hendrick"**; after c.1750 **Hendrick Peters**). Mohawk sachem, warrior, diplomat, and orator. He was the principal Iroquois spokesman at Albany, addressing the commissioners (reported around **July 2, 1754**) as speaker for the assembled delegates.
  *Source:* [Dictionary of Canadian Biography — Theyanoguin](https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/theyanoguin_3E.html); [Wikipedia — Hendrick Theyanoguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrick_Theyanoguin); [History Matters (GMU)](https://historymatters.gmu.edu/d/7804).
- **`[CRITICAL — DO NOT CONFLATE]` This is the LATER Hendrick.** Two Mohawk leaders baptized "Hendrick" were long merged in older books. Use only:
  - **Theyanoguin / Hendrick Peters (c.1692–1755)** — the man at Albany 1754; killed at the Battle of Lake George.
  - NOT **Hendrick Tejonihokarawa (c.1660–c.1735)** — the earlier leader, one of the **"Four Mohawk Kings"** who visited Queen Anne in London in **1710**.
  Late-20th-century scholarship separated them; do not give Theyanoguin the 1710 London visit or any Tejonihokarawa deeds.
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Hendrick Theyanoguin; [New York Almanack — "The Two Hendricks"](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2010/05/the-two-hendricks-a-mohawk-indian-mystery/).
- **`[CONTESTED]` Birth and origin.** Sources disagree:
  - **Dictionary of Canadian Biography:** born **c.1680**, probably near Westfield, Massachusetts, originally **Mahican**, adopted into the Mohawk **Wolf clan**.
  - **Wikipedia (current scholarship):** born **c.1691**, baptized 1692 by Dutch Reformed minister Godfridius Dellius; **Bear clan**, based at **Canajoharie (Upper Castle)**.
  *Author guidance:* use **"c.1692–1755"** and **"Mohawk leader"**; if origin is mentioned, hedge ("born around 1692"); do NOT assert clan without flagging the dispute. He **died September 8, 1755 at the Battle of Lake George** (bayonetted after his horse was shot).
  *Sources:* DCB; Wikipedia — Hendrick Theyanoguin.

### What Hendrick said — VERBATIM (real quotes; speaker = Theyanoguin/Hendrick, Albany, 1754)
> Provenance note: these quotes survive via the printed congress proceedings and contemporary records (reproduced by GMU's *History Matters* primary-source archive and O'Callaghan's *Documents Relative to the Colonial History of the State of New-York*, vol. 6). 18th-century transcription of Iroquois oratory is mediated by interpreters/clerks — present as "as recorded," not as a verbatim recording.

- The fallen Covenant Chain / "thrown behind your backs" (the famous staged gesture):
  > "the reason is, your neglecting us these three years past; *(then taking a stick and throwing it behind his back)* You have thus thrown us behind your backs, and disregarded us; whereas the French are a subtle and vigilant people, ever using their utmost endeavours to seduce and bring our people over to them."
- The "you are like women" rebuke of British defenselessness:
  > "Look at the French, they are men; they are fortifying every where; but, we are ashamed to say it, you are like women, bare and open, without any fortifications."
- The lost chance at Crown Point / colonial passivity:
  > "'Tis your fault, Brethren, that we are not strengthened by conquest, for we would have gone and taken Crown Point, but you hindered us… Instead of this you burnt your own fort at Saraghtoga, and ran away from it; which was a shame and a scandal to you."
- The opening reproach (no treaty in three years):
  > "Brethren, this is the ancient place of treaty where the fire of friendship always used to burn, and it is now three years since we have been called to any public treaty here."
- The land-quarrel grievance (recorded by William Johnson / proceedings):
  > "The Governor of Virginia and the Governor of Canada are both quarrelling about lands which belong to us, and such a quarrel as this may end in our destruction."
- Accepting the renewed chain (Covenant Chain renewed but kept on Iroquois terms, taken to Onondaga):
  > "We return you all our grateful acknowledgements for renewing and brightening the covenant-chain… We will therefore take it to Onondaga, where our council-fire always burns, and keep it so securely that neither thunder nor lightning shall break it."
  *Sources for all quotes:* [History Matters (GMU) — Hendrick Criticizes the British, 1754](https://historymatters.gmu.edu/d/7804); DCB — Theyanoguin; O'Callaghan, *Documents Relative to the Colonial History of the State of New-York*, vol. 6 (via [Early Treaties / UNL](https://treatiesportal.unl.edu/earlytreaties/treaty.00005.html)).

### Outcome for the Iroquois — only partly satisfied
- The Haudenosaunee **agreed to renew the Covenant Chain** and to reinstate the Albany Indian Commissioners. But their **core grievances — settler encroachment and land loss — were left unresolved in the colonists' favor.** The alliance was shored up only partially; the Iroquois went home with the chain "brightened" but their land problem worse, not better.
  *Source:* New York Almanack — Albany Congress of 1754.

## 4. THE HUMAN COST — land dispossession AT the congress (do not omit)

The congress was also a venue for fraudulent land transfer — a concrete cost the author should name.

- **Wyoming Valley purchase (Susquehanna/Susquehannah Company).** On/around **July 11, 1754**, agents of Connecticut's **Susquehannah Company** obtained from an Iroquois (chiefly Mohawk) delegation a deed to the **Wyoming Valley** (Susquehanna lands in present-day northeastern Pennsylvania) for about **£2,000** — a deal widely called fraudulent: negotiated secretly, reportedly with liquor and bribes, and selling land the Mohawk did not rightfully control.
  *Source:* [Connecticut History — The Susquehanna Settlers](https://connecticuthistory.org/the-susquehanna-settlers/); [Crucible of War excerpt (Anderson) — erenow](https://erenow.org/ww/crucible-of-war-the-seven-years-war/9.php).
- **The actual occupants were excluded.** The valley's real inhabitants were **Delaware (Lenape)** — already displaced by the fraudulent **Walking Purchase of 1737** — and **Shawnee**. Neither was party to the June–July 1754 conference. Their exclusion from this sale pushed Delaware and Shawnee toward the **French** alliance, with bloody consequences on the Pennsylvania frontier.
  *Source:* Connecticut History; New York Almanack — Albany Congress of 1754.
- **Pennsylvania's parallel grab.** Pennsylvania sent its own Indian diplomat **Conrad Weiser**; he secured a deed to unceded Iroquois lands west of the Susquehanna (extending toward the Ohio / Lake Erie) for a nominal sum and promised further payment — overlapping the Connecticut claim and seeding the later **Pennamite–Yankee Wars** (1769 onward).
  *Source:* Crucible of War excerpt (erenow); Connecticut History.

> Author note: this is the dispossession beat. The Iroquois "negotiated as a power," yes — but the same congress that renewed the chain also stripped land from Native nations (Delaware/Shawnee) who weren't even in the room. Hold both truths.

## 5. FRANKLIN'S PLAN OF UNION — structure and powers

- **Origin & authorship.** Franklin proposed an intercolonial union; the congress voted to take it up; a committee drafted it. **Thomas Hutchinson of Massachusetts helped draft / proposed modifications.** Final plan **adopted by the Congress (unanimously) on July 10, 1754.**
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Albany Congress; [State Dept — Albany Plan](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/albany-plan); [Avalon Project — Albany Plan of Union](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/albany.asp).
- **The executive — President-General:** a single executive **"appointed and supported by the Crown"** (i.e., chosen AND paid by the king). His **assent was required for all acts of the Grand Council** ("the assent of the President-General be requisite to all acts of the Grand Council").
  *Source:* [Avalon Project — Albany Plan of Union](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/albany.asp); [State Dept — Albany Plan](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/albany-plan).
- **The legislature — Grand Council:** members **"chosen by the representatives of the people of the several Colonies met in their respective assemblies"** — i.e., elected by the colonial assemblies, NOT by the Crown. To meet (annually) at **Philadelphia**. **3-year terms.**
  *Source:* Avalon Project.
- **Apportionment by colony (initial seats, total = 48), to be adjusted after 3 years by each colony's financial contribution (range 2–7 per colony):**
  - Massachusetts Bay 7 · Virginia 7 · Pennsylvania 6 · Connecticut 5 · New York 4 · Maryland 4 · North Carolina 4 · South Carolina 4 · New Jersey 3 · New Hampshire 2 · Rhode Island 2.
  *Source:* Avalon Project — Albany Plan of Union.
- **Enumerated powers** of the President-General + Grand Council:
  - **Indian affairs** — "hold or direct all Indian treaties in which the general interest of the Colonies may be concerned; and make peace or declare war with Indian nations"; regulate trade with Native nations.
  - **Western lands** — purchase lands from the Indians (for the Crown) and make/regulate new settlements until the Crown formed them into governments.
  - **Common defense** — raise and pay soldiers, build forts, equip armed vessels.
  - **Finance** — levy general duties, imposts, or taxes to fund the above.
  *Source:* Avalon Project; [Founders Online — Albany Plan of Union, 1754](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-05-02-0104).

> Author note on the three domains the brief names — **common defense, Indian affairs, western lands** — all three are explicitly in the plan's powers. Safe to state.

## 6. "JOIN, OR DIE" — the first American political cartoon

- **What & when:** a woodcut by **Benjamin Franklin**, published in his **Pennsylvania Gazette on May 9, 1754** — **widely regarded as the first American political cartoon** (earliest known pictorial representation of colonial union by an American colonist).
  *Source:* [Wikipedia — Join, or Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join,_or_Die); [National Constitution Center](https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/the-story-behind-the-join-or-die-snake-cartoon).
- **Image:** a snake **severed into eight segments**, each labeled with a colony's initials. **New England is a single segment** ("N.E.," standing for four colonies); the segments run S.C., N.C., V., M., P., N.J., N.Y., N.E. **Delaware and Georgia were omitted.** The motto **"JOIN, or DIE."**
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Join, or Die.
- **The symbolism:** drew on a folk superstition that **a snake cut in pieces could come back to life if the pieces were rejoined before sunset** — a metaphor for the danger of colonial disunion and the need to reunite.
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Join, or Die; National Constitution Center.
- **`[TIMING — DO NOT MISSTATE]`** The cartoon ran **May 9, 1754 — BEFORE the Albany Congress (June 19)** and accompanied Franklin's editorial on the Ohio defeats and **"the present disunited state of the British Colonies."** So it is the *prelude/argument* for union, not a product of the congress. Frame it as Franklin already making the case in print weeks before he carried the bolder plan to Albany — not as something produced AT Albany.
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Join, or Die; National Constitution Center.
- **Afterlife:** repurposed during the Stamp Act crisis (1765–66) and the Revolution as a symbol of resistance to the Crown — a meaning Franklin himself did not intend.
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Join, or Die.

## 7. THE DOUBLE REJECTION — adopted by the Congress, enacted by no one

- **Adopted at Albany** (July 10, 1754) — then submitted to the colonies and to London.
- **The colonial assemblies rejected it** — it took **too much** power away from their local legislatures (too much central/prerogative authority).
- **The Crown / Board of Trade declined it** — it gave the colonies **too much** union, autonomy, and democratic power; London preferred direct military control from home. (The plan was never formally enacted by the Crown.)
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Albany Congress; [State Dept — Albany Plan](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/albany-plan).
- **Franklin's own framing of the symmetry (VERBATIM, Autobiography):**
  > "The Assemblies did not adopt it, as they all thought there was too much prerogative in it; and in England it was judg'd to have too much of the democratic."
  And his reflection that the contrary objections proved it the right balance:
  > "The different and contrary reasons of dislike to my plan make me suspect that it was really the true medium; and I am still of opinion it would have been happy for both sides the water if it had been adopted."
  *Source:* Franklin, *Autobiography*, ch. "Albany Plan of Union" (via [SparkNotes full text](https://www.sparknotes.com/lit/franklinautobio/full-text/albany-plan-of-union/); [Bill of Rights Institute](https://billofrightsinstitute.org/essays/albany-plan-of-union/)). *(Verify exact spelling/punctuation against a clean edition before quoting; wording above matches multiple full-text reproductions.)*

## 8. THE LEGACY — the first rehearsal of union

- **First serious proposal of intercolonial union for a common government.** It failed in 1754 but became the **model/precedent** that resurfaced at the **Stamp Act Congress (1765)** and the **First Continental Congress (1774)** — the union that returned "in anger" ~20 years later.
  *Source:* Wikipedia — Albany Congress; [State Dept — Albany Plan](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1750-1775/albany-plan).
- **Franklin's "might-have-been" (VERBATIM, Autobiography)** — union would have made troops from England unnecessary and removed the later pretext for taxing America:
  > "The colonies, so united, would have been sufficiently strong to have defended themselves; there would then have been no need of troops from England; of course, the subsequent pretence for taxing America, and the bloody contest it occasioned, would have been avoided."
  *Source:* Franklin, *Autobiography* (SparkNotes / Bill of Rights Institute full text, as above). *(Verify against clean edition before quoting.)*

### S1. The 1753 Mohawk break in New York City — the proximate trigger for the congress

- **What happened, and when.** In **1753**, the Mohawk leader **Theyanoguin (Hendrick) led a delegation of 17 Mohawk leaders (Hendrick + 16 others)** who **bypassed Albany and went directly to Governor George Clinton in New York City**. There, before Clinton and the New York provincial council, they declared the **Covenant Chain (the Iroquois–British alliance) BROKEN** — angry at British/Albany-Commissioner neglect and at land fraud — and walked out. The break was the proximate event that pushed London to act.
  *Sources:* [Wikipedia — Covenant Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_Chain); [New York Almanack — Albany's Role in Three Little-Remembered Colonial Wars](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2022/01/albany-colonial-wars-in-northeast/).
- **`[CONTESTED — MONTH]` The "June 1753" month is NOT confirmed in these sources.** Every source dates the break to **1753** only; none gives a month. **Author guidance: write "in 1753," NOT "in June 1753."** (Delegation size of 17 / "Hendrick and 16 others" IS confirmed.)
  *Sources:* Covenant Chain (Wikipedia); New York Almanack (Colonial Wars).
- **`[CRITICAL — IDENTITY]` This is Theyanoguin / Hendrick Peters (c.1692–1755)** — the SAME later Hendrick who spoke at Albany 1754 and died at Lake George 1755 (see §3). **NOT** the earlier Hendrick Tejonihokarawa of the 1710 "Four Mohawk Kings." (Note: in the *Kayaderosseras* records below, the "Hendrick" who signed the 1701–04 deed is the EARLIER man, Tejonihokarawa — do not conflate the deed-signer with the 1753/1754 speaker.)
  *Sources:* Wikipedia — Hendrick Theyanoguin; Wikipedia — Kayaderossera patent.
- **VERBATIM quote (the broken-chain declaration), confirmed.** Hendrick, to Governor George Clinton, 1753:
  > "as soon as we come home we will send up a Belt of Wampum to our Brothers the other 5 Nations to acquaint them the Covenant Chain is broken between you and us. So brother you are not to expect to hear of me any more, and Brother we desire to hear no more of you."
  *Source:* [Wikipedia — Covenant Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_Chain), which attributes the passage to **Howard Zinn, *A People's History of the United States*** (Zinn in turn drawing on the colonial records). **Provenance flag:** this is a mediated 18th-century transcription quoted via a modern secondary work — present it "as recorded," consistent with the §3 provenance note, not as a verbatim audio record.
- **The Canajoharie-surveyor threat (also confirmed, usable as a second beat).** Hendrick warned that if **"any accident happens"** to surveyors working near **Canajoharie** (his own town, the Upper Mohawk Castle), **"we hope you Brother will not expect any satisfaction from us."**
  *Source:* New York Almanack — Colonial Wars.
- **THE CAUSAL LINK — the 1753 break is WHY the 1754 congress happened (confirmed).** When Hendrick's angry speech and threats reached London, **the Board of Trade instructed the colonial governors to meet with the Haudenosaunee and enter into a general treaty** — which became the **Albany Congress of 1754**. Wikipedia states it plainly: **"The Albany Congress was called to help repair the chain."**
  *Sources:* [New York Almanack — Colonial Wars](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2022/01/albany-colonial-wars-in-northeast/); [Wikipedia — Covenant Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_Chain). *(This reinforces §1's Board-of-Trade order with its specific trigger; safe to lead with the 1753 break as the cause.)*

### S2. The concrete land grievance — the Kayaderosseras Patent (RECOMMENDED specific to name)

> **Author recommendation:** use the **Kayaderosseras (Kayaderossera) Patent** as the named, well-documented grievance instead of generic "settlers pushing onto Iroquois land." It is the standard land-fraud story tied to the Mohawk anger of 1753 and was raised again at the 1754 congress.

- **What it was.** A huge land grant on **Mohawk land in the Saratoga region** (most of present-day **Saratoga County**, plus parts of Montgomery, Schenectady, and Fulton counties), **granted by Queen Anne to thirteen colonists**.
  *Source:* [Wikipedia — Kayaderossera patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaderossera_patent).
- **`[CONTESTED — ACREAGE]` Size: ~406,000 acres OR ~800,000 acres.** Wikipedia gives **"406,000 acres (164,000 ha)."** Other accounts (New York Almanack; the Zinn passage quoted via Wikipedia–Covenant Chain) cite **"800,000 acres" of Mohawk land** taken "through intricate swindling." **Author guidance: write "a huge grant — hundreds of thousands of acres" and, if a number is wanted, "around 400,000 acres (some accounts say twice that)," NOT a single hard figure.**
  *Sources:* Wikipedia — Kayaderossera patent (406,000); [Wikipedia — Covenant Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_Chain) / Zinn and [New York Almanack](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2022/01/albany-colonial-wars-in-northeast/) (800,000).
- **`[CONTESTED — GRANT YEAR]`** Wikipedia gives the patent grant as **1701** (license to purchase 1703; deed 1704); other accounts say the patent issued under Queen Anne **1708**. **Author guidance: "granted under Queen Anne in the early 1700s"** rather than a single year.
  *Sources:* Wikipedia — Kayaderossera patent (1701); search corroboration (1708 in some retellings).
- **Why the Mohawk called it fraud.** The Mohawk held that **the sale was never legally made, that the patent described a vastly larger tract than the original deed, and that it would strip them of hunting grounds**. The often-cited detail: the deed was for **"three small farms,"** but the issued patent ballooned the dimensions to roughly **five by fourteen by eight by twenty miles**. A signing irregularity (in 1702 the sachem Cornelius failed to appear and two others signed in his place) became the legal basis for the Mohawk's later contest. **`[CONTESTED]`** The "three small farms vs. miles" detail comes from New York Almanack / saratoganygenweb, **not** from the Kayaderossera Wikipedia article (which does not state it) — attribute it to those accounts.
  *Sources:* [New York Almanack — Kayaderosseras Patent Settlement: A Short History](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2018/07/kayaderosseras-patent-settlement-a-short-history/); Wikipedia — Kayaderossera patent (signing irregularity only).
- **Raised again at the 1754 congress.** At Albany in 1754 the Mohawk explicitly contested **"a large tract of land… called Kayadarosseras [now in Saratoga County] beginning at the Half Moon and so up along the Hudson's River to the third fall,"** and warned **"there are writings for all our lands, so that we shall have none left but the very spot we live upon."**
  *Source:* [New York Almanack — The Albany Congress of 1754](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2022/01/the-albany-congress-of-1754-native-people-colonists-the-monarchy/).
- **`[CONTESTED — RESOLUTION DATE]`** The dispute was settled long after, mediated/paid out under **Sir William Johnson**: Wikipedia says **"settled in 1761,"** other accounts say a **1768 settlement** (a payment to the Mohawk). For this 1753/1754 section the resolution is not load-bearing; if mentioned, hedge ("settled only years later, in the 1760s, under William Johnson").
  *Sources:* Wikipedia — Kayaderossera patent (1761); New York Almanack — Kayaderosseras (1768).

> Author note: the broader frame ("the Dutch at Albany and the Palatine Germans in the Schoharie and Mohawk valleys took more land than they had agreed to buy, bribing chiefs to sign deeds") is also verified (New York Almanack — Colonial Wars) and can stand behind the named Kayaderosseras example.

### S3. The Native attendance figure — replace "roughly 200 Native people present in the town"

- **THE DEFENSIBLE NUMBER: about 150 Iroquois.** Multiple sources agree that **about 150 Iroquois / Haudenosaunee** attended the 1754 Albany Congress (and were present for Lt. Gov. DeLancey's opening address, **June 29, 1754**). Use **"about 150 Iroquois"** as the headline figure.
  *Sources:* [New York Almanack — The Albany Congress of 1754](https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2022/01/the-albany-congress-of-1754-native-people-colonists-the-monarchy/) ("about 150 Iroquois in attendance"); [Wikipedia — Albany Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Congress); American History Central.
- **`[CONTESTED]` Where the "200" came from.** The "~200" is a **contested estimate** of how many Native people were present at the **reading of DeLancey's June 29 opening speech** — "other estimates placed the number closer to 200." So **200 is a high-end estimate for one event, not a verified town-wide headcount.** **Do NOT write "roughly 200 Native people present in the town"** as fact.
  *Sources:* Wikipedia — Albany Congress; American History Central — Albany Congress.
- **`[GAP — DO NOT INVENT]` "Several hundred camped around the fort/town."** This phrasing could **NOT** be confirmed in the sources checked — none of them describes a "several hundred camped" encampment. **Do not assert a "several hundred camped" figure.** The supportable statement is: **"about 150 Iroquois delegates attended; contemporary estimates of the broader crowd at the opening speech ran as high as ~200."**
  *Sources:* New York Almanack; Wikipedia — Albany Congress; American History Central.
- **Recommended replacement sentence (for the author):** *"About 150 Iroquois delegates came to Albany — the congress's true first business was them, not Franklin's plan."* If a higher crowd figure is wanted, hedge it: *"contemporary estimates of the gathering ran as high as roughly 200."* Drop "in the town" / any camped-encampment count.
