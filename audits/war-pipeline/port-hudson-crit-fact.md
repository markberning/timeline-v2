## ❌ MUST-FIX

### MF-1 — Gun count on the bluff is understated ("thirteen" is the wrong year)
**Draft (`the-last-lock`):** "Roughly **thirteen heavy cannon** were dug into the bluff
facing the water." Also the night-run section leans on this same "bluff guns" framing.

**Problem:** "Thirteen" is the **late-October 1862** count, not the figure at the time the
story is set (Farragut's March 1863 run and the May–July 1863 siege). By the 1863 siege the
river batteries were larger.

**Correction:** Use the siege-era figure. Wikipedia: the Confederates had "**over twenty
cannon covering the river arranged in eleven batteries**." Warfare History Network: "**The
21 heavy guns** placed there could methodically rake passing ships." Recommend wording like
"more than twenty heavy guns, in about a dozen batteries" or "some twenty heavy cannon."
Do **not** keep "thirteen."
**Sources:** [WIKI] en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Port_Hudson ("over twenty cannon …
eleven batteries"); Warfare History Network, *Siege of Port Hudson: Last Bastion on the
Mississippi* ("21 heavy guns"). The factpack's own §2 cite of "~13" traces to the Oct-1862
buildup and should be corrected upstream too.

## ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX

### SF-1 — Farragut "could barely make headway / fell back one by one" vs. the cleaner record
**Draft (`the-night-run`):** broadly accurate, but two small precision points:
- The draft says *Hartford* + *Albatross* "fought all the way through." Wikipedia notes the
  pair actually **ran aground** briefly on the west bank during the passage before getting
  clear ("had passed the last Confederate gun position by 12:15 am"). The draft's "fought
  all the way through" is not wrong in spirit but smooths over the grounding. Optional to
  add; not a factual error.
- *Mississippi*'s end: draft says she "burned through the night until her fire reached her
  magazine." Confirmed — she drifted, burned, and **exploded ~5:05 a.m.** Fine as written.

**Night-run casualties** "~78 killed/missing, ~35 wounded" — **CONFIRMED verbatim** by
Wikipedia ("seventy-eight killed or missing and thirty-five wounded"). No fix; clearing the
[VERIFY].

### SF-2 — Cailloux death: shell struck him in the HEAD (optional added precision)
**Draft:** "an artillery shell struck him and killed him." **CONFIRMED and accurate.** ABT
bio is more specific: "pieces of an artillery shell struck him **in the head**, killing him
instantly." Left arm shattered + sword in right hand = **CONFIRMED** (ABT: "His left arm was
broken, but he gestured forward with his sword in his right hand"). No fix required; the
"in the head" detail is optional.

### SF-3 — "before dawn … around 3:30 in the morning" (June 14) — confirm retained as approx.
Fact pack §4 gives ~3:30 a.m.; not independently re-found in the top sources this pass, but
consistent with the pre-dawn framing. Low risk. Keep the hedge "around."

## ✅ VERIFIED — the brief's required checks all PASS

**Casualty divergence correctly hedged, NOT collapsed.** ✅
- Battle/combat tolls kept separate: May 27 ≈ **293 KIA / 1,545 wounded / 157 missing
  (~1,995)** vs **~235 CS**; June 14 ≈ **1,792 Union** vs **47 CS**; "~3,800 down in two
  charges." All **CONFIRMED verbatim** (Wikipedia infobox + ABT).
- Disease/heat toll reported **separately** as "~4,000–5,000 Union over the campaign."
  CONFIRMED (Wikipedia "~5,000 died of disease"; NPS-CWSAC/ABT "~4,000 fell prey to disease
  or sunstroke"). The draft correctly does **not** assert a single hard grand total and does
  not fold disease into the battle count — exactly the required handling. No false grand
  total present.

**"One of the first major Black-troop combat actions" — NOT absolute "first."** ✅
The draft says "**one of the first major combat actions by Black soldiers**" and explicitly
places Milliken's Bend (~two weeks later, June 1863) and the 54th Mass/Fort Wagner (July
1863) as later. This is the correct hedge; NPS-TWHP's unqualified "first" is avoided.
Wikipedia itself uses hedged language ("did much to dissipate the belief that black troops
were unreliable under fire"). PASS.

**Cailloux details.** ✅ All CONFIRMED:
- Killed leading the May 27 charge — ✅ (ABT/WIKI).
- Body left **~47 days**, identified by a ring — ✅ ("47 days until Port Hudson surrendered
  July 9"; "identified by a ring … recognized by surviving members").
- **July 29, 1863** funeral, thousands, Father Claude Paschal Maistre — ✅.
- Born enslaved 1825 Plaquemines Parish; freed 1846; cigar maker; **equal pay $60/mo**;
  bilingual English/French orders; among first African American officers — ✅ all confirmed
  (ABT bio: "$60 per month, equal pay with white officers of the same rank"; "one of 19
  Black officers commissioned"). Rank **Captain, Co. E, 1st La. Native Guard** — ✅.
- NOTE: the church official who interdicted Maistre was **Archbishop Jean-Marie Odin**
  (Archbishop of New Orleans), a Confederate sympathizer. The draft's phrase
  "**Confederate-sympathizing archbishop**" is therefore **CORRECT** (not "bishop").

**Surrender July 9, 1863 (after Vicksburg July 4).** ✅ CONFIRMED. Gardner surrendered
**July 9, 1863**; Vicksburg fell to Grant **July 4, 1863** (one web-search *summary* misstated
"Vicksburg fell July 9" — that is a search-snippet error; the primary sources and the draft
both correctly use **July 4** for Vicksburg / **July 9** for Port Hudson). Surrender numbers:
"**~6,340 surrendered / ~5,935 paroled / 405 officers held**" — CONFIRMED (Wikipedia: "5,935
men and civilian employees … officially paroled"; 6,340 surrendered).

**Farragut's March 14, 1863 night run.** ✅ CONFIRMED: seven vessels; *Mississippi* burned
(ran aground, set ablaze, exploded); only **Hartford + Albatross** got past upstream;
*Richmond* and others fell back. Date, ship names, and outcome all correct.

**Shaky quotes rendered indirectly.** ✅ All four flagged quotes are paraphrased, not quoted:
- Gardner's refusal — actual text is "My duty requires me to defend this position, and
  therefore I decline to surrender" (Wikipedia). Draft paraphrases. ✅
- Confederate starvation diary — paraphrased. ✅
- Capt. Fitts (114th NY) June 14 description — paraphrased. ✅
- NYT editorial (June 11, 1863) — actual: "It is no longer possible to doubt the bravery and
  steadiness of the colored race, when rightly led." Draft paraphrases faithfully. ✅
- Banks's report — actual: "leaves upon my mind no doubt of their ultimate success." Draft
  paraphrases ("left him no doubt of their fitness") — faithful to meaning. ✅
- "Blackest man in New Orleans" and Lincoln "Father of Waters" correctly **omitted/deferred**. ✅

**Other figures confirmed:** 80-ft bluff ✅; ~150-degree hairpin bend ✅; Fort Desperate /
Priest Cap / Citadel ✅; garrison ~6,800–7,500 ✅; ~30,000 Union ✅; Gardner a NY native who
married into Louisiana ✅; Paine lost a leg June 14 ✅; "longest siege in American military
history, 48 days" ✅ (Wikipedia: "the longest siege in US military history" — the draft's
"frequently cited as" hedge is appropriate); ~200,000 Black men served the Union by war's
end ✅; commanders Weitzel/Grover/Augur/(Thomas W.) Sherman/Dwight all ✅ as Brig. Gens.
