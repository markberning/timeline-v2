## Summary lists

### MUST-FIX (12 items)

1. **"Union" undefined** — "the great Union army" appears before the reader knows Union = the United States fighting to preserve the country. Add a one-clause gloss at first use. Suggested: "...the great Union army (the United States' fighting force)..."

2. **"the Virginia Peninsula" defined too late** — Para 1 uses it bare; definition arrives in para 2. Move inline definition to first use in para 1: "the Virginia Peninsula (the finger of land between the York and James rivers)."

3. **"Army of the Potomac" never glossed** — Introduce it as: "his army, the Army of the Potomac — the Union's main eastern fighting force."

4. **"rearguard" undefined** — "the Union rearguard had backed up onto Malvern Hill." Add: "the rearguard (the troops assigned to cover the retreat while the rest of the army escaped)."

5. **"battery" defined at second use, not first** — First use: "Hunt's massed batteries." Definition appears in the next clause. Swap order so "batteries (clusters of roughly six guns that worked and moved together)" appears at first use.

6. **"converging fire" undefined** — "have them throw converging fire onto Hunt's guns." Add: "converging fire (cannon aimed from multiple directions at a single point, concentrating the damage)."

7. **"flank" defined at second use** — "(the exposed sides of the army's line)" appears after the word "flanks" is used twice. Move paren gloss to first use of "flank" / "flanks."

8. **D.H. Hill — no role/scene-set** — "one of Lee's division commanders" is thin. Add: "one of Lee's division commanders (a division being a fighting force of roughly eight thousand men — the largest unit a single general typically led into battle)."

9. **"brigade" undefined** — Used at least eight times, never defined. Add at first use (the "fifteen brigades" line): "fifteen brigades (each roughly 1,500–2,500 soldiers, the standard attack unit)."

10. **"tactical victory" undefined** — The entire closing argument turns on tactical vs. strategic. Add: "a tactical victory — winning the actual fight on the ground — even as McClellan lost the strategic prize, the campaign goal."

11. **Thomas J. Jackson — no role/scene-set** — First mention has a side tag but no identity. Add: "Maj. Gen. Thomas J. Jackson (South), Lee's most aggressive corps commander."

12. **North/South shorthand never decoded for the reader** — Before or during the first use of "(South)" / "(North)" tags, add one sentence establishing: "In what follows, North = the Union (the United States) and South = the Confederacy (the breakaway slaveholding republic)."

13. **John Pope — missing side tag** — "a new Union army under Maj. Gen. John Pope" — add (North).

### SHOULD-FIX (10 items)

1. **"Maj. Gen." vs "Gen." rank distinction** — Brief note on rank hierarchy not required but helpful.

2. **Battle name-pile** — "Mechanicsville, Gaines's Mill, Savage's Station, Glendale" — add "(all Confederate victories that week)" or similar.

3. **"mass" / "massed guns" mechanism** — Already partially explained by context but a one-clause clarification of "concentrated so their fire overlapped" would help.

4. **"Artillery Reserve" as proper name** — Add paren gloss on first use: "the Artillery Reserve (a dedicated pool of fresh gun batteries held back to replace those that ran low on ammunition)."

5. **"Magruder" — no role/scene-set** — "John B. Magruder's (South) arrival" — add "one of Lee's generals tasked with leading the assault."

6. **Brigade vs. division hierarchy** — When both terms appear in the same sentence ("brigades... some divisions"), add a note: "divisions, each made up of multiple brigades."

7. **"V Corps" naming** — Add: "V Corps (the large army sub-unit Porter commanded, roughly 15,000 men)."

8. **"Second Bull Run" undefined** — Add: "Second Bull Run (a major battle in northern Virginia that August, which Lee also won)."

9. **"the Peninsula Campaign" as proper name** — Introduce the official name when it first appears as a title, not just as "the campaign."

10. **Gunboat military logic** — Add a clause explaining why retreating to gunboats was tactically sound: "the navy's cannon could pound any Confederate pursuit from the river, protecting the army's rear."

---

## Worst offender

The military unit hierarchy problem (brigade / division / corps / army, none defined, all used) is the single most disorienting gap for a newcomer — the reader encounters these terms in the middle of the key causality argument (why the battle was so one-sided) without any handle on scale. Combined with the fact that "Union" itself goes undefined until the reader is asked to care about "Union losses," it makes the opening sections harder to follow than they need to be.
