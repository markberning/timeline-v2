### Casualties
- **Union ≈ 2,300** — VERIFIED (189 K / 1,033 W / 1,104 M = 2,326).
- **Confederate ~6,000–7,000; ~6,250 most-cited; ~1,750 killed; Hood reported lower** —

  6,252 (1,750 K / 3,800 W / 702 M); Hood's report ~4,500. The draft's "lost roughly

- **~55 regimental commanders** — VERIFIED (WIKI/ABT).

### The six generals (SPECIAL SCRUTINY) — **VERIFIED, exact.**
Five killed in action: **Cleburne, John Adams, States Rights Gist, Hiram Granbury,
Otho Strahl.** One mortally wounded: **John C. Carter** — draft says "died ten days
later." **VERIFIED: Carter died December 10, 1864** (Nov 30 → Dec 10 = ten days). Correct.
The "five killed + one mortal" framing is exactly the WIKI/ABT framing.

### "~fourteen generals went down" (SPECIAL SCRUTINY) — VERIFIED.
WIKI/ABT: **fourteen Confederate generals were casualties (6 killed/mortal, 7 wounded,
1 captured).** Draft hedges as "somewhere around fourteen … plus roughly a half-dozen
more wounded and one captured." The "half-dozen more wounded" = 7 (draft slightly
under-counts in the gloss but the headline "around fourteen" is right and the hedge
protects it). FAIR.

### General-death comparison — VERIFIED.
Draft: Franklin killed generals faster than "Gettysburg, Antietam, Chickamauga, or
Spotsylvania." ABT "10 Facts": Franklin 6 vs. Gettysburg 5, Antietam 3, Chickamauga 3,
Spotsylvania 2 — Franklin was the deadliest single engagement for general officers.
VERIFIED. (Antietam separately had "six generals killed" across the day per NPS, but
that conflates KIA+MW across both armies; ABT's apples-to-apples Confederate-general
comparison stands behind the draft's claim.)

## B. The Cleburne emancipation proposal (SPECIAL SCRUTINY) — VERIFIED.

- **Date Jan 2, 1864, Dalton GA, to Army of Tennessee commanders** — VERIFIED (ABT
  primary source / BOFT / Georgia Historical Society). Draft says "in January 1864" and
  "circulated a written proposal to the Army of Tennessee's commanders" — accurate,
  does not over-specify.
- **Arm enslaved men + guarantee freedom to those who fight** — VERIFIED. Exact proposal
  text: "guarantee freedom within a reasonable time to every slave in the South who
  shall remain true to the Confederacy." Draft paraphrases ("arm enslaved men as
  soldiers and promise freedom to those who fought for the South") — accurate paraphrase,
  not falsely quoted.
- **"in a military point of view, one of our chief sources of weakness"** — VERIFIED
  verbatim against ABT primary source. Draft renders it as reported indirect speech
  ("calling it, in a military point of view, one of the South's chief sources of
  weakness") — faithful; the load-bearing phrase matches the original.
- **Gist called it "monstrous"** — VERIFIED. BOFT (the draft's own [BOFT] source) and the
  NPS Chickamauga page both attribute "monstrous" to Brig. Gen. States Rights Gist
  specifically. The draft's hedge "reportedly called it monstrous" is appropriately
  cautious. VERIFIED.
- **Davis ordered the matter suppressed and copies destroyed** — VERIFIED (ABT/BOFT/
  multiple). Stated as fact in draft; supported.
- **"sent to die … without ever being promoted past division command"** — the literal
  fact (Cleburne died a major general / division commander, never promoted to corps) is
  TRUE. The *causal* implication (suppression cost him promotion) is what historians
  debate; BOFT itself calls it "speculative." The draft is careful here — it asserts the
  factual non-promotion, not the causal claim ("bury the man, professionally" is framed
  as the leadership's choice, hedged). ACCEPTABLE; see framing crit for the interpretive note.

## C. Tod Carter / Carter House (SPECIAL SCRUTINY) — VERIFIED.

- Capt. Theodrick "Tod" Carter, Confederate officer, son of the Carter family; captured
  1863 (Chattanooga), escaped, returned; Franklin his first time home in ~3 years —
  VERIFIED (TOD/ABT).
- Rode forward though duties didn't require it; "almost home" tradition — draft renders
  "by tradition, calling out that he was almost home," NOT as a verbatim quote. Correct
  handling (the verbatim "Follow me, boys, I'm almost home!" is traditional/unconfirmed).
- Shot "a few hundred yards" from his front yard — VERIFIED (sources say ~500 ft);
  draft does not print the precise figure. Good.
- **Found on the field, carried into the house, died "two days later"** — VERIFIED. Tod
  Carter was mortally wounded Nov 30 and **died December 2, 1864** in the Carter House.
  Nov 30 → Dec 2 = two days. Correct.
- Carter family sheltered in the cellar during the battle — VERIFIED (BOFT/Carter House
  site). ~24–28 people in the basement north room for ~8 hours. Draft's "sheltering in
  the cellar" is accurate. (Note for framing crit: the shelterers included the Carters'
  *enslaved* people — an omission, not an error.)
- Carter outbuildings still carry hundreds of bullet holes, among the most battle-scarred
  structures of the war — VERIFIED (WIKI/ABT/BOFT).

## F. ERRORS FOUND

**MUST-FIX 1 — "Ten months before he was killed, in January 1864."** §4 says Cleburne's
proposal came "Ten months before he was killed." The proposal is **January 2, 1864**;
he died **November 30, 1864** — that is **~11 months**, not ten. Fix to "Almost a year
before" or "eleven months before" (or simply "earlier that same year"). MUST-FIX (a
checkable arithmetic error).

**SHOULD-FIX 1 — "Forty-four enslaved people":** sources publish "over forty." Either
keep the pack's 44 or align to "more than forty." Low risk.

**SHOULD-FIX 2 — general-casualty gloss undercounts.** "roughly a half-dozen more
wounded and one captured … somewhere around fourteen." Wounded = 7, not ~6; the headline
"fourteen" is right. Tighten the gloss or drop the "half-dozen." Cosmetic.

**SHOULD-FIX 3 — "without ever being promoted past division command."** Factually true,
but it sits one clause away from implying the suppression *caused* the non-promotion,
which BOFT itself calls speculative. The draft's framing is defensible, but consider a
one-word hedge to keep the causal claim out of it. (Cross-ref framing crit.)

No other numeric, date, name, place, or sequence errors found. The casualty range, the
six-generals list, the Carter timeline, and the Cleburne-proposal facts all check out.
