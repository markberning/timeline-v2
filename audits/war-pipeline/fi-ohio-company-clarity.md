## Term-by-term

**CLEAR (well-handled, do not touch):**
- speculators — glossed ("the polite name for people who buy land cheap...")
- quitrent — glossed ("the small annual feudal rent colonists owed the Crown")
- bastion — glossed ("a projecting corner platform for guns")
- land cessions — glossed ("formal handovers of land")
- the Forks — defined ("where the Allegheny and the Monongahela join to form the Ohio")
- Iroquois Confederacy / Haudenosaunee / Six Nations — glossed and cross-referenced
- Beaver Wars — glossed in place
- Shawnee / Delaware (Lenape) / Mingo / Wyandot — each scene-set with who/where/why
- commandant — never glossed but context makes it unambiguous (= fort's commanding officer); not jargon to flag
- syndicate — effectively unpacked by the surrounding prose ("a group of Virginia gentlemen organized themselves")

**NEEDS-GLOSS (fix; none ship-blocking):**

1. **"adjutant"** (line 93: "a major and adjutant in the Virginia militia"). Genuine military jargon; a 15-year-old will not know it means a staff/administrative officer. "Major" carries the meaning, so the simplest fix is a 3-word gloss or dropping "adjutant."

2. **"1748/49" double-year notation** (lines 33, 35: "the 16th of March 1748/49"). This is the single most confusing thing in the draft for a cold reader — it looks like a typo or a range, and the old-style/new-style calendar reason is never explained. Either add a one-clause gloss the first time ("1748/49 — the old calendar's way of writing the new-year overlap") or just normalize to "March 1749." Right now a sharp kid stops and puzzles over it.

3. **"adjutant"** aside, **"Privy Council"** (lines 35, 41). British government body, unglossed. The context ("worked the paperwork through and the grant issued") tells you roughly what it does, so this is mild — a 2–3 word gloss ("the king's governing council in London") would close it.

4. **"tidewater" / "tidewater planter"** (lines 31, 39, 41: "a tidewater planter in coastal Virginia," "a who's who of tidewater Virginia"). The first use at line 15 ("a tidewater planter in coastal Virginia") actually does the glossing work in passing, so by line 39 it's mostly fine. Borderline — a one-time clarification that tidewater = the wealthy coastal-Virginia plantation country would help, but it's not a breaker.

5. **"watershed"** (lines 63, 73: "the entire watershed," "claim the whole Ohio watershed"). Geography term for all the land drained by the river. Inferable from "the river and all the land on both sides of it as far as the sources" right next to it (line 73), so it's self-glossing by proximity — leave or add three words.

**Borderline, judged CLEAR (no action — flagging so the reconciler sees they were checked, not missed):**
- "garrison" — context "build a fort, and pay to keep a garrison in it" makes function clear enough.
- "ensign" (line 145), "major general" (line 147) — military ranks; inferable, and Braddock is explicitly deferred to "another story."
- "Lieutenant Governor" — function is obvious from use.
