## SPECIFIC CHECKS REQUESTED — STATUS

### 1. Union strength ENGAGED ~5,000–6,500 (NOT ~22,500 departmental roll)
**CONFIRMED ✅**

Draft handles this correctly and explicitly: "Nathaniel Banks (North) had perhaps 5,000 to 6,500 exhausted soldiers… His command on paper was far larger — figures as high as 22,500 get thrown around — but that was the entire sprawling department he was responsible for, not the worn-down force actually present for this fight."

Independent verification:
- NPS: "approximately 5,000 men"
- Wikipedia: "6,500 engaged"
- ABT infobox: "22,500" (confirmed to be departmental figure, not battle strength — the draft's dismissal of it is correct)
- Encyclopedia Virginia: "3,500-man command" (lowest estimate; draft's floor of 5,000 is conservative and defensible)

The 5,000–6,500 framing with an explicit disclaimer about the 22,500 is accurate and appropriately handled. **No issue.**

---

### 2. Casualties: Union ~2,000 incl. many captured; Confederate ~400
**CONFIRMED ✅**

Draft states: "of Banks's roughly 2,019 casualties for the day, 1,714 — about 85 percent — were captured or missing" and "Jackson's whole army lost only about 400 men, killed and wounded."

Independent verification (Wikipedia, ABT, NPS — all in exact agreement):
- Union: 62 killed / 243 wounded / 1,714 missing-captured = **2,019 total**
- Confederate: 68 killed / 329 wounded = **397** (Wikipedia/NPS) or **400** (ABT)

Draft's "roughly 2,019" and "about 400" are accurate. The 85% captured/missing fraction is correct (1,714/2,019 = 84.9%). The "~5× lopsided" ratio is confirmed. **No issue.**

Note: The NPS article totals may include some Front Royal casualties in its 2,028 figure (May 23–25 cumulative); this is a minor discrepancy immaterial to the draft.

### 3. Taylor's flank attack on Bowers Hill
**CONFIRMED ✅ with minor SHOULD-FIX on Douglas quote wording**

Draft: "Taylor swung his Louisianans wide to the west, out around the end of the Union line, until they were positioned to come up the slope of Bowers Hill and roll up the Union right from the side… around 7:00 to 7:30 a.m."

Independent verification:
- ABT, NPS, Shenandoah at War, Wikipedia all confirm: Taylor's Louisiana Brigade (~2,000 men) flanked the Union right on Bowers Hill at approximately 7:30 a.m., moving west under fog cover, breaking the 27th Indiana, 29th Pennsylvania, and forcing the 2nd Massachusetts back. Gordon's brigade collapsed. Timing 7:00–7:30 a.m. confirmed across sources.
- "I will send you up Taylor" (to Winder): sourced to McHenry Howard (aide-de-camp to Winder) per the Shenandoah Civil War History blog and search results. Draft wording matches. **Confirmed.**
- Taylor "in a steady walk": confirmed across multiple sources.
- Federal artillery found them as they moved into position: confirmed (ABT).

**SHOULD-FIX — Douglas quote wording:**
Draft uses: *"a more beautiful charge… a line of glistening bayonets bright in that morning sun, its formation straight and compact."*
The Shenandoah Civil War History blog (the primary secondary source for this quote) renders Douglas as: *"I have rarely seen a more beautiful charge."* The Civil War Traveler blog does not reproduce the exact quote. The exact phrasing in *I Rode with Stonewall* could not be directly verified against the primary text in this gate pass; the blog source's wording differs slightly from the draft's rendering. This is a low-risk discrepancy (likely the same sentence, blog may have lightly paraphrased), but the exact words in the primary text should be confirmed before the section ships. **Attribution to Douglas / *I Rode with Stonewall* is correct.**

### 4. "Townspeople fired on retreating Union troops" framed as reported / "by some accounts"
**CONFIRMED ✅ — hedging is appropriate and well-calibrated**

Draft: "By some accounts, civilians also fired on the retreating Union soldiers from windows and doorways; this is reported rather than firmly established, and the better-documented scene is the cheering crowd, not the shooting."

Independent verification:
- NPS: "Winchester's residents threw objects at the retreating Federal soldiers, even fired weapons, killing several and wounding others" — stated without a hedge in the NPS article.
- Shenandoah at War: "angry civilians **reportedly** shot at the Union soldiers" — uses "reportedly."
- americanhistorycentral.com and multiple secondary sources corroborate the claim with language like "reportedly fired on."
- The claim is present across enough serious sources that it is more than a rumor, but the primary-source basis remains soldier accounts (Union-side) and Confederate accounts, both potentially biased.

**Assessment:** The draft's hedge ("by some accounts… reported rather than firmly established") is actually slightly more cautious than at least one authoritative source (NPS states it more bluntly). The hedged framing is defensible and appropriate given that the NPS and Shenandoah at War do not agree on confidence level. No change required; the draft handles this correctly.

### 5. Jackson's "Now let's holler" is NOT used
**CONFIRMED ✅**

Draft omits this quote entirely and the fact ledger explicitly notes it was omitted as FLAG (c) — "unverified." This is the correct call. Independent verification: the Shenandoah Civil War History blog reports the quote but notes no original primary source; NPS article does NOT include it; it does not appear in Wikipedia or ABT. The quote is likely apocryphal or unverifiable secondary attribution. **Correctly omitted.**

### 6. Lincoln's May 24 McDowell suspension: framed as ALREADY BEGUN (post–Front Royal) and DEEPENED by Winchester, not triggered by it
**CONFIRMED ✅**

Draft (Section 1, "Meanwhile in Washington"): "On May 24, the day after Front Royal, Lincoln made a fateful decision: he telegraphed McClellan that 'in consequence of General Banks's critical position I have been compelled to suspend General McDowell's movements to you.'" — explicitly placed before Winchester.

Draft (Section 3, "commissary-banks"): "Recall that on May 24, in response to the disaster at Front Royal, Lincoln had telegraphed McClellan… Winchester did not start that decision; it deepened it and seemed to confirm it."

Independent verification:
- Wikipedia (Valley Campaign): telegram dated **May 24**, at 4 p.m., triggered by Front Royal news. Confirmed.
- americanhistorycentral.com: telegram May 24 confirmed.
- All sources agree: the suspension preceded Winchester (which was May 25); Winchester deepened the reaction.

The draft's timeline is accurate and the causal framing ("already begun before Winchester… deepened it") is correct. **No issue.**

⚠️ **Note on Encyclopedia Virginia claim:** The WebFetch of encyclopediavirginia.org returned a claim that "Lincoln diverted McDowell's 40,000-man command from Fredericksburg to the Valley **after Winchester**, not Front Royal." This appears to be an imprecise summary of the redirecting of troops (post-Winchester) conflating with the initial suspension (pre-Winchester). The Wikipedia Valley Campaign article and multiple primary-source–adjacent sources confirm the suspension telegram was May 24 (pre-Winchester). The draft's framing is correct.

### 7. Captured supplies / "Commissary Banks" detail
**CONFIRMED ✅**

Draft: "half a million rounds of ammunition, more than 9,300 small arms (muskets and pistols), tens of thousands of pounds of food including some 15,000 pounds of bacon, over a hundred head of cattle, and wagonloads of clothing and blankets."

Independent verification:
- ABT / Shenandoah at War: 500,000 rounds + 15,000 lbs bacon + 100+ cattle confirmed.
- NPS / americanhistorycentral.com: 9,300 small arms + 34,000 lbs commissary stores + 500,000 rounds confirmed.
- civilwarmonths.com / civilwarbummer.com: 9,354 small arms, 500,000 rounds, 103 cattle, ~34,000 lbs stores confirmed.

The draft's inventory is consistent with the composite record across sources. Note: some sources separate "15,000 lbs bacon" from the "34,000 lbs commissary stores" total — draft's "tens of thousands of pounds of food including some 15,000 pounds of bacon" is accurate. **No issue.**

### 8. ~50,000–60,000 troops tied down
**CONFIRMED ✅ with SHOULD-FIX on high-end figure**

Draft: "roughly 17,000 men, Jackson had tied down elements of three separate Union armies totaling more than 50,000 — by some counts over 60,000 — soldiers."

Independent verification:
- warfarehistorynetwork.com: "tied up 50,000 Federals" (this gate pass retrieved "50,000" not the 60,000 the fact pack attributed to this source — see note below).
- Wikipedia (Valley Campaign): "52,000 opposing Union forces" over the full campaign.
- Multiple secondary sources (civilwarbummer.com context, others): "more than 60,000" and even "70,000" appear in some accounts.

**SHOULD-FIX — warfarehistorynetwork.com figure:** The fact pack (§7, §10) attributed "60,000+" to warfarehistorynetwork.com, but the gate-pass WebFetch of that article retrieved "50,000 Federals." Either the article was updated since the pack was written, or the pack's note was from a different passage. The draft uses "more than 50,000" as the conservative floor, which matches what the source actually says in the gate pass. The "by some counts over 60,000" high-end claim is supported by other secondary sources. The framing is defensible, but the specific sourcing note in the fact pack (attributing 60,000 to warfarehistorynetwork.com) should be corrected in the pack. **The draft itself is fine; the fact pack has a minor citation note error.**

## SHOULD-FIX ITEMS

### SF-1 — Douglas quote exact wording (low-risk)
**Claim in draft:** Henry Kyd Douglas wrote *"a more beautiful charge… a line of glistening bayonets bright in that morning sun, its formation straight and compact."*
**Issue:** The Shenandoah Civil War History blog (the source closest to the primary text in this gate pass) renders the Douglas quote as *"I have rarely seen a more beautiful charge."* The exact wording in *I Rode with Stonewall* could not be verified against the primary text. The draft's version may be a paraphrase or a composite.
**Recommendation:** Before final ship, pull the exact passage from Douglas's *I Rode with Stonewall* (full text available at archive.org/details/irodewonewa00doug or Project Gutenberg). If the primary text reads differently, update the quotation and use quotation marks only for the exact words. Attribution to Douglas is confirmed correct.

### SF-2 — Fact pack citation error (pack only, not draft)
**Issue:** The fact pack (§7, §10) attributes the "60,000+ troops tied down" figure specifically to warfarehistorynetwork.com. The gate-pass WebFetch of that article retrieved "50,000 Federals," not 60,000. The pack's source attribution appears to be inaccurate.
**Recommendation:** The draft handles this correctly ("more than 50,000 — by some counts over 60,000"), with the 60,000+ supported by other secondary sources. No change needed in the draft. The fact pack source note should be corrected.

### SF-3 — Sergeant quote attribution (low-risk)
**Claim:** "the grandest I saw during the war… every man was in his proper place" attributed to an unnamed Confederate sergeant.
**Issue:** The civilwartraveler.blog attributes this quote to Sgt. John H. Worsham, which is more specific than the draft's "one Confederate sergeant." The source is corroborated.
**Recommendation:** Consider adding the name (Sgt. John H. Worsham) to the attribution for verifiability. This is optional — "one Confederate sergeant remembered" is not wrong, just less specific.
