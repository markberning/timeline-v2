## UNEXPLAINED LEAPS / CONTEXT GAPS

These are places where the 15-year-old reader is expected to know something the draft never told them.

### LEAP 1 — Who is McDowell, and why does freezing his corps matter so much?
**Location:** §valley last para ("keep a second, larger Union corps under Maj. Gen. Irvin McDowell, waiting near Fredericksburg, too nervous to march south")
**Problem:** McDowell gets a name and a location (Fredericksburg) but zero context. Why is he near Fredericksburg? What is his relationship to McClellan? Why does his march "south" matter? The answer — McDowell's corps was promised as a reinforcement to McClellan's army driving on Richmond, and its absence is a strategic knife in McClellan's side — is implicit but never stated on first introduction. The payoff ("McDowell's corps…never came south") lands well in Section 3, but the setup is thin here.
**Verdict:** MUST-FIX — one sentence bridging McDowell → Richmond reinforcement at first mention.

### LEAP 2 — Why does losing Bowers Hill collapse the entire Union line?
**Location:** §the-rout §Taylor: "once the right flank went, the whole Union line went with it"
**Problem:** The mechanism is never explained. A 15-year-old doesn't know that a battle line is like a fence — pull out a post at the end and the whole fence falls. The draft defines "flank" as "the exposed end of a battle line, the place every general most fears having turned" but doesn't say *why* turning the flank is so decisive (attackers can shoot down the length of the line; defenders must turn to face them and expose their backs to the original threat; the line physically cannot hold two directions at once).
**Verdict:** SHOULD-FIX — one sentence on why a turned flank is fatal.

### LEAP 3 — "the Valley" used before it is named
**Location:** §1 para 1: "a long green valley that pointed, like a loaded rifle, straight at Washington, D.C." Then para 2: "Jackson would run one of the most studied campaigns in military history through that valley." Then the section header: "The granary aimed at Washington." Then §valley para 1: "The ground Jackson was defending is called the Shenandoah Valley…"
**Problem:** The draft uses "the Valley" (as a proper-noun-esque concept) three times before naming it. The phrase "the Valley Campaign" is the article's own title. A reader encounters the concept cold, with no anchor, for two full paragraphs.
**Verdict:** MUST-FIX — name the Shenandoah Valley at first mention in para 1.

### LEAP 4 — "the peninsula" unnamed
**Location:** §1 para 1: "A huge United States army…had landed on the Virginia coast and was grinding its way up the peninsula toward Richmond"
**Problem:** Which peninsula? The Virginia Peninsula (between the York and James rivers) is historically specific and is never identified. The word "peninsula" is used as if the reader already knows the geography.
**Verdict:** MUST-FIX — add "the Virginia Peninsula" or a brief geographic tag.

### LEAP 5 — How Jackson's Valley success → Lee at Richmond
**Location:** §commissary-banks final paragraphs
**Problem:** The causal chain (Jackson ties down Union troops → McDowell's corps stays frozen → Lee fights a weakened McClellan → Richmond saved) is present but the reader may not follow *why* fewer men facing Lee mattered. The draft assumes the reader understands that armies fight in proportion and that 20,000 missing men is decisive. The logic is there but could use one connective sentence.
**Verdict:** SHOULD-FIX — minor; the draft handles it reasonably, but a single "McClellan now faced Lee without the 20,000 men Jackson had kept busy" sentence would close it cleanly.

### LEAP 6 — "Camp Hill" and "Front Royal Pike" unnamed/unexplained
**Location:** §the-rout §Dawn on Bowers Hill: "pushing up the Front Royal Pike toward the Union brigade dug in near Camp Hill on the southeast edge of Winchester"
**Problem:** Camp Hill is named but never explained (it's a geographic feature / neighborhood). Front Royal Pike is named as a proper noun without any gloss — unlike the Valley Turnpike (defined earlier), this road appears without explanation. A teenager may not connect that a "pike" is a road at all.
**Verdict:** SHOULD-FIX — tiny gloss: "the Front Royal Pike (the road leading southeast out of town toward Front Royal)."
