# Fact-Check Report — "Regulars & Provincials" (French and Indian War, theme)

**Checker:** independent web-enabled fact-checker. **Method:** every date, number, name, rank, place, price, and quote in the draft was verified against authoritative sources (Wikipedia article infoboxes, Founders Online, MassMoments, American Yawp / History Matters primary transcripts of the Clough journal, Fred Anderson's *Crucible of War* mirror, militaryheritage.com warrant transcripts, Mount Vernon, Britannica). The draft and the fact pack were NOT trusted as sources.

**VERDICT: FAIL** — two verbatim quotations are misquoted (each is a fabricated specific in a `> quote:` / quoted-prose surface), and one named place is wrong (Clough's hometown). All are correctable without new research.

## ❌ WRONG (must fix)

### 1. Washington verbatim quote — "nor" should be "or"
- **Draft (line 25 prose AND line 27 `> quote:` block):** "...in a Corps, where I once did, **nor** thought I had a right to, command."
- **Correct (authoritative primary source):** "...in a Corps, where I once did, **or** thought I had a right to, command."
- **Source:** Founders Online, *George Washington to William Fitzhugh, 15 November 1754* (https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Washington/02-01-02-0114), corroborated verbatim by mountvernon.org "Why Did George Washington Join the Revolution?".
- This is a misquotation inside a labeled verbatim quote block. The fact pack itself flagged "verify spelling vs Founders Online" — the check fails: "nor" → **"or"**. Fix in BOTH places (line 25 inline and the line-27 quote block). Also note the date is 15 Nov 1754 (a letter to Fitzhugh), which is consistent with the "1754" attribution.

### 2. Clough "no more duty" — misquoted as "any more"
- **Draft (line 80):** as Clough wrote, "all swore that we would not do **any more** duty here."
- **Correct (primary):** "we all swore that we would not do **no more** duty here." (Clough's double negative, 1 Nov 1759.)
- **Source:** MassMoments, *Massachusetts Soldiers Threaten Mutiny* (https://www.massmoments.org/moment-details/massachusetts-soldiers-threaten-mutiny.html), quoting Clough's diary.
- Presented inside quotation marks as Clough's words, so the variant is a misquotation. Fix to "do no more duty here" (preserving the original's grammar) or drop the quotation marks and paraphrase.

### 3. Clough's hometown — Salem, not Lynn
- **Draft (line 59 prose AND line 71 FIGURE caption):** "Private Gibson Clough ... a soldier from **Lynn, Massachusetts**" / "Private Gibson Clough **of Lynn, Massachusetts**".
- **Correct:** Clough's own journal opens "I was born in **Salem** in New England in ye year 1738." Multiple transcripts (American Yawp, Krause House / Fortress of Louisbourg) confirm Salem.
- **Source:** American Yawp Reader, *Extracts from Gibson Clough's War Journal, 1759* (https://www.americanyawp.com/reader/colonial-society/extracts-from-gibson-cloughs-war-journal-1759/).
- The fact pack header also said "Lynn, Mass." — that is the pack's error, propagated into the draft. No source ties Clough to Lynn. Change both occurrences to **Salem, Massachusetts**.

## ✅ CONFIRMED (verified against authoritative sources)

**Definitions / structure**
- British regulars as standing-army professionals drilled in linear tactics — ✅ (Wikipedia, Provincial troops).
- Provincial = full-time but single-campaign, raised by assemblies via bounties, militia quotas, and the draft — ✅ (Wikipedia, Provincial troops).
- Militia = older part-time citizen-soldiery, provincials drawn from the same rolls — ✅ (same).

**The rank decree / Washington**
- George II decree of **21 November 1754** subordinating provincial officers of any rank to any king's-commission officer — ✅ existence and effect confirmed (Britannica on Washington; militaryheritage.com "Three Warrants on Rank in North America" describes the 1754 warrant). Draft does NOT quote the decree's literal text (correctly heeds the pack caution).
- A colonial colonel outranked by a British captain — ✅ (direct effect of the warrant).
- Washington colonel of the Virginia Regiment 1754; **Dinwiddie's** plan to break it into independent captain-led companies, reducing him to captain; Washington **resigned** rather than accept — ✅ (Wikipedia "GW in the F&I War"; Mount Vernon).
- 1755: Washington rejoined as **unpaid volunteer aide-de-camp** to **Braddock** to avoid being outranked — ✅ (Britannica; Wikipedia).

**Provincial grievances / the lash**
- Regular discipline by **cat o' nine tails**, sentences **25–500 lashes**, **up to 1,000** for mutiny/desertion, inflicted publicly — ✅ (fact-pack §1; corroborated by Clough's own journal, which records the high sentences).
- Clough's recorded floggings: one man **thirty stripes** for disobedience; a sentence of **one thousand lashes**; others of **five hundred each**; an artilleryman **200 stripes** — ✅ all four CONFIRMED verbatim against Clough's journal (American Yawp transcript).
- **Clough signature quote** "although we be Englishmen born, we are debarred Englishmen's liberty" and regulars "but little better than slaves to their officers." — ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim (MassMoments, quoting Clough; History Matters titles its excerpt with the line).
- Puritan covenant / contractual-enlistment ethos; "ordinary farmers led by neighbors" — ✅ (Anderson framing, fact pack §4; consistent with sources).

**Rogers' Rangers**
- **Robert Rogers** raised the ranger companies in **1755** in **New Hampshire** for long-range patrol/scouting/ambush/raiding; snowshoe marches, silent movement, cover, Native-derived woodland tactics — ✅ (Wikipedia "Rogers' Rangers").
- **28 Rules of Ranging**, first written code, trained regular and provincial officers — ✅.
- **1759 raid on the Abenaki village of Saint-François (Odanak)** — ✅ (attack 3 October 1759; Wikipedia / HistoryNet).

**Native allies / Johnson / Hendrick**
- **Sir William Johnson**, Irish immigrant, settled in the **Mohawk Valley in 1738**, learned Mohawk language/customs, **Superintendent of Indian Affairs** — ✅ (worldhistory.org; Wikipedia). [Sources say "about 1738" — fine.]
- Iroquois Confederacy = **Six Nations / Haudenosaunee**, trying to stay neutral — ✅.

- Jesuits active among **Seneca and Onondaga**, who leaned French; the Confederacy divided and acting on its own aims — ✅ (fact pack §9; consistent with sources).

**Pitt's 1758 reforms**
- **William Pitt the Elder**, late 1757 into 1758, **reimbursed colonial governments** for troop-raising costs and treated the colonies as allies, near-unlimited funds — ✅ (Britannica; u-s-history.com; americanheritage.com).
- **He raised the standing of provincial officers, easing the rank humiliation** — ✅ CONFIRMED (the draft's inline [VERIFY] at line 114 can be cleared). A warrant of **30 December 1757** under Pitt elevated provincial generals/colonels to rank "next after" regular colonels and provincial officers to rank "next after all Officers of the like Rank" — i.e., parity-but-junior, replacing the 1756 "eldest captains" cap. **Source:** militaryheritage.com "Three Warrants on Rank in North America" (https://www.militaryheritage.com/rank.htm). The draft's wording ("raised the standing of provincial officers, easing the rank humiliation that had festered since 1754") is accurate.
- **Louisbourg fell 1758, Quebec 1759, Montreal 1760** — ✅ (standard; history.state.gov milestones).

**Scale / human cost**
- **More than 30 percent** of Massachusetts military-age men served at least one tour — ✅ (MassMoments: "over 30% of all adult males in Massachusetts"; attributed to Anderson's research, as the draft does).
- "Tens of thousands" across the colonies kept qualitative — ✅ correctly NOT invented as a hard total (per pack [GAP]).
