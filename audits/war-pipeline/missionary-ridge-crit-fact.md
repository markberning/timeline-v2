## ❌ MUST-FIX (ship-blocking)

**MF-1 — "Who ordered those men up the ridge?" is set as a verbatim Grant quote, but
WP only PARAPHRASES it.**
- Draft line 69: `> "Thomas, who ordered those men up the ridge?"` in blockquote with
  quotation marks, sourced "AH, WP."
- Finding: **WP does not quote Grant verbatim** — it says only *"He asked first Thomas
  then Granger who had given the orders."* The direct-quote rendering "Thomas, who
  ordered those men up the ridge?" appears in popular/secondary retellings (American
  Heritage and others), with wording that varies ("ridge" / "hill"). It is a real,
  widely-printed line but not securely a verbatim primary quotation.
- **Correction:** keep it, but cite it to the popular tradition, not WP, OR soften the
  framing so it doesn't claim primary-source exactness — e.g. retain quotation marks
  but note the wording is the traditional rendering, and drop "WP" from the citation
  for this specific line (WP is a paraphrase, not the quote's source).
- Source: WP body ("who had given the orders" — paraphrase); American Heritage
  *Miracle on Missionary Ridge* (direct-quote tradition, wording varies).

**MF-2 — "Chickamauga! Chickamauga!" battle-cry is set in quotation marks as if a
verified quotation.**
- Draft lines 65 & (echo) 93: the charging men shouting `"Chickamauga! Chickamauga!"`
- Finding: this is a well-attested tradition (the Army of the Cumberland shouting
  "Chickamauga" as a revenge cry going up the ridge) and appears across secondary
  accounts, but it is a reported battle-shout, not a sourced individual quotation, and
  the fact pack carries it as narrative color (§5.5) rather than a cited quote.
- **Correction:** acceptable to keep as a battle-cry, but it should be framed as a
  reported shout ("men went up shouting the name of the field where they'd been
  beaten — Chickamauga") rather than a clean verbatim quotation pair, to stay inside
  the born-verified-quote rule. Low severity but same class of issue as MF-1; flagged
  must-fix because the directive is to gate quotation marks specifically.

## ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX (verify-or-soften; not ship-blocking)

**SF-1 — Bragg's relief (flag #20). Confirm and tighten.** Draft says only "he was
relieved soon after." Verified: Bragg **requested to be relieved Nov 29, 1863** and
his resignation of command was accepted **Dec 2, 1863** (Davis recalled him to
Richmond as military adviser; Johnston succeeded him later in December). Safe as
written; the draft could add "within days (early December 1863)" for precision. [WP
*Braxton Bragg*; ABT; Britannica]

**SF-2 — Sherman casualties "nearly 2,000" (flag #4).** Sources diverge: ~2,000 Union
casualties is the common Nov-25 figure; one source gives 1,697 killed+wounded over two
days' fighting. The draft's hedge "nearly 2,000 casualties" is inside the range and
safe. Keep the hedge; do not harden to a precise number.

**SF-3 — Ridge geography for map labels (flags #1, #8).** Independent check: Missionary
Ridge rises **~500 feet above the Chattanooga valley** and runs N–S east of the city.
Draft's "hundreds of feet" (line 22/59) is safe. "A couple of miles east" (line 22) is
consistent with general sources but WP gives no exact mileage — keep it soft for map
labels; do not stamp a precise distance you can't source.

**SF-4 — "Death-knell of the Confederacy" quote (flags #2, #10).** Verified: NPS renders
it *"After the fighting, a Confederate soldier ominously wrote, 'This…is the death-knell
of the Confederacy.'"* — an **anonymous soldier**, sourced. The draft's plan to (a) move
it out of the-siege opening and (b) render it indirectly if shaky is now resolvable: it
IS attributable to NPS as an anonymous soldier. Keep it, attribute to "a Confederate
soldier (NPS)," and place it at the rout/meaning, not the-siege. Remove the line-17–20
placeholder block (it's an author note, not shippable prose).

**SF-5 — Grant→Wood pre-charge quotes (flags #9, #3).** Verified verbatim on NPS-Grant:
*"I ordered your attack an hour ago. Why has it not been made?"* and *"I order you to
attack."* Wording and attribution confirmed — safe to use as quoted, cite NPS-Grant.
This is the one place a true verbatim primary-style quote is supported, so it can keep
its quotation marks (contrast MF-1).
