# NEWCOMER-CLARITY CRITIC · Battle of Long Island draft (rev-long-island)

Draft reviewed: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-long-island-draft.md`
Critic lens: reader with ZERO prior knowledge — no terms, no people, no map of New York.

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The draft's bones are excellent for a newcomer: the Boston→New York handoff is clean, the
ridge-as-wall-with-four-doors geometry is built twice (once per section that needs it), the
timeline never blurs, and most terms get their parenthetical gloss on first use
("demonstrate," "nor'easter," "militia," "Hessians," "Loyalist," "Continentals," "East
River" all done right). What remains is a layer of one-line gloss/introduction fixes — no
restructuring. Five items are blocking because they break the per-section rules or a
causal chain the page is required to carry.

---

## BLOCKER

**B1 — "siege approaches" undefined at FIRST use (Section 2). The gloss exists, but only
in Section 3 — sections are read independently, so Section 2 readers never get it.**
> "Howe said no, and ordered formal siege approaches instead."
Fix: "Howe said no, and ordered formal siege approaches (the slow, methodical digging of
protected trenches toward an enemy position) instead." Keep Section 3's existing gloss —
per-section reset is correct there.
The same term also appears unglossed in the Section-2 MEANWHILE block ("must fall" cheaply
to regular siege approaches") — covered once B1 lands earlier in the section.

**B2 — Washington appears in Section 2 with no introduction at all.** His only mention in
the section is mid-paragraph, deep in the Maryland-400 passage; a Section-2-only reader
has never met him.
> "...reported that Washington, watching from the Cobble Hill fort inside the lines, wrung
> his hands and cried out..."
Fix: "...reported that George Washington (American), the army's commander-in-chief,
watching from the Cobble Hill fort inside the lines, wrung his hands..."

**B3 — WHY New York must be defended though indefensible is never said (Section 1).** The
draft gives Lee's verdict (indefensible but can be made expensive) and then jumps to "So
the Americans dug anyway" — the "anyway" carries the entire political logic silently. The
newcomer's obvious question (if it can't be held, why not leave?) goes unanswered.
> "...against a navy, New York was indefensible, but it could be made expensive. So the
> Americans dug anyway, fortifying Manhattan and, crucially, Brooklyn Heights..."
Fix (one clause, fact-safe): "...but it could be made expensive. And abandoning one of the
new country's greatest cities without firing a shot was politically unthinkable weeks into
a declared revolution, so the Americans dug anyway, to make the British pay for it:
fortifying Manhattan and, crucially, Brooklyn Heights..." (Author/fact-check may rephrase;
the requirement is that the defend-it-anyway reason be stated, not implied.)

**B4 — The wind-stops-the-navy causal link rests on undefined sailing jargon (Section 3).**
"Beating up" means nothing to a newcomer, so the load-bearing mechanism (why didn't the
navy just close the river?) reads as a non-explanation.
> "But the same northeast wind that made the camp miserable kept the British warships from
> beating up into the East River."
Fix: "...kept the British warships out of the East River: a sailing ship cannot sail
straight into the wind, and the wind was blowing straight down the river against them."
Same fix rescues the follow-on sentence's logic ("the moment the wind shifted, British
frigates would stand into the river behind him" — see S13 for "stand into"/"frigates").

**B5 — "rear guard" never defined on any surface, and it appears on three** (hero caption,
Section 2, Section 3 twice). It is the page's emotional center (the Maryland stand) and
its near-disaster (Mifflin), so the term must be built, per surface:
> Hero caption: "Lord Stirling's rear guard fighting at the Gowanus..."
> Section 2: "Then he personally led the rear guard, roughly 260 to 270 men..."
> Section 3: "a garbled order pulled General Mifflin's rear guard out of the fortifications"
Fixes: Section 2 — "Then he personally led the rear guard (the detachment that stays
behind, still fighting, so the rest can get away), roughly 260 to 270 men..." Section 3 —
"...pulled General Mifflin's rear guard (the men still manning the fortifications to
screen the embarkation) out of the lines hours too early..." Hero caption — "...Lord
Stirling's rear guard (the men left behind to hold off the British) fighting at the
Gowanus marsh..."

## SHOULD-FIX

**S1 — "New York City is on Manhattan" is inferable but never stated (Section 1).** A
modern reader's mental map says Brooklyn IS New York City; the 1776 fact that the city was
only the lower tip of Manhattan island has to be built explicitly. The dossier `place`
line does this for Brooklyn ("then rural Kings County") but the section prose never does
it for the city.
> "...Brooklyn Heights, the high ground on Long Island that commands New York City across
> the East River..."
Fix: "...commands New York City (the city then sat only at the southern tip of Manhattan
island) across the East River..." [flag for fact-check sign-off on the wording]

**S2 — Staten Island is never located (Section 1).** It's the staging ground for the whole
armada and the newcomer doesn't know where it is.
> "Howe arrived off Sandy Hook in late June and landed unopposed on Staten Island on July
> 2 and 3..."
Fix: "...landed unopposed on Staten Island (the island at the mouth of the harbor, across
the water from Long Island) on July 2 and 3..."

**S3 — "Sandy Hook" and "the Narrows" undefined (Section 1).**
> "Howe arrived off Sandy Hook in late June..."
> "...crossed the Narrows from Staten Island and landed unopposed at Gravesend Bay..."
Fixes: "off Sandy Hook (the spit of New Jersey shore at the harbor's entrance)";
"crossed the Narrows (the channel between Staten Island and Long Island, the harbor's
gate)". The Locator-1 caption uses "the Narrows" too and inherits the prose gloss poorly —
captions are read alone; consider "landed across the Narrows, the channel between the two
islands, at Gravesend Bay."

**S4 — "pickets" undefined (Section 2, twice).**
> "...and pickets on the first three passes."
Fix: "...and pickets (small guard detachments posted out front to give warning) on the
first three passes."

**S5 — "light infantry and grenadiers" undefined (Section 2).**
> "...British light infantry and grenadiers rolled in from Bedford..."
Fix: "...British light infantry and grenadiers (the army's fast-moving skirmishers and its
biggest assault troops, the elite of both kinds) rolled in from Bedford..."

**S6 — "regulars" undefined (Section 2 and the dossier sides note).**
> "...with 5,000 regulars he could march from one end of America to the other..."
Fix: "...with 5,000 regulars (full-time professional soldiers) he could march..." The
dossier sides note "British regulars and hired German (Hessian) professionals" can absorb
it cheaply: "British regulars (career professional soldiers) and hired German (Hessian)
professionals."

**S7 — Per-section term resets missed for three terms the draft glosses elsewhere:**
"militia" (Section 2: "they had five militia officers on horseback" — add "(part-time
citizen soldiers)"); "Continental Army" (Section 3: "the heart of the Continental Army" —
add "(the new national army)"); "Loyalist"/"Patriot" (Section 3: "leaned Loyalist" /
"Loyalist and Patriot alike" — first Section-3 use needs "(Americans loyal to the king)"
and "Patriot" needs "(supporters of independence)"). The dossier sides note also says "new
Continentals and militia" with no gloss — the dossier is its own surface.

**S8 — "Bunker Hill" leans on another chapter (Section 1).** The lesson is given but the
event isn't; "the Connecticut folk hero of Bunker Hill" (Putnam) has the same gap. The
Section-2 MEANWHILE does this right ("where Howe's frontal assaults bled an army") — copy
that pattern forward.
> "The lesson of Bunker Hill the year before was burned into both armies: whoever holds
> the heights commands the town."
Fix: "The lesson of Bunker Hill the year before (the fight outside Boston where the
British stormed an American-held hill and bled terribly for it) was burned into both
armies: whoever holds the heights commands the town."

**S9 — "Trenton" lands with no content (Section 3 prose + MEANWHILE).** The dominoes
paragraph ends on it and the MEANWHILE's last beat is "fought at Trenton four months
later" — a newcomer doesn't know Trenton was the turnaround victory.
> "...and the long retreat across New Jersey, all the way down to the day after Christmas,
> and Trenton."
Fix: "...all the way down to the day after Christmas and Trenton, the surprise counterattack
in New Jersey that finally turned the war around." (The next sentence's "the army that
turned the war around that winter" can then shorten or stand as reinforcement.)

**S10 — The peace-conference cast is unintroduced (Section 3).** Adams, Franklin, and
Rutledge appear with no role; Adams appears even earlier ("John Adams, suspecting...")
cold. Also "Lord Richard Howe" is not identified as the admiral brother within this
section ("The Howe brothers" is established, but which Howe this is isn't).
> "On September 11, Adams, Benjamin Franklin, and Edward Rutledge met Lord Richard Howe at
> the Billopp House on Staten Island..."
Fix: introduce Adams at his first Section-3 appearance ("John Adams, the Massachusetts
delegate in Congress, suspecting exactly what the gesture was...") and frame the meeting:
"On September 11 a three-man delegation from Congress (Adams, Benjamin Franklin, and
Edward Rutledge of South Carolina) met Admiral Lord Richard Howe, the general's brother,
at the Billopp House on Staten Island..."

**S11 — "paroled"/"exchanged" undefined (Section 3 prose + Stirling and Sullivan dossier
bios).**
> "They paroled the captured General John Sullivan (American) to Philadelphia..."
Fix: "They paroled (released on his word not to fight until formally swapped) the captured
General John Sullivan (American)..." In the bios, "Exchanged, he served..." → "Exchanged
(swapped for a British prisoner), he served..." — once per surface is enough.

**S12 — The one-night stakes are implied, never stated (Section 3).** Why the whole army
in a single night? Because dawn with the army caught half-ashore and half-afloat under
British guns means destruction. The draft shows the timetable failing and the fog saving
it, but never names what daylight would have cost.
> "Toward dawn the timetable failed anyway. Much of the rear guard was still in the lines
> as the sky lightened, with the British close enough to hear."
Fix: one clause, e.g. "Toward dawn the timetable failed anyway — daylight would expose
half-empty lines and loaded boats to an army close enough to hear — much of the rear guard
was still in the lines as the sky lightened." (Use parentheses, not the em-dashes, per
house rules: "...failed anyway (daylight meant discovery, and discovery meant the lines
stormed and the boats shot out of the water).")

**S13 — "frigates" and "stand into" undefined (Section 3).**
> "...the moment the wind shifted, British frigates would stand into the river behind
> him..."
Fix: "...the moment the wind shifted, British frigates (fast medium-sized warships) would
sail into the river behind him..."

**S14 — MEANWHILE header "Wallabout Bay" is an unexplained place name (Section 3).** The
block's body never mentions or locates it; a newcomer can't connect header to content.
Fix: either locate it in the body ("...fed into the prison ships anchored in Wallabout
Bay, the inlet on the Brooklyn shore just east of the ferry landing...") or retitle the
block ("The prison ships | The other army").

**S15 — "Fort Ticonderoga" used as if known (Section 1).**
> "...had put cannon (the guns hauled overland from Fort Ticonderoga) on Dorchester
> Heights..."
Fix: "(the guns hauled 300 miles overland from Fort Ticonderoga, a captured British fort
in upstate New York)" — distance optional, the identification is the fix.

**S16 — "flanking" undefined on the dossier surface.** The sections wisely avoid the word
and describe the move instead, but the dossier uses it three times (Clinton role "Author
of the flanking plan," Howe bio "the war's most elegant flanking victory," Cornwallis role
"flanking column") and image caption 3 says "the flanking march around the American left."
One gloss on the first dossier use fixes the surface: Howe bio "...executed the war's most
elegant flanking victory (an attack swung around the side of the enemy line rather than
into its front) at Brooklyn..." For the caption: "...and the flanking march around the
eastern end of the American line." Related: Clinton bio "He led the van himself" — "van"
is jargon; "He led the lead column himself."

**S17 — "feint" undefined (Section 1).**
> "...he held troops back in Manhattan for days, suspecting the landing was a feint."
Fix: "...suspecting the landing was a feint (a fake attack meant to draw his army out of
position)."

**S18 — Lord Percy appears with no tag or role (Section 2).**
> "...Clinton's vanguard in front, then Lord Cornwallis (British), Howe himself, and Lord
> Percy, moving northeast by back roads..."
Fix: "...and Lord Percy (British), commanding the rear division, moving northeast..." Also
gloss "vanguard" at its first use: "Clinton's vanguard (the lead element) in front."

## NIT

**N1 — QUOTE 1 attribution is ambiguous (Section 1).** The "I thought all London was
afloat" block follows the McCurtin sentence, but the sentence already delivered its own
quote ("a wood of pine trees trimmed"), so the block can read as a second, unattributed
voice. The pack forbids extending the lead-in; the cheapest fix inside the rules is to
reorder so the prose quote comes first and the sentence ends pointing at the journal
("...wrote in his journal..."), letting the block land as his next line. Author's call.

**N2 — Stats list says "British/Hessian" before "Hessian" is defined.** The gloss lives in
the sides note below the stats. If the dossier renders stats first, the first "Hessian" a
reader meets is unglossed. Cheap fix: stats line "~370–400 British/Hessian (hired German)".

**N3 — Small undefined terms, one-word glosses each, take or leave:**
- "ebb tide" (Section 3) → "(the outgoing tide)"
- "council of war" (Section 3) → "(a formal vote of the senior generals)"
- "court-martial" (Section 2) → "(a military trial)"
- "trading volleys" (Section 2) → "still trading massed musket fire with Grant"
- "refit" (Section 1) → "to rest and resupply"
- "the passes" (Section 1, first use) → "...could cross only at the passes (the gaps where
  roads ran through the ridge)" — the four-doors metaphor is built well, this just locks it
- "the American works at Brooklyn" (Section 1) → "(the forts and trenches)"
- "requisitions, billeting" (Section 3) → "(supplies seized, soldiers quartered in homes)"

**N4 — Bare place names in the dominoes list (Section 3):** Harlem Heights, the Great Fire
of New York, White Plains pass as a deliberate fast montage; Kip's Bay could take "(on
Manhattan's east side)" since it explains how the city fell. "Long Island Sound" →
"(the water between Long Island and Connecticut)". "Charleston" (Section 1) → "Charleston,
South Carolina". All optional at this prose speed.

**N5 — Dossier bios lean on out-of-page history:** Cornwallis "handed his army over at
Yorktown" (→ "at Yorktown in 1781, the surrender that effectively ended the war"); Howe
"a combat legend since Quebec in 1759 (he led the climb to the Plains of Abraham)" — the
paren names a second unknown to explain the first; "the daring climb to the heights above
the city" would carry it; Washington/Putnam "French and Indian War" → fine as a proper
noun, but "(the previous war, against France)" wouldn't hurt. Bios are skimmed surfaces;
these are polish, not blockers.

**N6 — Untagged sides, inferable from context:** Mifflin (Section 3) and Tallmadge
(Section 3) carry no (American) tag; both sit in unambiguous American context. Add tags if
the side-tag pass wants consistency.

**N7 — Hero caption "at the Gowanus" is a cold place name** on the page's first surface.
"fighting at the Gowanus marsh in Brooklyn" anchors it (and pairs with the B5 caption fix).

## DIMENSION SCORECARD (per the critic brief)

1. **Terms inline-defined per section** — the draft's strongest habit, but misses cluster
   in Section 2 (siege approaches B1, pickets S4, light infantry/grenadiers S5, regulars
   S6, militia S7) and on the dossier surface (flanking S16, Continentals/militia S7).
2. **Persons with role+side per section** — near-perfect in Section 1; Section 2 misses
   Washington (B2) and Percy (S18); Section 3 misses the peace delegation (S10).
3. **Geography from scratch** — the ridge/passes/East River build is genuinely good and
   done twice; gaps are Staten Island (S2), Sandy Hook/Narrows (S3), and the
   city-is-Manhattan fact (S1). Escape destination (Manhattan) is explicit. Good.
4. **Causal chains** — passes-as-doors, Jamaica-Pass-behind-the-line, and the East River
   trap are all explicit; missing are why-defend-anyway (B3), the sailing mechanism of the
   wind (B4), and the named cost of daylight (S12). The silence/lie logic ("an army that
   knows it is retreating tends to bolt") is a model sentence.
5. **Timeline clarity** — PASS clean. Every beat is dated and ordered; buildup weeks,
   night march hours, morning collapse, two trapped days, one-night crossing all legible.
6. **Other-chapter dependencies** — Boston's fall and the Declaration are fully
   self-contained; Bunker Hill (S8) and Trenton (S9) need one clause each; the Loyalist
   civil-war beat stands alone well and the PILL is a bonus, not a crutch.
