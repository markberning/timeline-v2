# Framing / Lost Cause Critic Gate — Gaines' Mill
Gate run: 2026-05-30

---

## Checklist 1 — Slavery named as the cause in section 1

**PASS.**

The second paragraph of section 1 (`lee-attacks`) names it plainly and without hedging:

> "The fight for Richmond was, at bottom, a fight over a slaveholding republic — a country of roughly 4 million enslaved people whose forced labor was the South's economy, and whose continued bondage was the reason the Confederate states had walked out of the Union in the first place."

That is unambiguous: slavery is the cause of secession, Confederate independence is a defense of slavery's republic, and capturing Richmond in 1862 could have ended the war before slavery's republic consolidated. The framing is direct and central — not buried, not hedged, not euphemized. The author's self-report is confirmed.

## Checklist 2 — Lee's aggression presented with its appalling cost

**PASS — minor SHOULD-FIX (not ship-blocking).**

The draft does not lionize Lee. It names Mechanicsville a tactical failure. It frames the entire day at Boatswain's Swamp as "assault after assault thrown in one at a time, each one bleeding out alone." The closing paragraph of the final section states explicitly:

> "Lee won by spending more men than his enemy — a pattern that would shadow his generalship for the rest of the war."

That is the right anti-marble-man anchor. It names the pattern and locates it in the arc of Lee's war.

**SHOULD-FIX (framing sharpness):** The section-1 closing sentence reads:

> "Lee had his first victory: costly, ugly, imperfectly run — and decisive."

"Decisive" after "costly, ugly, imperfectly run" is accurate — Richmond did not fall — but the em-dash construction gives "decisive" the weight of a reversal, which can read as the negative adjectives being conceded away. Consider: "costly, bloody, and imperfectly run — and it worked" or similar language that keeps the cost primary rather than resolved-by-decisiveness. Not ship-blocking; the paragraph as a whole counterweights the word, but the sentence rhythm tilts the last impression toward triumph.

## Checklist 3 — Hood's Texas Brigade: valor-poetry or cost-honest?

**PASS — with one FAIR finding to note.**

The draft does not give Hood's Texans a Lost Cause aria. It follows the breakthrough description immediately with hard numbers:

> "The price was savage. Hood's part of the line — Whiting's division — lost 1,018 men in the assault. The 4th Texas alone lost about 570, including every one of its field-grade officers."

The private's "cutting down our ranks like wheat" quote is used to describe the incoming fire killing Confederates, not as battle-glory language. The prose is taut rather than romantic.

**FAIR (not tilted, not distorted):** The closing italic of the section —

> "A day of failed charges, and then one brigade walked up the same killing slope holding its fire, and the wall came down."

— reads as a clean tactical observation, not a valor-poem. But it is the section's last impression before the casualty math. The section ends structurally on the breakthrough rather than on the cost; the casualty paragraph appears *before* this italic. Reordering so the final note is the cost rather than the cinematic image would make it more cost-forward. This is a SHOULD-FIX, not a MUST-FIX, because the numbers are present and prominent.

There is no language in the Hood section about what the Texas Brigade was fighting *for* — slavery's republic, named in section 1. The prose rightly does not re-litigate cause at every paragraph, but a one-clause anchor ("the army defending slavery's republic") anywhere in `hoods-breakthrough` would close the loop from section 1 to the Confederate victory moment. **SHOULD-FIX.**

## Checklist 4 — Human cost central

**PASS.**

The draft accumulates bodies throughout: A.P. Hill's division over 2,000 in two hours, Ewell's men in "ferocious, doomed charges," 9th Massachusetts losing 206 men (most of any Union regiment), a "butcher's bill" frame on the final count, "~15,000" total, Confederate losses heavier in killed and wounded. The "Meanwhile" sidebar names it "the bloodiest field yet" and quantifies it against Shiloh. Human cost is not an afterthought.

## Additional distortion scan — miscellaneous

**The Stonewall Jackson treatment:** FAIR. The draft names Jackson's tardiness as a cause of the piecemeal slaughter ("Had Jackson attacked when he first arrived, or during A.P. Hill's attack, we would have had an easy victory") and treats his eventual appearance matter-of-factly. His battlefield reputation is not rehabilitated. No distortion.

**McClellan:** FAIR. The draft names his overcaution explicitly and pins the midnight telegram on him. It does not scapegoat him to the exclusion of Confederate agency; Lee's plan and Hood's tactics are credited where they apply. No distortion.

**"Change of base" framing:** FAIR. The draft notes this was army slang for retreat and then says, in plain language, "the man with the bigger army started planning his retreat the day the smaller army attacked him." No Lost Cause inflection.

**Stuart / Cooke family detail:** Neutral color detail. Not distorted.
