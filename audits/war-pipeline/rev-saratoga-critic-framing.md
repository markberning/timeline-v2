# FRAMING CRITIC — Battle of Saratoga draft (rev-saratoga)

*Critic pass 2026-06-12 against `rev-saratoga-draft.md` (all 391 lines, incl. FACT LEDGER
and compliance lists) and `rev-saratoga-factpack.md` (sole source). Scope: fairness,
emphasis, interpretive honesty. Cross-checked all handle-with-care surfaces against the
pack's DISPUTED register (§12) and the do-not-use-as-fact blacklist (§8).*

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

No blocker. The hardest framing duties are met: Arnold's ride is presented at exactly the
pack's evidence level, neither the mutineer-hero nor the gate-blessed soldier; the Murphy
tradition is correctly badged; Jane McCrea's killer is genuinely unknown and the propaganda
USE of the story is named as propaganda; Burgoyne is a competent professional who made a
planning gamble, not a buffoon; the Howe-Philadelphia gap is told as a London planning
failure, not a betrayal; the Convention Army's mistreatment is called "a genuinely shabby
episode on the winning side"; the blacklist is clean. Three should-fixes, one polish. None
rises to a blocker; together they matter because they each tip the same way: toward
inevitability framing and American-hero elevation at the margins.

## BLOCKER

None.

## SHOULD-FIX

**1. The Burgoyne "Gentleman Johnny" label in the commander caption reads as caricature.**
Caption for commander #15 (Burgoyne): "John Burgoyne painted by Joshua Reynolds around 1766:
'Gentleman Johnny,' the playwright-general who marched on his own plan and surrendered on his
own word, refusing to call it a capitulation."

The nickname is pack-sanctioned and the bio (correctly) immediately follows it with
humanizing context ("genuinely humane officer by the standards of the age... his men liked
him"). But the commander CAPTION — a reader's first encounter before the bio — stops at the
label without any rehabilitation, and the three appositives that follow all rhyme:
playwright/gambler/plan/his-own-word forms a tight loop that reads as a gentle sneer. Compare
the bio, which earns the nickname and then earns respect for the man. The caption is the
visible surface readers will see without the bio context; it should earn the label too.
FIX: Extend the caption one clause to break the sneer rhythm, e.g., "...the playwright-general
and genuinely capable officer who marched on his own plan and surrendered on his own word,
refusing to call it a capitulation." This is consistent with the bio already on the page and
stops the nickname from doing framing work the pack does not endorse.

**2. "the other was growing" as the section-closing line tips into inevitability.**
End of Section 1, last sentence: "One army on that river was shrinking. The other was growing."

This is a neat rhetorical pivot and the arithmetic is fully pack-supported. But as the
section's closing beat — after Burgoyne's plan has been described as fatally flawed from the
start, his supply line overextended, his allies gone home, and the ground picked against him
— the image of a single arrow pointing from doom to victory reads like the narrator endorsing
inevitability. It is the scrappy-underdogs-destined-to-win structure at the section level. The
pack's framing is careful throughout to preserve the contingency: Gates's militia swelled after
McCrea as a propaganda gift that could have gone differently; the ground that decided things
was chosen by Kościuszko's reading of the terrain, not foreordained. The closing line erases
that contingency by reducing the campaign to a single trajectory visible at Sept 13.
FIX: Add one word that restores the human reading of contingency rather than the retrospective
one: e.g., "One army on that river was shrinking. The other was growing, and no one on either
side yet knew what that arithmetic would produce in eighteen days." Or simply: "One army on
that river was shrinking; the other kept growing." Flat statement of fact without the
rhetorical flourish preserves honesty without weakening the prose.

**3. The Arnold bio erases his treason's framing relation to Saratoga.**
Commander bio for Arnold: "Three years later he sold West Point."

The pack's specific instruction is: "the road to West Point starts in this hospital bed
(standard framing; keep one line, the ARW vertical will tell the treason elsewhere)" — and the
DRAFT delivers that one-line cap in Section 3 prose ("the grievance that ended at West Point
three years later arguably begins in that hospital bed; that story belongs elsewhere in this
war"), correctly. But the commander BIO gives the treason bare — "Three years later he sold
West Point" — with no framing clause and no pointer outward, and the bio is a reader-facing
surface just like the prose. The Saratoga bio is Arnold's introduction in this vertical.
Readers who come to it first (from the cast, from a bookmark, from the commander panel) get
"the most aggressive battlefield general the Revolution had" and then a bare betrayal fact with
no framing. That is not hero-worship: it does not sanitize or omit. But it is not the
cross-linking style the app uses: it omits the "belongs elsewhere" framing that prevents the
bio from functioning as a miniaturized verdict on a man the vertical is still building.
FIX: Match the bio's last line to the prose's framing: e.g., "Three years later he sold West
Point — that story belongs to another chapter." One clause; the fact is named, the betrayal is
not laundered, and the vertical's own shape is preserved.

## POLISH

**4. The "army that GREW" dossier note (sides: American) buries the Native nations'
departure in a subordinate clause that reads as militarily instrumental.**
Dossier note for the American side: "An army that GREW all autumn: Continentals dug in on
Bemis Heights behind Kościuszko's entrenchments, Morgan's ~500 riflemen owning the woods, and
militia pouring in daily after Bennington and the Jane McCrea storm. Gates's doctrine was to
sit on chosen ground and let the enemy bleed; the ground did most of the choosing for
Burgoyne."

This is the dossier, not the prose — it reads fast, and that is appropriate. But the
omission of the Native departure from this summary means the reader who only reads the
dossier does not see the structural reason Burgoyne was "nearly blind in the woods." The
pack insists on naming the Native departure as the cause of British blindness (§7: "the army
that crossed the Hudson was nearly blind in the woods, while Morgan's riflemen and the militia
owned the forest"). The Section 1 prose carries this correctly and in full. The dossier's
British side note already covers it ("nearly blind in the woods after its Native scouts went
home") — which means the dossier IS balanced on this point. No change required to the dossier
itself. This finding is a reminder for the reviser: the cross-surface read is clean; no fix
needed, but confirm the British-side note's "scouts went home" line is preserved in any
revision pass.

## CHECKED AND PASSING (for the reviser's confidence)

- **Patriotic myth / inevitability — macro level:** "Historians have ranked Saratoga among
  the most decisive battles in world history" is framed as historians' verdict, not the
  narrator's, per pack instruction. "the surrender that brought France into the war" is
  factual. The American growth arc is told as contingent (militia "pouring in after
  Bennington and the Jane McCrea storm" — not inevitable, caused by named events). Freeman's
  Farm is correctly characterized as a field-holding draw bought at unaffordable cost, not
  a moral victory. Burgoyne's commitment on Sept 13 is given his own framing ("he said as
  much himself later, defending the campaign in Parliament"), not narrator-assigned doom.

- **Arnold's ride:** Handled at exactly the pack's evidence level. The traditional no-orders
  story is called "the famous version" and then every piece of counter-evidence is named
  (Burgoyne's statement, Lincoln's 1799 letter, Gates's own Oct 12 praise). "Whether Gates
  blessed the ride, tolerated it, or chased it with a messenger is genuinely disputed. What
  Arnold did on the field is not." This is the pack's safe telling rendered almost verbatim.

- **Arnold Sept 19 (on the field):** Tradition acknowledged ("puts Arnold on horseback in
  the thick of the smoke") and then corrected with pack authority ("the battlefield's own
  historian found no contemporary evidence"). His division is credited without staging him.
  The compliance note (§ Fact Ledger) records the handling. This is exemplary.

- **Timothy Murphy and Morgan's order:** Murphy named only as tradition, first-written-down
  68 years after the fact, from family stories. Morgan's famous order paraphrased only
  ("gives Morgan a grim order that the gallant officer had to die"), not quoted verbatim.
  Both are precisely at the pack's blacklist instructions.

- **Jane McCrea framing:** The fact and the propaganda are cleanly separated. What is
  certain named exactly. Both accounts of her killing given with no adjudication. "The
  forensics settle nothing" is the right close. Gates's letter is QUOTED as propaganda, not
  as fact. "The legend, not the girl, raised the regiments" names the mechanism honestly.

- **Burgoyne as competent:** The bio is generous and accurate: "genuinely humane officer by
  the standards of the age," "his men liked him," "he disliked the lash." His campaign
  decisions are explained from his own logic (the bridge of boats is a deliberate strategic
  commitment, not a blunder dramatized). Riedesel's retreat advice being overruled twice is
  told from the British side's internal debate, not as farce. Germain's latitude and the
  London planning failure are correctly named. Champagne/baggage decadence is omitted as the
  pack instructs.

- **Howe-Philadelphia gap:** "It was not a betrayal. It was a planning failure, and Burgoyne
  marched south into it." This is the exact framing the pack orders and it is delivered in
  Section 1 prose.

- **Native nations:** Told at the campaign-local level the pack mandates with a pill
  handoff ("native-nations"). The Haudenosaunee departure is named as a cause of British
  blindness, not as "savages going home." The McCrea affair is analyzed as a structural
  contradiction (couldn't punish without losing the alliance; didn't punish and lost it
  anyway). The nations are actors with interests, not props.

- **"Granny Gates":** Not used. Blacklist compliant.

- **"Britons never retreat!":** Not used. Blacklist compliant.

- **Arnold's battle cries:** Not used. Blacklist compliant.

- **Convention Army:** Told honestly and with the narrator's explicit "genuinely shabby
  episode on the winning side." Congress's pretexts are named individually. The
  distance-and-duration of the march is recorded. This is the anti-triumphalist beat the
  pack's §4 explicitly mandates and the draft delivers it.

- **Divided colonists:** Loyalists appear structurally (David Jones is named; the McCrea
  story's full irony — a Loyalist's own fiancée — is named; the provincial troops are
  in the surrender count). The Haudenosaunee civil war is gestured in the one-line handoff.
  The war's internal divisions are present, not erased.

- **Silver-bullet episode:** Told with named-actor clarity (Gov. George Clinton vs British
  Gen. Clinton; the same-surname confusion defused by side-tagging). No kinship claim.
  Lt. Taylor gets his name and his fate. The timing ("one day after the army he was riding
  to save had surrendered") lands the irony without editorializing.

- **Gates characterization:** Not strawmanned, not erased. Given his Camden failure as a
  structural counterweight to Saratoga, in the bio. The Section 2 opening credits him with
  the correct decision on the morning of Freeman's Farm: "the only major decision he had to
  make, and he made it right." His motive for accepting generous Convention terms
  (Clinton loose in the Highlands) is reported at pack confidence level.
