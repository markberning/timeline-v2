- British humanity: strong. The three climbs are told with explicit respect

  walked back up that hill, in order, into massed fire"), Howe's exposure and
  gutted staff are present in prose AND bio, the officer toll carries §5's

  No faceless-redcoat waves anywhere. §8.5 honored.

  resolving it; "tried and failed to rally a stand"). The retreat is "ragged but
  it was not a rout" — pack's exact balance.

## SHOULD-FIX

1. **The no-shows are missing — American failure-to-reinforce is the one
   untold half of the disorganization story.**
   Draft: "fresh men were streaming across the Neck to fill it" (section 2) and
   "they had little water and no relief coming" (section 1) — the only
   reinforcement traffic shown is successful and brave (Stark's deliberate
   march). The pack documents the opposite flow too: §3, "men rotating in and
   out, **many never crossing the shot-swept Neck**." American courage gets
   three set-pieces; the militia who balked get zero sentences, which quietly
   over-flatters the American side. FIX: one clause in section 2, most naturally
   at the redoubt-crisis or retreat beat, e.g. after "The powder was nearly
   gone": "...and no relief was coming: of the regiments ordered forward that
   afternoon, many never crossed the shot-swept Neck at all." (Also resolves the
   unexplained "no relief coming" in section 1.)

2. **Pyle caption asserts a universal the pack doesn't carry.**
   Draft: "it catches what the day's survivors all reported: the regulars came
   on in order, three times, into the worst infantry fire of the era." The
   substance is pack-backed (§4, §8.5); "the day's survivors all reported" is
   not. FIX: drop "all" — "it catches what survivors on both sides described:
   the regulars came on in order, three times..."

3. **Salem Poor petition recipient over-specified.**
   Draft: "moved fourteen officers to petition the Massachusetts legislature
   that he 'behaved like an experienced officer.'" Pack (§4, §8.6): "14
   officers petitioned" — no recipient body named. FIX: "moved fourteen
   officers to sign a petition that he 'behaved like an experienced officer'"
   (or verify the recipient and add it to the pack first).

## NIT

1. **"the best regulars in the world"** (section 3) — pack §6 says militia
   "could maul regulars"; the superlative is the draft's addition. FIX: "could
   maul the best infantry Britain had" → simply "could maul British regulars."

2. **"historians have stopped pretending otherwise"** (Putnam dossier bio) —
   the pack instructs "say so" that there's no clean answer; this flourish
   additionally characterizes the historiography's current posture, which the
   pack doesn't. FIX: end at "...has no clean answer." or "...has no clean
   answer, and never has."

3. **"fortified American lines he might well have carried"** (section 3) — pack
   §3/§6: "declined to storm fortified lines he might have carried." "Well"
   nudges the counterfactual up a notch inside a MED interpretation. FIX: drop
   "well."

4. **Warren's commission** (dossier bio: "commissioned a major general three
   days before the battle") — pack §3 adds "the commission not yet
   formalized/acted on." The refusal beat is rendered perfectly; consider
   "commissioned a major general three days earlier (the commission not yet
   acted on)" so the rank isn't read as fully seated. Low stakes; the bio
   already shows him as a volunteer.
