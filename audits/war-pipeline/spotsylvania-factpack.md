## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name:** Battle of Spotsylvania Court House (a.k.a. Spotsylvania, the Mule Shoe;
  signature horror = **the Bloody Angle**). **[WIKI]**
- **Dates:** **May 8–21, 1864** (roughly two weeks; some sources frame the main combat
  as May 9–21, and the race/Laurel Hill opens May 8). **[WIKI] [ABT] [EV]**
- **Location:** **Spotsylvania County, Virginia** — the crossroads at **Spotsylvania
  Court House**, ~10 mi SE of the Wilderness battlefield, between the Rapidan/Rappahannock
  and the North Anna. Coordinates ~38°13′N 77°36′W. **[WIKI]**
- **Theatre:** **Eastern Theater.** The **second major battle of Grant's Overland
  Campaign**, fought immediately after **the Wilderness (May 5–7, 1864).** **[WIKI] [WIKI-OC]**
- **Result:** **Inconclusive / tactically a draw** — both sides claimed it; strategically a
  Union continuation (Grant did not retreat, slid southeast). **[WIKI] [ABT] [EV]**

### Why Spotsylvania happened — the race to the crossroads (strategic spine)
- After the Wilderness ground to a bloody stalemate, **Grant (North) did NOT retreat north**
  (every previous commander had after a check by Lee); instead he ordered the army **southeast
  toward the Spotsylvania Court House crossroads**, trying to **slip around Lee's right and put
  the Army of the Potomac between Lee and Richmond.** Whoever held the crossroads controlled
  the road south. **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **The foot-race (night of May 7–8):** Lee **(South)** read the move and sent **Maj. Gen.
  Richard H. Anderson's (South) First Corps** (Anderson had just taken over First Corps after
  **Lt. Gen. James Longstreet (South) was wounded at the Wilderness**) racing for the crossroads
  on a **newly cut forest road** around 10 p.m. Anderson's infantry, fleeing the choking
  Wilderness fires, marched through the night and **won the race — beating the Union arrivals
  to the high ground (Laurel Hill) by a matter of minutes.** **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **The cavalry friction (Brock Road):** Union cavalry under **Maj. Gen. Philip H. Sheridan
  (North)** had been tangling with **Maj. Gen. J.E.B. Stuart's (South)** horsemen (Fitzhugh
  Lee's division) for control of the **Brock Road**, the direct route from the Wilderness to
  Spotsylvania; the contested road slowed the Union infantry and helped Anderson win the race.
  **[EV]**
- **Net effect:** the Confederates dug in first at the crossroads; the next two weeks were
  **Grant repeatedly attacking entrenched works, then sliding southeast again** — the
  template of the whole Overland Campaign. **[WIKI] [EV]**

## 3. Casualties (both sides; scholarly range)

The two-week toll is **heavy and genuinely contested**; give ranges.

**Union (North) — widely cited (Wikipedia/EV agree):**
- **Killed ~2,725 · Wounded ~13,416 · Captured/Missing ~2,258 · Total ≈ 18,399.** **[WIKI] [EV] [ABT]**

**Confederate (South) — contested, give a range:**
- One tally: **Killed ~1,515 · Wounded ~5,414 · Captured/Missing ~5,758 · Total ≈ 12,687.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- Encyclopedia Virginia tally: **Killed ~1,467 · Wounded ~4,783 · Captured/Missing ~6,171 ·
  Total ≈ 12,421.** **[EV]**
- *Use the range:* **Confederate loss ≈ 12,000–13,000.** **[WIKI] [EV] [ABT]**
- The large **captured/missing** share (~5,700–6,200) reflects the **wholesale capture of
  Johnson's division at the apex on May 12.** **[WIKI] [CWM]**

**Combined both sides ≈ 30,000–32,000** over the two weeks — the **costliest engagement of the
Overland Campaign** and among the bloodiest of the war. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

**Bloody Angle (May 12 alone), for scale:** roughly **~9,000 Union and ~8,000 Confederate**
casualties concentrated in and around the salient in ~24 hours (the Confederate figure
includes ~3,000 captured). **[WIKI]** *(Single-day figures vary; treat as approximate. [VERIFY]
before a hard number.)*

**Author guidance:** state Union loss ≈ **18,000** and Confederate loss ≈ **12,000–13,000**;
the disproportion is *smaller* than at Corinth-type assaults because much of the cost was Grant
attacking entrenchments — but the strategic point (§9) is that **Lee could not replace even his
smaller losses**, while Grant could.

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (**north = up**):

- **Spotsylvania Court House** — the **crossroads** village, the strategic objective, **SE of
  the main fighting.** Whoever held it held the road south to Richmond/the North Anna. **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **Brock Road** — the **principal Union avenue of approach from the NW (from the Wilderness)**;
  the contested road in the May 7–8 race. **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **Laurel Hill** — a **rise/ridge on the south side of the Brock Road**, the **last defensible
  ground north of the crossroads**; Anderson's Confederates reached it first and **Warren's V
  Corps battered it without success all battle.** **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **Po River** — marks the **western flank**; **Hancock's II Corps crossed the Po May 10** (the
  "Battle of the Po") and his rearguard (Barlow) was roughly handled withdrawing back across it.
  **[WIKI]**
- **Fredericksburg Road** — the **eastern approach**; **Burnside's IX Corps advanced down it**
  on the Union left. **[WIKI]**
- **Ni River** — small stream near the **eastern Union positions** (May 9 action). **[WIKI]**

### The Mule Shoe / the salient — the defining terrain
- The Confederate line, dug the night of **May 8** by Ewell's corps (Johnson's division), had to
  **follow a patch of higher ground** that bulged **north out in front of the main line** — a
  **horseshoe-shaped salient** the soldiers called the **"Mule Shoe"** (or "horse shoe"). **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **Geometry:** the salient projected **more than half a mile (up to ~1 mile) north-to-south
  in front of the main line**, **~1,160 yards (about ⅔ mile) wide near the McCoull house**, and
  enclosed **~250 acres.** **[CWM] [WIKI]**
- **Inherent weakness:** a salient is **exposed to converging (cross) fire from three sides**;
  Lee and his engineers **knew it was weak** but accepted it to hold the high ground. To
  compensate they **packed it with ~30 cannon** and stout trenches. **[CWM]**
- **The apex = "the Bloody Angle."** The **NW shoulder/apex of the salient**, where the line
  turned, is the ground of the May 12 horror. **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **Lee's new line (the base):** during/after May 12 the Confederates built a **fresh line
  straight across the BASE of the salient, ~½ to ¾ mile south of the apex** (sources say ~500
  yards / ~¾ mile), abandoning the bloody tip. Withdrawal to it completed by **dawn May 13.**
  **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **Map orientation summary:** crossroads village **SE**; Brock Road entering from the **NW**;
  **Laurel Hill** on the W/center (Warren's front); the **Mule Shoe bulging NORTH** out in front
  of Ewell's center, **apex = the Bloody Angle at the top of the bulge**; **Po River to the W**
  (Hancock's flank crossing), **Fredericksburg Road to the E** (Burnside); **Lee's new base line**
  drawn as a chord straight across the bottom of the horseshoe. Union attack arrows strike the
  **apex from the N** (Hancock May 12) and the **west face** (Upton May 10).

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup:** Grant **(North)** raced Lee **(South)** for the Spotsylvania crossroads after the
Wilderness; Anderson **(South)** won the race and the Confederates entrenched at Laurel Hill and
along the line that became the Mule Shoe. **[WIKI] [EV]**

### May 8 — Laurel Hill (the race ends)
- **Warren's V Corps (North)** attacked the just-arrived **Anderson (South)** on **Laurel Hill**
  and was **repulsed with heavy loss**; **Sedgwick's VI Corps (North)** joined and also failed.
  The Confederate line was set. **[WIKI] [EV]**

### May 9 — entrenchment & Sedgwick's death
- The Confederates dug ~4 miles of works, including the **Mule Shoe salient.** **[WIKI]**
- **~9 a.m. — Maj. Gen. John Sedgwick (North), VI Corps, killed by a Confederate sharpshooter**
  (Whitworth rifle, ~1,000 yards) while siting artillery — moments after mocking his men for
  ducking (the "elephant" line, §7). **Wright (North)** took the corps. **[WIKI] [WIKI-SED]**
- **Sheridan (North) departed on his cavalry raid toward Richmond** (May 9), setting up Yellow
  Tavern. **[WIKI] [WIKI-YT]**

### May 10 — Upton's column (the tactic that almost worked)
- **~6 p.m. — Col. Emory Upton (North)** led **12 hand-picked regiments (~5,000 men) stacked in
  four close battle lines** across ~150 yards of open ground at the **west face of the salient
  ("Doles's Salient")**. The innovation: **rush the works WITHOUT stopping to fire**, hit the
  trench before the defenders could reload. **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- It **worked — Upton's column broke into the line** and took a stretch of trench and a battery,
  **but no supporting attack came** (**Gershom Mott's division (North) faltered/fell back under
  artillery fire**), so Confederate counterattacks drove Upton back out. **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **Significance:** Upton's success proved a **dense column could crack an entrenched line** —
  Grant resolved to **repeat it at corps scale on May 12.** Grant promoted Upton on the spot.
  **[WIKI] [CWM]** (British historian Charles Francis Atkinson later called it *"one of the
  classic infantry attacks of military history"* — **[WIKI], [VERIFY] before quoting verbatim.**)

### May 11 — the pause, the dispatch, Yellow Tavern
- Grant planned the **big version of Upton's attack** for May 12 — Hancock's whole II Corps at
  the apex. **[WIKI]**
- **~8:30 a.m. — Grant's dispatch to Halleck (North)** containing *"I ... propose to fight it
  out on this line if it takes all summer"* (full text + recipient, §7). **[CSS-GRANT] [WIKI]**
- **Fatal artillery order:** believing Grant was **disengaging/sliding off again**, Lee **(South)
  ordered ~29 cannon withdrawn from the Mule Shoe.** Johnson **(South)** later asked for them back
  near midnight; the order to return the guns **didn't reach the batteries until ~3:30 a.m. May
  12 — ~30 minutes before Hancock struck.** **[WIKI] [CWM]** (Confederate artillerist **E. Porter
  Alexander (South)** later called pulling the guns *"the one fatal Confederate blunder of this
  whole campaign."* — **[CWM], [VERIFY] exact wording.**)
- **Yellow Tavern (May 11, ADJACENT action — context, not the main battle):** ~6 mi N of Richmond,
  **Sheridan's (North) ~12,000 troopers met Stuart's (South) ~4,500**; **Stuart was mortally
  wounded** (shot by dismounted Union trooper **Pvt. John A. Huff (North)** at close range) and
  **died in Richmond the next day, May 12.** **Fitzhugh Lee (South)** took the cavalry. Union
  victory. *(Place as a parallel cavalry tragedy that bookends the same days; not the Spotsylvania
  fight itself.)* **[WIKI-YT]**

### May 12 — the apex assault and the Bloody Angle (the horror)
- **~4:35 a.m. (delayed from 4:00 by darkness/mist/rain) — Hancock's II Corps (North), ~15,000–
  20,000 men in a massed column, charged the Mule Shoe apex.** With the guns gone, **Johnson's
  (South) ~4,000 defenders were overwhelmed; Barlow's division (North) overran the works and
  CAPTURED most of the division** — including **Maj. Gen. Edward "Allegheny" Johnson (South)**
  and **Brig. Gen. George "Maryland" Steuart (South)** — plus thousands of men and the guns.
  **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **The breach is sealed:** **Gordon (South)** threw counterattacking brigades in to plug the
  gap; **McGowan's, Ramseur's, and other brigades (South)** fed into the apex. The Union spread
  **southward along the WEST face of the salient** — and there the line locked.
  **The "Bloody Angle"** is that west-shoulder ground. **[WIKI] [EV] [CWM]**
- **"Lee to the rear":** **Lee (South)** rode forward to personally lead the counterattack and
  was **stopped by Gordon and his men shouting "Lee to the rear!"** (a near-repeat of the Widow
  Tapp episode at the Wilderness). **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **~20+ hours (commonly "20–24 hours") of the war's most savage close combat in the rain** along
  the works: muzzle-to-muzzle, bayonet and clubbed-musket, men firing point-blank over the same
  log parapet through the night. **Recent rain had soaked/ruined much of the powder, forcing
  hand-to-hand fighting**; the trench filled with mud, water, and bodies. **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **The gnawed oak (signature artifact):** the storm of rifle fire was so dense it **cut down a
  full oak tree — its ~22-inch-diameter trunk severed entirely by bullets/minié balls** (see §6).
  **[SMITH] [WIKI]**
- **~3–4 a.m. May 13 — the Confederates withdrew** regiment by regiment to **Lee's new line at
  the base of the salient**, abandoning the bloody apex; completed by dawn. **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **Warren's failure (same day):** **Warren (North)** attacked **Laurel Hill** yet again (a 4th/5th
  attempt for some units) under Meade's order to go in *"at once at all hazards"* — and failed,
  drawing off **no** Confederate reserves as Grant had hoped. **[WIKI]**

### May 13–21 — sidesteps, the final repulse, and the slide south
- **May 13–16:** Grant **shifted the army eastward** in heavy rain and mud, probing for a new
  opening; couldn't mount a coordinated assault. **[WIKI]**
- **May 18 — final assault:** Hancock, with VI and IX Corps support, attacked the **old Mule Shoe
  ground again** at dawn, assuming Lee had thinned it; **Ewell's (South) Second Corps held the
  improved works with artillery and repulsed it.** **[WIKI]**
- **May 19 — Harris Farm:** Lee sent **Ewell (South)** on a reconnaissance-in-force toward the
  Union right; **Ewell's divisions (Rodes, Gordon) met green Union "heavy artillery" regiments
  newly converted to infantry**; a costly, inconclusive fight (~9 p.m.). Confederates lost ~900;
  Lee recalled them. **[WIKI]**
- **May 21 — Grant disengaged** and **moved SOUTHEAST toward the North Anna River**, continuing
  the Overland Campaign — exactly the pattern: blocked, but not retreating; sliding around Lee's
  flank again. **[WIKI] [EV]**

## 6. Human cost — named people / vivid documented detail

- **Maj. Gen. John Sedgwick (North), VI Corps — killed May 9.** Beloved corps commander ("Uncle
  John"). While siting guns under long-range sniper fire he **chided his men for ducking** with
  the "elephant" line (§7); he was **shot moments later** by a sharpshooter's bullet **under the
  left eye** (Whitworth rifle, ~1,000 yards). His chief of staff **Martin T. McMahon (North)**
  saw him fall; he **never regained consciousness.** **[WIKI-SED]** Wikipedia explicitly notes
  the legend that he *never finished the sentence* is **apocryphal**, "although the sentence was
  certainly among his last words." **[WIKI-SED]** *(Author: do NOT claim the bullet cut him off
  mid-word.)* Sedgwick was the **highest-ranking Union officer killed in the Civil War**
  ([VERIFY] phrasing — often stated; confirm before printing as superlative).
- **Maj. Gen. J.E.B. Stuart (South) — mortally wounded May 11 at Yellow Tavern, died May 12** in
  Richmond. Lee's cavalry chief; an adjacent loss the same days as the Mule Shoe. **[WIKI-YT]**
- **The captured division (May 12):** **Maj. Gen. Edward "Allegheny" Johnson (South)** and **Brig.
  Gen. George "Maryland" Steuart (South)** captured at the apex with the bulk of Johnson's division
  — one of the largest mass captures of the campaign. **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **Brig. Gen. Abner M. Perrin (South) — killed May 12; Brig. Gen. Junius Daniel (South) —
  mortally wounded May 12.** **[WIKI]**
- **The Bloody Angle, the documented horror:**
  - Grant's aide **Lt. Col. Horace Porter (North)** (*Campaigning with Grant*) on the aftermath:
    *"Our own killed were scattered over a large space near the 'angle,' while in front of the
    captured breastworks the enemy's dead, vastly more numerous than our own, were piled upon each
    other ... four layers deep."* **[WIKI], [VERIFY] exact wording/ellipsis from Porter.**
  - A Confederate (per CWM) on the trench: *"If a man wants to see hell upon earth, let him come
    and look into this black, bloody hole."* **[CWM], [VERIFY] exact attribution before quoting
    as a named soldier.**
- **The Spotsylvania Stump (the indelible artifact) [SMITH] [WIKI]:**
  - At the Bloody Angle on **May 12, 1864**, the volume of rifle fire was so intense it **completely
    severed a living oak tree** — *"all but twenty-two inches of the [oak] trunk"* was shot away,
    i.e., **a ~22-inch-diameter oak cut down entirely by bullets.** Several **conical minié balls
    remain embedded** in the wood. **[SMITH] [WIKI]**
  - The stump was **transferred to the Smithsonian in 1888** and is **on display at the National
    Museum of American History** (the *Price of Freedom: Americans at War* exhibition). **[SMITH]**
  - *Attribution caution:* the cutting is sometimes credited to the fire of the **24th Michigan /
    6th Wisconsin (Iron Brigade) (North)** — **[VERIFY] / treat as "believed," not asserted.** **[WIKI]**
  - *Phrasing caution for the author:* the precise figure is **"a 22-inch oak, severed entirely by
    rifle fire"** — sources are slightly ambiguous on whether 22 in. is the original diameter or
    the surviving piece; safest wording is **"an oak nearly two feet thick, cut clean through by
    bullets."** **[SMITH] [VERIFY]**

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the local hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and each side — was ultimately fought over. Omission is the
> default Lost Cause failure; "a crossroads," "attrition," or "states' rights" framing alone fails
> the gate. Spotsylvania makes the throughline **literally local**, because the men freeing
> themselves were from this very county.

### The documented throughline
- The Confederacy fought to preserve and extend **slavery**; Lee's **(South)** Army of Northern
  Virginia was the shield of that slave society. The Union's Overland Campaign was the hammer
  meant to destroy it. Grant's attrition strategy was, at bottom, a plan to **break the army that
  defended slavery faster than the South could replace it.** **[WIKI-OC]**
- **The proof walked onto this battlefield wearing Union blue.** While the two armies ground each
  other apart at the Mule Shoe, formerly enslaved men — many from **Spotsylvania and the
  surrounding Fredericksburg-area counties they had escaped** — fought Lee's army a few miles
  from the apex. The battle and the emancipation it advanced are the same story.

### STRONG LOCAL HOOK — the 23rd USCT at the Alrich Farm, May 15, 1864 **[ABT-23]**
On **May 15, 1864 — mid-battle, during the Spotsylvania fighting — in Spotsylvania County itself**,
the war's cause stepped into the open:
- At the **intersection of the Catharpin Road and the Orange Plank Road** (near Chancellor /
  the Spotsylvania battlefield), Confederate cavalry under **Brig. Gen. Thomas Rosser (South)**
  probed forward and pushed back the **2nd Ohio Cavalry (North)**. The **23rd United States
  Colored Infantry (North)** rushed up in support, **skirmished, then charged — and drove Rosser
  back.** **[ABT-23]**
- **The first directed combat of the Civil War between United States Colored Troops and the Army
  of Northern Virginia** (the first such fight north of the James River). **[ABT-23]**
- **Who these men were:** the **23rd USCT was organized in late 1863 (mustered from Nov 1863) from
  formerly enslaved and escaped people** — many of them **from Virginia and the very counties
  around Fredericksburg, including Spotsylvania, Stafford, Orange, Culpeper, and Caroline**, who
  had fled to Union lines (thousands escaped to Union protection from these counties in 1862).
  **[ABT-23]** Men who had been **property in this county** a year or two earlier came back to it
  **in uniform and fought the army fighting to keep them enslaved.**
- **What it proved (the payoff for the war's cause):** *"This battle action proved to the white
  troops that black soldiers would fight against the Confederate army."* Their own freedom was the
  cause, and they fought for it on the ground they had been enslaved on. **[ABT-23]**
- *(Wider Overland-Campaign context, for the author's framing only:* the **Emancipation
  Proclamation (Jan 1, 1863)** opened the army to Black enlistment, and by 1864 **USCT regiments
  were a growing part of Grant's force**; Lee's army would meet them again at **Wilson's Wharf
  (May 24)** and, devastatingly, at **the Crater (July 1864).** **[WIKI-OC]** *[VERIFY] any of these
  if used beyond a one-line reference.*)*

**Author directive:** weave the cause in where the section sets its stakes — **the crossroads was
the object, slavery was the reason**, and the **23rd USCT's charge at the Alrich Farm, by men from
this county who had freed themselves, is the human proof.** That hook satisfies the NAME-THE-CAUSE
gate concretely and locally; it is the section's emotional anchor.

## 9. Significance

- **The Overland Campaign template, locked in:** Spotsylvania confirmed what the Wilderness began
  — **Grant would not retreat after a check.** Blocked at the crossroads, he **slid southeast
  toward the North Anna and kept Lee in continuous contact.** Tactically a draw; strategically the
  start of the end. **[WIKI] [EV]**
- **Attrition as strategy — the real meaning:** Grant could **replace ~18,000 casualties; Lee
  could not replace ~12,000–13,000.** Lee's army would **never again take the strategic offensive**
  in the East. Grant *"hammer[ed] continuously ... until by mere attrition ... there should be
  nothing left to him."* **[WIKI-OC]** *(Grant's own framing — [VERIFY] exact wording before quoting.)*
- **The Mule Shoe / Bloody Angle entered the language** as a byword for the war's worst
  hand-to-hand horror — and as the proving ground of the **massed-column assault** (Upton May 10 →
  Hancock May 12) that briefly cracked a modern entrenched line. **[WIKI] [CWM]**
- **A cluster of irreplaceable losses:** **Sedgwick (North) killed**, **Stuart (South) mortally
  wounded** (Yellow Tavern, same days) — both armies lost a beloved senior commander in the same
  week. **[WIKI-SED] [WIKI-YT]**
- **Toward Petersburg:** the campaign that Spotsylvania anchored drove Lee back through the North
  Anna and Cold Harbor to the **Petersburg lines by mid-June 1864** — the siege that would end the
  war. **[ABT] [WIKI-OC]**
