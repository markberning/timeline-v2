**Gate:** FACT-CHECK (independent web verification)
**Sources consulted:** Wikipedia "Battle of Island Number Ten"; Essential Civil War Curriculum (essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com); American History Central; mycivilwar.com; historyofwar.org; Civil War Monitor; battlefields.org; Fort Pillow Wikipedia; Naval History and Heritage Command search results; Anaconda Plan Wikipedia + Encyclopedia Virginia

## Verdict: FAIL — 1 MUST-FIX, 1 SHOULD-FIX

## MUST-FIX

### M1 — "Six ironclads" is wrong; Foote had seven ironclads

**Draft says:** "six ironclads (warships sheathed in iron plate so cannonballs bounced off) plus a flotilla of mortar rafts"

**What sources say:** Every independent source confirms **7 ironclads** in Foote's Western Gunboat Flotilla at Island Number Ten:
- mycivilwar.com: "7 ironclad gunboats, among them the massive Benton, and 10 mortar rafts"
- Essential Civil War Curriculum: Lists 8 ironclads (Benton, Mound City, St. Louis, Louisville, Carondelet, Pittsburg, Cincinnati, and wooden Conestoga — but "wooden" Conestoga is not an ironclad, giving 7 true ironclads)
- Search result corroboration: "The impressive flotilla included 7 ironclad gunboats … and 10 mortar rafts"
- The fact pack's own infobox (§1) correctly lists "6 ironclad gunboats + 10–14 mortar rafts" — but this figure appears to be the pack's own error, contradicted by the sources the pack cites

**Fix:** Change "six ironclads" to "seven ironclads" in the bombardment section.

---

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1 — Union casualties understated; "two dozen to three dozen" omits naval losses

**Draft says:** "The North's combat losses in this campaign were a few dozen men — on the order of two dozen to three dozen killed, wounded, and missing all told"

**What sources say:** The sources disagree among themselves, and the draft's range only covers one slice:
- Wikipedia infobox (combined Army + Navy): **23 killed, 50 wounded, 5 missing = 78 total**
- Army of the Mississippi alone (Wikipedia article text): 8 killed, 21 wounded, 3 missing = **32 total**
- americanhistorycentral.com (New Madrid–Tiptonville phase, army only): 7 killed, 14 wounded, 4 missing = **25 total**
- Multiple round-figure summaries: "fewer than 100" (americanhistorycentral.com); "fewer than 50" (americanhistorycentral.com separate passage)

The draft's "two dozen to three dozen" accurately captures army-only losses (~25–32) but is wrong if read as the campaign total including naval casualties (~78). The contrast with Antietam is the whole point of this paragraph, so precision matters. The phrase "a few dozen" is also potentially misleading since the real figure with naval is closer to 80.

**Fix options (choose one):**
- Restrict explicitly to army losses: "The Army of the Mississippi's combat losses were fewer than 35 men — about 8 killed, 21 wounded, 3 missing — with total campaign losses including naval under 80."
- Or broaden to combined and say "fewer than 80 killed, wounded, and missing combined" — still dwarfed by Antietam's 22,000.

The existing framing ("a few dozen / two dozen to three dozen … fewer than fall in a single bad afternoon's skirmish") is directionally right but factually imprecise because it silently omits naval losses. The contrast with Antietam's 22,000 still works with any version of the true number.
