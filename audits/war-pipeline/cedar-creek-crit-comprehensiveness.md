## SHOULD-ADD (enrichment, not required)

1. **Union human cost is carried entirely by Thoburn (one line) and the abstract
   casualty math; Bidwell is absent and Lowell is omitted.** Brig. Gen. Daniel D.
   Bidwell (North) was *killed* and Col. Charles Russell Lowell (North) was mortally
   wounded (strapped to his horse, led the 4:15 charge, died next morning). The draft
   gives the Confederacy a named, fully-humanized death (Ramseur) but the Union side a
   nameless rout. For balance, one clause naming Bidwell and/or a sentence on Lowell —
   the NPS "Parallel Lives and Deaths of Ramseur & Lowell" pairing is *built* for this —
   would prevent the human-cost beat from reading as one-sided sympathy for the
   Confederate general. The draft's ledger flags Lowell as a conscious cut "for space";
   acceptable, but this is the single most defensible add. SHOULD-ADD.

2. **The Union army's identity ("Army of the Shenandoah") is never named.** The draft
   names Crook's Army of West Virginia, the XIX Corps, the VI Corps, but never the parent
   Union army by name, while naming Early's "Army of the Valley" repeatedly. Minor
   asymmetry; one mention would tidy it. SHOULD-ADD.

3. **The enslaved people of Belle Grove appear only as labor, never as people.** The
   section (rightly) makes Belle Grove the cause's proof, but the enslaved are referenced
   in the aggregate ("worked by enslaved hands," "hundreds of men, women, and children").
   This is honest and not a Lost Cause failure — but a single concrete human detail (the
   documented families, the named individuals on Belle Grove's own pages) would convert a
   statistic into a person and strengthen, not lengthen-bloat, the cause beat. Optional;
   the framing gate does not require it.
