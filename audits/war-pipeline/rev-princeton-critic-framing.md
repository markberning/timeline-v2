## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

This is a disciplined draft. The traditions are all badged in prose (bag-the-fox, fox
chase, "Parade with us," Fitzgerald's hat, mistaken-for-Washington, the 1905 cannonball
legend, the Cornwallis toast); the British casualty dispute is presented as a genuine
three-way disagreement with all three counters named, and the prose explicitly refuses to
pick ("depends on whose arithmetic you trust"); Mawhood and the 17th Foot get full
soldiering credit in both armies' own words; the Leslie burial and the Potter error are
both carried with their humanity and without gotcha; the no-likeness cards are honest;
Mercer's death stays at evidence level with the wound count and the Washington-mistake
tradition correctly withheld/badged. No blocker. Four should-fixes, mostly at the seams
where house voice slightly outruns the pack.

## SHOULD-FIX

### SF-1 · Hero caption drops "two years later" from the Charles Peale sketches — inflates the image's eyewitness authority
Draft (dossier `heroCaption` + IMAGES #1):
> "working partly from sketches his brother Charles made on the field"

Pack (§8.3): "with sketches by his brother Charles **made on the field two years later**."

As written, a reader pairs "veteran who fought in the battle" with "sketches made on the
field" and concludes the sketches are battle-day eyewitness records. The pack says they
are terrain sketches made on the field two years after the battle. The omission is the
exact kind of quiet documentary-status inflation this page otherwise avoids. The
"veteran / Maryland ensign" claims are pack-supported and fine; only the sketch timing
needs the qualifier.
**Fix:** "...working partly from sketches his brother Charles made on the field two years
later" (both occurrences: `heroCaption` line 20 and IMAGES item 1 caption, line 66).

### SF-2 · Dossier note: "the ten days that turned the war around" — beyond the pack's scoped claim
Draft (dossier `note`, line 16):
> "closing the ten days that turned the war around"

Pack (§1): the ten days "reversed the momentum of **the war in the north**." The pack's
strategic-meaning claims are momentum, invincibility-aura, enlistments, New Jersey — never
"turned the war around" unqualified. This is the one spot the draft does the
turning-point-of-the-war inflation the rest of the page resists; it is also the first
sentence most readers will see.
**Fix:** "closing the ten days that turned the war in the north around" — or "closing the
ten days that swung the war's momentum back" — keep the pack's scope.

### SF-3 · "it never afterward came so close to dying again" — unsupported superlative
Draft (section 3 closer, line 211):
> "The army that should have died in December was still in the field in January, and it
> never afterward came so close to dying again."

The second clause is a war-spanning comparative judgment nowhere in the pack — and it is
contestable on the merits (Valley Forge, the 1780–81 mutinies and currency collapse are
real rivals for "closest to dying"). The first clause is the earned, pack-supported
ending; the second is a free superlative tacked onto it.
**Fix:** End the sentence at "January": "The army that should have died in December was
still in the field in January." (The Cresswell quote and Harcourt MEANWHILE already carry
the swing; nothing is lost.)

### SF-4 · The ~30-yards exposure asserted flat — the pack's own uncertainties list calls it a cinematic specific
Draft (section 2, line 186):
> "rode out in front of the wavering militia, and reined up within about thirty yards of
> the British line."

Pack §4 carries the figure in the HIGH rally block, but Known Uncertainty 7 explicitly
groups "Washington's exposure at 30 yards" with Fitzgerald's hat as "cinematic specifics
[that] are anecdote" (only "the personal rally in close fire" is multiply attested). The
draft's own fact ledger admits the specifics are "carried with care," but the prose
asserts the yardage with no carrier. The hagiography risk here is mild — the paragraph
already hedges the quote and the hat — but the yardage is the one cinematic number stated
as plain fact.
**Fix:** Attach it to the witnesses: "...and reined up, by the accounts of men in the
line, within about thirty yards of the British line." (The parenthetical caveat later in
the paragraph then covers the whole tableau.)

## NIT

### N-1 · "a brilliant delaying action" (dossier sides note, line 33)
"Brilliant" is a superlative the pack never uses (its words: "turned and fought,"
"admired on both sides," "Gallantry and good Conduct"), and "delaying action" mislabels
what Mawhood did — he attacked, was broken, and cut his way out; the pack's delaying
action is Hand's, on January 2. The both-sides generosity is right; the label is wrong.
**Fix:** "Mawhood's regiments turned and went straight at the Americans." Let the quoted
Howe/Washington praise carry the admiration — it does it better than our adjective.

### N-2 · Trevelyan endorsed in house voice: "and for once the grand Victorian sentence is roughly fair" (line 211)
Quoting Trevelyan loosely and attributed is exactly per pack (§7.13). Adding the house's
own endorsement converts an attributed superlative ("effects on the history of the
world") into a near-asserted one. Hedged with "roughly," so a nit, not a should-fix —
but consider letting the quote stand on attribution alone, especially if SF-3's trim
already softens the closing paragraph.

### N-3 · The reader-instruction tic appears twice: "it should be enjoyed as the legend it is" (line 165) and "so enjoy the line, and hold it loosely" (line 190)
Each is honest tradition-framing — the badge content is right — but the identical
"enjoy it" move twice in two sections reads as a formula and edges toward telling the
reader how to feel. Keep one, and let the other carry the caveat plainly ("Wilkinson
wrote that memory down thirty-nine years later in a memoir not famous for reliability"
is already complete without the instruction).

### N-4 · "When the smoke thinned Washington was untouched, waving the line on" (line 186)
Washington being untouched is fact; "when the smoke thinned... waving the line on" is the
staging of the Fitzgerald anecdote continued in narrator voice after the badge. Minor,
because the pack's own §4 narration does the same — but folding it into the anecdote
sentence ("...certain the general was down, until the smoke thinned and he was untouched,
waving the line on") keeps the tableau inside its badge.

### N-5 · "194 British soldiers surrendered" stated flat in Locator 2 caption and section 2 prose
194 is Boatner's figure, MED per the pack, inside the firm ~230–300 total. The dossier
casualties block and section 3 carry the honest range, so the surface is covered overall;
if the fact critic wants a carrier, "some 194" / "the 194 reported" works — flagged here
only because captions are gated surfaces.

## AREA-BY-AREA NOTES (per the framing brief)

1. **Victory proportion** — Battle size kept honest throughout: "small, sharp" scale is
   explicit (~1 hour, ~350-man brigade fight at the core, "the bill for the morning's
   work"), and the weight is correctly placed on the arc (the night march, New Jersey
   abandoned, Morristown, recruiting). The only inflation is SF-2's dossier note and
   SF-3's closing superlative; everywhere else the big claims are attributed (Trevelyan,
   Cresswell, Harcourt, Knox) rather than asserted.
2. **Both-sides** — Strong. Mawhood's breakout and the 17th's charge told as soldiering,
   with Howe's AND Washington's praise quoted; the casualty dispute presented three-way
   with no quiet preference (the "lopsided" framing is fair even on Howe's own return,
   ~276 vs ~70–100); the Leslie burial carried with the Rush connection and full
   humanity. "Admitted" for Howe's return is the pack's own verb, so no flag.
3. **Mercer** — At evidence level: refusal-of-quarter and bayoneting asserted (HIGH),
   wound count withheld, mistaken-for-Washington badged "some traditions hold... cannot
   be proven," Mercer Oak correctly absent. The nine-day vigil and the 30,000-funeral
   figure are carried with "reported" plus an honest gloss on what the number means. The
   Trumbull caption carries the not-at-Princeton / scene-is-a-construction caveat while
   preserving the documented core. No martyr-tableau beyond the pack.
4. **Tradition honesty** — All seven traditions badged in prose, none resolved by fiat;
   Wilkinson named with the 39-years + unreliable-memoirist caveat; the 1905 legend vs
   the documented 1783 commission distinction is exactly right ("no contemporary account
   ties Hamilton to the shot... first appears in print in 1905" vs "the portrait really
   was damaged"). The Potter beat is told as fog-of-war with sympathy, not gotcha. Only
   N-3's repeated "enjoy it" formula to trim.
5. **Emphasis** — No beat weighted beyond pack confidence except the SF items; banned
   lines (Frederick, "fine spot of work") verified absent; the rally told with the
   hagiography valves open (quote hedged, hat badged) except the SF-4 yardage.
6. **Captions/dossier** — Discipline holds across the manifest (Trumbull 1792 "not an
   eyewitness record," Ranney "romantic," Aula Nassovica dated 1760 per pack, Clarke
   House attribution flag carried, no-likeness cards honest, winner line proportionate)
   — except SF-1, the one caption claim that outruns the pack.
