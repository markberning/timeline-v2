## SHOULD-CONSIDER (strengthens; not blocking)

These are real gaps against a maximal telling, but storytelling-first justifies
leaving them out. Listed so the author/integrator can choose:

1. **Pope's four ignored warnings about Longstreet on his flank (Aug 30).** The
   draft says Pope "did not realize Longstreet was on the field at all" — true, but
   the sharper, more damning fact is that Pope was *told* and refused to believe it.
   Reynolds personally rode into Longstreet's men south of the turnpike and reported
   it; Pope dismissed it as mistaken-identity ("that's Porter's corps"). Sources note
   Pope received ~four separate flank warnings and waved them off. This converts
   "Pope was blind" into "Pope was warned and chose not to see," which is a stronger,
   more tragic version of the key decision the draft already centers. Worth one
   sentence in §3 or §4. (WP; Emerging Civil War; Warfare History Network.)

2. **Pope's removal date / the McClellan reinstatement.** Draft says Pope was
   "folded back into McClellan's army" (correctly flagged soft). The crisp facts:
   Army of Virginia dissolved, Pope relieved and exiled to the Dept. of the
   Northwest (Sept 12, 1862), his troops merged into McClellan's Army of the
   Potomac — and Lincoln turning back to the general he'd just sidelined is a nice
   irony given how the campaign was meant to reduce reliance on McClellan. Tightens
   §5's "Pope was finished" beat with a date. (WP Northern Virginia Campaign;
   Britannica.)

3. **Stuart's cavalry deception (dragging brush to raise dust).** A vivid, true
   detail — Confederate cavalry dragged branches to fake marching-column dust clouds,
   feeding Pope's confusion. Pure storytelling color; omit unless a sidebar wants it.
   (WP.) NOT essential.

4. **Chantilly / Ox Hill (Sept 1) coda.** The fact pack offers it (§5/§7); the draft
   wisely cuts it to keep the three-day frame clean. Generals Kearny and Stevens
   killed in a thunderstorm as Lee tried to cut the retreat. A one-line transition to
   the Maryland Campaign *could* use it, but it is correctly optional — the draft's
   jump straight to "Lee pointed north" works. NOT essential.

5. **Strength figures (~65,000 Union vs ~50,000 CSA).** The draft never states the
   army sizes. For a battle whose whole drama is Lee *splitting a smaller force* and
   beating a *larger* one, one number-pair would underline why the flank march was a
   gamble and why the victory was impressive. Currently the reader gets "much larger
   Union force" qualitatively only. Minor; a single clause would do.

## FACT-PACK BLIND SPOTS (flag for the pack, not the draft)

The pack is thorough. Genuine blind spots, in priority order:

1. **Pope's ignored flank warnings / Reynolds's personal discovery of Longstreet.**
   The pack records that "Pope did not realize Longstreet was on the field" (§5) but
   does NOT capture that Pope was *actively warned* (Reynolds rode into Longstreet
   and reported it; ~four warnings dismissed; McDowell sat on Buford's report). This
   is arguably the single most important *decision* of the battle and the pack
   under-supplies it — which is why the draft could only write the weaker "blind"
   version. Add to a future §5 with WP/ECW cites. (See SHOULD-CONSIDER #1.)

2. **Pope's removal specifics.** Pack §10 says the loss drove the army back but does
   not give the Sept 12 relief / Dept. of the Northwest exile / Army of Virginia
   dissolution / merger into the Army of the Potomac. The draft had to hedge ("folded
   back into McClellan's," flagged soft) for lack of a pack line. Add the dates.

3. **Stuart's brush-dragging deception** is absent from the pack. Minor color, but a
   documented, vivid detail the author could not use because it wasn't supplied.

4. **Strength numbers exist in the pack (§2) but were not surfaced as an author
   "use this" hook**, so the draft omitted them. If the campaign's drama (smaller
   force splitting and winning) is to land numerically, the pack should flag the
   65k-vs-50k pair as recommended-for-use, not just available.

None of these blind spots rise to ship-blocking for the DRAFT — the draft's story
is complete without them — but #1 and #2 are worth back-filling into the pack so a
future revision can strengthen the key-decision and aftermath beats.
