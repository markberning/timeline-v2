## MUST-ADD (genuinely essential — should block until addressed)

1. **How the Confederates found the flank "in the air" — Stuart's cavalry reconnaissance.**
   The draft's whole §lee-divides / §the-flank-march hinges on Hooker's right being
   unanchored, but it never explains *how Lee and Jackson knew that*. The historical
   answer is concrete and load-bearing: Stuart's cavalry (intel from Fitzhugh Lee)
   reported the open flank the night of May 1, which is what made the cracker-box plan
   possible. Without it, the flank march reads as Lee psychically intuiting a weak spot.
   One or two sentences in §lee-divides closes the causal hole. (Fact-pack BLIND SPOT —
   the pack never mentions Stuart's recon; it only states the flank *was* in the air.)

2. **Stoneman's cavalry raid — the missing leg of Hooker's plan AND a real cause of the
   defeat.** Hooker's design was a *double* envelopment: while the infantry turned Lee's
   left, Maj. Gen. George Stoneman's cavalry corps was sent on a deep raid to cut Lee's
   supply/rail line (Gordonsville/the Orange & Alexandria RR) and force him to retreat.
   The raid accomplished little — and, critically, it stripped Hooker of his cavalry
   screen, so the Union army was nearly blind during the flank march (part of why
   Jackson's column went undetected). Hooker himself counted Stoneman among the chief
   reasons for the loss. The draft currently gives Hooker's plan with the cavalry leg
   amputated, which both understates the plan's ambition and leaves the "how did 30,000
   men march across the front unseen" question half-answered. (Fact-pack BLIND SPOT — the
   pack omits Stoneman/the cavalry entirely.) A compact mention in §the-plan (the plan's
   cavalry arm) and a clause in §the-flank-march (no Union cavalry screening) suffices —
   does not need its own section.

3. **The Northern consequence: Hooker fired, Meade installed days before Gettysburg.**
   The closing leans hard on "the perfect battle was the beginning of the end" but tells
   only the Confederate half of the consequence. The Union half is essential and is in
   the fact pack (§9): Chancellorsville cost Hooker his command; Lincoln replaced him with
   Meade on June 28, on the eve of Gettysburg. This is the symmetry the ending is reaching
   for and a one-clause add to §hooker-quits or the closing. (In the pack, just not in the
   draft.)

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (strengthens, not blocking)

4. **The burning Chancellor house with wounded inside.** One of the battle's iconic
   images and thematically of a piece with the Wilderness-fire horror the draft already
   uses: on the morning of May 3 a bursting shell set the Chancellor house ablaze and
   troops carried out wounded men (and the Chancellor women) as it burned. It would land
   especially well in §jackson-falls right where Hooker is already on that same porch.
   Verify a citation (NPS Chancellor House Site / ABT confirm ~33 wounded + 3 women) per
   the same [VERIFY]-the-fire discipline already applied.

5. **Lee's 2→3 corps reorganization after Jackson, and the Gettysburg command failure.**
   Make the implied Jackson→Gettysburg line explicit: Lee split the army into three corps
   because no one could fill Jackson's shoes, and the new corps commanders (Ewell on the
   first evening at Gettysburg) hesitated where Jackson would have pressed. Pack §9 has
   it; the draft only gestures. A clause in the closing converts a hint into the actual
   causal argument.

6. **U.S. Ford as the army's lifeline** (already in the pack §4). The draft never names
   the crossing Hooker's main army used to come and go / retreat over. Minor; a
   map-label/one-word add, not prose-essential.

7. **Local-imbalance nuance (May 1: ~48k Confederate vs ~30k Union in the immediate
   sector — Sears's point that Hooker's caution wasn't pure cowardice).** Pack §2 flags
   it. The draft commits fully to the "whipped man / lost his nerve" reading, which is
   the dominant and defensible one; adding the local-numbers nuance would be fairer to
   Hooker but is an interpretive choice, not a factual gap. Optional.

## FACT-PACK BLIND SPOTS (independent of the draft)

- **Stoneman's cavalry raid is entirely absent from the fact pack** — both as a component
  of Hooker's plan and as a cause of the Union cavalry's absence/blindness. This is the
  single biggest comprehensiveness hole upstream; the draft can't cover what the pack
  never gave it. (See MUST-ADD #2.)
- **The pack never explains how Lee learned the flank was "in the air"** (Stuart's recon /
  Fitzhugh Lee). It states the condition, not the discovery. (MUST-ADD #1.)
- **The burning Chancellor house** is not in the pack's Human-Cost section (§7), which
  covers Jackson, the XI Corps, and the Wilderness fires but not this. (SHOULD-CONSIDER #4.)
- **Hooker's relief and Meade's appointment** are present in pack §9 but did not make the
  draft — a transcription gap, not a research gap. (MUST-ADD #3.)
- Pack's casualty *proportions* ("~21–22% vs ~13%") were softened in the draft to "about a
  fifth vs about an eighth" (≈20% vs ≈12.5%); the draft already self-flags this. Not a
  comprehensiveness issue, noted for the fact-check gate.

## BOTTOM LINE
The draft is comprehensive on the battle's dramatic spine and the slavery throughline.
Three connective-tissue items are essential enough to block: (1) Stuart's cavalry recon
that revealed the open flank, (2) Stoneman's raid as the cavalry leg of Hooker's plan +
the resulting Union blindness, and (3) Hooker's relief / Meade's appointment before
Gettysburg. Two of the three are also fact-pack blind spots, so the pack should be
patched, not just the prose. Everything else is polish.
