## BLOCKERS

**B1 · Lee's will burial wish — draft paraphrase diverges from the pack-prescribed
rendering and contradicts the verifiable record**

> (commander bio, line 45): "His will asked that he not be buried in any churchyard;
> his friends buried him in one anyway."
>
> (Section 3 prose, line 269): "His will asked that he not be buried in any churchyard,
> and his friends buried him in one anyway."

The pack (§5, lines 78–79) is explicit: *"His will forbade burial 'in any church or
churchyard'; his friends buried him in Christ Church's yard in Philadelphia anyway, in
an unmarked grave. [HIGH; will-wording paraphrase — verify exact words before quoting]"*
The pack's rendered paraphrase is **"in any church or churchyard"** — both church
building AND churchyard. The draft drops "or church" and writes only "in any
churchyard." That is narrower than the pack says and narrower than what web sources
confirm. Multiple web sources (Wikipedia, NPS, Revolutionary NJ biography) quote the
will as forbidding burial near "any church or churchyard" or forbidding burial near a
church or religious meeting house; the vivid variant circulating is "I have kept so
much bad company when living that I do not choose to continue it when dead" alongside
the burial prohibition. The draft's truncation is a factual understatement.

Web verification: Charles Lee Wikipedia and NPS bio both record the will as forbidding
burial in "any church or churchyard," consistent with the pack's framing. The pack
also notes "in an unmarked grave," which is verifiable and adds the resonant coda the
draft omits.

**Fix:** In both occurrences change to: *"His will asked that he not be buried in any
church or churchyard; his friends buried him in Christ Church's yard in Philadelphia
anyway, in an unmarked grave."* (Two occurrences: bio and Section 3 prose.) This
matches the pack's prescribed paraphrase and the web-verified record.

## SHOULD-FIX

**SF1 · Waldo's diary quote — draft silently drops the final clause; the pack marks
it MED-required and the dropped clause is meaningful**

> (Section 3 prose, line 259): *"she immediately took up his gun and cartridges and
> like a Spartan heroine fought with astonishing bravery."*

The pack (§7, item 11) gives the full Waldo passage as: *"Her gallant, whom she
attended in battle, being shot down, she immediately took up his gun and cartridges
and like a Spartan heroine fought with astonishing bravery, discharging the piece with
as much regularity as any soldier present."* The pack then notes *"[MED-HIGH —
reproduced by Monmouth battlefield historians]"* and the important caveats (it is a
musket, not a cannon; no name). The draft drops *"discharging the piece with as much
regularity as any soldier present"* without indication. This clause is load-bearing:
it is the element that makes Waldo's account a direct comment on Steuben's training
thesis — a woman at the battle performing "with as much regularity as any soldier
present" is the drill's proof made vivid. Suppressing it also creates an asymmetry
with the Martin quote, which the draft renders more fully.

Web verification: the Friends of Monmouth Battlefield website and multiple secondary
sources reproduce the full Waldo passage verbatim, confirming the pack's text.
The Waldo diary source is dated July 3, 1778 (days after the battle), consistent with
the pack's note "days after the battle."

**Fix:** Restore the full Waldo passage in the prose, completing the quote through
*"discharging the piece with as much regularity as any soldier present."* The musket /
no-name caveats following it are already correctly in the draft.

**SF2 · Pennsylvania pension act wording — draft says "services rendered" but the
verifiable text is "her service" or "her services during the revolutionary war"**

> (Section 3 prose, line 259): *"the Pennsylvania legislature, decades later in 1822,
> did pension Mary Hays McCauley by a special act, and pointedly changed the bill's
> reasoning from a widow's relief to her own 'services rendered' in the war."*

The pack (§9.1, KU-10) says the substance is HIGH but notes *"exact statutory text
not seen this session — quote at most 'for her services rendered,' attributed."*
Web verification of the 1822 act produces the actual recorded wording: the bill was
amended from *"widow of a soldier of the Revolutionary War"* to *"for her service
during the revolutionary war"* (some sources give *"for her services during the
revolutionary war"*). The phrase *"services rendered"* is not the statutory wording;
it is a secondary paraphrase used by several historians but not the act's language.
The pack explicitly allows quoting only *"for her services rendered"* as an attributed
summary, and the draft presents it in quotation marks as if from the act, without
attribution to a secondary chain.

**Fix:** Close the quotation marks and render as a characterization, not a statutory
quote: *"the Pennsylvania legislature, decades later in 1822, pensioned Mary Hays
McCauley by a special act that pointedly changed the bill's rationale from a soldier's
widow's relief to her own services during the war."* This matches the verifiable
substance without asserting a specific statutory phrase the pack flags as unconfirmed.

**SF3 · Martin memoir — draft adds "went on with her work" as if quoted; the pack's
verified text does not contain this phrase**

> (Section 3 prose, line 259): *"...which she looked at, by his account, with apparent
> unconcern, and went on with her work."*

The pack (§7, item 10) gives the verified Martin text as the petticoat clause plus
*"apparent unconcern"* continuation: *"While in the act of reaching a cartridge...
a cannon shot from the enemy passed directly between her legs without doing any other
damage than carrying away all the lower part of her petticoat..."* and the "unconcern"
continuation is tagged MED. The pack's own boundary instruction is *"the central
clause + the unconcern continuation"* — and crucially Martin notes she *"attended with
her husband at the piece the whole time"*, which the pack says to use. The phrase *"and
went on with her work"* is a paraphrase that reads as embedded quotation. It is not in
the pack's verified text nor in the web-available transcript of Martin's memoir. The
Friends of Monmouth site's rendering of the Martin passage ends with the petticoat
clause and "apparent unconcern"; it does not include a separate "went on with her
work" clause.

**Fix:** Drop *"and went on with her work"* from the end of the Martin rendering. The
sentence works without it, and the pack's text does not support the phrase. If the
continuation is needed for narrative closure, use the pack-authorized Martin note that
she *"attended with her husband at the piece the whole time"* (pack §7.10).

compression choice, but the pack offers it as available. No factual error either way.

**N3 · "Father of his Country" — "by year's end" slightly compresses the print record
but is consistent with the pack**

> (commander bio, Washington, line 44): *"By year's end 'Father of his Country' was
> attaching to him in print."*
>
> (Section 3 prose, line 271): *"the year ended with 'Father of his Country' current
> in print."*

Pack §2 says *"1778 is when 'Father of his Country' first attaches to him in print
[MED-HIGH]."* Web verification: a Pennsylvania German almanac published in Lancaster,
PA in 1778 first used *"Des Landes Vater"* (Father of the Country) for Washington.
The draft's "by year's end" is technically permissive but not contradicted by the
record — an almanac published in 1778 would have appeared by year's end or earlier.
The pack's own tag is MED-HIGH, consistent with the draft's careful framing. No
factual error, but the reviser should know the exact vehicle was a 1778 Lancaster
almanac if sharper attribution is wanted.
