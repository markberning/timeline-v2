## BLOCKERS

### B1 — "~7,800–8,000 regulars" mislabels the figure (dossier + Section 1; internally contradictory)
- Dossier sides: "strength: ~7,800–8,000 regulars (≈3,981 British + ≈3,116 Brunswick and Hesse-Hanau Germans), plus several hundred Native allies"
- Section 1: "started south from Canada with roughly 8,000 regulars: about 3,981 British infantry from seven regiments, and about 3,116 Germans... With artillerymen, a few hundred Canadians and loyalists, and several hundred Native allies, the fighting force ran toward 8,000"
- Pack §3: 3,981 + 3,116 = **~7,100 regulars**; "with artillerymen, ~250 Canadians/loyalists, and Native allies the fighting force is commonly given as ~7,800–8,000." The draft attaches "regulars" to the 7,800–8,000 number, and the Section 1 sentence contradicts itself (8,000 regulars = 3,981+3,116, then 8,000 again only after adding the auxiliaries). Web check (Wikipedia, Siege of Fort Ticonderoga 1777): "approximately 7,000 regulars" plus ~800 Indians, Canadiens, Loyalists — pack is right, draft label is wrong.
- FIX (dossier): "strength: ~7,800–8,000 fighting men (≈3,981 British and ≈3,116 Brunswick and Hesse-Hanau regulars, plus gunners, Canadians, and loyalists), and several hundred Native allies; 10,000+ counting followers"
- FIX (Section 1): "...started south from Canada with an army built around roughly 7,100 regulars: about 3,981 British... and about 3,116 Germans... With artillerymen, a few hundred Canadians and loyalists, and several hundred Native allies, the fighting force ran toward 8,000..." (the existing second half then reads correctly).

### B2 — Out-of-pack 1758 figures in Section 1 (true, but not in this pack; one over-claims)
Draft: "the largest army yet assembled in North America came up Lake George... the bloodiest British defeat of the French and Indian War... Abercromby ignored the hill, attacked head-on, and lost two thousand men." and later "The British had ignored it as attackers and paid two thousand men."
- The pack (§2) gives only "Abercromby's engineer Lt. Matthew Clerk urged hauling guns up Rattlesnake Hill... Abercromby ignored it" — no army-size, no casualty figure, no "bloodiest" superlative. The draft's ledger cites "figures match the F&I page this pills to," but the pack is the sole permitted source, so these are out-of-pack assertions on a gated surface.
- Web spot-check (Wikipedia, Battle of Carillon): ~16,000 men, "the largest single force ever deployed in North America at the time"; British official return 547 k / 1,356 w / 77 m ≈ 1,980 ≈ "two thousand"; "the bloodiest [battle] of the war, with over 3,000 casualties." So "largest army yet" and "two thousand men" are TRUE and match the in-repo `fi-carillon-factpack.md` ("~1,900–2,000 total casualties"; "largest army yet in North America").
- BUT: the carillon pack itself hedges to "**one of** the bloodiest British defeats of the war"; the draft's "THE bloodiest British defeat of the French and Indian War" over-claims past both packs (the web supports "bloodiest battle of the war" — total both sides — not specifically "bloodiest British defeat").
- FIX (preferred): amend the Ticonderoga pack §2 to carry the three verified figures (army ~16,000, "largest yet assembled in North America," British losses ~2,000) — they are born-verified via the carillon pack — and change the draft's superlative to "**the bloodiest battle of the French and Indian War**" or "one of the bloodiest British defeats of the war." The two "two thousand men" beats can then stand.

### B3 — Image 5 caption asserts an invented detail and misreads as the photo's content
Draft (image 5, Mount Defiance from Fort Ticonderoga, 2009 photo): "This is what the garrison saw on the morning of July 5, 1777, **with red coats and gun carriages visible on the summit**."
- The pack (§4) supports "visible movement/scarlet on the rock" the morning of July 5. **"Gun carriages visible" is in no pack section** — the guns reached the summit "by midday July 5," after the morning sighting; what the garrison saw at discovery was movement and scarlet. And as written the clause reads like a description of the 2009 photograph, which shows neither.
- FIX: "This is what the garrison saw on the morning of July 5, 1777 — except that morning there was movement and a flash of scarlet on the bare summit." (or simply end the caption at "...inside Fort Ticonderoga.")

## SHOULD-FIX

### S1 — Schwalm hardened to "Brunswick"; the source calls him Hessian
Draft: "One Brunswick soldier, Johannes Schwalm, wrote afterward..." Pack §8.8 hedges: "Hessian/Brunswick soldier Johannes Schwalm [MED — via Revolutionary War Journal]." Web check: the Revolutionary War Journal source itself says "**Hessian** Johannes Schwalm wrote in his diary." The draft picked the wrong half of the hedge. FIX: "One German soldier, Johannes Schwalm, wrote afterward..."

### S2 — Schwalm quote first-letter case altered inside quotation marks
Draft: wrote afterward that "if the enemy had made a truly determined effort to defend the post, we could not have taken it." Pack/source text begins "**If** the enemy..." Clipping is permitted (pack: "clipped partial quote"), silently changing the case inside quote marks is not byte-exact. FIX: re-frame so the capital survives — `wrote afterward: "If the enemy had made a truly determined effort to defend the post, we could not have taken it."`

### S3 — Dossier weekly British/German casualty range drifts at the low end
Draft stats: "British/German ~240–270 across the week." Pack §11: ~200–230 (Hubbardton) + ~35–40 (Fort Anne) = **~235–270**. FIX: "~235–270."

### S4 — "3,981 British infantry from seven regiments" narrows the pack twice
Pack §3: "~3,981 British **regulars**" and regiments "**included** the 9th, 20th, 21st, 24th, 47th, 53rd, 62nd" (a non-exhaustive list — flank battalions etc. are inside the 3,981). FIX (Section 1): "about 3,981 British regulars" (drop "infantry from seven regiments").

### S5 — "It became the hardest small battle of the year" (Hubbardton) — out-of-pack superlative
The pack supports "the only pitched battle of the episode," the ~1-in-5 / 40% loss rates, and "only Revolutionary War battle on Vermont soil" — not a year-wide superlative. FIX: cut, or replace with a pack-backed line, e.g. "It became a brutally compressed fight" / lean on the loss-percentage sentence that already closes the paragraph.

### S6 — Image 6 caption: "No period painting of Hubbardton exists" over-broadens the pack
Pack §9.12: "No period painting of Hubbardton exists **on Commons**." The flat universal negative is unverified. FIX: "No period painting of Hubbardton survives in the public collections" → safest: "No period painting of the battle is known; the ground itself is the honest image." (or restore "on Commons"-scoped phrasing in the working caption note and drop the claim from the reader-facing caption: "...the ground of Monument Hill, where the American rearguard turned and fought Fraser's pursuit on July 7, 1777.")

### S7 — Outcome line drops the "+" on the garrison figure
Draft outcome: "cost Britain a 900-man garrison." Pack §6: "~900+ men." Section 3 prose correctly says "900-plus." FIX outcome text: "a garrison of 900-plus men."

## NIT

1. **Pack-internal arithmetic (flagging as pack error, not draft error):** pack §11 gives American casualties "~320–370 (≈40 killed / ≈100 wounded / ≈230–300 captured)" — the components sum to ~370–440, so the headline range can't contain its own parts. The draft inherits "~320–370." Suggest the reviser keep the killed/wounded/captured components (which match §5) and restate the headline as "~370 and up, depending on how prisoners and stragglers are counted," or just lead with the components.
2. Fraser's pursuit force described as "750 to 850 grenadiers and light infantry" — pack composition also includes the 24th Foot and loyalist/Native scouts. Harmless compression; add "and the 24th Foot" if convenient.
3. "tried in 1778 on every charge his enemies could draft" — rhetorical flourish; the pack supports "acquitted of all charges" (and web confirms: court sat Aug 25–Sept 29, 1778, unanimous acquittal "with the highest honor," Congress confirmed Dec 16). Acceptable as voice; noting for the record.
4. Brown's raid rendered "retook the outworks on the portage and Mount Defiance itself for a few days and freed a haul of prisoners" vs pack "retook the Defiance/portage outworks briefly and freed prisoners" — within tolerance.
5. "the easiest invasion route on the continent in either direction" — editorial superlative beyond the pack's "water highway"; conventional and low-risk, but the reviser may soften.
6. "three weeks after the shooting started at Lexington" (Apr 19 → May 10, 1775) — date arithmetic not in the pack but exact; fine.
