## CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Casualties

**Union ~1,384** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Stable across all sources: Wikipedia, ABT, NPS CWSAC all give exactly 1,384 (203 killed, 980 wounded, 201 missing). Draft calls this "firm" — correct.

**Confederate ~2,000 modern estimate, Van Dorn's ~800–1,000 called a lowball** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Van Dorn's official report gave ~800–1,000 killed/wounded plus 200–300 prisoners. All serious sources (NPS CWSAC, Wikipedia) give ~2,000 as the modern scholarly estimate. ABT's facts page gives 2,500; the scholarly range spans 2,000–2,500. The draft's framing as "roughly 2,000" with Van Dorn's figure as an undercount is accurate. The reasons given (attacker shouldn't lose fewer than defender; Native troops omitted; incomplete records) are all confirmed by Wikipedia and ABT.

### Deaths of McCulloch and McIntosh on March 7

**McCulloch killed while scouting alone near a belt of trees, black velvet civilian suit, shot by Osterhaus's skirmishers, 36th Illinois soldier later claimed the shot** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Wikipedia (Battle of Pea Ridge, Benjamin McCulloch article), ABT, NPS, historynet.com, and warfarehistorynetwork.com all confirm: McCulloch was reconnoitering the Union line near the timber on the north side of Oberson's Field, wearing a black velvet civilian suit, and was shot through the heart by skirmishers from Osterhaus's command. Peter Pelican of the 36th Illinois later claimed the shot. The draft says he "rode forward alone to scout" and was shot by "a Union skirmisher's bullet from Osterhaus's men" — confirmed.

**McIntosh killed "less than fifteen minutes later, leading a mounted advance from the front"** — ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED WITH A NUANCE. The "less than fifteen minutes" timing is confirmed by Wikipedia (James M. McIntosh article) and by the NPS Pea Ridge page ("less than 15 minutes after McCulloch" and "in nearly the same spot"). HOWEVER: multiple sources describe McIntosh as leading a **dismounted** advance — he led "his former regiment, the dismounted 2nd Arkansas Mounted Rifles" (Wikipedia, Battle of Pea Ridge; search result from warfarehistorynetwork.com). The NPS page says he "immediately advanced toward the Union line with his former regiment, the 2nd Arkansas Mounted Rifles" suggesting a forward advance, with one source (historynet.com) calling it "a dismounted assault." The draft calls it "a mounted advance from the front." The regiment was "Mounted Rifles" but was acting dismounted at the time; calling it a "mounted advance" is potentially misleading. This is a SHOULD-FIX.

Note: One search result (from civilwarmonths/a summary) gives McIntosh's death as "about 2:00 p.m.," which would put it hours after McCulloch, not 15 minutes. However, this appears to be an error in that secondary summary — the Wikipedia article on James M. McIntosh, the NPS page, and the warfarehistorynetwork source all explicitly say "less than fifteen minutes," which is also in the fact pack. The "2:00 p.m." figure in the summary search result is likely a misattribution (possibly referencing another event). The "less than fifteen minutes" framing is the dominant, better-sourced account and aligns with both the NPS and Wikipedia.

### Van Dorn's Flank March and Curtis's 180° Pivot

**Bentonville Detour night march, attack from the north, Price → Elkhorn Tavern, McCulloch → Leetown** — ✅ CONFIRMED by Wikipedia, ABT, NPS, and multiple sources. The route, the split by Pea Ridge, and the twin objectives are all confirmed.

**Curtis's 180° pivot** — ✅ CONFIRMED. ABT, NPS, and historynet.com all describe Curtis turning his army to face north — "turned his entire army around 180°" (ABT's language). Carr sent to Elkhorn Tavern; Osterhaus and Davis to Leetown. Confirmed.

**Army severed from its ammunition wagons by the march** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Wikipedia, ABT, warfarehistorynetwork.com, and historynet.com all confirm Van Dorn's supply and ammunition trains were left far to the rear (at or near Camp Stephens), separated from the army by Union-held ground. Wikipedia says "Van Dorn was unaware that his supply train had been mistakenly ordered back to Camp Stephens." ABT says reserve ammunition was "a six-hour march away." The draft's framing ("five or six hours away by road") is confirmed.

### Native American Troops (Pike's Cherokee)

**Pike's force ~900 men: Stand Watie's 2nd Cherokee Mounted Rifles (pro-CS), a largely Unionist Cherokee regiment, plus a Texas cavalry squadron; other units didn't arrive in time** — ✅ CONFIRMED. ABT (Cherokees at Pea Ridge) confirms: ~900 troops under Pike, comprising Col. John Drew's 1st Cherokee Mounted Rifles (largely Unionist sympathizers), Col. Stand Watie's 2nd Cherokee Mounted Rifles (pro-Confederate), and Capt. Welch's Texas Cavalry Squadron (~100 men). The Choctaw/Chickasaw and Creek regiments did not arrive in time. Confirmed.

**Stand Watie: signed Treaty of New Echota (1835), forced removal / Trail of Tears, political rival of Cherokee principal chief, only Native American promoted to general on either side, last Confederate general to surrender (>6 weeks after war's main armies)** — ✅ CONFIRMED. All claims confirmed: Treaty of New Echota was signed December 29, 1835 (draft says "1835" ✅). Stand Watie was the only Native American brigadier general on either side (Confederate Army). He surrendered June 23, 1865, at Doaksville — Appomattox was April 9, 1865, making it more than 10 weeks after Appomattox (not "six weeks"). The draft says "more than six weeks after the war's main armies had given up" — this is accurate as a floor; it was actually over 10 weeks. The claim is technically correct (more than six weeks is true), though undershooting.

**Five Tribes named correctly** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Cherokee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Creek, Seminole — correct.

**Slavery in Indian Territory ~14% (~1 in 7)** — ✅ CONFIRMED. ABT and the fact pack both give ~14% enslaved African Americans in Indian Territory. "Roughly one in seven" is an accurate translation of 14%.

### Foster's Farm Scalping

**~8 Iowa dead scalped, several mutilated; NO perpetrator assigned; both Cherokee and Texas cavalry present; probably fewer than a dozen of ~800–900 Native soldiers involved; Pike horrified, issued orders against repeat; Northern press attacked Pike** — ✅ CONFIRMED. ABT (Cherokees at Pea Ridge) states "at least eight of Trimble's cavalrymen were scalped"; perpetrators "never identified"; both Cherokees and Texas cavalry (both with frontier backgrounds where scalping was known) were present; "probably fewer than a dozen" participated; Pike was "horrified" and issued orders. Northern press "pilloried Pike." All confirmed.

**Draft assigns NO blame to a specific perpetrator** — ✅ CONFIRMED CORRECT HANDLING. The draft explicitly states "There is no way, from the surviving evidence, to say which men committed it" and names both Cherokee and Texans as possibilities. This matches ABT's own framing exactly. The draft does NOT assign blame. This is the correct and appropriately hedged presentation.

**Iowa cavalry unit: "a couple of companies of Iowa cavalry" under "their commander"** — ✅ CONFIRMED. ABT confirms two companies of the 3rd Iowa Cavalry under Lt. Col. Henry H. Trimble, who was "seriously wounded." The draft calls him the "Iowans' commander" (not named by name in the narrative, which is fine) and says he was "wounded" — confirmed.

### Day 2 Artillery / Confederate Ammunition Failure

**Sigel massed "on the order of twenty cannon"** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Multiple sources, including NPS (Stop 10: Federal Artillery Day 2) and Wikipedia, confirm Sigel massed 21 cannons on "Welfley's Knoll" west of Elkhorn Tavern. The draft says "on the order of twenty cannon" — accurately approximate.

**"Continual thunder ... day of judgment" — attributed as "one man ... remembered"** — ✅ ACCEPTABLE. The fact pack flags this as [VERIFY, unspecified participant]; the draft uses it only as a paraphrase ("one man on the receiving end remembered as"), not as a hard attributed quote. This is the correct hedged handling.

**Confederate guns fell silent from ammunition shortage; trains five or six hours away; Van Dorn never ordered trains kept close** — ✅ CONFIRMED. ABT, Wikipedia, and NPS all confirm Van Dorn's ammunition trains were roughly a "six-hour march away" (ABT) and that he had not issued orders to keep them close.

**Van Dorn ordered retreat ~11 a.m.; army fell apart in three separate directions** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Wikipedia and historynet.com confirm retreat ordered approximately 11:00 a.m.; Van Dorn's army retreated in multiple directions (Huntsville Road, Bentonville Detour, etc.) — not as a coherent force.

**Wilson's Creek: August 10, 1861; McCulloch + Price victory; Lyon killed; first Union general killed in the war; SW Missouri left in rebel hands** — ✅ CONFIRMED. Date, commanders, outcome, and Lyon's status as first Union general killed all confirmed by Wikipedia (Nathaniel Lyon), NPS, and ABT.

**Price captured Lexington, Missouri, September 1861; forced south by year's end** — ✅ CONFIRMED by ABT and the fact pack citing ABT.

**Price = Missouri politician-general / Missouri State Guard; McCulloch = former Texas Ranger with Confederate commission; feuded all fall 1861** — ✅ CONFIRMED.

**Curtis appointed Christmas Day 1861; Army of the Southwest ~12,100; winter pursuit drove Price out of Missouri; Confederates burned Fayetteville** — ✅ CONFIRMED. CivilWarOnTheWesternBorder.org confirms Christmas Day 1861 appointment. ABT confirms 12,100 force. Fayetteville burned February 21 confirmed by Encyclopedia of Arkansas.

**Van Dorn took formal command Army of the West March 2, 1862; plan to retake Missouri and seize St. Louis** — ✅ CONFIRMED. The fact pack (citing Essential Civil War Curriculum and Encyclopedia of Arkansas) confirms March 2, 1862 as the formal command date for the Army of the West. Van Dorn assumed district command January 29 but met and formally took field command of the concentrated army around March 2–3. The draft's "March 2" is the figure in the fact pack and is acceptable.

**Forced march March 4; council of war (described in draft as occurring before the march); blizzard; ~50 miles in 3 days; stripped to 40 rounds / 3 days' rations / no tents or heavy clothing; Van Dorn ill, rode in wagon** — ✅ CONFIRMED. The council of war was held March 3 (warfarehistorynetwork.com); the march departed March 4 in blizzard conditions. The draft says "he held a council of war and ordered an immediate offensive" — not giving the council date, which is fine. The light-march orders (one weapon, 40 rounds, 3 days' rations, no tents/heavy clothing) are confirmed across multiple sources.

**~12,000 fit men by battle's start (down from ~16,000)** — ✅ CONFIRMED by warfarehistorynetwork.com.

**Van Dorn ordered east to Corinth; arrived April 23; stripped Arkansas of defenders; too late for Shiloh** — ✅ CONFIRMED. The April 23 arrival date is confirmed by the fact pack (citing Encyclopedia of Arkansas) and one search result. Shiloh was April 6–7, 1862. Van Dorn arrived after Shiloh. Arkansas stripped of defenders — confirmed. The draft attributes the order to "the Confederate high command" without naming Johnston or Beauregard — technically correct (the order came through Johnston/Beauregard channels), and is a safe hedge given ambiguity in sourcing.

## SHOULD-FIX FINDINGS

**1. McIntosh's "mounted advance" — likely dismounted**  
The draft says McIntosh was killed "leading a mounted advance from the front." Multiple sources indicate he was leading a dismounted advance with his former regiment, the 2nd Arkansas Mounted Rifles (which was acting as dismounted infantry at this action — Wikipedia and warfarehistorynetwork.com both describe "dismounted" or "led his former regiment into the attack" without cavalry language). The NPS page says he "immediately advanced toward the Union line." The Wikipedia article on McIntosh says only "leading an advance." The fact pack says "leading a cavalry advance from the front" — but the unit was dismounted. Recommend revising to "leading an advance from the front" (dropping "mounted"), which all sources support and avoids the misleading implication of a cavalry charge.

**2. Stand Watie's surrender — "more than six weeks" understates the gap**  
The draft says Watie was "the last Confederate general to surrender, more than six weeks after the war's main armies had given up." Watie surrendered June 23, 1865; Appomattox was April 9, 1865 — that is **75 days, or more than 10 weeks**. "More than six weeks" is technically true but materially understates it. Recommend: "more than ten weeks after Lee's surrender at Appomattox" or "nearly three months after Appomattox."
