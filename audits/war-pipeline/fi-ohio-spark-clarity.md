## MUST-FIX (genuine comprehension breakers)

1. **"casus belli"** — untranslated Latin, no definition. A first-time reader hits a wall. This is the single fully opaque phrase in the chapter.

2. **"regulars vs. provincial troops"** — the friction between Mackay and Washington is explained by rank hierarchy, but that hierarchy is never explained. A first-timer cannot understand why the arrival of 100 soldiers *caused problems* for Washington rather than just being welcome reinforcement. The Fort Necessity defeat's human context is partially lost without this.

3. **"Covenant Chain"** — called "their long alliance with the British," which is a near-miss: technically correct but undersells that this was a formal diplomatic institution, not just good relations. The phrase "broken in 1753" carries real diplomatic weight that a zero-knowledge reader can't feel without a slightly fuller gloss. This is the Albany section's key setup — if the reader doesn't understand what the Covenant Chain was and why losing it was alarming, the whole urgency of the Albany Congress is muted.

---

## SHOULD-FIX (minor, won't block comprehension but create stumbles)

- **"lieutenant governor"** — add a short parenthetical explaining the title means he was running the colony for the Crown.
- **"colonial charters"** — add a phrase explaining these were the founding legal documents describing each colony's territory.
- **"Mingo/Seneca"** — add a one-line gloss at first mention in § Forts, not only at the backstory section.
- **"bastions"** — either gloss or drop the word.
- **"Ensign"** — gloss as lowest commissioned rank.
- **"Sieur de"** — either drop or gloss as a French title.
- **"Onondaga"** — add a geographic tag at first mention.
- **"Six Nations"** — a brief parenthetical naming them (or just noting "the six member nations of the Iroquois Confederacy") would help.
- **Tanaghrisson's first introduction** — add one grounding sentence so the reader isn't in two-section suspense about who he is.

---

## Overall Verdict

The draft is remarkably clear for its length and density. A 15-year-old with no prior knowledge can follow the main thread — two empires, one young officer, one Native leader pulling the strings — without difficulty. The voice is strong, the logic is sequential, and the motives are explained where they matter most (Tanaghrisson's section is excellent).

The three MUST-FIX items are all fixable in a single line each. The "regulars vs. provincial" gap is the worst single comprehension failure because it makes the Fort Necessity section feel unmotivated; "casus belli" is the most obviously broken word (pure Latin, zero context); the Covenant Chain near-miss is a close third.
