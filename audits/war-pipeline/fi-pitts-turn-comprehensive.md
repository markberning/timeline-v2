## Verdict Summary

### MUST-ADD (blocking — revise before this ships)

1. **Treaty of Easton — the Ohio nations' own calculation, named concretely:** The Walking Purchase fraud (1737), Teedyuscung's conference-by-conference extraction of concessions since 1756, and the military calculus of impending French defeat are what made 1758 the specific tipping point. The draft leaves the reader with only the structural framing (farm vs. fort) and "their own reasons." That's not enough — the specificity is the story. One substantive paragraph or a strong sentence cluster. This is the single most important gap.

2. **Indigenous warriors at Carillon (~250) must be named in the battle section.** They are present in the fact pack; absent from the draft. A French defensive victory at 3,500 vs. 15,000 should not appear as a purely regular-army affair. One clause or sentence.

### SHOULD-CONSIDER (quality improvements, not blocking)

- Pitt's partnership shift: add a before-state data point (colonial contributions pre-Pitt were minimal) to show the scale of the change, not just describe the policy.
- Mi'kmaq fate after Louisbourg capitulation: one sentence.
- Ohio nations' names at Grant's Defeat (Shawnee, Mingo, Ohio Lenape) for before/after contrast with Easton.
- Quaker/Friendly Association role in creating treaty conditions — adds complexity to Teedyuscung's political leverage.
- Iroquois role at Easton as nominal overlords who legitimized the structure.
- Montcalm primary-source voice from Carillon (for the dossier, not necessarily the spine chapter).
- Forbes Road's modern legacy (US Route 30) — closer detail for the dossier.
