# NEWCOMER-CLARITY CRITIC · Battle of Princeton draft (rev-princeton)

Draft reviewed: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-princeton-draft.md`
Reader model: zero prior knowledge; has NOT read the Trenton page; sections read independently.

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

The hard structural requirements mostly hold: Section 1 stands alone without the
Trenton page (the Trenton recap, Cornwallis's motive, and the trap geometry are
all built in two clauses each); the night-march decision, the campfire trick, and
Mercer's why-was-he-off-to-the-side are all explicitly reasoned; the Jan 2 evening
→ night → dawn Jan 3 → ~hour fight → same-day march-away timeline is clean across
all three sections; rifles/bayonet, grapeshot, Hessians, Continental Army, militia,
fords, Post Road, and Associators are all glossed in parentheses at first use.

But five reader-facing comprehension breaks remain (two of them terms the brief
explicitly names — "council of war" and "winter quarters" — plus Howe appearing on
four surfaces with no identification anywhere, "the college" at Nassau Hall never
being established, and an internal-jargon role line on the Cornwallis commander
card). With those and the should-fixes below, this passes.

---

## BLOCKER

**B1. "council of war" never glossed (Section 1, para 4).**
> "Across the creek, Washington held a council of war, and the council refused both obvious options."
The newcomer does not know this is a meeting, not a tribunal or a body of
government. Proposed: "Washington held a council of war (a formal meeting of his
senior officers to decide the army's next move), and the council refused both
obvious options."

**B2. "winter quarters" never glossed (Section 3, para 1 + dossier outcome text).**
> "…and on January 6 it went into winter quarters at Morristown, perched where it could threaten the flank of any British move across New Jersey."
This term carries the whole "why does the campaign just end?" answer, and it is
undefined. Proposed: "…went into winter quarters (the fixed camp where an
eighteenth-century army sat out the cold months, since winter made marching and
supply nearly impossible) at Morristown…" With that gloss, "why Morristown ends
the campaign" is fully closed; without it, the campaign appears to stop for no
reason. The outcome text's "The army wintered in the hills at Morristown" is fine
once the section gloss exists, but the section is where it must live.

**B3. Howe is never identified on ANY surface, and he appears on four.**
- Casualties block: "Howe's official return admitted 18 killed…"
- Outcome text: "…pushed Howe to pull his outposts back…"
- Section 2: "Howe's official dispatch praised the brigade's 'Gallantry and good Conduct'…"
- Section 3: "General Howe's official return admitted 18 killed…"
The newcomer has met Cornwallis and Mawhood; Howe arrives with no role, no side
beyond inference, and outranks both. Gloss at first use PER SURFACE; proposed
wording: "General William Howe (the British commander in chief in America, and
Cornwallis's superior)". Minimum: full gloss in Section 2 and Section 3 first
uses, plus the casualties block (the dossier's likely first-read surface).
Section 1's quote-intro mentions "Genl Howe" inside Washington's quote — that one
is acceptable as-is only if the surrounding sentence identifies him; currently it
does not ("Washington explained the thinking in his own words" gives no help).

**B4. "the college" at Nassau Hall — no college has been established at Princeton (Section 2, para 6).**
> "A remnant, mostly of the 40th, barricaded itself inside Nassau Hall, the college's great stone building and then the largest academic building in America."
"The college's" assumes the reader knows Princeton is a college town; nothing in
the draft says so before this sentence, and "the college got its revenge" and
"the college's own archivists" later in the same paragraph lean on it twice more.
Proposed: "…barricaded itself inside Nassau Hall, the great stone main building
of the College of New Jersey (the colonial college in the town, today's Princeton
University) and then the largest academic building in America." The Nassau Hall
image caption ("the last British position of the battle") is fine once the prose
carries this.

**B5. Cornwallis commander-card role line is internal jargon on a reader surface (dossier, commanders).**
> "Charles, Earl Cornwallis | role: Lt. Gen., the Assunpink frame, British"
"The Assunpink frame" is pipeline-speak; a newcomer cannot parse it (and barely
knows what the Assunpink is at card-reading time). Proposed role line: "Lt. Gen.,
commanding the British army at Trenton" (or "…the army that trapped Washington at
Trenton").

## SHOULD-FIX

**S1. "Ten Crucial Days" used as an unglossed proper name (dossier stats + outcome verdict).**
> "Winner: America, the capstone of the Ten Crucial Days"
The dossier note's "closing the ten days that turned the war around" primes the
idea but never attaches it to the capitalized name. Gloss once on the dossier:
"the Ten Crucial Days (the name historians give December 25–January 3: Trenton,
the Assunpink stand, and Princeton)."

**S2. "rear guard" undefined (dossier note + Section 2 MEANWHILE).**
> note: "…and broke its rear guard at sunrise…" · MEANWHILE: "The fox he was going to bag in the morning had spent the morning eating his rear guard."
Term of art the brief names. In the note, simplest fix is substitution: "…and
smashed the brigade he had left behind him at Princeton." In the MEANWHILE, gloss:
"…eating his rear guard (the force an army leaves behind it to cover its back)."

**S3. The Delaware never located; the crossing direction is unreadable (Section 1, para 1).**
> "Then, on the day after Christmas, he had crossed the icy Delaware and destroyed the Hessian garrison…"
The reader is never told the Delaware divides New Jersey from Pennsylvania, so
"crossed," "pulled back across the river with his prizes," and "crossed again" have
no from/to. Proposed, at the river's first mention: "…the Delaware River (the wide
river dividing New Jersey from Pennsylvania; Washington's army had been camped on
the Pennsylvania side)…" One clause fixes all three crossings.

**S4. Mawhood not tagged British at his first appearance in Section 2 (para 2).**
> "At that same hour, Lieutenant Colonel Charles Mawhood was marching the 17th Regiment of Foot and part of the 55th OUT of Princeton…"
His side is inferable only from "the lightly held British post" a paragraph
earlier. Sections must carry role + side at first use: "…the British garrison
commander, Lieutenant Colonel Charles Mawhood, was marching…" Also gloss
"Regiment of Foot (an infantry regiment)" here — first use in the section.

**S5. Hand unintroduced in Section 2 (para 4).**
> "Washington galloped up with reinforcements, New England Continentals under Hitchcock and Hand's Pennsylvania riflemen…"
Section 1 introduced "Colonel Edward Hand," but sections read independently.
Proposed: "…and Colonel Edward Hand's Pennsylvania riflemen…"

**S6. Hitchcock has no rank or first name (Section 2, para 4 — same sentence as S5).**
Every other named officer carries a rank. Add rank + first name from the fact
pack (do not invent; if the pack carries only the surname, render "Continentals
under Colonel Hitchcock"-style per pack).

**S7. Greene has no role in Section 3 (para 1).**
> "…Henry Knox (the artillery chief) and Nathanael Greene helped talk Washington out of reaching for it…"
Knox is glossed; Greene is not, and Section 2's "Major General Nathanael Greene"
doesn't carry over. Proposed: "…and Major General Nathanael Greene (one of his
senior generals)…"

**S8. New Brunswick unexplained in Section 2 (para 6).**
> "…the 55th and the 40th Foot fell back toward New Brunswick."
In Section 2 the reader has never heard of New Brunswick (the pay-chest gloss is
Section 1's). Proposed: "…fell back toward New Brunswick (the British supply base
farther up the road toward New York)." No mileage exists in the pack — the
relative phrasing is the right move; do not invent a number.

**S9. Rush's substantive gloss arrives one use too late (Section 3, paras 2–3).**
> first use: "…at the request of Dr. Benjamin Rush, a family friend." · second use: "…with Rush (a physician and a signer of the Declaration of Independence) attending him…"
Swap the glosses: identify him fully at first appearance ("Dr. Benjamin Rush, a
Philadelphia physician and a signer of the Declaration of Independence and a
friend of the Leslie family"), and let the second use go bare or short.

**S10. Yorktown unglossed where the toast tradition lands (Section 3, para 5; also Cornwallis bio).**
> "…that Cornwallis himself, at the surrender dinner at Yorktown in 1781, told Washington that his brightest laurels would come from the banks of the Delaware rather than the Chesapeake."
The reader is not told WHO surrendered at Yorktown, so the line's whole point
(the loser toasting the winner) and the Delaware-vs-Chesapeake geography are
unreadable. Proposed: "…at the dinner after Cornwallis surrendered his army to
Washington at Yorktown, Virginia, in 1781 (the defeat, on the Chesapeake Bay,
that effectively ended the war)…" The Cornwallis bio's "ended it surrendering to
him at Yorktown" is self-contained enough to stand.

**S11. WHY taking Princeton empties New Jersey is asserted, not mechanized (Section 3, para 4).**
> "Howe pulled the British outpost line all the way back from the Delaware to a cramped corridor around New Brunswick and Perth Amboy, abandoning nearly all of New Jersey for the winter…"
The reader has watched two garrisons destroyed but the causal link is left
implicit. One clause closes it: "…pulled the outpost line back — after Trenton and
Princeton, any garrison posted out alone across New Jersey was a target Washington
could destroy before help arrived — to a cramped corridor…" (rendered with
parentheses, not dashes, per house rules: "…pulled the British outpost line all
the way back (after Trenton and Princeton, any post strung out alone across New
Jersey was a meal Washington could eat before help arrived) to a cramped
corridor…").

**S12. Dossier sides/stats carry unglossed terms of art.**
- "most of its Continentals' enlistments had expired" (AMERICAN note) — gloss
  "Continentals (its full-time regular soldiers)".
- "with dragoons and guns" (BRITISH strength) — gloss "dragoons (mounted
  soldiers)".
- "17th, 40th, and 55th Regiments of Foot" (BRITISH strength) — "(infantry
  regiments)".
- "The 17th Foot's bayonet charge" (BRITISH note) — the bayonet gloss lives in
  Section 2; on the dossier surface either gloss it ("bayonet (the blade on a
  musket's muzzle) charge") or rephrase "charge with the bayonet, the musket's
  muzzle blade".
- Stats duration: "(roughly 90 minutes including Nassau Hall)" — Nassau Hall is
  meaningless on the stats surface; render "(roughly 90 minutes including the
  final surrender at the college building in town)".

**S13. Morristown gets no distance or direction scale anywhere in prose.**
The brief asks the geography be built in miles. The pack carries no Morristown
mileage, so do not invent one — but the dates support "three days' march north":
proposed (Section 3, para 1): "…the column marched north, three days through
Somerset Courthouse and Pluckemin, into the hills behind the Watchung Mountains,
and on January 6…" This gives the newcomer the scale the map can't.

**S14. Hero caption drops two unintroduced names on the page's first surface (dossier heroCaption + IMAGES #1).**
> "…Washington on his chestnut horse Nelson rallying the militia, Mercer fallen, Moulder's guns at work, the Clarke farmhouse behind."
Proposed: "…Gen. Hugh Mercer fallen, Capt. Joseph Moulder's cannon at work…"
(Clarke farmhouse is acceptable as a depicted-place label.)

## NIT

**N1.** "vanguard" unglossed (Section 1 para 2 "Cornwallis's vanguard reached
Trenton at dusk"; Section 2 MEANWHILE "his vanguard reached Stony Brook").
Gloss once per section: "vanguard (the leading troops of the column)."

**N2.** "Amboy" (dossier outcome) vs "Perth Amboy" (Section 3) — use "Perth
Amboy" both places; a newcomer can't tell they're the same town.

**N3.** "Howe's official return" (Section 3) — "return" is a term of art; gloss
"(his official casualty report)".

**N4.** "garrison" carries the story but is never glossed (S1 "the Hessian
garrison," "Mawhood's garrison"). One gloss at first use: "(the troops stationed
to hold a town)".

**N5.** "volleys" (S2, "traded volleys at close range") — "(massed simultaneous
musket fire)" if room allows.

**N6.** "two four-pounder cannon" (S2) — "(light cannon firing four-pound balls)".

**N7.** "flanked" (S2, "Outnumbered now and flanked") — "(attacked from the
side)".

**N8.** Washington bio (dossier): "French and Indian War veteran" — gloss
"(Britain's earlier war with France in America)".

**N9.** Mercer bio (dossier): "served the Jacobite army at Culloden in 1746" —
gloss "(the failed Scottish rising against the Crown, crushed at Culloden)".

**N10.** Mawhood bio (dossier): "a cavalry cornet in 1752" — "(the most junior
cavalry officer's rank)".

**N11.** "ten-dollar bounty" (S1) — "(a cash bonus for re-enlisting)" if room
allows; mostly inferable.

**N12.** "Burlington" (S1, "baggage was sent quietly south to Burlington") —
"(a town down the Delaware)".

**N13.** Hero caption "a Maryland ensign" — "(the most junior officer rank)";
or "a junior Maryland officer".

**N14.** Section blurbs (dossier): blurb 1 opens "Cornwallis reached Trenton at
dusk with 5,500 men" with no role; acceptable if the blurbs render below the
commanders/note, but if they render above, lead with "the British general
Cornwallis."

**N15.** "foraging parties" (S3) — "(detachments sent out to gather food and
hay)" if room allows; "Forage War" is otherwise well-handled by apposition.

## REQUIREMENT-BY-REQUIREMENT

1. **Terms inline-defined at first use, parentheses:** mostly yes (rifles/bayonet,
   grapeshot, Hessians, Continental Army ×3 sections, militia, fords, Post Road
   ×2 sections, Associators ×2 surfaces, pay chest, Forage War). Misses: council
   of war (B1), winter quarters (B2), rear guard (S2), plus the dossier-surface
   terms (S12) and the NIT list. All glosses that do exist correctly use
   parentheses, not em-dashes.
2. **People with role + side per section:** strong overall. Misses: Howe
   everywhere (B3), Mawhood's side tag at S2 first use (S4), Hand in S2 (S5),
   Hitchcock (S6), Greene in S3 (S7), Rush gloss order (S9), Cornwallis role line
   on the card (B5).
3. **Section 1 stands alone:** PASS. Trenton recapped ("destroyed the Hessian
   garrison… taking some 900 prisoners"), Cornwallis's motive ("marched from
   Princeton… to finish the rebellion"), and the trap ("the stream that runs into
   the Delaware… with the river at Washington's back and no good way out") are
   all built without the Trenton page — modulo the Delaware orientation gap (S3).
4. **Geography from scratch:** good skeleton — Princeton 11 mi up the Post Road,
   Clarke farm 1.5 mi SW of town, the 12–14-mile night route, "twelve miles to
   the northeast" landing point. Gaps: the Delaware's two banks (S3), New
   Brunswick in S2 (S8), Morristown scale (S13).
5. **Causal chains:** night-march-vs-fight-vs-retreat — explicit and excellent
   (para 4 of S1 walks all three options). Campfires — explicit ("doing
   everything an army settling in to fight at dawn would do"). Mercer off to the
   side — explicit (Greene's wing peeling left to break the Stony Brook bridge
   "and block the road Cornwallis would come back on"; Mercer "detached… to deal
   with the British"). Princeton → NJ abandoned — asserted, mechanism missing
   (S11). Morristown ends the campaign — flank-threat clause present, but hinges
   on the unglossed "winter quarters" (B2).
6. **Timeline:** PASS. Jan 2 dusk arrival → three twilight repulses → the
   morning decision → ~midnight march → dawn Jan 3 collision → "roughly an hour
   to ninety minutes" → mid-morning march-away → Jan 6 Morristown. Stats
   duration line agrees with the prose.
