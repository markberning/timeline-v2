# FRAMING CRITIC — Vincennes draft (rev-vincennes)

*Critic pass 2026-06-12 against `rev-vincennes-draft.md` (all 330 lines, incl. FACT LEDGER
and compliance lists) and `rev-vincennes-factpack.md` (sole source). Scope: fairness,
emphasis, interpretive honesty. Cross-checked all handle-with-care surfaces against the
pack's DISPUTED register (§12) and the do-not-use-as-fact blacklist (§13/§APOCRYPHA).*

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

No blocker. The draft's heaviest framing duties are all met: the executions are not sanitized
and not melodramatized — they land in the middle of the story with Clark's own stated rationale,
Hamilton's labeled hostile-witness testimony, and the unresolved personal-participation question
handled at exactly the pack's evidence level; the "Hair-Buyer" epithet is correctly badged as
propaganda with the Pennsylvania scalp-bounty beside it; the "won the Northwest" overclaim is
never asserted and is explicitly contested across both the dossier outcome and Section 3 prose;
the lakes-vs-Wabash distinction is doing real narrative work (the killing is staged "at the
expense of men who were not their own"); Macutté Mong is named; the habitants' four oaths are
read as survival politics; the blacklist is clean. Four should-fixes, one polish. None rises to
a blocker; together they tip the same way — toward mild Clark heroization and mild Hamilton
caricature at surfaces the pipeline checks individually.

## BLOCKER

None.

## SHOULD-FIX

**1. The dossier `note:` field asserts the "teeth in the bluff" with no acknowledgment that
the executions were an atrocity and not just a tactical instrument.**

The dossier note is a reader-facing summary surface (the first thing a reader sees before
entering a section). It reads: "The teeth in the bluff were four captured warriors tomahawked
in sight of the fort gate." This is accurate as far as it goes, but the register is purely
tactical — it frames the executions entirely through Clark's stated rationale (the bluff got
its teeth). The pack's framing doctrine is "the cost of the frontier war, told precisely" — and
the "cost" framing requires that the victims be visible as people who died, not as mechanism.
The dossier surface has no space for the full treatment the prose gives, but it needs one
phrase that holds the weight. The current phrasing makes it read like a clever finishing move.
FIX: One additional clause naming what actually happened: e.g., "The teeth in the bluff were
four captured warriors tomahawked in sight of the fort gate — the price the frontier war
extracted from men who were nowhere near it when it chose them." Or more plainly: "He also
had four captured warriors executed in front of the fort gate, a calculated act that broke the
garrison's will and taught the watching nations that British protection was worthless." The
note should hold both the instrument and the cost in the same breath, as the prose does, not
the instrument alone.

**2. The Section 1 "Hair-Buyer" introduction front-loads the name and defers its dismantling
too far — the gap lets the label do unchecked propaganda work for three sections.**

Section 1 introduces the Hair-Buyer epithet cleanly ("Hold that name; we will come back to
what it does and does not mean") and then never returns to it until Section 3. That is a
~4,000-word gap across two full sections during which "the Hair-Buyer" is the only label the
reader has for Hamilton. The pack insists the epithet is propaganda doing propaganda work, and
the honest version of it is a both-sides documentation. The Section 3 payoff is excellent. But
the defer-across-two-sections structure means the word "Hair-Buyer" is the name readers see
repeated in Section 2 (the siege, Hamilton's own journal prominently quoted) and in the
commanders panel — without the context that the per-scalp charge was never documented and was
eventually dropped by Jefferson's own government. Readers who stop after Section 2 (the
dominant mobile reading pattern) leave with the propaganda framing intact.
FIX: The Section 1 promise ("we will come back to what it does and does not mean") needs
either a brief one-clause partial payoff at the moment of introduction — e.g., "The frontier
called him the Hair-Buyer General, the accusation being that he paid per scalp; the record
doesn't go quite that far, but it doesn't have to — hold the fuller accounting for later" — or
the word "Hair-Buyer" should be avoided as a standalone label for Hamilton in Section 2 prose
and in the commanders panel, with "the British governor" or "Hamilton" used instead until the
Section 3 documentation earns the epithet.

**3. Clark's ruthlessness in the commander bio is correctly named but immediately softened
with "when he judged ruthlessness useful," a qualifier that reads as sympathetic framing.**

The bio reads: "Tall, red-haired, persuasive, and ruthless when he judged ruthlessness useful."
The pack bio template (§3) uses this exact phrase. The phrase is not wrong — Clark genuinely
was calculating rather than reflexively cruel — but in the commander bio, "ruthless when he
judged ruthlessness useful" is doing the work of explaining away the executions before the
reader reaches them. It converts a description of what he did into an account of his good
judgment: he was ruthless *when useful*, implying the restraint is the baseline. That framing
makes Clark a man in control of his violence, which may or may not be the honest register for
a commander who had prisoners publicly tomahawked in a sequence Hamilton's own journal calls a
"butchering in succession." The bio should let the facts do the characterizing.
FIX: Render the ruthlessness as a fact without the sympathetic qualifier: e.g., "Tall,
red-haired, persuasive, and capable of deliberate brutality — the executions outside Fort
Sackville's gate were his order and his calculation." This is consistent with the draft's own
best prose (Section 2: "Clark did it in full view of the walls on purpose, and he later said
why"), and it removes the framing that the qualifier "when useful" implies.

**4. The Section 3 "less than the legend says" passage for Native nations omits the executions'
consequences for the Lakes nations as a distinct harm — it tells what the victory did NOT win
without telling what the executions cost the people who bore the cost.**

Section 3 correctly records that the fall of Fort Sackville meant less than the legend says for
Native nations — Detroit never fell, Egushawa fought on, the frontier war got worse, and the
longer catastrophe was unrestrained settlement. This is the pack's §6 framing rendered
accurately. But the passage treats the campaign's Native-nations impact entirely at the
structural level (what it won or failed to win for the American cause) and never circles back
to the executed men themselves: the Lakes warriors who came in from a raid, walked into a
town that had changed hands, and were killed in a public ceremony in front of a fort gate for
the strategic message their deaths would send. The pack names Macutté Mong by name precisely
so the dead are not abstracted into a lesson. The Section 2 prose does this right. Section 3's
retrospective accounting of what the campaign meant for Native nations should include a sentence
that returns to the specific men who bore the most immediate cost — not to moralize, but because
the story of "what Vincennes meant for the nations" is incomplete without it.
FIX: One sentence in the Section 3 Native-nations paragraph, after noting that the frontier war
got worse: e.g., "The men who actually bore the sharpest immediate cost were the Odawa warriors
from the Lakes, far from home on a raiding mission, who walked into the town and were killed in
a ceremony staged for the watching nations — men whose deaths were useful to Clark's lesson
precisely because they were not the nations he was teaching."

## POLISH

**5. The `sideNames` dossier field labels the British side as "BRITISH" but Hamilton's garrison
was majority French-Canadian militia. The label is accurate for the regulars but could mislead
readers who never leave the dossier.**

The pack is explicit (§3): "33 regulars of the 8th (King's) Regiment, ~36–40 Detroit militia/
volunteers, plus Indian Department officers; 79 surrendered... Hamilton counted only '35 or 36'
of the garrison as reliably loyal — the French-Canadian militia would not fight their kin in
town." The label "BRITISH" on the side card is standard war-vertical format and not wrong, but
the sides-note (correctly) specifies the mixed composition. No change to `sideNames` is needed
given the notes field's handling. This is a reminder for the reviser: confirm the note's
"French-Canadian militia would not die fighting their own kin" language is not dropped in any
revision, because that detail is what makes the surrender structurally legible.

## CHECKED AND PASSING (for the reviser's confidence)

- **The executions — central placement and register:** Placed in the middle of Section 2,
  not in a footnote, not in the ledger only. The "this is the cost of the frontier war, made
  visible, and it belongs in the middle of the story, not in a footnote" closing sentence is
  correct framing doctrine and correctly delivered.

- **Macutté Mong named:** The young Odawa war chief is named in the prose ("a young Odawa war
  chief named Macutté Mong") with Hamilton's journal account paraphrased in close detail. He
  is not anonymous. The pack's instruction — "Native warriors as named actors with stakes vs
  anonymous props" — is followed.

- **Lakes vs Wabash distinction doing work:** The sentence "The men who died were Lakes warriors
  far from home, not from the Wabash towns next door, and that was part of the calculation:
  the killing was staged to teach the watching nations a lesson at the expense of men who were
  not their own" is the pack's core analytical point (§6) rendered in full and in the right
  place. This is the draft's best framing sentence.

- **The "Hair-Buyer" Section 3 payoff:** The full both-sides documentation is executed
  correctly: raids organized/supplied, rewards-in-goods yes; per-scalp payment no
  documentary evidence, charge dropped by Virginia itself; AND Pennsylvania's literal
  written scalp bounty ($2,500 Continental / $3,000 prisoner). "Both of those things are true
  at once, and the page holds them together" is exactly the right close for this surface.

- **Hamilton as caricature check:** The commander bio gives Hamilton credit as "a capable
  administrator and amateur artist" with the Harvard sketches noted; his December counterstroke
  called "bold and well run"; his journal called "candid and observant." The bio earns the
  "Hair-Buyer" role rather than asserting it. His decision to winter with a reduced garrison is
  rendered as a strategic miscalculation (he trusted the floods to do his scouting), not as
  stupidity or villainy.

- **The "won the Northwest" overclaim:** NEVER asserted. The dossier outcome, the fact ledger,
  and Section 3 prose all use the real-vs-claimed split. The Downes quote is the explicit
  corrective. "The march made the legend. The negotiators made the map." is accurate and
  covers all surfaces.

- **Clark's fall carried without moralizing:** The debt/drink/stroke/amputation arc lands as
  a plain biographical fact ("The hero of Vincennes at 26 was a debt-ruined, hard-drinking,
  half-forgotten man by his fifties") without the narrator editorializing. The pack's instruction
  ("let the arc land without moralizing") is followed.

- **Memoir-only color correctly quarantined:** Face-blackening, war whoop, drummer boy, "land
  the tallest men first" — all rendered only as "Clark told it... a decade later... partly to
  salvage his reputation" with the explicit note that none of it is in Bowman or the 1779
  letter. The blacklist is clean.

- **Hamilton's personal participation DISPUTED:** The Hamilton-and-Schieffelin accusations
  are labeled as hostile witnesses with every reason to blacken Clark; the American silence is
  not treated as exculpatory; "Historians have never resolved it, and the honest answer is that
  we do not know" is the correct close. This is the pack's evidence level rendered exactly.

- **The 4-vs-5 executed:** Written "four, five by some counts" throughout. The known-uncertainties
  handling is correct.

- **Habitants' four oaths read as survival politics:** The MEANWHILE block in Section 1
  ("Read it as survival politics, not fickleness") is the right register. "Decisive twice"
  (Gibault; the militia refusing to fight kin) gives the habitants narrative agency rather than
  treating them as passive background.

- **Tobacco's Son as named actor with stakes:** Named in Section 2, his offer of warriors
  declined for named reasons, his 1778 reasoning ("the Big Knife was in the right") rendered
  as his own stated position (correctly tagged MED / via Clark's telling). He is not
  anonymous scenery.

- **Egushawa named and survived:** Named in Section 3 as "Pontiac's political heir, who had
  advised Hamilton and run his scouts, escaped Vincennes with his war band intact and fought on
  into the 1790s." The pack's instruction that the Native threat was "blunted but not
  eliminated" is carried.

- **The Northwest Ordinance generation's self-image (the Memorial beat):** The 1936 building
  is correctly named as "high-imperial commemoration of exactly the conquest narrative the
  record will not quite support." The fort footprint being unlocated is noted. The building is
  used as a "what we made of it" beat, not as validation of the legend.

- **Blacklist compliance:** No per-scalp price for Hamilton; "170 looked like 1,000" kept soft
  ("several times the real number"); the drummer boy and face-blackening provenance-tagged;
  Helm's cannon-and-whiskey scene badged as tradition; "Pontiac's son" not used; no
  "empire with 170 men" assertion; the Maj. Hay turns-pale scene omitted. All clean.

- **Frontier-war "savagery" tropes:** No "savage" or "barbaric" applied to Native warriors
  or their methods; "raid" and "war party" used in the neutral register the pack uses. The
  executions are called an "atrocity" and a "calculated act" — neither noble nor bestial,
  instrumental, which is what the record supports.

- **Pills and off-field links:** Two native-nations pills (S1 and S3); none in S2, which is
  correct per war link doctrine (battle section, not a leaf). The pill-twice question is
  flagged for the link critic in the compliance notes, which is the right place for it.
