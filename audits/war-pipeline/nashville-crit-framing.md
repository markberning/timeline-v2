# Nashville — FRAMING / LOST CAUSE GATE (crit-framing)

> War content pipeline, step 4 (critic, framing gate). Adversarial. Tests for Lost Cause
> distortion, soft-pedaling, false balance, romanticization, euphemism, presentism, unfair
> sympathy, and whether human reality is sanitized OR made gratuitous. FIRST and
> ship-blocking: does the section name **slavery** as the cause, in its OWN prose?
>
> **VERDICT: FAIR (PASS).** Slavery is named, repeatedly, in the section's own voice, and
> the USCT-at-Overton-Hill cause-hook is told centrally, accurately, and without
> tokenism. No MUST-FIX. Two SHOULD-FIX notes on tone calibration below.

---

## A. NAME THE CAUSE — the ship-blocking first check

**REQUIRED:** the section must, in its own prose (not a footnote, not a quote it's merely
relaying), name slavery as what the Confederacy — and the war — was fought over.

**RESULT: PRESENT and CENTRAL.** Not borderline. The §4 `overton-hill` section opens with:

> "The Confederacy existed to preserve **slavery.** Eleven states had broken from the
> United States rather than accept any limit on the right to own human beings, and the
> Army of Tennessee in front of Nashville was the field army of that slaveholders'
> republic in the West."

It is reasserted at the close of §4 ("**Slavery was the reason.** Overton Hill is the
proof") and again in §5 ("the very men the enemy army had gone to war to keep enslaved").
This is the strongest possible satisfaction of the gate — slavery is not merely named, it
is made the *organizing claim* of the centerpiece section. **MUST-FIX gate: CLEARED.**

The draft also does the harder thing the gate really wants: it refuses the terrain-as-
subject substitution that lets Lost Cause framing hide ("the railroad, the redoubts, the
hills with people's names on them — is *terrain.* Slavery is the reason the armies were
there at all"). That sentence is an explicit, in-voice rejection of the euphemism that
usually does the soft-pedaling. Excellent.

## B. Is the USCT cause-hook told accurately and centrally (not tokenized)?

**CENTRAL — not tokenized.** Overton Hill is the longest, most weighted section in the
draft (§4), titled "The hill the freedmen charged." The USCT are the subject, not a
diversity garnish:

- They are given agency and stakes ("sent at the strongest ground on the field to help
  destroy the army of the people who claimed to own them").
- The cost is told honestly and specifically (13th USCT: ~221 / ~40%, single heaviest
  regimental loss of the two days; sister-regiment losses; men shot down on the parapet).
- The valor is corroborated from BOTH sides — the enemy's own report (Holtzclaw), the
  Union commander (Steedman), and Thomas — which is exactly how you avoid both tokenism and
  hagiography: the claim is sourced, not asserted.
- It explicitly refuses the footnote framing: "the thing that makes Overton Hill impossible
  to file away as a footnote."

**Is the "formerly-enslaved-men-destroying-the-slaveholders'-army" throughline honest to
the record?** YES, with the correct restraint. The draft asserts the USCT were "free and
formerly enslaved … men" as *sound general USCT history* but does NOT claim a precise
regiment-by-regiment "raised in Tennessee / formerly enslaved" origin for the 12th/13th/
100th — which the fact pack flagged [VERIFY] as unconfirmed unit-by-unit. The draft's
ledger confirms this restraint was deliberate. So the throughline is told at the level the
evidence supports (these were Black regiments, free and formerly enslaved men, fighting the
slaveholders' army) without overclaiming a specific biographical origin it can't prove.
That is the honest line. FAIR.

**Tactical honesty:** the draft is careful that the assault *failed* tactically ("the
Confederates held the hill … thrown back with terrible loss") and reframes the meaning
correctly ("failing in the right place can still win a battle" — the assault drained Hood's
left). It does NOT inflate a repulse into a triumph. This is the honest version: the USCT's
significance is real (proving ground, drained the flank, won acknowledgment) without
pretending they took the hill. FAIR and important — a lesser draft would have fudged this.

## C. Lost Cause / distortion checks

- **Soft-pedaling the cause:** NONE. Slavery is foregrounded, not buried. PASS.
- **False balance ("both sides," "states' rights," "way of life"):** NONE. No euphemism for
  slavery; "slaveholders' republic" is used pointedly. PASS.
- **Romanticization of the Confederacy / "noble lost cause":** NONE. Hood's army is "the
  wreckage," its commander "a fighter to the point of self-destruction," Franklin "the kind
  of attack that gets men killed by the thousand for nothing." No moonlight-and-magnolias.
  PASS.
- **Unfair sympathy / hero-laundering:** Forrest is handled carefully. He is credited with
  the rearguard (accurate) but NOT laundered — the draft does not reach for the "wizard of
  the saddle" hagiography and frames his role with irony ("the unit that might have
  prevented the disaster … was the only thing that saved anyone from it"), not admiration.
  Given Forrest's history (Fort Pillow, postwar Klan), keeping him to his documented
  tactical role without celebration is the right call. **SHOULD-FIX (light):** consider a
  half-clause acknowledging *who* Forrest was, since the section names him repeatedly with
  some operational respect; not required here (this is a Nashville battle section, not a
  Forrest profile, and the draft never praises the man), but flagging it as the one place a
  reader could come away with an uncomplicated impression of him.
- **Euphemism for the human cost:** NONE — the draft is plain ("slaughter pen," "mow down
  … almost to a man," "shot down on and at the Confederate parapet"). PASS.

## D. Sanitized vs. gratuitous — the human-reality calibration

- **Sanitized?** No — the deaths are concrete and named at the unit level.
- **Gratuitous?** Mostly no. The violence is in service of meaning (the parapet deaths *are*
  the point about USCT valor and cost). **SHOULD-FIX (light):** the §3 close ("One of them
  would become a slaughter pen, and the other would become the place where the line finally
  broke") and a few other lines lean slightly purple/foreshadow-heavy. This is a house-voice
  question, not a framing violation — the imagery is earned, not invented — but a touch
  more restraint on the foreshadowing drumbeat would keep the gravity from tipping into
  melodrama. Non-blocking.

## E. Presentism check

- The draft applies a clear moral frame (slavery was the cause, the Confederacy fought for
  it) — this is NOT objectionable presentism; it is the historical consensus and the
  explicitly-mandated frame. It does not anachronistically import 21st-century language onto
  the soldiers or pretend the USCT understood themselves in modern terms; it lets the
  period testimony (Holtzclaw, Steedman, Thomas) carry the contemporary acknowledgment.
- Thomas's "the question is settled; negro soldiers will fight" is handled with the right
  historical framing — it is presented as the *period's* grudging, real-time recognition
  (and the draft keeps the period word "negro" inside the attributed-substance frame rather
  than scrubbing or sensationalizing it). The draft does not present this as unalloyed
  enlightenment — it sits inside a battle where Black men had to die in front of a parapet
  to earn a white general's acknowledgment, and the draft lets that weight land. FAIR.

## F. Is the throughline overclaimed?

Checked the one place framing gates often over-rotate: does the draft claim the USCT *won*
Nashville, or that Overton Hill was the decisive blow? **No.** It is careful — the decisive
break was Shy's Hill in the west (§5), and the Overton assault's role is correctly the
*secondary* one of draining the Confederate right. The draft gives the USCT their honest due
(centerpiece of the *cause*, proving ground, flank-drainer, morally central) without
inflating their *tactical* role into something the record doesn't support. This is the
distinction a distorted draft would blur, and this one keeps it sharp. FAIR.

The USCT-at-Overton cause-hook is accurate, central, non-tokenized, and honest about being

**SHOULD-FIX: 2** (light — a half-clause on who Forrest was; trim the purple/foreshadow
