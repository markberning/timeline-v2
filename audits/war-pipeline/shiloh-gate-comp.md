## CHECKLIST STATUS

### 1. Unentrenched camps + the surprise debate
**STATUS: COVERED — STRONG**
Draft opens with this cleanly: Grant's no-entrench decision, his stated reasoning (preserve offensive spirit), Sherman's explicit agreement and quote, and the surprise debate handled honestly ("modern historians land on yes"). The April 3 Grant letter quote is included. No gaps.

### 2. Johnston's plan and the march from Corinth
**STATUS: COVERED — ADEQUATE**
Johnston's strategic logic (destroy Grant before Buell arrives), Corinth as the prize, April 3 departure, three-day march, rain/bad roads/inexperience, Beauregard's cold feet and Johnston overruling him — all present. The corps-line structure of the Confederate attack (three parallel lines) is noted.

THIN spot: Johnston's specific tactical plan — to push the Union left into Owl Creek swamps and cut off the river — is nowhere in the draft. Wikipedia and ABT both call this out as the plan's key feature (drive northwest, not northeast, to prevent Union escape to the river). The draft only says the Confederate lines advanced "toward the river" without explaining the intended axis was designed to trap Grant, not push him to the boats. This is a nuance, not a must-add for a selective telling.

### 3. Sherman's stand and redemption arc
**STATUS: COVERED — STRONG**
Full redemption arc: prior breakdown, whispers about his sanity, the day of his life, two wounds, three horses, stubborn fighting retreat, reputation made. The emotional beat ("walking out of Shiloh with his reputation not just restored but made") lands well.

THIN: The famous Sherman/Grant evening exchange — "Well Grant, we've had the devil's own day, haven't we?" / "Lick 'em tomorrow, though" — is in the fact pack (Section 8, rated RELIABLE, in Sherman's own Memoirs) but is completely absent from the draft. This is the most quotable moment in the entire battle and the cleanest two-line encapsulation of Grant's character. Its absence in the "reckoning" section (or end of Day 1) is a genuine hole.

### 4. The Hornet's Nest / Sunken Road
**STATUS: COVERED — STRONG**
Six-hour stand, Prentiss + W.H.L. Wallace, 8–14 charge count flagged as uncertain, Bragg's frontal attrition, Ruggles' 62-gun battery (largest N. American concentration), W.H.L. Wallace's mortal wound, Prentiss's 2,200-man surrender. The Sunken Road myth and the Bloody Pond legend are both handled correctly with italic asides. Prentiss vs. W.H.L. Wallace credit debate noted.

### 5. Johnston's death
**STATUS: COVERED — STRONG**
All elements: 2:30 pm timing, right knee, popliteal artery, survivable wound, 1837 duel numbness, physician sent to Union prisoners, boot filling with blood, Harris's question, last words, Bell Farm, highest-ranking officer killed, friendly-fire possibility hedged correctly, Jefferson Davis quote. This section is the draft's best.

### 6. The Peach Orchard / Bloody Pond
**STATUS: COVERED — ADEQUATE with one flag**
Peach Orchard named and placed correctly (Hurlbut, open artillery ground, Johnston killed nearby). The "blossoms falling like snow" line is already flagged in the draft's own VERIFY section as undocumented — good self-awareness. Bloody Pond handled correctly as probable legend.

THIN: The Peach Orchard's role as a distinct, ferocious *contested zone* is underplayed — the draft mentions it as context for Johnston's death rather than as its own brutal fight that ground down both sides before the Hornet's Nest fell. ABT and Wikipedia both treat it as a major tactical node. Not a must-add for a selective telling, but worth a sentence if the section is revised.

### 7. Grant's last line + gunboats
**STATUS: COVERED — ADEQUATE**
Webster's 50-gun ridge, USS Tyler and USS Lexington, opening times (~4:00 and ~4:30 pm), shelling Confederate infantry, line holds. The dramatic detail that the Tyler fired "32 rounds in 10 minutes" (per Wikipedia) is missing but not required.

MISSING: The human drama of the landing itself — thousands of demoralized Union soldiers cowering under the riverbank, refusing to fight, Grant and Sherman riding among them. This is a standard Shiloh beat in every account (Wikipedia, ABT, HistoryNet all cover it) and the draft skips it entirely. The army that held the last line was partly sustained by the refuse-to-break remnants under the bluff. The contrast between the huddled men under the bank and the line that held above them is a vivid storytelling moment. This is a SHOULD-CONSIDER, not a must-add.

### 8. Beauregard's halt decision
**STATUS: COVERED — ADEQUATE**
Covered: disorganized army, false Buell report, low ammo, "complete victory" telegram to Richmond, wrong on all counts. The "Meanwhile in Richmond" sidebar handles this well tonally.

THIN: The Confederate plundering of Union camps — soldiers stopping to eat, drink, and loot rather than pressing the attack — is a significant reason historians give for Beauregard's chaos and the halt decision. Every major source (ABT, Wikipedia, HistoryNet) cites it. The draft omits it entirely. Beauregard's halt looks like a pure command failure in the draft; the camp-plundering context explains why the Confederate army was so disorganized by evening. This is a SHOULD-CONSIDER.

### 9. Buell's arrival + Lew Wallace's march (Day 2 setup)
**STATUS: COVERED — STRONG**
Both get dedicated treatment. Lew Wallace: Crump's Landing, wrong-road controversy, modern vindication, lost written orders, ~7:15 pm arrival, Ben-Hur kicker. Buell: Nelson, Crittenden, McCook (~18,000), steamboats, overnight crossing, thunderstorm. Forrest's ignored warning included. The Lew Wallace section is notably good.

### 10. The Day 2 counterattack
**STATUS: COVERED — ADEQUATE**
Nelson's brigades striking first, Confederate left, Grant's army advancing simultaneously, landmark-by-landmark reversal, Hornet's Nest retaken ~noon, Sherman/McClernand slowed near Shiloh Church, Beauregard's recognition and withdrawal timeline, Breckinridge rear guard, last guns off at ~3:30. Solid.

THIN: The *grinding, hard* character of Day 2 fighting — it was not a walkover, it took all day, and the Confederates fought stubbornly even while retreating — is undersold. The draft's "the direction never changed" framing is accurate but makes Day 2 sound inevitable and easy. A beat on why it was still costly would strengthen it.

### 11. The casualty shock

The "more than all previous American wars" myth is explicitly called false and corrected — exactly right. Samuel Todd (Mary Todd Lincoln's brother) included. The emotional landing ("a scale of loss the country had simply never imagined") works.

### 12. Grant nearly relieved

Drinking rumors revived, calls for removal, Halleck's effective sidelining, Lincoln's "I can't spare this man" used with full McClure-1892 hedging, apocryphal verdict correctly stated, Lincoln keeping Grant framed as consequential. All correct.

### 13. The strategic meaning toward Corinth/Vicksburg

"Meanwhile in the Western Theater" sidebar: western army wrecked, Johnston dead, Corinth fell end of May, Mississippi River campaign, Vicksburg, Grant established as most important general. Grant's "complete conquest" quote from Memoirs closes the whole piece cleanly. The short-war illusion section is the draft's strongest thematic landing.

## MUST-ADD (ship-blocking gaps)

**MUST-ADD 1 — The Sherman/Grant evening exchange**
The "Well Grant, we've had the devil's own day" / "Lick 'em tomorrow, though" exchange (Sherman's *Memoirs*; rated RELIABLE in the fact pack) is the single most quotable moment of the entire battle and a perfect encapsulation of both men's character at the hinge of the war. It belongs at the close of Day 1 or as a bridge to Day 2. The fact pack explicitly documents it (Section 8); the draft inexplicably omits it. A SELECTIVE telling of Shiloh that skips this exchange is missing a load-bearing moment.

**MUST-ADD 2 — Grant's resolve quote on the night of April 6**
The pack documents Grant's "Retreat? No! I propose to attack at daylight and whip them" (Section 8, Pack — attributed to McPherson's account, also variant "not beaten yet by a damn sight"). This is distinct from the Sherman exchange and equally essential: it establishes Grant's character before Day 2 begins. The draft jumps from Beauregard halting to "overnight reinforcements" without showing Grant's mental state in the hinge moment. Both quotes (the Sherman exchange AND Grant's night resolve) can coexist in a paragraph; together they are the emotional fulcrum between the two days.

**MUST-ADD 3 — Confederate camp plundering on Day 1 evening**
Every major independent source (ABT, Wikipedia, HistoryNet) identifies the Confederate soldiers stopping to loot and eat captured Union camps as a significant contributor to the army's disorganization by evening and Beauregard's halt decision. The draft presents Beauregard's halt as the result of battle exhaustion + false intelligence alone, omitting this. It's not just color — it explains *why* Beauregard's army was in worse shape than raw casualty counts suggested. One sentence suffices; its absence leaves the halt feeling like a pure command miscalculation when it was also a discipline collapse.

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (strong additions, not ship-blocking)

**SC-1 — Soldiers huddled under the riverbank bluff**
The thousands of demoralized Union men sheltering under the Pittsburg Landing bluff, refusing to fight, are a standard Shiloh image in every account. Grant and Sherman riding among them, unable to get them moving, sets up the last-line drama. The draft skips this entirely. It's vivid, it's documented, and it contextualizes how desperate the situation actually was before the last line held.

**SC-2 — Johnston's plan to cut off the river (trap, not push)**
Johnston intended to drive the Union left northwest into the Owl Creek swamps — to trap Grant away from the river, not push him toward it. The draft says the Confederates advanced "toward the river" but never explains the trap design. Knowing the plan's geometry makes the Confederate failure more resonant (the plan partially worked but the line of battle rotated away from the intended axis). Brief; high payoff for a reader who wants to understand how the battle unfolded.

**SC-3 — The Peach Orchard as a distinct brutal fight**
Currently subordinated entirely to Johnston's death. Worth one sentence of stand-alone treatment as a ferocious contested zone, not just a backdrop.

**SC-4 — Day 2 grinding character**
The draft makes Day 2 sound clean and inevitable. A brief note on how stubbornly the Confederates fought back, how it was costly fighting all day, would honor the soldiers and make Beauregard's eventual withdrawal more credible than a simple "he saw the truth."

## FACT-PACK BLIND SPOTS

The pack is comprehensive. Three gaps worth flagging for any follow-up research:

1. **"Four million" enslaved people figure** — Already flagged in the draft's own VERIFY list. Not in the pack. Standard historical estimate (1860 census: ~3.9 million enslaved); safe to use as "nearly four million" or soften to "millions."

2. **W.H.L. Wallace / Lew Wallace relationship** — Pack treats them as distinct men but never states they were unrelated. The draft calls them "no relation, despite the matching surname." Easily verifiable: they were not related. W.H.L. (William Harvey Lamb) Wallace was from Illinois; Lew Wallace was from Indiana; different families entirely. The draft phrasing is correct but the pack should have confirmed it.

3. **The Sherman/Grant evening exchange sourcing** — Pack Section 8 rates this RELIABLE (Sherman's *Memoirs* + corroborating witnesses). But the draft author did not use it. This is the pack's biggest omission-by-neglect: it documented the quote and then the author didn't deploy it.

4. **Van Dorn's reinforcements** — Wikipedia notes Beauregard withdrew partly because he learned Gen. Earl Van Dorn's forces (expected reinforcement) would not arrive in time. The pack does not mention Van Dorn at all. This is a small but real blind spot in the pack's Day 2 withdrawal explanation.
