# STORYTELLING CRITIC — Battles of Lexington & Concord (`rev-lexington`)

Draft: `audits/war-pipeline/rev-lexington-draft.md`
Bar: `audits/war-pipeline/fi-carillon-draft.md` (shipped, gated)
Lane: storytelling only; accuracy is the fact critic's lane. Any proposed added language below must clear the fact critic / fact pack before integration.

## VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FIXES

This is close to the bar and in places above it. The arc is right: section 1 is a genuine
thriller of hours with a clean cliffhanger ("That message would produce Percy's brigade,
and it would save the column's life"); section 2 escalates correctly (green → bridge →
road → Menotomy → the Neck) and closes on the best two-sentence beat in the draft ("But
the militia did not go home. They camped in a ring around Boston."); section 3 lands the
inversion hard ("by morning the king's army in America was surrounded in its own
headquarters town"). The first-shot mystery is a story asset, not a hedge ("a shot came
from somewhere... What followed is certain." / "they were produced as weapons, not as
evidence" / "The leak is a fact. The leaker is a blank."). The badged legends are handled
with grace, not throat-clearing ("his actual sworn deposition, dispersal and no firing, is
better testimony anyway"). Voice ship-blockers: CLEAR — no meta-narration, no drama-bold,
no em-dashes in reader prose (the em-dashes present are all in internal production notes,
not shipped surfaces).

What keeps it from a clean PASS: the one thing the brief and the Carillon bar both demand
that the draft does not have — a sustained inside-it passage. Carillon's "Imagine being
one of them" paragraph at the abatis is the house signature; this day's natural home for
it is the gauntlet of the retreat, and the retreat paragraph is instead an itinerary
(At Meriam's Corner... At a double bend... At the town line... at Fiske Hill...). We are
told the road, never put on it. That also leaves brief item 6 half-paid: the British
soldiers' day (rowed at 11 p.m., soaked wait, ~21 hours under arms, fired on by men they
never see) lives in the dossier note and in single clauses, never in the body. One
passage fixes both. Everything else is duplication trim, which conveniently is also the
answer to the section-2 overage.

## BLOCKER

**B1. No sustained inside-it passage; the retreat is narrated as an itinerary.**
The retreat paragraph ("At Meriam's Corner, a mile east... and the retreat was nearing a
rout.") covers four ambush sites in catalog rhythm. Compare the bar (Carillon): "Imagine
being one of them. You hit a belt of felled trees too thick to run through... close enough
that you can hear the order to fire before each one lands." The Lexington draft never does
this once across three sections.

Fix: insert one ~90–120 word interior passage, regulars' POV, between "The pattern held
for 16 miles." and "At a double bend in the Lincoln woods..." — written from facts already
in the ledger (mustered 10 p.m., soaked wait at Lechmere, march at 2 a.m., ~21 hours under
arms, walls/houses on both sides, flanker mechanics, ambushers who fire and are gone).
Sketch, for shape only (fact critic must clear final wording):

> Imagine the road from inside the column. You have been awake since the muster on the
> Back Bay beach the night before, you waded ashore through a marsh and stood soaked for
> hours, you marched seventeen miles in the dark, and you have been fighting since dawn.
> Now every stone wall on both sides of the road is a firing line that empties and refills.
> The men shooting at you fire from behind cover and are gone before the smoke clears,
> and the only answer is the flanking parties sweeping the fields, so the column walks,
> and bleeds, and walks. Behind you the road is marked with the men who could not keep up.

This single addition also discharges the both-sides-humanity requirement (see S5). With
the trims below it costs the section nothing net.

## SHOULD-FIX

**S1. Section 3 MEANWHILE duplicates the paragraph directly above it, nearly beat for
beat.** Body: "Within days the Provincial Congress had collected roughly a hundred
depositions... and had an account on a fast schooner to Britain; the American version ran
in London newspapers nearly two weeks before Gage's official report arrived." MEANWHILE:
"The Provincial Congress put its hundred depositions on a fast schooner... beat Gage's
official report to the London papers by nearly two weeks." The bar's MEANWHILEs always add
a new angle (Carillon: "a single death, at the right place, can be worth more than a
thousand soldiers"). Keep the MEANWHILE's two genuinely new ideas ("the version of April 19
that reached Britain first would frame everything after" / "It was never meant to settle
anything. It was ammunition.") and cut its restated logistics; the body paragraph keeps
the schooner and the two weeks.

**S2. Section 1 MEANWHILE re-explains what paragraphs 1 and 5 already established.**
"a standing alarm network of riders, bells, and signal guns, and town militia companies
with officers, muster rolls, and a rapid-response tier that had been drilling for months"
is the third pass at the system (after "None of this was spontaneous. It was a system" and
"It propagated through the pre-built system, fresh riders, church bells, signal guns,
drums"). The MEANWHILE's earners are the legend-vs-reality frame and the closing image
("it was running before their boats touched the Cambridge shore"). Compress to those:
legend remembers one rider and impulse; the reality was better organized than the raid it
defeated; running before the boats touched the shore. Cut the inventory re-list.

**S3. The Hancock-Clarke figure is unanchored — the prose never mentions the house.**
Section 1's inline image is the Hancock-Clarke House, captioned "where Revere arrived from
Boston with the warning," but the body never says where Revere was riding TO or why. One
clause in the riders paragraph anchors it, e.g. "...and Paul Revere across the water by
the Charlestown crossing, riding for the Hancock-Clarke House in Lexington" (destination
detail to be confirmed against the pack by the fact critic). An image the text never
touches reads as decoration; the bar's figures are all paid off in prose.

**S4. Heath has a commander card but zero presence in the story.** His dossier bio carries
the day's best afternoon-tactics idea — the militia formed "a moving ring of skirmishers
around Percy's column rather than letting them mass into targets for the cannon" — and the
section 2 prose then uses the ring ("The militia ring of skirmishers that had hung on the
column all afternoon broke off at the Neck") without ever crediting or explaining it. One
clause in the Percy paragraph fixes the orphaned card AND gives the American side its only
named afternoon commander: e.g. "...while Brigadier General William Heath, the first
American general officer on a field of the war, fed the arriving companies into a moving
ring of skirmishers around the column" (keep the bio's "by his own later account" hedge if
the fact critic requires it in body prose).

**S5. The British soldiers' bodily day is asserted in the dossier, not rendered in prose.**
"under arms about 21 hours and marched roughly 35 to 40 miles" lives only in the sides
note; in the body, British exhaustion is one clause ("Smith's exhausted men collapsed
inside its shelter"). The militia get Harrington dying at his wife's feet, Whittemore,
Jason Russell on his doorstep; the regulars get a casualty percentage. B1's interior
passage is the fix — no separate change needed if B1 lands. (Menotomy's both-ways
ugliness, by contrast, is already honest and well balanced: Barker's "The plundering was
shameful" against "the militia shot men from ambush all day, and both sides' propaganda
inflated both." Keep exactly as is.)

**S6. (Conditional) Section 1 ends before the green fills.** The brief's thriller arc runs
leak → lanterns → riders → "the Green filling at dawn"; the draft cuts from Smith's 2 a.m.
march straight to section 2's "a little before 5 a.m." with Parker's 77 already formed.
The handoff is clean and works, but one bridge sentence at section 1's end — Parker's
company mustering on the green in the dark while the column was still on the road — would
complete the hours-ticking structure and make the section's two clocks (column and
countryside) converge on screen. Only if the fact pack supports the muster timing; if it
does not, the current cut is acceptable.

## TRIM (the section-2 overage: ~110 words of duplication, not beats)

Every beat in section 2 earns its place — the Concord search pays off the empty-warehouse
setup, the two-hour delay escalates ("while the hills kept filling"), Menotomy is the peak
and must not be touched. The flab is material that section 3 also carries:

**T1. The Emerson badge runs twice.** Section 2: "The phrase is not contemporary: Emerson
wrote it in 1837, for the hymn sung at the dedication of the bridge monument." Section 3:
"Emerson's shot that rang round the world belongs to the bridge, sixty-two years after the
fact." Cut the section 2 sentence (~22 words); keep "and that volley, not Lexington, is
what 'the shot heard round the world' commemorates" where it is, and let section 3's
memory roundup carry the vintage.

**T2. The first-shot passage pre-spends section 3's deposition war.** In "Some regulars
swore it came from behind a hedge or a tavern corner; both sides later swore the other
began it; and a captured British officer..." cut "both sides later swore the other began
it;" (~9 words) — that exact idea is section 3's subject ("Both sides understood within
hours that who fired first... mattered as much as who won the road"). Keep Gould in
section 2 (at the moment, where the mystery lives); section 3's "Strip the instruments
away" reprise works as a deliberate echo.

**T3. The hatchet paragraph carries the rumor's full career, which section 3 re-tells.**
Section 3 already has "carried the scalping rumor from the North Bridge along with it."
In section 2, cut the burial-deposition sentence ("The Provincial Congress answered with
sworn statements from the men who buried the bodies: 'neither of those persons were
scalped, nor their ears cut off.'", ~30 words) — or, if the verbatim denial is wanted
somewhere, move it to section 3's deposition paragraph where it belongs. Keep the killing,
the monstrous growth, and "some historians read the regulars' rage that afternoon partly
through the rumor" — that clause is the causal thread to Menotomy and must stay.

Net: T1+T2+T3 ≈ −60 words, B1 ≈ +100; section 2 lands roughly where it is now but with
zero duplication and the signature passage in place. If a harder cut is wanted, the
remaining candidate is the parenthetical "(the town later fished most back out)" — but I
recommend keeping it; it is exactly the house voice.

## NIT

**N1. The signature inversion line ships near-verbatim on three surfaces.** Outcome text:
"The army that marched out to prevent a war marched back into a siege." Section 3 body:
"the army that had marched out to prevent a war marched back into a siege." Section 3
blurb: "The raid that was supposed to prevent a war had started one instead." Dossier and
prose are different reading moments, but a reader who taps through hits it twice in a
minute. Vary the outcome-text version (e.g. end it on the siege ring instead) and let the
section 3 body keep the line — it is the better placement.

**N2. "exactly this" tic ×3.** Dossier note: "organizing for exactly this day for months";
section 1: "a system... waiting for a trigger" then "built for exactly this night" is fine
once, but the s1 MEANWHILE adds "had been built for exactly this." Vary one (S2's
MEANWHILE compression likely removes it for free).

**N3. "Police raid" frame appears in both the sides note ("A police raid on paper") and
section 1 ("On paper it was a police raid"). Different surfaces, same sentence shape;
acceptable, but if touching the dossier anyway, vary one.
