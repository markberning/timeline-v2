## BLOCKER (1)

### B1. The no-quarter QUOTE block fails byte-verification, and the pack's cited source does not contain the quote (PACK ERROR + unverifiable wording)

Draft (QUOTE block, section `the-night-march`):
> The Hessians and our brave Highlanders gave no quarters; and it was a fine sight to see with what alacrity they dispatched the rebels with their bayonets.

Pack §8.3 claims the clause "matches multiple printings" and that the "full text [is] on Wikisource" in E.J. Lowell, *The Hessians*, ch. 6. **Both claims failed the gate-time re-verify:**

1. **The quote is NOT in Lowell ch. 6.** I fetched the Wikisource chapter ("6 The Battle of Long Island, August, 1776") and an independent mirror (americanwars.org/hessians-revolution/chapter-VI.htm). Neither contains it. The chapter's no-quarter material is Colonel von Heeringen (a *Hessian*): "The English did not give much quarter, and constantly urged our people to do the like." The pack's source citation is wrong.
2. **The draft's exact wording appears nowhere on the web.** A search for the literal string `"gave no quarters; and it was a fine sight to see with what alacrity they dispatched the rebels"` returns zero genuine hits. The form that IS attested (American Battlefield Trust, "Hessians Auxiliaries," and echoed across secondary sources) reads, verbatim:
   > "The Hessians and our brave Highlanders gave no quarters, and it was a fine sight to see with what alacrity they dispatched rebels with their bayonets, after we had surrounded them so that they could not resist."
   Two drifts in the draft: **semicolon for the attested comma** after "quarters," and **"the rebels" for the attested "rebels."**
3. **The provenance chain in the draft's prose ("One British officer's letter, printed in the London papers that year") is itself unverified.** ABT and the other quality sources I could reach attribute it only to "a British officer"; I could not confirm a 1776 London-paper printing in any reachable primary source.

**Exact fix (either):**
- Replace the QUOTE block text with the ABT-attested wording (preferably the full sentence including "after we had surrounded them so that they could not resist."), and soften the prose lead-in from "printed in the London papers that year, put it plainly" to "One British officer wrote home" (or similar) unless a 1776 printing is actually located and cited; **also correct the pack §8.3** (drop the Lowell/Wikisource citation, cite ABT or the located primary).
- OR drop the QUOTE block entirely and keep only the already-pack-supported paraphrase sentence ("British and Hessian letters home boast of bayoneting rebels as they tried to give up...").

## SHOULD-FIX (5)

### S1. Dossier wounded figure "~290–319" is an over-read of an ambiguous pack itemization and makes the casualty line internally impossible
Draft: "British/Hessian: ~370–400 total (~63–69 killed, **~290–319 wounded**, ~30 missing)".
The draft built 290–319 by treating the pack's "British ~290–293 wounded" as British-only and adding the 26 Hessian wounded. But the web shows the standard split is British 267 wounded + Hessian 26 = **293 combined** (Wikipedia; ABT total 388) — the pack's 290–293 is effectively already the combined figure. As written, the components' high ends (69 + 319 + 30 = 418) exceed the draft's own ~400 total ceiling.
**Fix:** "~290–293 wounded." (Killed ~63–69 and missing ~30 check out: Wikipedia 60+5 killed = 65, 31 missing.)

### S2. Hero caption asserts painting content that contradicts the painting's standard description
Draft (hero caption, used twice): "...Lord Stirling's rear guard fighting at the Gowanus while the rest of his command escapes across the marsh **behind them**."
Wikipedia's description of this exact Chappel 1858 canvas: smoke marks where Stirling and the Marylanders attack "while the rest of the American troops **in the foreground** escape across Brouwer's mill pond." The "behind them" spatial claim is authored beyond the pack (pack §11.3 says only "the Stirling/Gowanus fight") and appears wrong.
**Fix:** "...while the rest of his command escapes across Brouwer's millpond in the foreground" — or drop the spatial clause: "...while the rest of his command escapes across the Gowanus marsh."

### S3. "his teenage son" (Howard's tavern) — beyond-pack detail
Pack §4 gives only "William Howard and his son William Jr." Web sources support "teenage" but disagree on the age (NYC Parks: a 14-year-old son, b. 1762; Brownstoner-cited tradition: 16). Not a hallucination — but it is an unpacked fact on a gated surface.
**Fix:** drop "teenage" ("...compelled him and his son to guide the army..."), or add the sourced age range to the pack first.

### S4. "Good God" line: quoted text doesn't match the printed form the pack supplies
Draft: cried out, "Good God, what brave fellows I must this day lose!" — inside quotation marks.
Pack §8.2's verbatim Force *American Archives* printing: "Good God! **W**hat brave fellows I must this day lose!" (exclamation after "God," capital "What"). The draft matches the pack's loose header line, not the printing. Since the words sit inside quote marks, match the printing.
**Fix:** "Good God! What brave fellows I must this day lose!"

### S5. MEANWHILE (section 1) over-tightens the landing/Declaration coincidence
Draft: "Congress voted for independence on July 2 and declared it on July 4, **the same days** Howe's army was landing on Staten Island." The landing was July 2–3 (pack §2, Wikipedia); July 4 was not a landing day. The pack carries the loose "(the same days Congress voted and declared independence)" framing, but the MEANWHILE turns it into a literal day-by-day claim.
**Fix:** "...the same days Howe's army was coming ashore on Staten Island" → "the days Howe's army was landing on Staten Island — the vote on the very day the landing began" or simply "the same week." (The block's closing "in the same week" sentence is already correct.)

## NIT (4)

### N1. Internal time drift on the first shots
Section 2 prose: "Around midnight Grant's advance ran into American pickets near the Red Lion Inn"; dossier duration line: "first shots near the Red Lion Inn ~1 a.m." Both ends are inside the pack's "around midnight–1:00 a.m." window, but the page disagrees with itself. Harmonize (suggest "around midnight" in both, or "in the small hours").

### N2. Locator 2: "Brooklyn lines / Cobble Hill fort" merges two pack points
The dot at 40.686, −73.996 is the pack's **Cobble Hill fort** coordinate; the pack puts the **Brooklyn lines** at ~40.696, −73.995 (where Locators 1 and 3 correctly put them). The combined label sits ~0.7 mi south of the lines' anchor. Either split into two dots or relabel "Cobble Hill fort (Washington's viewpoint)."

### N3. Locator 2 caption names Bedford; no Bedford dot
"The night column marched from Flatlands, around the open door, **to Bedford** behind the American line" — Bedford village (~40.689, −73.953, inside the frame) isn't dotted, so the caption's terminus is invisible on the map. Add a Bedford dot or end the caption at "behind the American line."

### N4. "called Hessians because most came from Hesse" — unpacked gloss (true)
Pack says only 'hired German ("Hessian") regiments.' The gloss is standard and accurate (majority from Hesse-Kassel/Hesse-Hanau) but is technically beyond the pack; fine to keep, noting it here for the record.
