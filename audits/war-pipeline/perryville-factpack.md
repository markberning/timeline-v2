# Battle of Perryville (Chaplin Hills) — Sourced Fact Pack

**Subject:** Battle of Perryville, Kentucky — October 8, 1862. Also called the **Battle of Chaplin Hills**. The climax of Bragg's Confederate invasion of Kentucky (the Confederate Heartland Offensive / Kentucky Campaign).
**Purpose:** Raw verifiable ground truth for authoring + fact-checking (Step 2, war content pipeline). Zero-hallucination floor — every fact tied to a source. NO narrative prose.

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy — relative positions)

| Feature | Description / relative position | Source |
|---------|--------------------------------|--------|
| **Drought / water shortage** | Severe drought parched central Kentucky from ~June 1862; streams shrank to **stagnant puddles**. Both armies were maneuvering toward the few remaining water sources — the proximate trigger of the collision. | WP; ABT; KY |
| **Doctor's Creek** | Small creek **west of town**; its standing pools were the prize. Opening clash (evening Oct 6 → morning Oct 8) was a fight for its water, near **Peters Hill** and **Squire Henry P. Bottom's** farm. | WP; ABT; KY |
| **Chaplin River** | Runs roughly N–S **just west of/through the Perryville area**; Confederate brigades (Donelson) crossed it to attack the Union left. Chaplin River + Doctor's Creek define the battle's water-and-ridge terrain ("Chaplin Hills"). | WP; ABT |
| **Springfield Pike** | Main road running SW out of Perryville; the opening cavalry/infantry skirmish ran along it; anchored Sheridan's (and the Union center/right) sector. | WP; ABT |
| **Mackville Road / Benton Road** | Roads NW and N of town; **Dixville Crossroads** is where Benton and Mackville Roads met — a key Union defensive node where the last Confederate assault (Liddell/Wood) was checked at dusk. | WP; ABT |
| **Lebanon Pike** | South of town; Crittenden's II Corps axis (the corps that stayed largely out of the fight). | WP |
| **Peters Hill** | Hill W of town near Doctor's Creek; site of the morning skirmish; Sheridan seized it, then was ordered back by Gilbert. | WP; ABT |
| **Open Knob / Parsons' Ridge** | Prominent hill on the **Union LEFT (north)**; Terrill's brigade + Parsons' battery posted here; Cheatham/Maney's assault overran Parsons' guns after three charges — **the bloodiest, most pivotal sector**. | WP; ABT; Noe |
| **Starkweather's ridge** | Just behind/SE of Open Knob; where Maney's breakthrough was finally stopped (~5:30 p.m.). | WP; ABT |
| **Perryville (town)** | ~300 residents; served as a Confederate supply point; Union pursuit (Carlin/Wagner) pushed toward the town cemetery before Gilbert halted it. | WP; ABT |
| **Frankfort, KY** | State capital ~40 mi N; site of the Confederate-governor inauguration (§6) disrupted on Oct 4 — the political objective of the whole campaign. | WP; KY |
| **Cumberland Gap** | Bragg's retreat route SE out of Kentucky into Tennessee (toward Knoxville). | WP; ABT |

> **Map shape in one line:** Both armies groped toward the shrinking pools of **Doctor's Creek** west of Perryville; the Confederates massed against the **Union LEFT (north)** at **Open Knob/Parsons' Ridge**, attacking *en echelon* from north to south, while **Crittenden's whole corps to the south and most of Buell's army never joined**. The fight rolled from Open Knob (north) down through the center to **Dixville Crossroads** at dusk.

> **Drought "cause" caveat [VERIFY]:** One source attributes the drought to the May 1861 eruption of **Mt. Dubbi** (East Africa). The drought itself is well documented; the volcanic-cause attribution is a single-source claim — **mark [VERIFY] and do not present the volcano as established fact** in prose. (WP body — single source)

## 5. Sequence / Turning Points

### Lead-up (Oct 6–7)
- **Oct 6 evening:** elements of Gilbert's corps and Confederate pickets skirmished over the **water pools in Doctor's Creek** — the literal fight-over-water that opened the battle. (KY; ABT)
- **Oct 7:** three Union columns converged on Perryville; cavalry skirmished with the Confederate rearguard. Buell intended to attack the next morning. (WP; ABT)

### Morning, Oct 8
- Fighting flared near **Peters Hill / Doctor's Creek** when the **10th Indiana** went for water. Sheridan seized Peters Hill, then was pulled back by the cautious Gilbert (orders to avoid a general engagement). (WP; ABT)

### The acoustic shadow — the battle's defining oddity (THE hook)
- **Buell, ~2 miles to the rear, never heard the battle.** A weather/terrain inversion ("acoustic shadow") over the rolling Chaplin Hills muffled the sound; **everyone at Buell's HQ agreed they heard nothing** of a fight that had raged for hours. (WP; ABT; historynet)
- Consequence: **Buell committed no reserves until late afternoon**; the bulk of his army stood idle while one corps was mauled. He reportedly took the distant gunfire he *did* notice for artillery practice. (WP; ABT) **[VERIFY exact "artillery practicing" wording before quoting.]**

### Afternoon — Confederates attack *en echelon* (north to south)
- **~12:30 p.m.:** Cheatham's artillery opened the bombardment. (WP)
- **~2:00 p.m.:** **Donelson's** brigade crossed the Chaplin River expecting an open Union flank, instead hit a strong position on **Open Knob**; repulsed, ~20% losses. (WP)
- **~2:45 p.m.:** Jones's brigade attacked the center across the valley; cut down by artillery and musketry. (WP)
- **~3:00 p.m.:** **Maney's** brigade assaulted **Open Knob/Parsons' Ridge** — the bloodiest, most crucial action; broke through on the third assault and **captured Parsons' guns**, then was finally stopped at **Starkweather's ridge ~5:30 p.m.** Brig. Gens. **Terrill and Jackson (North) killed** here. (WP; ABT; Noe)
- **~3:40 p.m.:** Cleburne entered on the Confederate left (horse killed, ankle wound, kept on). (WP)
- **~4:00 p.m.:** Powel's brigade hit Sheridan on the Springfield Pike, quickly repulsed. (WP)
- **Sheridan idle:** though only hundreds of yards from McCook's desperate I Corps, Sheridan stayed largely out of it under Gilbert's restraining orders — a major reason the Union never massed its strength. (WP)

### Dusk
- Union reinforcements finally arrived; **Carlin/Wagner** pursued toward the Perryville cemetery but were **halted by Gilbert** over Mitchell's protests. (WP)
- At **Dixville Crossroads**, the last Confederate assault (Liddell/Wood) was checked; **Polk personally rode into the 22nd Indiana's line, bluffed the men by posing as a Union officer, escaped, and called off the attack citing darkness.** (WP; ABT) **[VERIFY the Polk-bluff anecdote's exact details before quoting — widely repeated, confirm in Noe/OR.]**

### The retreat (the strategic turn)
- **Bragg held the field at nightfall — a tactical win — but realized his peril:** Crittenden's fresh corps on his flank, Gilbert's corps in the center, the rest of Buell's army arriving. He **withdrew during the night**, joined Kirby Smith, then **retreated SE through Cumberland Gap toward Knoxville/Tennessee**, abandoning **~900 wounded**. The Kentucky campaign was over. (WP; ABT)

> **Two turning points to name:** (1) the **acoustic shadow** that kept Buell's army out of the fight (why a smaller force could win tactically); (2) **Bragg's decision to retreat** despite winning the field (why the tactical Confederate win was a strategic Union victory). Both are the spine of the section.

## 6. The Cause — Slavery (SHIP-BLOCKING throughline)

- **Kentucky was a slave state that stayed in the Union.** Bragg's invasion existed to **flip a slave state into the Confederacy**: enlist 25,000–30,000 Kentucky recruits and **install a Confederate state government** in place of the loyal Unionist one. (WP; ABT; KY)
- **The political climax was a few days before the battle:** on **October 4, 1862**, Bragg's army oversaw the **inauguration of Richard Hawes as Confederate "governor" of Kentucky at Frankfort** — the only time Confederate forces seated a government in a Union state capital. Hawes declared "the late Union cannot be restored." **Union artillery fired on the town mid-ceremony**; the Confederates abandoned Frankfort before the inaugural ball. Perryville four days later finished the job. (WP; KY; ExploreKYHistory)
- **The war over slavery, with national stakes that had just changed:** Lincoln had **announced the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation on September 22, 1862** — weeks before Perryville. The war's purpose was visibly shifting toward ending slavery exactly as Bragg tried to add a slave state to the Confederacy. A Confederate-held Kentucky would have been a slave state secured for the South against that tide. (Standard EP date; project framing rule)
- **Author guidance (standing framing gate — NAME THE CAUSE in the section's OWN prose):** the section must state, in its own words, that the war (and this campaign) was fought over slavery — the cleanest documented vehicles are (a) **Kentucky as a slave state both sides were fighting to keep**, (b) the **Confederate attempt to install a slave-state government at Frankfort**, and (c) the **preliminary Emancipation Proclamation announced weeks earlier**. Do NOT let a "states' rights / border-state loyalty" framing crowd slavery out. Omission = Lost Cause auto-fail. (Project framing rule; `feedback_name_the_cause`)

## 7. Human Cost / Vivid Documented Detail

- **~7,600 casualties in a single afternoon — roughly one in five men engaged.** Relative to numbers present, one of the war's bloodiest days. (KY; ABT)
- **Open Knob / Parsons' Ridge** — the killing ground; three Confederate charges, captured Union guns, two Union generals (**Terrill, Jackson**) dead. Noe calls Maney's final repulse here **"the high-water mark of the Confederacy in the western theater, no less important than the Angle at Gettysburg."** (WP; ABT; Noe — **[VERIFY exact Noe wording]**)
- **The fight over water** — men killed for the stagnant pools of Doctor's Creek in a drought; **Squire Henry P. Bottom's farm** along the creek became part of the battlefield. (KY; ABT)
- **The acoustic shadow** — thousands of Union soldiers stood within a few miles, unaware, while their comrades were slaughtered; a uniquely eerie, well-documented human fact. (WP; ABT)
- **~900 Confederate wounded abandoned** in the night retreat. (WP)
- **Civilian cost:** the town of ~300 and surrounding farms (Bottom, etc.) became a vast field hospital; Frankfort's residents had artillery fired into their town during the Hawes inauguration. (KY; WP)

## 9. Significance / What It Changed

- **Tactical Confederate win, strategic Union victory ("the Battle for Kentucky").** Bragg held the field but retreated, so the campaign — and the bid for Kentucky — was lost. (WP; ABT; CWSAC)
- **Kentucky was secured for the Union for the rest of the war.** No serious Confederate attempt to take the state followed; the slave state stayed loyal. (WP; ABT; KY)
- **The Confederate Heartland Offensive collapsed:** Bragg and Kirby Smith withdrew through Cumberland Gap to Tennessee; the high tide of Confederate fortunes in the West receded. (WP; ABT)
- **Command fallout:** **Buell was relieved**, faced a military commission, and resigned in 1864; **Bragg was summoned to Richmond** to explain the failed campaign, deepening the feud with his subordinates that would dog the Army of Tennessee. (WP; ABT)
- **Reorganization that set up the next campaign:** the Union created the **Department of the Cumberland under William S. Rosecrans**; Bragg's army (renamed the **Army of Tennessee** in Nov 1862) and Rosecrans's force would collide ten weeks later at **Stones River**. (WP; ABT)
- **Cost vs. result:** a real strategic win bought at a savage rate (~20% of those engaged), on a field where most of the Union army never fought — the win was the *retreat it forced*, not the field it lost. (KY; ABT)
