# Gaines' Mill — Fact-Checker Gate Report

**Gate:** FACT-CHECKER (independent web verification)
**Draft:** `audits/war-pipeline/gaines-mill-draft.md`
**Fact pack:** `audits/war-pipeline/gaines-mill-factpack.md`
**Sources checked:** Wikipedia "Battle of Gaines' Mill"; Wikipedia "Fitz John Porter"; ABT Battle Facts; ABT "10 Facts"; ABT "5th U.S. Cavalry at Gaines's Mill"; Warfare History Network; Encyclopedia Virginia; Liberty Rifles / 4th Texas unit history; CivilWarTraveler.blog; NPS Richmond NBP
**Date:** 2026-05-30

## MUST-FIX (ship-blocking)

### ❌ 1. Porter's rank — "Maj. Gen. Fitz John Porter" is WRONG for June 27, 1862

**Draft says:** "Maj. Gen. Fitz John Porter (North)"
**Finding:** Multiple independent sources confirm Porter held the rank of **Brigadier General of Volunteers** on June 27, 1862. His promotion to Major General of Volunteers was dated **July 4, 1862** — one week after Gaines' Mill. The Wikipedia infobox and ABT use "Major General" loosely in later summary context, but his actual commissioned rank on the day of battle was Brigadier General. Wikipedia's Fitz John Porter article is unambiguous: "promoted to major general of volunteers on July 4, 1862 for his performance during the Peninsula Campaign." The fact pack's own [VERIFY] flag on this was correct and the wrong answer was picked.
**Fix:** Change every instance of "Maj. Gen. Fitz John Porter" in the draft to "Brig. Gen. Fitz John Porter" — this is his correct rank on June 27, 1862.

---

### ❌ 2. "4th Texas alone lost about 570" — figure is contested and likely inflated

**Draft says:** "The 4th Texas alone lost about 570, including every one of its field-grade officers."
**Finding:** This is a genuinely contested figure. The Liberty Rifles 4th Texas unit history (a specialized regimental source) gives the regiment **253 casualties** at Gaines' Mill (roughly half the regiment), with another passage in the same source stating 44 killed, 208 wounded, 1 captured = **253 total**. The 570 figure appears in secondary blog sources (CivilWarTraveler) but not in regimental records or the most granular unit histories. Wikipedia's 4th Texas Infantry article cites conflicting figures. The Warfare History Network mentions "more than 500 men" advanced together between the 4th Texas and 18th Georgia — it is possible the 570 number conflates the losses of both units combined rather than the 4th Texas alone.

Additionally, "every one of its field-grade officers" requires softening: Col. Marshall was killed, Lt. Col. Warwick was mortally wounded, Maj. Key was wounded — all three field-grade officers were indeed casualties, but Warwick died a month later and Key survived. Saying "every field-grade officer was a casualty" is accurate; "every one of its field-grade officers" as written implies killed outright, which is wrong for at least two of them.
**Fix:** Either (a) soften to "somewhere around 250 to 570 casualties" with a note that regimental sources are contested, OR (b) drop the regiment-specific figure and keep only the Whiting division total of 1,018 (which is consistently sourced across ABT, Warfare History Network, and CivilWarTraveler). Correct "every one of its field-grade officers" to "every field-grade officer became a casualty."

---

### ❌ 3. Reinforcements identified as "two brigades from Sumner's II Corps" — incomplete/misleading

**Draft says:** "McClellan… sent Porter two brigades from Sumner's II Corps."
**Finding:** The reinforcements were more than just two II Corps brigades. Multiple sources (Wikipedia, Essential CW Curriculum, ABT) confirm that **Slocum's division from the VI Corps** also crossed to reinforce Porter — three brigades (Newton, Taylor, Bartlett) from Slocum. The two Sumner/II Corps brigades (Meagher and French) also arrived, but they arrived *during* the final assault phase, largely too late. Attributing all reinforcements to "Sumner's II Corps" ignores the VI Corps reinforcement that actually arrived earlier and engaged. The fact pack (§4 and commander list) mentions Slocum as a Union commander with reinforcements from VI Corps — the draft silently dropped this.
**Fix:** Revise to note that reinforcements came from both Slocum's division (VI Corps) and two brigades from Sumner's II Corps — the former arriving somewhat earlier, the latter arriving effectively during or after the final collapse.

## SHOULD-FIX (quality issues, not outright errors)

### ⚠️ 4. Ewell's attack timed at "around 4 p.m." — sources say 3:30 p.m.

**Draft says:** "Around 4 p.m., the division of Maj. Gen. Richard S. Ewell (South) fed into the same meat grinder."
**Finding:** Encyclopedia Virginia gives the time as **3:30 p.m.** Wikipedia states Lee met Ewell "around 3:30–4 p.m." The Warfare History Network says "almost 4 p.m." The preponderance of sources leans toward 3:30 p.m. as the more precise figure, though there is minor variation. "Around 4 p.m." is in the defensible range but slightly off the best-sourced figure.
**Fix:** Adjust to "around 3:30 p.m." or "between 3:30 and 4 p.m." to reflect the most consistent sourcing.

---

### ⚠️ 5. Cooke cavalry charge — "about 200 troopers" is slightly low; composition is incomplete

**Draft says:** "about 200 troopers under Brig. Gen. Philip St. George Cooke (North)"
**Finding:** The ABT article on the 5th U.S. Cavalry specifies **237 men** in the 5th Cavalry's charge specifically; Warfare History Network gives 250. The draft's "about 200" is at the low end of the sourced range. More importantly, the draft implies it was Cooke's charge generically, whereas sources confirm it involved specifically the **1st and 5th U.S. Cavalry** — Cooke directed both regiments. The "roughly a quarter cut down" framing is accurate (55 casualties from 237 = ~23%, and sources consistently describe "one-fourth of the oncoming cavalrymen" dropping from the initial fire, though total casualties were slightly below a quarter of the full force).
**Fix:** Change "about 200 troopers" to "about 250 troopers" (or "roughly 237 men of the 5th U.S. Cavalry"). Optionally note the 1st and 5th U.S. Cavalry were both involved.

---

### ⚠️ 6. Total guns captured — draft's "14 cannon" vs. "22 Union guns total" framing is accurate but needs tighter presentation

**Draft says:** "Hood's Texans drove through all three tiers and onto the plateau, capturing 14 cannon at the crest. By the time the battle ended, the Confederates had taken around 22 Union guns in all."
**Finding:** This 14/22 breakdown is confirmed across multiple sources (Liberty Rifles, Encyclopedia Virginia, search results). The 14 Hood/Texas Brigade captured at the crest, and 22 total for the battle. This is CORRECT as written. No fix needed, flagged here for confirmation only. ✅

---

### ⚠️ 7. "~100,000 men" for McClellan's army — accurate but the draft's own [VERIFY] flag was right to raise it

**Draft says:** "He had something like 100,000 men."
**Finding:** For the Army of the Potomac's total strength at the time of the Peninsula Campaign, sources give: ~105,000 by late May (ABT Peninsula Campaign article), ~115,350 (some sources), ~121,500 at the campaign's outset. The "something like 100,000" is a reasonable floor-round, though slightly conservative. Since this is characterizing the whole army's size (not forces engaged — the 34,214 engaged figure is for V Corps only), "something like 100,000" reads as an understatement. "Well over 100,000" or "roughly 105,000" would be more precise.
**Fix:** Soften to "He had something like 100,000 men — some counts put it closer to 105,000 or more" or simplify to "more than 100,000 men." The current phrasing is not false, but it shades low.

---

### ⚠️ 8. "4th Texas + companies of the 18th Georgia" — composition is accurate but incomplete

**Draft says:** "He took his old regiment, the 4th Texas, plus companies of the 18th Georgia"
**Finding:** Sources (Warfare History Network, Liberty Rifles) confirm Hood specifically took the 4th Texas and **companies** of the 18th Georgia to the favorable ground on the right — this framing is correct for that lead element. However, the full Texas Brigade assault also included the 1st Texas, 5th Texas, and Hampton's Legion coming up in support. The draft's framing of the vanguard is accurate, but readers may think Hood's assault was only 4th Texas + a few Georgians. The draft does say "Hood's Texans" generically after, which partially covers this.
**Fix (optional):** Could add a parenthetical noting the rest of the brigade (1st and 5th Texas, Hampton's Legion) followed in support. Not a factual error as written; just incomplete on brigade composition.

---

### ⚠️ 9. "~16 brigades" for the 7 p.m. assault — confirmed; "largest Confederate assault of the Civil War" hedge is correct

**Draft says:** "roughly 32,000 men in about 16 brigades… likely the largest Confederate assault of the Civil War"
**Finding:** ABT 10 Facts: "upwards of 32,000 men — sixteen brigades." This figure is confirmed. ABT also states: "likely the largest of the Civil War." The draft appropriately hedges with "likely." The Wikipedia article on Gaines' Mill calls it "the largest of the Seven Days" — notably more modest than "largest of the Civil War." ABT's institutional framing carries the broader claim with the qualifier. The draft's preservation of the "likely" hedge is correct.
**Status:** ✅ Hedge correctly preserved. No fix needed.

### ⚠️ 11. Whiting's division casualties — "Hood's part of the line — Whiting's division — lost 1,018 men" is slightly imprecise

**Draft says:** "Hood's part of the line — Whiting's division — lost 1,018 men in the assault."
**Finding:** The 1,018 figure is for **Hood and Law's brigades** specifically (two of Whiting's brigades), not the full division. CivilWarTraveler and Warfare History Network are explicit: "Hood and Law's brigades suffered 1,018 casualties." If Whiting's division included other brigades, the division total would be higher. Attributing 1,018 to the full "Whiting's division" overstates what the cited figure covers.
**Fix:** Change "Whiting's division — lost 1,018 men" to "Hood and Law's brigades lost 1,018 men" (or "Hood's brigade and Law's brigade within Whiting's division").
