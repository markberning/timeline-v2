# FACT PACK — Battle of Fort Oswego (August 10-14, 1756)

*French and Indian War. ONLY source for the writer. Imperial + Native stakes (control of Lake Ontario / western communications; Native plunder-captive-prestige war aims). NO slavery angle. Use ranges; flag contested numbers.*

## 1. IDENTITY & FRAMING
- French siege/capture of the British fort complex at Oswego, SE shore of Lake Ontario. Siege Aug 10-14, 1756; surrender Aug 14 (one Canadian source says Aug 11 — discrepancy, use Aug 14). Location present-day Oswego, NY, mouth of the Oswego River (~43.46,-76.51). State NY.
- Significance: Montcalm's FIRST major North American victory; secured undisputed French control of Lake Ontario; big blow to British prestige in a bad year.

## 2. BACKGROUND
- Oswego = the only British naval/trading base on Lake Ontario, a wedge into French communications west (Niagara, Ohio, the pays d'en haut). Three poorly-sited posts: Fort Ontario (1755, high ground, NE/east bank — the commanding position); old Fort Oswego/Fort Pepperrell (1727, low ground, west bank at the river mouth); Fort George ("Fort Rascal", incomplete palisade ~½ mi SW, ~150 NJ militia). Garrison over-wintered, short of food, sickly, few mounted cannon.

## 3. FORCES & COMMANDERS
- French: Maj. Gen. Louis-Joseph de Montcalm, Marquis de Montcalm (newly arrived 1756 to command French regulars in New France). ~3,000 men: regulars (La Sarre, Guyenne, Béarn) + troupes de la marine + Canadian militia + ~250 Native allies (from across New France / the pays d'en haut). Pierre de Rigaud de Vaudreuil (the governor-general's brother) commanded the Canadians; Canadians/Natives had isolated Oswego earlier in the summer. Siege guns: ~9 cannon emplaced on captured Fort Ontario heights (ABT says 12).
- British: Col. James Mercer (garrison cmdr, KILLED — struck/beheaded by a cannonball during the bombardment; "killed by a shell" in gentler tellings); Lt. Col. John Littlehales took over and surrendered ~within an hour. Units: Shirley's (50th) + Pepperrell's (51st) Regiments (raised in Massachusetts) + ~150 NJ militia. Numbers contested: nominal ~2,000, fewer than ~1,200 fit for duty (ABT ~1,300; one source ~1,100). ~1,600-1,700 people surrendered (incl. many non-combatants: laborers, shipbuilders, women, children).

## 4. THE SIEGE & FALL
- Aug 10: French landed ~2 mi east of Fort Ontario, staging from Fort Frontenac across the lake. Aug 11-12: opened siege trenches toward Fort Ontario. Aug 13: British abandoned Fort Ontario, fell back across the river into the old fort. Aug 14: French mounted ~9 guns on the commanding Fort Ontario heights and fired down on the exposed old fort (which crumbled); troops crossed upstream, putting it in crossfire. Mercer killed by artillery. Littlehales surrendered ~within an hour, Aug 14. Montcalm DENIED the honours of war (judged the brief defense hadn't earned them) — which also angered the Native allies who saw prisoners/plunder as their due.

## 5. AFTERMATH — THE KILLINGS + DESTRUCTION
- After surrender, Canadian militia + Native allies plundered and got drunk on captured rum; some British prisoners trying to escape were tomahawked/killed; Lt. Col. Littlehales was seized and badly beaten by Abenakis. Montcalm intervened to stop the killing, saying it would "cost the King eight or ten thousand livres in presents" (gifts to buy back order from the allies).
- SCALE HEAVILY CONTESTED: from ~30 (a group shot/tomahawked escaping at night) to "as many as 100" soldiers/civilians (ABT). Several Canadian-leaning sources omit the killings entirely. Use a range (a few dozen to ~100+) and flag it as disputed; do NOT assert one number. A prelude to the more infamous Fort William Henry episode (1757).
- Montcalm's responsibility debated (denied honours → provoked allies; yet personally stopped the slaughter).
- French then DESTROYED/burned the Oswego forts and withdrew; site abandoned until 1758.

## 6. CONSEQUENCES
- Undisputed French control of Lake Ontario; secured western communications; removed the threat to Fort Frontenac; wrecked British 1756 plans (esp. Shirley's intended Niagara expedition). ~1,600-1,700 prisoners taken to Montreal/Canada. Captured: 121 cannon, several ships/boats, large stores (ABT: 9,000 barrels of provisions). Casualties: British ~80-150 killed (ABT 110); French ~30-80 (lean ~30). Major prestige blow.

## 7. NATIVE PERSPECTIVE / STAKES (frame fairly — agency, not trope)
- French victories at the Monongahela (1755) and now Oswego drove a huge Native recruiting surge from the pays d'en haut (the Great Lakes basin), encouraged by Vaudreuil. Native allies fought for their OWN war aims: captives, scalps/trophies, weapons, supplies — proof of courage, prestige, plunder — culturally central, not aberrations. These collided with European siege etiquette (a capitulation = the commander disposes of prisoners/spoils). When Montcalm denied the honours of war and tried to control prisoners/plunder, the allies were angered — they regarded captives and goods as their earned due. This tension is the root of the post-surrender killings. The Haudenosaunee (esp. the nearby Onondaga) watched; the land = the Lake Ontario frontier, their country. Report the killing plainly, attribute agency, set it inside the colliding war conventions — neither the "savage" trope nor sanitizing.

## 8. INTRODUCING MONTCALM (central French figure for the rest of the war)
- Louis-Joseph de Montcalm, Marquis de Montcalm; b. Feb 28, 1712, Candiac (near Nîmes), provincial nobility. Soldier from boyhood (ensign at nine); first action 1733 (War of Polish Succession); distinguished in the defense of Prague (1742); colonel 1743; at Piacenza (1746) took five sabre wounds and was captured, exchanged; brigadier 1747. Appointed 1756 maj. gen. (maréchal de camp) to command French regulars in Canada; arrived Quebec May 11, 1756. A conventional European professional, skeptical/disdainful of Canadian + Native irregular warfare; authority limited, repeatedly clashed with Gov.-Gen. Vaudreuil. Arc (context only): Fort William Henry 1757 (+ massacre), Carillon/Ticonderoga 1758 (great defensive win, promoted lt. gen.), mortally wounded at Quebec/Plains of Abraham Sept 13, died Sept 14, 1759, age 47.

## 9. VERIFIED QUOTES
- Montcalm on quelling the killings: stopping it would "cost the King eight or ten thousand livres in presents." VERIFIED.
- Montcalm refused the honours of war (paraphrase of reasoning, not a verbatim quote).
- No reliable verbatim battlefield quote from Mercer/Littlehales — do not invent.

## 10. MAP GEOGRAPHY (state = New York)
- Oswego ~43.46,-76.51 (Fort Ontario E/high bank; old Fort Oswego/Pepperrell W/low bank; Fort George/"Rascal" ~½ mi SW). Fort Frontenac (Kingston, ON ~44.23,-76.48, the French base across the lake). Montreal ~45.50,-73.57 (prisoners taken there). Albany ~42.65,-73.75 (British supply hub). Theatre = the Lake Ontario frontier.

## KNOWN UNCERTAINTIES
1. Scale of post-surrender killings (a few dozen to ~100+; some sources omit it) — biggest uncertainty, use a range, flag disputed. 2. Garrison size (~1,100-2,000 nominal; ~1,100-1,300 effective). 3. Prisoners ~1,600-1,700 incl. non-combatants. 4. Mercer's death ("beheaded by a cannonball" well-attested; gentler "killed by a shell" exists). 5. Casualties British ~80-150 killed; French ~30-80. 6. Surrender date Aug 14 (vs Aug 11 in one source). 7. Siege guns 9 vs 12. 8. Native allies ~250 approximate.
