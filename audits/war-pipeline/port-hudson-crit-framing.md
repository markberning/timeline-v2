## FIRST CHECK — is SLAVERY named as the cause, in the draft's OWN prose?

**YES.** Named explicitly and repeatedly, in the draft's own voice, not deflected to a
source or a euphemism. The word "slavery" / "enslaved" / "slaveholders'" appears across
every section that touches the meaning of the fight. Key load-bearing instances:

- `the-doomed-charges`, §53 (the explicit cause statement): *"The Confederacy existed to
  keep human beings enslaved — that was the institution it had seceded to protect, and the
  thing its armies were in the field to defend… The South was fighting to keep slavery;
  these soldiers were fighting to end it, with their own bodies, in the open, against the
  guns. No one watching that day could mistake what the war was about."* This is the
  cause named cleanly, in the draft's own prose, with NO hedging, NO "states' rights,"
  NO "way of life" euphemism. Model passage.
- `the-doomed-charges`, §51: Native Guard "many of them men who had been enslaved."
- `the-meaning`, §97 & §103 & §105: the cause restated — "charged a slaveholders'
  fortress," "the system that had owned them," "many of them formerly enslaved."
- Cailloux "born enslaved" (§57, §61, §99).

**Slavery-named gate: PASS.** No automatic MUST-FIX triggered.

## CENTERPIECE CHECK — Native Guard / Cailloux: agency & humanity central, not tokenized?

**FAIR — strong PASS, arguably the best framing in the war corpus to date.**

- The Native Guard is the structural heart of the section, not a sidebar. The cause is
  routed THROUGH them ("the larger meaning of the war walked onto the field"), and their
  agency is explicit: they are *fighting to end the system that owned them, with their own
  bodies* — self-emancipation, not passive recipients of Northern benevolence.
- Humanity rendered concretely without tokenizing: Cailloux as cigar maker, property
  owner, community leader, bilingual commands in English and French, equal pay drawn at
  $60/month. These are dignity-restoring specifics, not a saintly cardboard cutout.
- The "first" claim is correctly hedged ("one of the first major combat actions"),
  Milliken's Bend and Fort Wagner credited as the larger pattern — no over-claim that
  would invite a debunk and undercut the men.
- Courage shown, not asserted: "went in, and went in again, into fire that could not be
  survived in the open" — earned, not decorative.

This is the opposite of tokenization. The men are the subject of the section, the cause
is theirs, and the prose treats them as agents of their own freedom.

## FUTILITY / BANKS'S RESPONSIBILITY — sanitized?

**FAIR — not sanitized.** Web-verified against historian consensus.

- Banks's responsibility is named squarely and repeatedly: "He did not understand the
  ground"; attacks "came apart instantly," went in "piecemeal"; "to take ground he did
  not take. … The works were untouched"; June 14 "the same story written in more blood";
  "nearly 3,800 men down in two doomed charges, to take nothing." No glory-wash, no
  "gallant assault" euphemism. The section title is literally "The futile assaults."
- Verified: historians "consistently criticize Banks," fault "poor planning and
  coordination," note he "took little responsibility" and scapegoated Sherman. The
  draft's framing matches the consensus and does not exceed it. (Emerging Civil War;
  Warfare History Network; Wikipedia — see Sources.)
- **One UNDER-statement, not over:** the truce denial. The draft renders it passively —
  "the Native Guard were not permitted to retrieve their own fallen." The historical
  record is sharper and more damning of Banks personally: Gardner *requested* a truce to
  let the Native Guard dead be recovered, and Banks reportedly *refused*, claiming "I have
  no casualties in that area" — a callous denial of his own Black dead, in stark contrast
  to recovering the white dead elsewhere on the field. The draft is thus slightly GENTLER
  on Banks than the record supports, not harsher. This is a SHOULD-FIX (a chance to make
  the framing even truer), never a distortion. Flagged for the fact-checker to confirm the
  "I have no casualties in that area" wording before it could be quoted/rendered.

## CONFEDERATE TREATMENT — romanticized? false balance?

**FAIR.** No romanticization. Gardner is "a professional soldier with a complicated
story" / New-York-born — a neutral biographical note, not a noble-Lost-Cause portrait.
The garrison's suffering (eating mules, dogs, rats; scurvy; the "wilderness of death")
is rendered as the consequence of defending a slaveholders' fort whose cause the draft
has already condemned — sympathy for the men's physical ordeal does NOT bleed into
sympathy for the cause. Critically, §87 refuses to ennoble their endurance: they "had
been defending a position that the rest of the war had already made pointless." That is
the correct anti-Lost-Cause move — the suffering is real but not redemptive, not heroized
into a "valiant doomed South." No false balance: there is no "both sides had their
reasons" equivalence anywhere.

---

## HUMAN REALITY — sanitized OR gratuitous?

**FAIR — well-calibrated.** Cailloux's death (shattered arm, killed by a shell), the
ditch "filled with the living and the dead," the body left 47 days, the ring
identification: each detail carries meaning (the cost of the cause, the indignity of
racism) and none is lingered on for shock. The 47-days/ring beat is the moral hinge of the
section, not gore for its own sake. Disease toll given as a sober count, not exploited.
Nothing gratuitous; nothing sanitized.

---

## PRESENTISM

**None.** Judgments rendered are the period's own (Northern opinion shifting, the NYT
editorial, Banks's own report on the troops' fitness) plus the war's own stakes. The
condemnation of slavery is not anachronistic moralizing — it is the war's actual cause,
named as contemporaries named it.

## FINDINGS

### MUST-FIX
**None.** The slavery-named gate passes; the centerpiece is handled with care; no
distortion, romanticization, euphemism, false balance, or presentism found.

### SHOULD-FIX (framing improvements — optional, none ship-blocking)
1. **Truce denial is too passive (§61).** "the Native Guard were not permitted to retrieve
   their own fallen" softens Banks's personal agency. The record has Banks actively
   refusing Gardner's truce request with "I have no casualties in that area" — a sharper,
   truer indictment that the draft currently blurs into faceless passive voice. Consider
   restoring Banks as the actor (fact-checker to confirm the quote wording first; flag it
   for factpack §6/§7). This makes the framing MORE accurate, not less — the only direction
   this draft errs is toward gentleness on Banks.
2. **(Very minor, optional)** §45 "He expected the math to settle the matter" + "He did not
   understand the ground" already lands Banks's overconfidence well; no change needed, noted
   only as the place any added Banks-culpability would live.
