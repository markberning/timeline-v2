## Verdict summary

This is a strong, story-first draft. It opens with a genuine hook (the war's own name hides who it was really about), keeps a clear through-line of stakes (the collapse of two-empire leverage), and lands the human dimension hard at the end (burned cabins, missing children, bodies on the portages). The pacing accelerates correctly through the fort-fall sequence and slows for the two great sieges. No heading grades below GOOD; no STRONG/GOOD heading is dragged down by play-by-play tedium. House voice is clean: no prose em-dashes, no bold, cross-refs are standalone pill lines, the Native-nations-first framing the fact pack demanded is honored without sanitizing or making the dispossession gratuitous.

No NEEDS WORK / REWRITE headings and no house-voice violations, so this **PASSES** the storytelling gate. The items below are SHOULD-FIX polish, not blockers.

## Heading-by-heading

### Hook / intro (untitled lede) — STRONG
"When the British colonists named their war, they named it for their enemies… that name hides the most important thing about it." This is the best kind of opening: it reframes something the reader thinks they know and immediately raises the stakes (who this war was actually about). The closing line of the lede ("the largest coordinated war for their own land and sovereignty that North America had yet seen") sets scale without overclaiming. Keep as is.

### A Game With Two Empires, Then One — STRONG
The "play two empires against each other… that need was leverage" framing makes a structural argument feel like a story with a clock on it. "This was a catastrophe before a single shot of the new war was fired" is an excellent pivot line. Pacing is tight; the three capitulation dates (Quebec/Montreal/Paris) are delivered as a falling sequence, not a list.

### Amherst Pulls the Alliance Apart — GOOD
The "three things that mattered" structure is clear and the gift-giving explanation ("the giving of gifts *was* the alliance") does real teaching work without going dry. Human dimension is present ("treated no better than slaves or dogs"). The reason this is GOOD and not STRONG: it leans slightly on a numbered-mechanism structure (First/Second/Third) that is the most list-like passage in the piece. It works, but it is the spot most at risk of reading as policy memo rather than story. SHOULD-FIX 2 below.

### The Prophet — STRONG
Neolin is given genuine human texture (the fast, the season of dreaming, the vision). The "as remembered by Neolin, as recited by Pontiac, as written down and translated by a colonial hand. It is not a transcript" sequence is exactly the honest-sourcing the fact pack required, and it is written as story rhythm rather than disclaimer. The "a reason that reached past politics into the spirit" line earns the section.

### A Coalition, Not a King — STRONG
Does the fact pack's central correction ("not one man's war") as narrative, not as caveat. Charlot Kaské "who would later cross the Mississippi rather than make peace" is a great forward-hook seeding a character. The Detroit ruse (dance to count soldiers; come back to seize it; find Gladwin warned) is tense and well-paced.

### Eight Forts in a Summer — STRONG
This is the pacing high point. "Faster than any messenger could carry the news" sets the tempo; "They fell to nerve and deception, to garrisons that opened their gates because they had no idea the war had reached them" gives the sequence a theme so the fort-by-fort run reads as escalation, not a roll call. The Michilimackinac lacrosse set-piece is the most cinematic paragraph in the draft and rightly given room. Anti-tedium: the author resisted turning the fort table into eight equal-weight sentences; the short ones are clipped, the famous one expands. Exactly right.

### The Two That Held — GOOD
Bloody Run is well told ("left the water running red… changed nothing decisive"). The October 31 ending ("not in a storm but in a slow draining of hope that France would ever return") is a strong, melancholy beat. GOOD rather than STRONG only because the Fort Pitt half is a deliberate cliff-hanger handoff ("smallpox was already loose") — effective, but it means this heading's second prize is set up rather than paid off here. That is a structural choice, not a flaw.

### The Blankets — STRONG
The hardest material in the piece, handled with discipline. The act/intent vs. effectiveness distinction the fact pack flagged as contested is rendered cleanly ("The intent is not in question. Only the result is."). "without a flicker of shame" earns its place. The author kept the two-events-are-separate point ("it matters that they were") which protects accuracy without killing momentum. The Bushy Run relief is folded in to release the tension well; Devil's Hole as a coda ("proof that the rising was nowhere near contained") keeps stakes alive.

### Nobody Could Win — STRONG
The stalemate-forces-reversal logic is the spine of the back half and it's stated as a turn, not a summary ("the stalemate is what forced Britain to do something it would never have done from a position of comfort: back down"). The Pontiac peace terms ("no land was ceded, no prisoners surrendered, no hostages taken… ended remarkably like a peace between powers") is a quietly devastating, well-chosen detail.

### The Line on the Map — GOOD
Clear and well-stakes'd; the watershed mechanic is explained plainly. The Washington detail (promised ~20,000 acres, told his agent to keep buying) is the right human hook to make the colonists' fury concrete. GOOD not STRONG because the closing ("a king telling them where they could not go") is slightly the most conventional line in the piece, and the section briefly tips toward civics-textbook register before recovering. Minor.

### What the Rising Won, and What It Could Not Stop — STRONG
The dual ledger (real concessions / unreversible catastrophe) is the correct shape for an aftermath theme and is paid off with the strongest human-cost paragraph in the draft ("burned cabins and missing children on one side and bodies left on the portages… on the other"). The final paragraph ("never been conquered, never been consulted, never agreed to belong to anyone") delivers the Native-nations-first thesis as an ending punch rather than a thesis restatement. Closes the piece on theme and on stakes.

## SHOULD-FIX (polish, non-blocking)

1. **Full-rank-first on Amherst and Gladwin.** Amherst is introduced as "Jeffery Amherst, the British commander-in-chief in North America" and is never given his military rank (General) anywhere in prose; the fact pack calls him "General Jeffery Amherst." Gladwin is "the commander, Henry Gladwin." Both get a role/appointment instead of a rank at first mention. Other officers (Captain Dalyell, Captain Ecuyer, Colonel Bouquet, General Gage) are correctly rank-first. Recommend "General Jeffery Amherst, the British commander-in-chief" and a rank for Gladwin (Major Henry Gladwin, if the fact-checker confirms his rank) for consistency. Note this is a borderline read of the rule, not a clear breach — "commander-in-chief" is arguably a rank-equivalent — which is why it is a SHOULD-FIX, not a mustFix.

2. **"Amherst Pulls the Alliance Apart" — soften the memo cadence.** The First/Second/Third scaffold is the most list-like passage. Consider a connective lead-in between the second and third items so the contempt point doesn't read as the third bullet of a policy list (e.g., lead "Third, he made no secret of his contempt" with a clause that ties it to the gift/powder decisions as expressions of the same posture). Small touch; the content is strong.

3. **One forward-hook left dangling: Pontiac's death.** The fact pack offers Pontiac's 1769 assassination as optional. The draft omits it, which is a defensible scope call for an aftermath theme — flagging only so the integrator knows it was a conscious omission, not a miss. No action required.

A story, not a fact dump. Hook, stakes, human dimension, pacing, and voice are all present and largely excellent; the contested-fact discipline (fort count, smallpox effectiveness, colonial-recorded quotes) is woven into the prose without deadening it. House voice is clean across all locked rules. **PASS.** The three SHOULD-FIX items are polish for the reconcile pass.
