## §0 Battle-level facts (true across all sections)
- **Dates:** July 1–3, 1863, at and around Gettysburg, Pennsylvania.
- **Armies & commanders:** Union — **Maj. Gen. George G. Meade**, Army of the
  Potomac. Confederate — **Gen. Robert E. Lee**, Army of Northern Virginia.
- **Strength:** Union ~**93,500–104,000** (≈360 guns). Confederate
  ~**71,000** (range 65,000–80,000; ≈270 guns). (Use "roughly 70,000–95,000" or
  the rounded sides; both ranges are legit.)
- **Casualties (contested — give ranges):** Union **~23,055** (3,155 killed,
  14,531 wounded, 5,369 captured/missing). Confederate **~23,000–28,000** (Busey &
  Martin: 23,231 — 4,708 killed, 12,693 wounded, 5,830 captured/missing).
  **Combined ~46,000–51,000 casualties** (killed + wounded + captured/missing).
  ⚠️ This is the TOTAL including captured/missing — do NOT say "fifty thousand
  killed or wounded"; say "killed, wounded, or captured" / "fifty thousand
  casualties."
- **Outcome:** Union victory; Lee's invasion of the North halted; Confederate
  retreat began July 4. Widely considered the war's turning point — paired with
  **Vicksburg's surrender to Grant on July 4, 1863** (the Confederacy split in two
  the same day Lee began retreating).

## §1 Campaign background (for `setting`)
- After Lee's victory at **Chancellorsville** (April 30–May 6, 1863, where he beat
  a Union army roughly twice his size), he proposed invading the North; the army
  began moving **June 3, 1863**.
- Strategic logic (real, multi-part): relieve war-torn Virginia + feed the army off
  Pennsylvania; threaten Northern cities; possibly draw Union force away from
  Vicksburg; possibly push Lincoln toward negotiation and Europe toward
  recognition. (All genuine motives; the "food/shoes in Pennsylvania" logistics
  motive is real at the army level — but see §FIXES on the Heth "shoes" myth.)
- Route: up the **Shenandoah Valley** into Pennsylvania; ~70,000+ men spread across
  the countryside, under Lee's orders restraining looting/destruction.
- **Joseph Hooker** commanded the Army of the Potomac, feuding with Washington;
  he resigned and was replaced by **George Meade** on **June 28, 1863** — three
  days before the battle. Meade had not sought the job.
- **J.E.B. Stuart's** Confederate cavalry — Lee's reconnaissance — was off on a
  long ride around the Union army and was effectively absent during the approach,
  leaving Lee blind to the enemy's location.
- Gettysburg's significance: a small market town (~2,400 residents — NOTE: the v0
  "ten thousand people" is too high; ⚠️ verify, the 1860 town was ~2,400) where
  **ten roads converged** — a road hub, no prior military value.
- **July 1 opening:** Confederate infantry (Heth's division) advancing toward town
  on the Chambersburg Pike met Buford's Union cavalry west of town; first shots
  ~7:30 a.m. at/near McPherson's Ridge.

## §2 Day 1 — July 1 (for `mcpherson`)
- **John Buford** (Union cavalry) had ~**2,748 troopers**, dismounted, armed with
  **breech-loading carbines** (faster fire than Confederate muzzle-loaders); fought
  a delaying action from Herr Ridge → McPherson Ridge → Seminary Ridge to buy time
  for the infantry. He had chosen the ground deliberately the night before.
- **Henry Heth's** Confederate division (~7,600 infantry) advanced east. **The
  "looking for shoes" story is a LEGEND, not documented** — Heth's move was a
  reconnaissance in force. (See §FIXES.)
- **John F. Reynolds** (senior Union I Corps commander) rode up, committed his
  infantry (incl. the **Iron Brigade** — Indiana/Michigan/Wisconsin regiments), and
  was **killed instantly ~10:15–10:30 a.m., shot behind the ear** at the east edge
  of Herbst Woods. (⚠️ v0 says "through the neck" — CORRECT to "behind the ear.")
- Through the day the outnumbered Union I and XI Corps were driven back through the
  town to the high ground of **Cemetery Hill** by late afternoon (multiple
  Confederate divisions — Heth, Pender, and Rodes/Early from the north — converging;
  "three divisions / two directions" is fair for Day 1).
- **Winfield Scott Hancock** arrived after 4 p.m., sent ahead by Meade with
  authority to judge and command; he chose to hold. Verbatim quote (confirmed):
  **"I think this the strongest position by nature upon which to fight a battle
  that I ever saw."**
- Lee arrived on Seminary Ridge that evening and resolved to attack the next day,
  though one of his corps (Longstreet's) was not yet up.

## §3 Day 2 — July 2 (for `hooks`)
- **The fishhook:** the Union line by Day 2 ran from **Culp's Hill** (the barb, NE)
  over **Cemetery Hill**, south down **Cemetery Ridge** ~2 miles to **Little Round
  Top** and the Round Tops (the shank/anchor). Interior lines = Meade could shift
  troops fast. (The v0 fishhook passage is geographically correct — keep it.)
- **Lee's plan:** Longstreet to attack the Union LEFT (oblique, roll up the flank);
  Ewell to demonstrate/attack the RIGHT at Culp's Hill. Longstreet's attack was
  **delayed until ~4 p.m.** (waiting on Law's brigade; a concealed approach march).
- **Daniel Sickles** (III Corps) advanced his corps ~¾ mile forward **without
  authorization** into the **Peach Orchard / Wheatfield** salient — exposed,
  un-anchored. Meade to Sickles: **"this is neutral ground, our guns command it, as
  well as the enemy's."**
- **Devil's Den (~4:30 p.m.):** **John Bell Hood's** division attacked; Hood was
  **wounded by an air-bursting artillery shell (left arm)** early. **J.H. Hobart
  Ward's** Union brigade (~2,200; six regiments incl. the **124th New York** — Col.
  Ellis and Maj. Cromwell both killed) held the boulders, then was driven off.
  Casualties at Devil's Den: 821 Union of 2,423 engaged; ~1,814 Confederate of
  5,525. (v0 "New Yorkers and New Hampshiremen" — prefer "New Yorkers and others"
  or name the 124th NY.)
- **Little Round Top (~4:30–5 p.m.):** **Gouverneur K. Warren**, the army's chief
  engineer, rode up, found the hill nearly undefended, saw the Confederate line
  coming, and urgently sent for troops — the genuinely decisive act. **Strong
  Vincent's** brigade arrived first (Vincent mortally wounded); **Patrick "Paddy"
  O'Rorke** (140th NY) rushed up (killed); artillerist **Charles Hazlett** killed.
  On the far left, **Col. Joshua L. Chamberlain's 20th Maine** held against **Col.
  William Oates's 15th Alabama** through repeated assaults and, low on ammunition,
  made a downhill **bayonet charge** (documented in Chamberlain's official report;
  he received the Medal of Honor). **NUANCE TO KEEP:** popular culture treats
  Chamberlain's charge as "the" battle; in truth it was one ~20-minute action, and
  Warren's rush to the undefended hill was the larger decisive moment. The v0's
  framing of this is GOOD — keep it.
- **The Wheatfield (~5:30 p.m.+):** ~19–20 brigades fought across ~19 acres; it
  changed hands repeatedly (commonly "six times"; ⚠️ treat "six" as the popular
  figure). ~20,444 engaged, ~30% casualties. Union Cols. **Edward Cross** and
  **Samuel Zook** mortally wounded. A field of horror — keep it as the "first
  modern war" image.
- **The Peach Orchard (~6 p.m.):** **William Barksdale's** Mississippi brigade
  broke Sickles's salient (Barksdale killed leading the charge on horseback).
  Sickles **wounded by a cannonball in the right leg; leg amputated that evening**
  (he reportedly was carried off sitting up, smoking a cigar). His self-justifying
  "my advance won the battle" argument lasted the rest of his life.
- **Culp's Hill (evening):** **Maj. Gen. Edward "Allegheny" Johnson's** Confederate
  division attacked **Brig. Gen. George S. Greene's** lone XII Corps brigade, dug in
  behind **breastworks** Greene had insisted on building. Greene held until
  reinforcements arrived after dark. ⚠️ **v0 says "three Confederate divisions" —
  WRONG. It was Johnson's ONE division (and even that minus a brigade). CORRECT to
  "Johnson's division."** Greene: 62 years old, West Point class of 1823, a
  distinguished civil engineer recalled to service — the breastworks were why one
  brigade could hold. (He was NOT a classmate of Lee; do not imply it.)
- **East Cemetery Hill (~7 p.m.+):** Early's brigades briefly reached Union guns,
  were repulsed. (Optional detail.)
- **Day 2 result:** the Union line bent but held everywhere. Day-2 casualties est.
  ~10,000 Union / ~6,800 Confederate (Trudeau). Lee resolved to attack the CENTER
  on Day 3.

## §4 Day 3 — July 3, Pickett's Charge (for `pickett`)
- Lee, having hit both flanks without breaking the line, chose to attack the
  **center** of Cemetery Ridge. **Longstreet opposed it** (he had opposed the whole
  invasion and the Day-2 attack) and urged maneuvering around Meade instead; Lee
  refused.
- The assault force: **~12,500 men**, three divisions — **Pickett**, **Pettigrew**,
  **Trimble** (under Longstreet's command). Target: the **copse of trees / "the
  Angle"** (a stone wall) at the Union center, **~¾ mile across open ground**.
  (Modern scholarship debates whether Lee aimed slightly north at Ziegler's Grove;
  the copse-of-trees / Angle is the traditional and reader-clear target — fine to
  use, optionally noting historians debate the exact aim point.)
- **The cannonade (~1 p.m., ~1–2 hours):** ~**150–170 Confederate guns** vs ~**80
  Union guns** — the largest artillery bombardment of the war on the continent.
  Smoke hid the fall of shot; **most Confederate shells overshot** the infantry on
  the ridge. Union guns slackened (to save ammunition and feign being silenced).
- **The charge (~3 p.m.):** the divisions advanced in long lines ~a mile wide;
  Union artillery (canister at close range) and rifle fire tore them apart. A few
  hundred reached the wall at the Angle; **Brig. Gen. Lewis Armistead** led men over
  the wall (hat on his sword) and was mortally wounded there — the spot is called
  the **"high-water mark of the Confederacy."** Armistead died two days later in a
  Union hospital.
- **Cost:** Confederate losses in the charge were **over 50%** — roughly half the
  ~12,500 did not return (total Confederate ~6,555 in the charge; Pickett's division
  alone ~2,655). Modern scholarship notes a number of Confederates sheltered at the
  Emmitsburg Road rather than reaching the wall.
- **The quotes — DOCUMENTED BUT DISPUTED (must hedge):**
  - Lee meeting the survivors: traditionally **"This has all been my fault"** /
    "It is all my fault." ⚠️ Historians (Hess, Sears, Wert) **dispute** that Lee and
    Pickett met personally as the story has it. Attribute carefully: "Lee is
    remembered to have said," "by the traditional account."
  - **Pickett's "General Lee, I have no division"** — likewise traditional and
    **questioned by historians.** Hedge identically. Do NOT state either as flat
    fact.

## §5 Aftermath + the Address (for `aftermath`)
- **July 4:** rain; the dead still lay in the fields. Lee's ~15-mile wagon
  train/ambulance column began the retreat south, escorted by Stuart's cavalry;
  Meade did not aggressively pursue (the "should he have pursued?" debate followed
  Meade for life).
- **Same day, July 4:** **Vicksburg surrendered to Grant** — the Mississippi open
  end to end, the Confederacy cut in two as Lee retreated.
- **The dead/photography:** **Timothy H. O'Sullivan's** photograph "A Harvest of
  Death" recorded the Union dead in the days after. (PD.)
- **The Gettysburg Address:** **November 19, 1863**, the dedication of the
  **Soldiers' National Cemetery**. Featured orator **Edward Everett** spoke ~**two
  hours** (13,607 words); **Lincoln spoke ~two minutes, ~271 words** (Bliss copy,
  10 sentences). Opening (Bliss, verbatim): **"Four score and seven years ago our
  fathers brought forth on this continent, a new nation, conceived in Liberty, and
  dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal."** Closing: "...that
  government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the
  earth." ⚠️ v0 issues: says "two hundred and seventy-two words" (use **271**, or
  "about 270"); the "wrote it on the train" detail is a **MYTH** (drop it); the "he
  thought it a flat failure / won't scour" story rests on Lamon's **unreliable**
  recollection (drop it, or hedge hard as "by one unreliable account"). The deeper
  point — Lincoln elevating the Declaration's "all men are created equal" as the
  nation's founding promise over the slavery-compromised Constitution — is sound and
  is the section's payoff. Cross-links to "A New Birth of Freedom" (civ) and the
  war's meaning.

## §6 COMPREHENSIVENESS ADDITIONS (from the completeness gate — sourced & verified 2026-05-23)
These were ESSENTIAL omissions the comprehensiveness reviewer caught. All now
verified; authors must work them in.

- **Harrison the spy resolves the "Lee was blind" arc (→ `setting`/end, or `mcpherson`/open).**
  With Stuart's cavalry absent, Lee did not know where the Union army was — until
  **Henry Thomas Harrison**, a scout/spy working for **Longstreet (South)**, reached
  camp on the **night of June 28, 1863** and reported the Army of the Potomac had
  crossed into Maryland (around Frederick) and was moving north — and that **Meade
  had replaced Hooker.** Lee, who had distrusted Harrison, believed it, and ordered
  his scattered army to concentrate near **Cashtown** (≈8 miles from Gettysburg).
  Lee later wrote: **"A battle thus became, in a measure, unavoidable."** (Source:
  Wikipedia, *Henry Thomas Harrison*.) This is the missing hinge between "Lee is
  blind" and "the battle starts."

- **Ewell declines Cemetery Hill, evening of July 1 (→ `mcpherson`/end).** As the
  Union streamed back onto the heights, Lee sent **Ewell (South)** a DISCRETIONARY
  order: take the hill **"if he found it practicable, but to avoid a general
  engagement until the arrival of the other divisions."** Ewell judged it not
  practicable and did not attack — and the Union dug in on that ground overnight.
  It became one of the war's great "what ifs." **CRITICAL NUANCE (do not repeat the
  myth as fact):** the famous **"if practicable"** wording first appeared only in
  Lee's REVISED report filed **January 1864**, and the blame-Ewell story was pushed
  hard AFTER Lee's death by **Jubal Early** as part of **"Lost Cause" mythology**;
  modern historians regard Ewell's call as a defensible reading of a discretionary
  order that expressly forbade a general engagement. Frame it as a real decision +
  a famous controversy + flag the Lost-Cause scapegoating — NOT as Ewell's blunder.
  (Sources: Wikipedia *Battle of Gettysburg, first day*; HistoryNet on Ewell.)

- **The Confederate slave-hunt during the march north (→ `setting`).** This is the
  on-theme blind spot. Under **March 1863 orders from Richmond** about "fugitive
  slaves," and led by the cavalry vanguard (**Brig. Gen. Albert Jenkins's (South)**
  Virginia brigade), the Army of Northern Virginia **seized free Black people —
  hundreds, men, women, and children — across Maryland and Pennsylvania (Franklin
  County, Mercersburg, Chambersburg, the Gettysburg area) and sent them south into
  slavery.** A soldier of the **4th Georgia (South)**, John Gay, wrote home that the
  army had "captured… over a hundred negroes." Many of those seized were free-born,
  not escapees. This is documented (Smithsonian; American Battlefield Trust;
  Emerging Civil War; House Divided/Dickinson). It belongs in the march-north
  material: the army that was fighting to preserve slavery carried slavery north
  with it — directly on-theme for this app and for the "A New Birth of Freedom"
  cross-link. State it soberly and factually; it is not a flourish.

- **"Pickett's Charge" is a misnomer (→ `pickett`).** The assault was **three
  divisions** under **Longstreet's (South)** overall command: **Pickett (South)**
  (all-Virginia), **Pettigrew (South)** (North Carolinians, Mississippians,
  Tennesseans, Alabamians, Virginians), and **Trimble (South)**. Pickett commanded
  only **3 of the 11 brigades.** The Virginia-centric name erased the others; some
  historians use "the Pickett–Pettigrew–Trimble Assault." Casualties in the charge:
  **Pickett's division ~2,655; Pettigrew's ~2,700 (roughly equal); Trimble's two
  brigades ~885; total ~6,555** (≥1,123 killed, ~4,019 wounded, many of the wounded
  also captured); Union losses in the repulse ~1,500. (Source: Wikipedia, *Pickett's
  Charge*; American Battlefield Trust.) Name the others; note the misnomer.

- **Lee never invaded the North again (→ `aftermath`, the turning-point mechanism).**
  Gettysburg ended Lee's second and **last** invasion of the North; the Army of
  Northern Virginia fought the rest of the war essentially on the defensive. ONE
  crisp sentence to this effect earns the "turning point" label (don't just assert
  it). (Source: American Battlefield Trust, *Pickett's Charge*.)

- **Deepen the Confederate POV in the Charge (→ `pickett`).** The draft narrates the
  charge mostly from the Union side. Give the Confederate soldier's subjective
  experience of the doomed walk into the guns real weight (it's the signature
  dual-POV feature, and the human heart of the day) — using only pack facts (the
  ¾-mile open ground, the canister, ~half not returning, men sheltering at the
  Emmitsburg Road, Armistead over the wall).

## §FIXES — mandatory corrections to the v0 draft (the fact-checker will police)
1. **Heth "in search of shoes"** → LEGEND. Recast as a reconnaissance in force /
   drop the shoe motive (or explicitly mark it the popular myth it is).
2. **Reynolds "shot through the neck"** → "shot behind the ear," killed instantly.
3. **Culp's Hill "three Confederate divisions"** → "Johnson's division" (one).
4. **"kill or wound fifty thousand men"** → "fifty thousand casualties" / "killed,
   wounded, or captured."
5. **Gettysburg Address "two hundred and seventy-two words"** → 271 (or "about
   270"); **drop the train-writing myth**; drop/hard-hedge the "flat failure" story.
6. **Lee "all my fault" + Pickett "I have no division"** → keep but HEDGE as the
   traditional/disputed accounts they are ("Lee is remembered to have said…").
7. **Town size "ten thousand people"** → ~2,400 (1860). ⚠️ verify.
8. **Devil's Den "New Yorkers and New Hampshiremen"** → "New Yorkers and others" /
   name the 124th New York.
9. **Chancellorsville→invasion "two weeks earlier"** → it was ~4 weeks (battle
   ended May 6; army marched June 3) → "weeks earlier" / "about a month earlier."
   Same for "Jackson a month before" → "weeks before."
10. **"a thousand miles down the Mississippi" (in `setting` AND `hooks`)** → WRONG
    and self-contradicting (`aftermath` correctly says "eight hundred miles to the
    southwest"). Use "hundreds of miles to the southwest" (or drop the figure);
    keep `aftermath`'s "eight hundred miles."
11. **Retreat wagon train "escorted by Stuart's cavalry" (`aftermath`)** → the
    wounded train was **Gen. John Imboden's** command, not Stuart's. → "Confederate
    cavalry under Gen. John Imboden" / "Confederate cavalry."
12. Minor (should-fix): Hood "asked twice" → "asked repeatedly"; Sickles argued
    "for the next sixty years" → "for the rest of his long life"; Devil's Den "two
    Confederate brigades" → "the weight of Hood's attack."
