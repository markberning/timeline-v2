# Crater — LOST CAUSE / FRAMING GATE

**Verdict: FAIR.** This is a model of how to frame a USCT-massacre section. Slavery is named
as the cause in the section's own prose, repeatedly and centrally. The USCT story is told
with dignity and documented accuracy — neither sanitized nor exploited. The racial murders
are handled as cross-confirmed history, not spectacle. No Lost Cause soft-pedaling, no false
balance. Only minor SHOULD-FIX polish.

---

## FIRST CHECK — is slavery named as the cause, in the section's OWN prose? **YES.**

Not gestured at — stated flatly, more than once, in the section's voice:
- §pulled-from-the-lead: *"The Confederacy existed to preserve slavery; eleven states had
  broken away rather than accept any limit on the enslavement of Black people, and built
  their armies to defend it."* This is the ship-blocking requirement met unambiguously, in
  the section's own narration, not buried in a quote.
- §pulled-from-the-lead heading itself: *"Why slavery is the spine of this battle."*
- §the-massacre closing: *"The war was fought over slavery, and this battlefield is the
  proof."*
- §what-it-meant closing: *"The Confederacy fought to keep human beings as property… The
  cause and the crime are the same thing."*

The naming is not decorative — the whole section is built to *demonstrate* the cause through
the USCT's treatment. **Name-the-cause gate: PASS, emphatically.**

## USCT story — told with dignity AND accuracy?

**YES.** The draft threads the needle the brief asks for:
- **Dignity:** the Black soldiers are agents, not props — "fighting in Union blue against
  the slave republic that had once owned them," "the one unit on the field prepared for this
  exact attack," "fought hard." Their competence (the *trained* division) is foregrounded;
  their deaths are not aestheticized.
- **Accuracy / not sanitized:** the optics calculation is named for what it was — *"The
  lives of the Black soldiers were weighed as a political-perception problem — a calculation
  no white division on that field had to survive."* That is exactly right and refuses the
  comfortable reading (that pulling them was protective). It correctly frames the pull as a
  failure of regard, then the late commitment as compounding it.
- **Not gratuitous / not exploitative:** the massacre is told through cross-confirmed
  testimony and stops short of the goriest documented detail (the "let me kill one more"
  throat-cutting is omitted). The murders are presented as historical fact with named
  Confederate witnesses, not as shock-content. The kill-ratio is explicitly subordinated to
  the eyewitnesses ("The numbers do not prove the massacre by themselves. The eyewitnesses
  do that."). This is documented history, not spectacle.

## Lost Cause / soft-pedaling / false-balance hunt

- **No "both sides" hedging.** The atrocity is stated as fact, and the draft preempts the
  classic Lost Cause dodge by anchoring it in *Confederate* sources: *"It is important that
  this is not a Northern accusation. It is confirmed by the Confederates who were there."*
  This is the correct refutation of the "Union propaganda" framing the fact pack warned
  against. **Strong.**
- **Mahone's intervention does NOT become exoneration.** The draft explicitly blocks that
  reading: *"That does not soften the verdict. The killing was general enough that a general
  had to beg to slow it… and Pegram was busy calling the whole business a good thing."*
  Exactly the guardrail the pack demanded. **Strong.**
- **No romanticization of the Confederacy or the engineering.** The "audacious feat" framing
  is reserved for the Union miners (the ranks), and is immediately set against the moral
  catastrophe on top of it. No "brilliant Mahone counterattack" gloss that would launder the
  rim murders.
- **No passive-voice laundering of the killers.** The murders have agents: Confederate
  soldiers, an approving Confederate colonel, a private who killed several. The racial logic
  is named as the Confederacy's own — "the same racial logic that built it reached down into
  the hole."

## SHOULD-FIX (polish only — nothing ship-blocking)

1. **"murdered for the color of their skin" (closing MEANWHILE).** Accurate and earned, but
   appears twice in close proximity ("killed for trying to surrender" / "murdered after
   surrender for the color of their skin" / "executed them for raising their hands"). The
   repetition is rhetorically deliberate and defensible; if trimming for register, keep the
   plainest one. **Optional.**

2. **The Union's own racism deserves one more degree of clarity.** The draft names the
   optics calculation sharply, but a reader could still leave thinking the Union's sin was
   only callous PR. The deeper point — that the same nation fielding these men also discounted
   their lives enough to make their deaths a *presentation* problem — is *implied* well
   ("a calculation no white division had to survive") but could be made one notch more
   explicit so the Union is not let off as merely clumsy. The draft already does this better
   than most; this is a deepening, not a correction. **Optional / SHOULD-FIX-lite.**

3. **"slave republic"** (twice) is strong, accurate house-voice; no change needed — flagged
   only to confirm it is supported, not editorializing beyond the record. It is supported.

## MUST-FIX

**None.** Slavery is named as the cause in the section's own prose; the USCT are rendered
with dignity and documented accuracy; the massacre is handled as cross-confirmed history
without spectacle; Lost Cause dodges (propaganda-claim, Mahone-as-exoneration, both-sides
hedging) are each preempted by name. This section is, if anything, an exemplar for the
vertical.
