## ❌ ERRORS — MUST-FIX

### ❌ 1 — "Both World Wars, Korea, Vietnam, the whole list, added together, and the Civil War still tops it"

**Location:** "The arithmetic of the dead" section, third paragraph.

**Claim:** "More American soldiers died in the Civil War than in all the nation's other wars *combined* — the Revolution, the War of 1812, the Mexican War, both World Wars, Korea, Vietnam, the whole list, added together, and the Civil War still tops it."

**Reality:** FALSE at the traditional 620,000 figure and marginal even at the Hacker 750,000 estimate.

Verified US military deaths (Wikipedia / Congressional Research Service):
- American Revolution: ~23,800
- War of 1812: ~15,000
- Mexican-American War: ~13,283
- Spanish-American War: ~2,446
- World War I: ~116,516
- World War II: ~405,399
- Korean War: ~36,574
- Vietnam War: ~58,220
- **All other wars combined (excl. Civil War): ≈ 671,000+**

At 620,000 (traditional), the Civil War falls **short** of all others combined. Even the Wikipedia figure of ~655,000 falls short of the ~671,000 sum. Only at the Hacker high end (~750,000–850,000) does the claim hold. The draft has already led with the range framing ("about 750,000, possibly as many as 850,000"), but then asserts the all-wars-combined claim as settled fact without tying it to the Hacker number, which it depends on. More cautious sources (American Battlefield Trust, NPS) state the narrower correct claim: "The numbers of Civil War dead were not equaled by the combined toll of other American conflicts **until the War in Vietnam**" — i.e., the Civil War exceeded all pre-Vietnam wars combined, not the entire list including both World Wars.

**CORRECTION REQUIRED:** Either (a) restrict the comparison to pre-Vietnam wars — "more than in every American war from the Revolution through Korea combined" — which is defensible at 620,000, OR (b) tie the all-wars-combined assertion explicitly to the Hacker range — "if Hacker's revised count holds, more than all other American wars combined." Do not state the all-in version as unqualified fact.

**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_casualties_of_war · https://www.battlefields.org/learn/articles/civil-war-casualties

### ❌ 2 — KKK founding year stated as 1866; most primary sources give December 1865

**Location:** "The counter-revolution" section.

**Claim:** "The Ku Klux Klan, founded in Tennessee in 1866…"

**Reality:** The preponderant historical record places the KKK's founding in Pulaski, Tennessee, on **December 24, 1865**, not 1866. This date appears in the Zinn Education Project, the Equal Justice Initiative, the PBS American Experience, Britannica, and most standard references. Some sources (the Tennessee Encyclopedia) give spring/early 1866 for when it was formally named or became operational, but the founding meeting is December 1865.

Using "1866" is either wrong or reflects an outlier framing and will flag for any reader who knows the history.

**CORRECTION REQUIRED:** Change to "founded in Pulaski, Tennessee, in late 1865" — or "founded in Tennessee in 1865" — to match the dominant scholarly consensus. If the fact pack specifies 1866, flag that discrepancy to the author; the external record strongly favors 1865.

**Source:** https://www.zinnedproject.org/news/tdih/ku-klux-klan-founded/ · https://www.britannica.com/topic/Ku-Klux-Klan · https://calendar.eji.org/racial-injustice/dec/24

### ⚠️ 4 — "Sixteen Black men served in Congress between 1870 and 1877"

**Location:** "When Black men held office" section.

**Claim:** "Sixteen Black men served in Congress between 1870 and 1877 — fourteen in the House, two in the Senate."

**What sources say:** The figure of 16 Black congressmen serving during the 1870s is confirmed by the US House of Representatives History, Art & Archives ("During the 1870s, 16 Black Members held seats in Congress — 14 in the House, two in the Senate"). However, the claim bundles the date range as "between 1870 and 1877." Blanche Bruce was seated in 1875 and served until **1881** — beyond 1877. The two Senate members (Revels and Bruce) do not both fit "between 1870 and 1877" if Bruce's term ran to 1881. The 14/2 split is the right count for the Reconstruction era broadly, but the bracketed dates are slightly imprecise.

**Risk:** Low — the claim is directionally correct and the 16/14/2 split is confirmed. The date endpoint "1877" could be read as "serving during the Reconstruction period ending in 1877" rather than "all terms ending by 1877," which is a fair reading. Flag for author awareness but not a hard error.

**Source:** https://history.house.gov/Blog/2020/November/11-25-Bulwark/ · https://www.senate.gov/senators/FeaturedBios/Featured_Bio_Bruce.htm
