## ❌ WRONG (must fix)

### 1. New Orleans clause — fabricated wording inside quotation marks (draft line 31)
The draft presents this as a verbatim quotation of the treaty:
> "the town **and port** of New Orleans and the island in which it is situated, **shall** remain to France."

The actual Article VII text (verified against Avalon Project, Wikisource, and AmericanHistoryCentral — all three agree):
> "except the town of New Orleans and the island in which it is situated, **which** shall remain to France"

Two errors inside the quote marks: (a) **"and port"** is not in the treaty — the treaty says "the town of New Orleans," not "the town and port of New Orleans"; (b) the draft drops **"which"** ("which shall remain" → "shall remain"). A quoted treaty clause must be verbatim. Either correct the quotation to "the town of New Orleans and the island in which it is situated, which shall remain to France," or paraphrase outside quote marks. Note the fact pack itself rendered this clause wrong ("the town and port of New Orleans… shall remain to France") — the author inherited the error; it must not ship.
**Sources:** [Avalon Project — Treaty of Paris 1763](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/paris763.asp); [Wikisource — Treaty of Paris (1763)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763)); [American History Central — Treaty of Paris 1763 text](https://www.americanhistorycentral.com/documents/treaty-of-paris-1763-text/)

### 2. "roughly ten British forts" — overcount (draft lines 85 and 119)
The draft twice states Pontiac's War "would seize roughly ten British forts" / "took roughly ten British forts." The authoritative figure is **nine** ("Nine forts were destroyed" — Wikipedia, "Pontiac's War"; the detailed fall-of-forts table lists eight captured May–June 1763). "Roughly ten" rounds in the wrong direction past the firm figure. Change both occurrences to **"nine British forts"** (or "roughly nine"). The hedge "roughly" does not rescue a number that is higher than the sourced count.
**Source:** [Wikipedia — Pontiac's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_War)
