**Britain's system**

- ✅ Ohio Company of Virginia: royal grant of **200,000 acres** in 1749, plus **300,000 more** promised if it settled **100 families** within **seven years**. Verbatim match (Wikipedia/Ohio Company; America's Best History; grant July 12, 1749 — draft says only "1749," fine).

- ✅ Sir William Johnson (British): Irish-born, settled Mohawk Valley NY, learned Mohawk language; adopted ~1742 as honorary sachem, named **Warraghiyagey** = "a man who undertakes great things"; made **Superintendent of Indian Affairs, Northern District, 1756**, reporting directly to London over the colonies. All verbatim.
- ✅ Molly Brant (Konwatsi'tsiaienni), Mohawk clan mother, Johnson's household partner / power broker — draft uses "household partner," NOT a formal marriage, exactly per the fact pack CAUTION. Correct.
- ✅ Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea), Molly's younger brother, born early 1743, "about thirteen" when he went with Johnson to the **Battle of Lake George, 1755** (his first action); fame came later. Confirmed (NPS/Wikipedia: "at age 13 he accompanied … to the Battle of Lake George"). Draft correctly does not overstate his 1754–63 role.

**Monongahela (1755)**
- ✅ **9 July 1755**, ~10 miles short of Fort Duquesne (forks of the Ohio, present Pittsburgh), Braddock's column met a force sent out to meet it.
- ✅ Force "overwhelmingly Native": of ~900, roughly 250 French troops + Canadian militia (108 regulars + 146 militia) and ~640 Native warriors (Wikipedia ~637). Native ≈ two-thirds. Verbatim match.
- ✅ French field commander (Beaujeu, unnamed in draft) killed almost at once; warriors carried the fight from forest cover vs. tight European formations.
- ✅ Casualties: of Braddock's ~1,300–1,460 engaged, >900 killed or wounded (~456 killed / ~422 wounded); French/allied losses trivial (~23–39 killed). Confirmed.
- ✅ Braddock mortally wounded, died **13 July 1755**; George Washington helped organize the survivors' retreat. Confirmed (Mount Vernon).

**Fort William Henry (1757)**
- ✅ Siege; **Montcalm** (~8,000: ~3,000 French regulars, ~3,000 Canadian militia, **nearly 2,000 Native warriors** from both alliance circles incl. Abenaki) vs. Fort William Henry at south end of Lake George, held by **Lt. Col. George Monro** (British), garrison ~2,300, many sick with smallpox. Confirmed.
- ✅ Monro surrendered **8 August** (white flag late Aug 8); terms = march out under French escort with honors of war, keep muskets but **no ammunition**, one symbolic cannon, **18-month** parole. The attack on the column began the morning of **9 August**. Draft's sequencing (surrender Aug 8 → column marches/attacked Aug 9) matches the source ("August 8–9"). ✅
- ✅ Warriors promised plunder/scalps/captives; terms gave the war stores to the French and left the warriors nothing → looked like a French deal with the enemy.
- ✅ QUOTE (Ian K. Steele): the terms bred "resentment, as it appeared that the French were conspiring with their enemies, the British, against their friends, the Indians." — verbatim match (Steele, *Betrayals*, via Wikipedia). Attributed in-draft as a historian's line. ✅
- ✅ Attack on Aug 9: sick/wounded in camp struck first, then the column; killing, scalping, stripping, seizing women/children/servants/enslaved as captives.
- ✅ QUOTE (Col. Joseph Frye, British, eyewitness): "...the savages still carrying away officers, privates, women and children, some of which later they kill'd & scalpt in the road." — matches the pack's sourced wording; draft attributes correctly.
- ✅ Scale: legend ~1,500; modern (Steele) ~**69–184** killed/missing, under 8% of the ~2,300 who surrendered; forensic work found disease killed more of the dead than weapons. Confirmed (Steele "69 to 184," "at most 8% of the 2,308"). Draft uses the modern range, as mandated. ✅
- ✅ Montcalm's culpability presented as genuinely contested (officers-overwhelmed vs. saw-it-coming) — correct, not picking a side.
- ✅ Smallpox carried home: garrison riddled with smallpox; Great Lakes warriors carried captives/plunder/disease back to distant villages, sparking epidemics (Potawatomi nearly wiped out). Confirmed via multiple independent sources (Penn Museum; Warfare History Network; Upper Peninsula History). NOTE: the current Wikipedia FWH article does not itself state the carried-home epidemic — but the claim is well-attested elsewhere and the pack cited Penn Museum. Treated as ✅ CONFIRMED.

**Easton (1758)**
- ✅ **October 1758, Easton, Pennsylvania**; **more than 500** Native people; colonial governors of Pennsylvania and New Jersey; ~**13 nations** incl. Ohio Delaware/Lenape (eastern + western), Shawnee, three Iroquois nations; **Teedyuscung** (Delaware) prominent. Confirmed.
- ✅ Terms reached **26 October 1758**: Britain pledged to recognize the nations' Ohio hunting-ground rights and to keep settlers out of lands **west of the Allegheny/Appalachian Mountains after the war**; Pennsylvania returned lands taken in earlier fraud (Walking Purchase); governor (Denny) agreed to deal directly with the Delaware. Confirmed.
- ✅ Military effect: Ohio Delaware/Shawnee pulled away/pledged neutrality → Fort Duquesne lost its Native shield; as **Brig. Gen. John Forbes** (British) closed in **November 1758**, the French (outnumbered, abandoned) blew up Fort Duquesne and left; British built Fort Pitt (Pittsburgh). Confirmed.
- ✅ Easton fed the 1763 Royal Proclamation line; the promise didn't hold. Confirmed.

**Framing / human cost**
- ✅ Tanaghrisson the Mingo "Half King," Hendrick, Teedyuscung each pursued a Native aim. Supported. Walking Purchase fraud against the Delaware; ~800,000-acre Mohawk grievance; Ohio Company 200,000-acre grant. All consistent with above.

**Post-conquest collapse**
- ✅ France lost: Quebec 1759, Montreal 1760, **Treaty of Paris 1763** handed New France to Britain; France's defeat removed the second empire the nations balanced against. Confirmed.
- ✅ Jeffery Amherst ended customary **gift-giving from ~early 1761**, restricted gunpowder/ammunition to Native traders, treated nations as conquered subjects; Native people complained of being treated "no better than slaves or dogs." Confirmed (Pontiac's War / Amherst articles).
- ✅ QUOTE (Amherst → Bouquet, 1763): "Could it not be contrived to send the Small Pox among those disaffected tribes of Indians? We must on this occasion use every stratagem in our power to reduce them." — verbatim (letter dated July 7, 1763, per UMass/Derrico). ✅
- ✅ In-prose paraphrase of the follow-up: "inoculate the Indians by means of blankets … to try every other method that can serve to extirpate this execrable race." — verbatim from the July 16, 1763 follow-up. ✅
- ✅ Pontiac's War: **May 1763**, broad coalition led by Odawa leader **Pontiac (Obwandiyag)** among others, against British posts across the Great Lakes and Ohio; trigger = Amherst's policy + the news France had ceded the homeland to Britain. Confirmed.
- ✅ Fort Pitt smallpox-blanket episode (summer 1763): British gave besieging Delaware **two blankets and a handkerchief** from the smallpox hospital, recorded in a trader's journal (Trent, "two Blankets and an Handkerchief out of the Small Pox Hospital," entry dated May 24, 1763); whether it caused an outbreak is debated, but Amherst's documented intent is not. Draft frames exactly this way. ✅

## ⚠️ / SHOULD-FIX (non-blocking — no fabricated specific)

1. ⚠️ **Cooper attribution of the "1,500 dead" figure (draft line 98).** The draft reads: "Early accounts, and later James Fenimore Cooper's novel *The Last of the Mohicans*, claimed as many as 1,500 dead." Per the Siege of Fort William Henry article, the ~1,500 figure ORIGINATED with **Jonathan Carver's 1778 account** (and was echoed by early historians like Benson Lossing); Cooper's 1826 novel dramatized and popularized the massacre but is not the source that "claimed 1,500 dead." The claim is not fabricated — the 1,500 legend is real and the draft hedges it correctly against the modern range — but tying the number specifically to Cooper's novel is an imprecise attribution. **Recommend:** soften to "Early accounts (Jonathan Carver's 1778 narrative chief among them), and the legend later cemented by James Fenimore Cooper's novel *The Last of the Mohicans*, put the dead as high as 1,500." Should-fix, not ship-blocking.

2. ⚠️ **Amherst quote presented as a single utterance.** The draft's blockquote (line 131) joins "Could it not be contrived to send the Small Pox…" with "We must on this occasion use every stratagem in our power to reduce them," and the prose later renders the "inoculate … by means of blankets … extirpate this execrable race" line. Both phrases are genuine Amherst–Bouquet correspondence, but they come from two different letters (~July 7 and ~July 16, 1763), not one continuous sentence. The draft does not falsely claim they are one sentence (it presents them in separate places), so this is acceptable as written; flagging only so the integrator does not later merge them into a single fabricated quotation. No change required.
