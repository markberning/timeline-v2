## Independently Built Must-Cover Checklist

1. The scale — roughly how many men in captivity, roughly how many died, some combat comparison to anchor the reader
2. The exchange system that kept camps empty — need to explain WHY it existed and how it worked before explaining its failure
3. The mechanism of collapse — racial POW policy as the load-bearing cause; Grant's role correctly framed (kept the door shut; didn't slam it)
4. Andersonville as the worst single place — physical dimensions, peak count, causes of death, the deadline, the water

8. The reckoning — grave identification, Atwater, Barton

## SHOULD-CONSIDER Items (enriching, non-blocking)

### 1. Atwater's arrest and pardon — a missing grace note in the Barton beat

The draft says Atwater "secretly kept a second copy... smuggled it out... carried that list to the government." What it omits: after the Andersonville expedition, Atwater was court-martialed and sentenced to 18 months for larceny — for refusing to surrender his own death register to military authorities. He served two months before President Andrew Johnson pardoned him (with Barton lobbying on his behalf). This is a vivid irony: the man who preserved 12,000 names was jailed by his own government for doing it. The draft's Barton beat is good; this detail would make Atwater's arc feel complete rather than merely instrumental. Non-blocking because the existing treatment covers his essential role, but the arrest-and-pardon is a strong story beat that's sourced (NPS Andersonville, Clara Barton Museum, New England Historical Society).

### 2. Davis named and dropped from the original Wirz indictment

The draft covers the scapegoat controversy well but doesn't note that the original charges explicitly named Jefferson Davis (and Robert E. Lee) as co-conspirators before the War Department ordered their names removed. This fact sharpens the "convenient scapegoat" argument — Wirz was *literally* the substitute for Davis on the indictment. It also explains why the Lost Cause could plausibly argue political prosecution: the government dropped the man who ran the system and hanged the man who ran the pen. Non-blocking because the scapegoat debate is already framed honestly, but this specific detail gives it a harder edge (sourced: UMKC Famous Trials, Famous-Trials.com account).

### 3. The "Wirz's prisoner delegation to Stanton" moment

In July 1864, Wirz sent a committee of Andersonville prisoners on parole to Washington to explain the camp's conditions directly to Secretary Stanton and plead for resuming exchanges. Stanton refused to see them. This is a strong story beat: it complicates both the Grant-blame narrative AND the pure-Wirz-guilt narrative, and it shows that people inside the camp itself were trying to force a resolution. Non-blocking (the exchange-collapse mechanics are already well-covered), but it would add a human-scale dramatic moment to what is currently an institutional-level account.

### 4. Lieber Code as the legal context behind "laws of war"

The draft uses "laws of war" twice (in the Wirz charges and the "meanwhile handoff") without defining what that meant in 1863–65. The Lieber Code (General Orders No. 100, issued April 1863) was the Union's codification of the laws of war — the document that made prisoner mistreatment prosecutable as a war crime. Without it, "laws of war" floats as a vague phrase. A single sentence would ground it. Non-blocking; this is context the reader can intuit, and adding it risks slowing the narrative.

## Fact Pack Blind Spots

The fact pack is comprehensive and well-structured. Two gaps worth noting for the authoring team:

**1. Atwater's arrest and pardon are absent from the pack.** Beat 7 covers Atwater's list and the expedition but stops at the flag-raising. The court-martial and Johnson pardon (sourced at NPS Andersonville, Clara Barton Museum) should be added to Beat 7 as a supplemental fact, flagged as SHOULD-CONSIDER for the author to deploy as enrichment if the Atwater/Barton beat feels thin.

**2. The Davis/Lee co-conspirator omission from the Wirz indictment is not in Beat 3.** The pack covers the scapegoat controversy in general terms but doesn't document the specific fact that Davis and Lee were named and then removed from the indictment by War Department order. This is sourced (UMKC Famous Trials; Famous-Trials.com) and is the hardest concrete evidence for the "convenient scapegoat" reading. Worth adding to Beat 3 as a CONTESTED/enrichment item.

**3. No material gap in scale, exchange mechanics, camp conditions, or memory-war beats.** The pack's sourcing is solid and the CONTESTED section correctly flags all the genuinely disputed numbers.
