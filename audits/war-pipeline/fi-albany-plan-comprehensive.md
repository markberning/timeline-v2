## Native nations' perspective and aims — the gate's priority dimension

**Correctly weighted. This is the draft's strongest feature.** It does the four things
a Native-first treatment must do:
1. **Leads with the alliance as the congress's FIRST business**, not a footnote
   (dedicated section before the colonial roll-call, explicit "ahead of anything else").
2. **Frames the Iroquois as sovereign actors with their own aims** — protecting land,
   playing the empires off each other — stated twice (Iroquois section + close).
3. **Holds the dual truth** — the same congress renewed the chain AND stole land from
   Delaware/Shawnee who weren't present. The draft refuses to separate them ("the
   difference between the two is the whole truth of the event").
4. **Closes on the Native stakes**, not the colonial union story — the name itself
   "hides a larger truth," and the war's end as catastrophe for the eastern woodlands.

No correction needed here. The weighting is right.

## SHOULD-FIX (not ship-blocking)

**S1 — The 1753 break is thin/absent, and it is the actual trigger.** The draft
explains London's order as a response to generic "three years of neglect" and a
chain gone "rusty." The sharper, more dramatic, and more *Native-agency* fact is that
in June 1753 Hendrick led 16 Mohawk leaders past Albany directly to Governor Clinton
in New York City and **declared the Covenant Chain broken** — and *that* is what
prompted the Board of Trade to order the congress. Multiple sources (American History
Central, EBSCO, New York Almanack) make this the proximate cause. Including it would
(a) tighten the causal chain (Native action → London's order, not just British
drift), and (b) reinforce exactly the "Iroquois as agents" thesis the draft is
building. This is a storytelling upgrade, not an essential omission — hence SHOULD,
not MUST. **Needs a fact-pack addition first** (the fact pack does not currently carry
the 1753 NYC break; §1 only says "no public treaty at Albany for ~3 years").

**S2 — The specific land grievances behind Hendrick's anger are stated only in the
abstract.** The draft says "settlers pushing onto Iroquois land" and "the grievance
underneath." Research surfaces concretes the draft could draw on for force: large-scale
Mohawk-land fraud in the Mohawk Valley (the ~800,000-acre figure appears in sources),
and the Treaty of Lancaster (1744) / Ohio Company being used to claim far more than the
Iroquois thought they ceded. The draft's abstraction is *defensible* under
storytelling-first economy, but one concrete (the Mohawk Valley squatting / Canajoharie
pressure on Hendrick's own town) would make the grievance land harder. **Would need
fact-pack support before authoring** any specific figure.

## Fact-pack blind spots

**FP1 — The 1753 break is missing from the fact pack.** §1 frames the cause purely as
"no public treaty at Albany for ~3 years." The proximate trigger — Hendrick's June 1753
declaration to Clinton in NYC that the chain was broken, which is *why* the Board of
Trade ordered the meeting — is not in the pack, so the author could not write it even
if they wanted to. Recommend adding it to §1/§3 (sourced: American History Central;
New York Almanack; EBSCO). This is the one genuine content gap.

**FP2 — Wampum belt is absent.** Research (American History Central) notes the Covenant
Chain was physically embodied in a wampum belt. Minor; optional color, not required.

**FP3 — No essential structural facts about the plan are missing.** Powers, structure,
apportionment, dates, the double rejection, and both Franklin quotes are all in the pack
and correctly used.

Nothing ESSENTIAL is MISSING; every load-bearing beat is COVERED, and the dimension this

storytelling sharpening that requires a fact-pack addition first, not a ship-blocker.
**PASS.**
