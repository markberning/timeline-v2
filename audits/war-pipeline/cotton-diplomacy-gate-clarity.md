## MUST-FIX (genuine comprehension breakers)

**1. "blockade" used without definition in "Why the bet went bad"**
> "Once the Union navy's blockade of the Southern coast tightened (more on that shortly)…"
First use of the word "blockade" — no gloss. The reader is told "more on that shortly" but has no working concept to follow the argument. The distinction between the embargo (South's choice) and the blockade (Union's imposition) is the section's entire third point, and it is lost if the reader doesn't understand what a blockade is.
**Fix:** Inline gloss at first use — "(a ring of warships sealing Southern ports to stop all trade)" or similar.

**2. "emancipation" used without definition in the Lancashire section**
> "resolved to support the Union and the cause of emancipation"
The Emancipation Proclamation isn't explained until two sections later. A 15-year-old who doesn't know what "emancipation" means cannot understand the moral force of the Lancashire workers' choice — which is the entire point of the section.
**Fix:** "(emancipation — the freeing of enslaved people)" inline at first use.

**3. "raider program" used without definition before the gloss for "commerce raider" arrives**
> "Bulloch's job was to procure warships from British shipyards… the architect of its raider program."
The term "raider" and "raider program" appear before the inline gloss two paragraphs later. A zero-knowledge reader doesn't know what a raider is when they first hit the concept.
**Fix:** Gloss "raider program" at first use: "…the architect of its raider program — a plan to build warships that would hunt and destroy Union merchant ships."
