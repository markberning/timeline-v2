# Fact Ledger: "Pontiac's War & the Aftermath for Native Nations"

Maps every concrete claim in `fi-pontiacs-war-draft.md` to its fact-pack support.

## Intro / framing
- "French and Indian War" is the colonists' name, named for their enemies; "Indians" fought on all sides and for themselves → Pack §intro note + §9 framing; brief F&I framing rule.
- Britain won in 1763; nations of Great Lakes/Ohio rose against the victor → Pack §1, §4.

## A Game With Two Empires, Then One
- *pays d'en haut* = Great Lakes "upper country"; played France and Britain against each other; neither could hold interior alone; balance = leverage → Pack §1.
- Quebec fell Sept 1759 (Plains of Abraham) → Pack §1.
- Montreal capitulated Sept 1760, end of French Canada → Pack §1.
- Treaty of Paris signed Feb 10, 1763; transferred Canada + French claims east of the Mississippi to Britain → Pack §1.
- With France gone, no second empire; diplomacy evaporated; single contemptuous power → Pack §1.

## Amherst Pulls the Alliance Apart
- Amherst = British commander-in-chief in North America; treated nations as conquered subjects → Pack §2.
- (a) Ended gift-giving ~1761; gifts (guns/knives/ammo/tobacco/clothing) were the alliance, not bribes; French practiced for generations; nations read it as being treated as conquered → Pack §2(a).
- (b) Restricted gunpowder/shot; basis of food supply + fur economy; read as prelude to further conquest; Aug letter to William Johnson, "purchasing the good behavior of the Indians," limit ammo, feared powder turned on British → Pack §2(b). William Johnson tagged "(British)" + titled superintendent of Indian affairs → Pack §7.
- (c) Open contempt; "treated no better than slaves or dogs" → Pack §2(c).
- QUOTE "extirpate this execrable race." — Amherst, July 16, 1763, to Bouquet → Pack §2 VERBATIM box + §6 (July 16 letter) + §10. Well-attested per pack.
- [VERIFY] flag raised on the "vilest race of beings... meritorious act" wording; NOT used in draft, only flagged → Pack §2 [VERIFY-WORDING], §contested #4.

## The Prophet
- Neolin = "the Enlightened One," Lenape (Delaware) holy man; ~1761 fast + vision; journeyed to the Master of Life, "thought us into being" → Pack §3.
- Message: reject European goods/rum/dependence, return to old ways, drive out British; blended Christian + traditional; spread across Ohio/Great Lakes → Pack §3.
- Pontiac recited vision at 1763 council; recorded by a French chronicler at Detroit; relayed/translated, not a transcript → Pack §3, §10 caveat.
- QUOTE "This land where ye dwell... did you not live by bow and arrow?... You had no need of gun nor powder..." — Master of Life via Neolin via Pontiac, Detroit 1763 → Pack §3 VERBATIM box.

## A Coalition, Not a King
- "Pontiac's War" is the colonial name; broad decentralized multi-nation coalition, no supreme commander; Pontiac prominent at Detroit, not sole organizer → Pack §intro, §4.
- Pontiac (Obwandiyag), war chief of the Odawa (Ottawa) → Pack §4, §intro naming.
- Coalition nations: Odawa, Ojibwe, Potawatomi, Wyandot (Huron), Lenape (Delaware), Shawnee, Seneca/Mingo, Miami, Wea, Kickapoo, Mascouten → Pack §4.
- Leaders: Guyasuta (Seneca/Mingo); Charlot Kaské (Shawnee), most uncompromising, crossed Mississippi rather than make peace → Pack §4.
- April 27, 1763, war council on the Ecorse River, ~10 miles SW of Detroit, retold Neolin's vision → Pack §4. (km conversion 16 km added by author per miles-first rule.)
- QUOTE "...exterminate from our lands this nation which seeks only to destroy us." — Pontiac, April 1763; translated summary → Pack §4 VERBATIM + §10.
- May 1: entered Fort Detroit with ~50 Odawa, ostensibly to dance, to scout → Pack §4.
- May 7: surprise seizure failed, commander Henry Gladwin (British) warned; siege began → Pack §4. Gladwin tagged "(British)."
- Siege ~May 9 → Pack §4, §6.

## Eight Forts in a Summer
- "about eight of roughly twelve" posts taken in weeks → Pack §5 (hedge enforced per contested #1).
- Sandusky (Ohio) May 16, Wyandots via false council, ~15 killed, burned → Pack §5 table.
- St. Joseph (Michigan) May 25, Potawatomis, deception, most of 15-man garrison killed → Pack §5 table.
- Fort Miami (Indiana) May 27, commander lured by a Miami woman, 9-man garrison surrendered → Pack §5 table.
- Ouiatenon (Indiana) June 1, Weas/Kickapoos/Mascoutens, ~20 men, no bloodshed → Pack §5 table.
- Venango (Pennsylvania) mid-June, Senecas killed entire ~12-man garrison → Pack §5 table.
- Le Boeuf June 18, most of ~12-man garrison escaped to Fort Pitt → Pack §5 table.
- Presque Isle June 19–20, ~250 warriors, surrendered after two days → Pack §5 table.
- Michilimackinac "early June" (date hedged); baggataway/lacrosse; king's birthday; ball near palisade; women smuggled weapons under blankets; ~half of ~35-man garrison killed, rest taken → Pack §5 + §contested #5 (date hedge).

## The Two That Held
- Detroit + Fort Pitt did not fall; largest/most important posts → Pack §5, §6.
- Detroit siege began ~May 9 → Pack §6.
- July 31: Captain James Dalyell (British) led ~250 in night sortie; ambushed at Parent's Creek; ~20 British killed incl. Dalyell; "ran red"; Bloody Run; coalition victory, garrison held → Pack §6. Dalyell tagged "(British)."
- Pontiac lifted siege Oct 31 after word French would not help; warriors dispersed to winter hunting grounds → Pack §6.
- Fort Pitt ("where three rivers meet" = author gloss for the Forks of the Ohio, consistent with pack's Fort Pitt location; safe geographic fact) besieged ~June 22; ~550 people, 200+ women/children; Captain Simeon Ecuyer (British); smallpox already present → Pack §6. Ecuyer tagged "(British)."

## The Blankets
- One of the documented attempts by a European power to use disease deliberately as a weapon → Pack §6 (intent + act not contested).
- June 24, 1763 parley; British gave besieging Lenape (Delaware) emissaries items from smallpox hospital → Pack §6.
- William Trent (British) journal → Pack §6. Trent tagged "(British)."
- QUOTE "Out of our regard for them, we gave them two Blankets and an Handkerchief out of the Small Pox Hospital. I hope it will have the desired effect." — Trent, June 24, 1763 → Pack §6 VERBATIM.
- Invoice "taken from people in the Hospital to Convey the Smallpox to the Indians"; fort reimbursed Trent → Pack §6 VERBATIM.
- Amherst→Bouquet c. early July; QUOTE "Could it not be contrived to send the small pox among the disaffected tribes of Indians? We must on this occasion use every stratagem in our power to reduce them." → Pack §6 VERBATIM. Bouquet (British) tagged.
- Bouquet replied he would try via blankets, taking care not to catch it → Pack §6 (Bouquet July 13).
- Amherst replied July 16 approving, the "execrable race" letter → Pack §6 (July 16).
- June 24 gift and July letters are SEPARATE, both real, dates not merged → Pack §6 author note.
- Effectiveness contested: smallpox already circulating; material may be too old; Ranlet says no evidence it worked, others think it may have; intent not in question, only result → Pack §6 contested + §contested #2.
- Bushy Run Aug 5–6: Bouquet ~500 men, intercepted ~25 miles (40 km) east by Lenape/Shawnee/Mingo/Wyandot; feigned retreat; ~50 killed, ~60 wounded (~quarter of force); siege lifted ~Aug 20 → Pack §6 Bushy Run.
- Devil's Hole Sept 14: Senecas ambushed supply train + relief at Niagara portage; 70+ killed; deadliest single British loss → Pack §6 optional.

## Nobody Could Win
- Neither side could win; stalemate forced Britain to reverse → Pack §7.
- Amherst recalled to London autumn 1763; replaced by General Thomas Gage (British) → Pack §7 (1763, typo "1863" disregarded per contested note). Gage tagged "(British)."
- William Johnson authorized to restore gift-giving + diplomacy; deliberate reversal → Pack §7.
- Niagara congress July–Aug 1764, ~2,000 Native people → Pack §7.
- Bouquet to the Muskingum Oct 1764; Ohio nations peace; returned 200+ captives → Pack §7.
- Pontiac peace July 25, 1766, Fort Ontario (Oswego); no land ceded, no prisoners surrendered, no hostages; Johnson exceptional deference → Pack §7.
- (Pontiac's 1769 murder NOT used — optional per brief.)

## The Line on the Map
- Royal Proclamation issued by George III Oct 7, 1763, war still ongoing, to calm frontier + reassure nations → Pack §8.
- Line along Appalachian crest, watershed logic (east-draining open / west-draining reserved); settlement west barred; "Indian Reserve"; private land purchase forbidden, only Crown → Pack §8.
- QUOTE "...reserved to them ... as their Hunting Grounds" → Pack §8 VERBATIM + §10.
- "forthwith to remove themselves" → Pack §8 VERBATIM.
- Speculator defined (investors buying frontier land cheap to resell) = author gloss per voice rule; concept from Pack §8 ("speculators").
- Washington promised ~20,000 acres western bounty land; called line a temporary expedient; told agent to keep securing land; Virginia companies lobbied → Pack §8.
- Line never enforceable; settlers crossed; later treaties pushed boundary west, opened Kentucky + West Virginia; grievance toward Revolution → Pack §8.

## What the Rising Won, and What It Could Not Stop
- Won concessions: Amherst gone, gift-giving/diplomacy restored, royal line drawn, Pontiac peace without cession → Pack §9.
- First major multi-nation resistance not ending in outright defeat; precedent for Brant + Tecumseh → Pack §9.
- French counterweight gone for good; leverage permanently lost; one empire alone → Pack §9.
- Proclamation Line ignored; dispossession continued slower but unbroken → Pack §9.
- Casualties: ~400 British soldiers killed (+~50 captured/killed); George Croghan (British) estimated ~2,000 settlers killed/captured + ~4,000 displaced; 200+ Native warriors killed, more if disease counted → Pack §9. Croghan tagged "(British)."
- Framing: nations never conquered/consulted/agreed; Treaty of Paris handed their country king-to-king; defended their land, not rebels against a master; won treatment as nations, could not win the longer war over land → Pack §9 framing.
