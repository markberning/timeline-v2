## 1. Identity / Date / Location / Theatre

- **Name:** **Battle of Jonesborough** (a.k.a. Battle of Jonesboro). The **last battle of
  the Atlanta Campaign** — the engagement that forced the fall of Atlanta. **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **Dates:** **August 31 – September 1, 1864** (two days). **[WIKI] [ABT] [AHC]**
- **Location:** **Jonesborough, Clayton County, Georgia** — about **15–20 miles south of
  Atlanta.** Coordinates ~33°31′51″N 84°21′44″W. **[WIKI] [ABT]** *(Exact mileage south of
  Atlanta varies by source ~15–22 mi; author should write "roughly 20 miles south" and
  [VERIFY] if a precise figure is wanted.)*
- **Theatre:** **Western Theater.** The **climactic battle of the Atlanta Campaign**
  (May–September 1864). **[WIKI]**
- **Result:** **Union victory.** Hood evacuated Atlanta the night of Sept 1; the city fell
  Sept 2. **[WIKI] [ABT] [AHC]**
  - **Historiographical note (the one nuance):** historian **Albert Castel** argued the
    **Sept 1 fight was a Confederate *tactical* success** — Hardee's three divisions held off
    a much larger Union force long enough to let Hood's army escape Atlanta intact. But the
    *strategic* result is uncontested: **Atlanta fell.** Author may note Hardee's stubborn
    defense bought the Confederate escape, but Jonesborough is unambiguously the Union win
    that took the city. **[WIKI]** *(Treat Castel's "Confederate victory" as a minority
    tactical reading, not the verdict; [VERIFY] before quoting Castel directly.)*

### Why Jonesborough mattered — the last railroad (strategic spine)

- By late August 1864, after four months of maneuver, **Atlanta was nearly encircled** but
  still supplied by rail. Sherman **(North)** had cut every line *except one*: the
  **Macon & Western Railroad**, running **south out of Atlanta through Jonesborough** — the
  city's **LAST open supply line.** **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- Rather than batter the Atlanta fortifications head-on, **Sherman swung the bulk of his
  armies south** in a wide wheeling march to **cut the Macon & Western near Jonesborough.**
  Sever that line and **Atlanta could not be held.** As ABT/AHC frame it, *"he moved the
  bulk of his troops against the railroads leading into the city, destroying them beyond
  immediate repair."* **[AHC] [ABT]**
- The Atlanta & West Point Railroad (running southwest) had already been torn up days
  earlier; the **Macon & Western was the survivor**, and Jonesborough was where it could be
  reached and broken. **[WIKI] [ABT]** *(Author: the object was the *railroad*; the prize was
  *Atlanta*.)*
- **Hood's response (South):** when Sherman's host appeared at Jonesborough, **Hood sent
  Hardee (South)** with two corps (Hardee's own + S.D. Lee's) to drive the Federals back off
  the railroad. **[WIKI]**

## 4. Geography (for map accuracy)

Relative positions (**north = up; Atlanta is to the NORTH, off the top of a battle map**):

- **The town of Jonesborough** sits **~20 miles south of Atlanta**, astride the **Macon &
  Western Railroad**, which runs roughly **north–south through the town** (Atlanta to the
  north, Macon to the south). **The railroad through Jonesborough is the literal objective.**
  **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **The Flint River** runs **just west of Jonesborough**, north–south, paralleling the town.
  The **Union army approached from the WEST**, crossing the Flint to come at Jonesborough; the
  river is the key west-side terrain feature. On **Aug 31, Lowrey's diverted Confederate
  division (South) crossed the Flint** chasing Kilpatrick's cavalry and was halted by a Union
  (XVII Corps) division on the river's west bank. **[WIKI]**
- **Hardee's line (South):** the Confederates entrenched on a front **covering the town and
  the railroad**, facing **west** toward the Union approach. On **Sept 1** Hardee held a
  **~2-mile line** with a pronounced **salient (the apex)** held by **Govan's brigade** — the
  weak point the XIV Corps broke. **[WIKI]**
- **The Union assault axis (Sept 1):** **from the west / northwest.** Davis's **XIV Corps
  (North)** attacked the salient — **Estey's brigade (North) struck it from the north**, while
  **Morgan's division (North) struck from the west**, surrounding **Govan's brigade (South)**
  inside the salient. **[WIKI]** *(Map: attack arrows converge on the salient from W and N;
  Govan's brigade is the encircled apex.)*
- **Terrain:** the Sept 1 attack pushed through **dense underbrush / thick woods**, which
  slowed Carlin's and Stanley's advances — a real factor in why the breakthrough was
  uneven. **[WIKI]**
- **The retreat (South):** the night of **Sept 1, Hardee slipped away undetected south to
  Lovejoy's Station** (down the railroad). Atlanta's garrison under **Hood evacuated north of
  the field, out of Atlanta via the McDonough Road**, the night of Sept 1. **[WIKI]**
- **Map orientation summary:** **Atlanta NORTH (top)** · **Jonesborough town center** on the
  **Macon & Western RR (north–south)** · **Flint River just to the WEST** (north–south) ·
  **Hardee's entrenched line facing WEST**, with **Govan's salient (the apex)** the focal
  point · **Union attack arrows from the W/NW** converging on the salient · **Confederate
  retreat arrow exits SOUTH to Lovejoy's Station.**

## 5. Sequence & turning points

**Setup:** Sherman **(North)** wheeled the bulk of his armies **south to cut the Macon &
Western Railroad** near Jonesborough — Atlanta's last supply line. Hood **(South)** sent
**Hardee (South)** with two corps (Hardee's + S.D. Lee's) to drive the Federals off the
railroad. **[WIKI] [ABT]**

### August 31 — Hardee's attack repulsed
- **Howard's Army of the Tennessee (North) reached Jonesborough first** and entrenched on the
  **west bank approaches / near the Flint River.** Confederate forces arrived through the
  morning: **Hardee's corps by dawn, S.D. Lee's corps by afternoon.** **[WIKI]**
- **~3:00 p.m. — the Confederate attack opens** (artillery, then infantry in echelon).
  **Lee's corps (South)** struck the Union line (Hazen's division), **overran the picket line
  but was stopped cold by rifle and artillery fire** from the entrenched Federals. **[WIKI]**
- **The attack faltered fast:** Confederate infantry, per Wikipedia, **refused to renew the
  charge** despite officers urging them on — the men had seen what charging entrenchments
  cost. **[WIKI]**
- **Cleburne's wing diverted:** **Lowrey's division (South)**, hit by a **fusillade from
  Kilpatrick's dismounted cavalry (North) firing Spencer repeating rifles**, **veered west,
  crossed the Flint River**, and was halted by a Union XVII Corps division — wasting the
  attack's left. **[WIKI]**
- **Result of Aug 31:** **Hardee canceled further assaults.** The Confederate attack was
  **"easily repulsed"** at roughly **ten-to-one cost** (~175 Union vs ~1,300–2,200
  Confederate). **[WIKI] [ABT]**
- **The fatal evening order:** that night, **Hood — fearing a Union assault on Atlanta
  itself — recalled S.D. Lee's corps (South) to Atlanta** (~2 a.m.). This left **Hardee alone
  at Jonesborough with ~12,000 men** for Sept 1. Meanwhile **Schofield's and Stanley's corps
  (North) had already reached and cut the Macon & Western Railroad** that afternoon — Atlanta
  was now strategically lost. **[WIKI]**

### September 1 — the line breaks, Govan's brigade overrun
- **Morning:** Lee's corps gone, **Hardee (South) stretched to defend a ~2-mile front with
  ~12,000 men.** **Govan's brigade (South) held the apex of the salient.** **[WIKI]**
- **After ~4:00 p.m. — Davis's XIV Corps (North) attacks the salient.** **Carlin's division
  (North)** advanced with difficulty through **dense underbrush**; **Morgan's division (North)**
  attacked from the west. **[WIKI]**
- **The breakthrough (the decisive moment):** **Estey's brigade (North, ~1,139 men) assaulted
  the salient from the north** — the **10th Kentucky and 74th Indiana overran the 6th/7th
  Arkansas (consolidated)**; the **14th Ohio and 38th Ohio broke through** — while **Morgan's
  division (North) struck from the west**, and the two **surrounded Govan's brigade (South).**
  **[WIKI]**
- **Govan captured:** **Brig. Gen. Daniel Govan (South) and ~600 of his men were taken
  prisoner**, along with **~8 guns (Key's Arkansas + Swett's Mississippi batteries).** This is
  the battle's signal capture — a whole brigade swept up in the salient. **[WIKI]**
- **The line stabilized:** **Patrick Cleburne (South) led Vaughan's Tennessee brigade into the
  gap**, halting the Federal advance; **McCulloch's brigade (South) rallied** and repulsed a
  Union (Moore's) brigade on the eastern sector. The break was contained, but the line was
  shattered. **[WIKI]**
- **Stanley's IV Corps (North)** advanced slowly through thick woods on the east and **dug in
  rather than exploiting the breakthrough** — a missed chance to bag Hardee's whole corps.
  **[WIKI]**

### The evacuation of Atlanta — the climax
- **Night of Sept 1:** **Hardee (South) slipped away undetected south to Lovejoy's Station.**
  **[WIKI]**
- **With the last railroad cut, Hood (South) evacuated Atlanta that same night** (out via the
  **McDonough Road**). Before leaving, the Confederates **torched their own stores and
  ordnance** — most famously, a trapped **ammunition train: ~5 locomotives and ~81 boxcars,
  ~28 of them loaded with ammunition**, were set ablaze, producing a **spectacular,
  ground-shaking explosion heard for miles** (the famous exploding-ammunition-trains image,
  later dramatized in *Gone with the Wind*). **[WIKI]** *([VERIFY] the exact "5 locomotives /
  81 cars / 28 ammunition" figures before printing as precise — [WIKI] gives them; corroborate
  if possible.)*
- **September 2:** **Slocum's XX Corps (North) occupied Atlanta**; Slocum wired the first word
  that the city had fallen. **[WIKI] [WIKI-ATL]**
- **September 3:** **Sherman (North) telegraphed Washington: "Atlanta is ours, and fairly
  won."** (see §7). **[WIKI-ATL] [WQ]**

## 6. Human cost — named people / vivid documented detail

- **Brig. Gen. Daniel C. Govan (South)** — his brigade held the salient apex on Sept 1 and was

## 8. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (ship-blocking) + the documented hook

> **NAME THE CAUSE (locked, ship-blocking).** The section MUST, in its own prose, name
> **slavery** as the thing the war — and each side — was ultimately fought over. Omission is
> the default Lost Cause failure; "a railroad town" / "supply lines" framing alone fails the
> gate. Jonesborough makes this concrete in **two** documented ways — use either or both.

### The throughline
- The Confederacy fought to preserve and extend **slavery**; Hood's **(South)** army defended
  Atlanta — the Confederacy's arsenal-and-rail heart — to keep that slave society's war effort
  alive. The Union cut the railroad and took the city as part of breaking that society apart.
  The railroad was the *object*; **slavery was the reason.**

### HOOK A (primary) — the people who freed themselves by following Sherman's army **[GPB] [EBEN]**
The fall of Atlanta opened the road to what is now called **"the largest emancipation event in
U.S. history"** — Sherman's March to the Sea, which Jonesborough set up. **[GPB]**
- From the moment Sherman's armies moved through Georgia, **enslaved people freed themselves by
  the thousands, walking off plantations to join the Union columns** — arriving day and night,
  as families and as lone escapees, **guiding troops down hidden paths, pointing out hidden
  stores, and feeding Union scouts military intelligence.** A caravan of self-emancipated
  people moved with the army. **[GPB]** This was self-emancipation in motion: the war's purpose
  made visible on the roads south of Atlanta.
- **The dark, documented hook that closes the loop — Ebenezer Creek (Dec 9, 1864):** the very
  Union general whose corps **broke Hardee's line and won Jonesborough — Brig. Gen. Jefferson
  C. Davis (North)** — **ordered the pontoon bridge over Ebenezer Creek pulled up before the
  hundreds of Black refugees following his column could cross**, to be rid of them. Trapped
  between the deep creek and pursuing Confederate cavalry, **men, women, and children plunged
  into the water and many drowned.** **[EBEN]** The man who won the city that decided
  emancipation's political fate also committed one of the march's cruelest acts against the very
  people the war was freeing — a single human throughline tying the battle to its cause, with
  no flinching. *(Use Ebenezer Creek as the unforgettable, sourced anchor; [VERIFY] the drowning
  count is best left unfixed — sources don't agree on a number, "many"/"hundreds" is the safe
  framing.)*

### HOOK B (co-primary) — the election Atlanta's fall decided was an election about slavery **[ELECT]**
- Atlanta fell **two months before the 1864 presidential election**, at the moment Lincoln
  **(North)** believed he would *lose.* As late as **August 23, 1864, Lincoln wrote it
  "exceedingly probable" he would not be reelected.** **[ELECT]**
- The Democratic platform called the **war "a failure"** and demanded a negotiated peace —
  which would have meant **leaving slavery (and the Confederacy) intact.** Their nominee,
  **George B. McClellan (North)**, repudiated the peace plank, splitting his own party. **[ELECT]**
- **The fall of Atlanta detonated that platform.** Northern morale surged; **Lincoln made
  emancipation a central issue** of the campaign and won decisively — **212 electoral votes to
  21** (~55% of the popular vote). **[ELECT]** Lincoln's reelection guaranteed the war would be
  fought to **end slavery**, not negotiated to preserve it — and the **13th Amendment** followed.
  **Jonesborough is the battle that, by taking Atlanta, secured that outcome.** *(This is the
  full-weight significance — the battle whose result decided whether emancipation would stand.)*

**Author directive:** name slavery plainly as the war's cause where the section sets its
stakes. The strongest construction pairs **Hook A** (the self-emancipating thousands on the
roads, with Ebenezer Creek as the unforgettable, unsanitized anchor — and Jefferson C. Davis as
the chilling link back to the man who won the battle) with **Hook B** (the election about
slavery that Atlanta's fall decided). Either alone satisfies the gate; both make the section.

## 9. Significance (FULL weight — this is the battle that took Atlanta)

- **The fall of Atlanta.** Jonesborough cut the last railroad and **forced Hood (South) to
  abandon Atlanta the night of Sept 1**; the city fell **Sept 2.** It was the **decisive,
  climactic battle of the Atlanta Campaign.** **[WIKI] [ABT] [AHC]**
- **Transformed Northern morale.** After Grant's bloody Overland stalemate and the Petersburg
  siege, the North was war-weary and Lincoln expected to lose. **Atlanta's fall reversed the
  national mood overnight** — the most-covered Northern victory of the year. **[ELECT] [WIKI-ATL]**
- **Secured Lincoln's reelection.** Widely credited as **the** event that turned the 1864
  election: the Democratic "war is a failure" platform collapsed, and **Lincoln won 212–21.**
  Because Lincoln's victory guaranteed the war would be fought through to **end slavery**, the
  military fact of Jonesborough carried an emancipation outcome. **[ELECT]**
- **Set up the March to the Sea.** With Atlanta taken, Sherman **(North)** rested, burned the
  city's war infrastructure, and in November launched the **March to the Sea** to Savannah —
  cutting the Confederacy's heart out and triggering the **largest emancipation event in U.S.
  history.** **[GPB] [WIKI-ATL]**
- **Hood's army escaped — the war went on.** The one caveat: Hardee's stubborn Sept 1 defense
  let **Hood's Army of Tennessee escape intact** (south to Lovejoy's). The army would fight on
  to **Franklin and Nashville** that winter; Jonesborough took the *city*, not the *army.*
  **[WIKI] [ABT]**

## 10. Blind spots / open items for the fact-checker

- **Sherman's telegram — date, recipient, and the "So" prefix CONFLICT across sources.**
  [WIKI-ATL] = Sept 3 to Washington (Halleck/War Dept); [WQ] = Sept 2 to Lincoln; some
  printings open "So Atlanta is ours…". **Pin the date, recipient, and exact opening against
  the Official Records / Sherman's Memoirs before printing as a verbatim, attributed quote.**
  The four-word core is safe; the wrapper is not.
- **Engaged-strength numbers are genuinely contested** (Aug 31 vs Sept 1 differ sharply once
  Lee's corps is recalled; ABT's 94k/70k are *campaign* totals, not field strength). Author
  must use **ranges** and not present campaign totals as the men on the Jonesborough field.
- **Casualty figures vary** (~1,149 Union [ABT/AHC] vs ~1,150 summed [WIKI]; Confederate
  ~2,000 [ABT/AHC] vs higher daily figures in [WIKI]; the Aug 31 Confederate count itself runs
  1,300–2,200). Use **ranges**; the ~10:1 Aug 31 disparity is the load-bearing stat.
- **Govan's captured count (~600) and the captured-gun count (~8, Key's/Swett's batteries)** —
  [WIKI]-sourced; [VERIFY] before printing as exact.
- **The exploding ammunition train figures (5 locomotives / 81 cars / 28 ammunition)** come from
  [WIKI]; corroborate before printing the precise counts (the *event* is solid; the exact
  numbers need a second source).
- **Logan's specific role at Jonesborough** is not foregrounded in the [WIKI] infobox; verify
  before giving him a featured part (he commanded a corps under Howard but the marquee Sept 1
  credit is Davis/XIV Corps).
- **Brigade/officer spellings** (Estey, Lowrey, Vaughan, McCulloch) and the regiment list
  (10th KY, 74th IN, 14th/38th OH, 6th/7th AR) are from [WIKI]; verify spellings before print.
- **Ebenezer Creek drowning count** — sources disagree; do NOT fix a number. "Many" / "hundreds
  of refugees, many drowned" is the safe framing. The *Davis-won-Jonesborough / Davis-at-
  Ebenezer-Creek* link is the [VERIFY]-confirmed throughline ([WIKI] Jonesborough + [EBEN]).
- **Castel's "Confederate victory" reading** is a real minority tactical interpretation; verify
  exact wording before quoting Castel, and frame it as tactical-only against an uncontested
  strategic Union win.
