## 1. Dates / Location / Theatre

- **Dates:** **September 18–20, 1863.** Sept 18 = approach + cavalry/bridge fighting (minor); the main two-day battle is **Sept 19–20**. [WP, ABT]
- **Location:** **Catoosa County and Walker County, Georgia** [WP, ABT], along **West Chickamauga Creek**, roughly **~12 miles south of Chattanooga, Tennessee**. (Note: ABT/WP phrase it loosely as "~20 miles south"; the battlefield is ~12 mi by the most-cited figure — `[VERIFY]` the exact mileage and pin the prose to one number.) [WP, ABT]
- **Theatre:** **Western Theater** of the American Civil War. [WP, ABT]
- **First major battle of the war fought in Georgia.** [WP, NGE]
- Etymology of "Chickamauga" is disputed; folklore translates it "river of death" (Cherokee), but that is **not** secure — most plausible derivation is a Chickasaw word *chokma* ("be good"). Treat "river of death" as **legend, not fact** (`[VERIFY]` if used — present as the folk name, not the literal meaning). [WP]

## 2. Forces & Commanders (each tagged SIDE)

### Union — Army of the Cumberland (North)
- **Maj. Gen. William S. Rosecrans (North)** — overall commander. [WP, ABT]
- **Maj. Gen. George H. Thomas (North)** — XIV Corps; the day-2 rearguard hero, "the Rock of Chickamauga." [WP, ABT]
- **Maj. Gen. Thomas L. Crittenden (North)** — XXI Corps. [WP]
- **Maj. Gen. Alexander McD. McCook (North)** — XX Corps. [WP]
- **Maj. Gen. Gordon Granger (North)** — Reserve Corps; marched to the sound of the guns to reinforce Thomas on Snodgrass Hill (Sept 20). [WP]
- **Brig. Gen. James A. Garfield (North)** — Rosecrans's chief of staff; future U.S. president; source of the "standing like a rock" report (see Quotes). [WP-Thomas, CWM]
- **Brig. Gen. Thomas J. Wood (North)** — division commander whose withdrawal opened the fatal gap (he was obeying Rosecrans's order). [WP]
- **Col. John T. Wilder (North)** — "Lightning Brigade" of mounted infantry armed with **Spencer repeating rifles**; defended Alexander's Bridge. [WP]

### Confederate — Army of Tennessee (South)
- **Gen. Braxton Bragg (South)** — overall commander. [WP, ABT]
- **Lt. Gen. James Longstreet (South)** — commanded Bragg's **Left Wing**; arrived by rail from **Lee's Army of Northern Virginia in Virginia** with two divisions of his First Corps; his column made the Sept-20 breakthrough. [WP, ABT]
- **Lt. Gen. Leonidas Polk (South)** — commanded the **Right Wing**; his delayed dawn attack on Sept 20 is a flashpoint. [WP, ABT]
- **Maj. Gen. John Bell Hood (South)** — division/wing leader in Longstreet's assault; severely wounded here (right leg amputated — see Human Cost). [WP, HISTORY]

### Sizes (a rare Confederate numerical edge)
- **Union: ~60,000 engaged.** [WP, NGE]
- **Confederate: ~65,000 engaged.** [WP, ABT]
- This is **one of the few battles where the Confederates outnumbered the Union** — the edge came from reinforcing Bragg with Longstreet's corps from Virginia plus troops from Mississippi. [WP, NGE]
- **Range / caution:** ABT's "10 Facts"-style page lists Union "Forces Engaged: 125,000" — that is a **combined-armies / total-present figure, not Union-only**, and would be wrong as a Union strength. **Use ~60,000 (US) vs ~65,000 (CS) for the at-a-glance stats; do NOT report 125,000 as the Union number.** `[VERIFY]` that the author/cards use the per-side ~60k/~65k figures. [WP, ABT]

## 3. Casualties (second-bloodiest battle of the war, after Gettysburg)

The most-cited (Livermore-derived) totals, used by WP and ABT:

| Side | Killed | Wounded | Captured/Missing | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Union (North)** | 1,657 | 9,756 | 4,757 | **16,170** |
| **Confederate (South)** | 2,312 | 14,674 | 1,468 | **18,454** |
| **Combined** | | | | **~34,624** |

[WP, ABT]

- **Second-costliest / second-bloodiest battle of the entire war, behind only Gettysburg.** [WP, ABT]
- The Confederates **won the field but bled more** than the side they beat (~18,454 vs ~16,170). [WP, ABT]
- **Scholarly range (contested, as always for ACW):** historian **Peter Cozzens** estimated **first-day (Sept 19) losses alone** at "between 6,000 and 9,000 Confederates and perhaps 7,000 Federals." Use ranges, label estimates. [ABT, citing Cozzens]
- **~34,000 total casualties** is the safe round figure for prose. [NGE: "34,000 total casualties—16,000 Union and 18,000 Confederate"]

## 4. Geography (relative positions for map accuracy)

North is up; the Union line of retreat is **northwest toward Chattanooga**.

- **West Chickamauga Creek** — runs roughly **north–south**, **east** of the LaFayette Road; the armies fought in the woods **west of the creek**. The Confederates crossed it (west) to attack; their bridges (Reed's, Alexander's) are on the creek. [WP, NGE]
- **LaFayette Road** — the **main north–south road and the axis of the battle**; the Union line formed along/west of it. Running it north leads toward Rossville/Chattanooga. [WP, NGE]
- **Lee & Gordon's Mills** — on the creek at the **south** end of the field; an early anchor of the Union line and a key landmark. [WP, NGE]
- **Reed's Bridge** (north) and **Alexander's Bridge** (south of Reed's) — crossings over Chickamauga Creek on the Confederate (east) side, where the Sept-18 cavalry/mounted-infantry delaying fights happened (Minty at Reed's; Wilder at Alexander's). [WP]
- **Brotherton field / Brotherton cabin** — **center of the line**, on the LaFayette Road. **This is where the fatal gap opened** on Sept 20 (Wood pulled his division out here) and **precisely where Longstreet's assault column struck** — "by coincidence to precisely the point where Wood's Union division was pulling out of the line." [WP]
- **Kelly Field** — open ground in the **center-north**; Thomas's troops fortified here overnight with **log breastworks**. [WP, NGE]
- **Snodgrass Hill / Horseshoe Ridge** — **a series of steep, wooded knolls** at the **northwest/rear** of the field. **Thomas's last-stand position on Sept 20** — he rallied broken units here and held against repeated assaults to cover the army's escape. [WP, ABT, NGE]
- **Winfrey field** — where Cleburne's twilight attack closed Sept 19. [WP]
- **Chattanooga & Missionary Ridge** — to the **north/northwest**, the direction the army retreated. **Missionary Ridge** and **Lookout Mountain** ring Chattanooga and become the siege lines (and the November breakout battlefields). [NGE, WP-Chatt]
- **Terrain note (load-bearing for the story):** the field was **densely wooded** — thick second-growth forest that **made it nearly impossible to see, control, or coordinate units.** Both armies fed troops blindly into the trees; commanders lost track of their own lines. This is why Sept 19 was a confused "meeting engagement" and why the Sept-20 gap went unseen until Longstreet was through it. [WP, NGE]

## 5. Sequence (turning points)

1. **Campaign setup — Rosecrans maneuvers Bragg out of Chattanooga.** After the brilliant, nearly bloodless **Tullahoma campaign (June 24 – July 3, 1863)** pushed Bragg out of Middle Tennessee, Rosecrans crossed the Tennessee River (late Aug) and advanced his ~60,000-man Army of the Cumberland in **three separated columns** through NE Alabama / NW Georgia, threatening Bragg's supply lines and **forcing him to abandon Chattanooga by September 9, 1863** without a fight. [WP-Tullahoma, NGE]
2. **Bragg turns; the columns are exposed.** Reinforced (Longstreet from Virginia, troops from Mississippi), Bragg did not keep retreating — he turned to strike Rosecrans's strung-out, separated columns south of Chattanooga near Chickamauga Creek. [WP, NGE]
3. **Sept 18 — the bridges.** Confederates pushed for the Chickamauga Creek crossings; Union cavalry/mounted infantry (Minty at Reed's Bridge, **Wilder's Spencer-armed brigade at Alexander's Bridge**) delayed them. [WP]
4. **Sept 19 — the meeting battle in the woods.** A confused engagement sprawled across nearly four miles of thick forest; both commanders fed brigades in piecemeal; disjointed Confederate attacks; the day ended with **Cleburne's twilight attack near Winfrey field.** No decision — but Rosecrans kept shifting strength **north** to protect his Chattanooga lifeline. [WP, NGE]
5. **Sept 20 morning — Polk's late attack.** Bragg's plan was a dawn assault rolling north-to-south; **Polk's Right Wing attacked hours late (~9:30 a.m.)**, hitting Thomas's fortified left (Kelly Field log breastworks) with limited success. [WP]
6. **THE TURNING POINT — the fatal order & the gap.** Mid-morning, Rosecrans, told (mistakenly) of a hole in his line, ordered **Brig. Gen. Thomas J. Wood** to **"close up on Reynolds as fast as possible, and support him"** — i.e., shift Wood's division north. Obeying literally, **Wood pulled his division OUT of the line at the Brotherton field, opening a REAL quarter-mile gap.** [WP]
7. **Longstreet hits the gap.** At the same moment (~11:10 a.m.) **Longstreet launched a massed assault column (~8 brigades / thousands of men, several lines deep)** straight at the Brotherton field — **by sheer coincidence exactly where Wood had just vacated.** The Confederates poured through unopposed. [WP, NGE]
8. **The Union right collapses and routs.** Longstreet's breakthrough shattered the Union right and center; **roughly half the army — along with Rosecrans, McCook, Crittenden, and Garfield — was swept off the field and fled north toward Chattanooga.** Rosecrans himself rode back to the city, believing the battle (and his army) lost. [WP, NGE]
9. **Thomas saves the army — the stand on Snodgrass Hill / Horseshoe Ridge.** **George Thomas** took command of everything left on the field, rallied broken and orphaned units on **Snodgrass Hill / Horseshoe Ridge**, and held against repeated Confederate assaults (from ~1 p.m. to twilight), **reinforced by Gordon Granger's Reserve Corps marching to the guns.** His stand bought time for an **organized withdrawal** rather than annihilation. [WP, ABT, NGE]
10. **Retreat into Chattanooga → the siege.** Under cover of Thomas's stand, the Army of the Cumberland fell back **into Chattanooga.** Bragg followed and occupied the surrounding heights — **Missionary Ridge and Lookout Mountain** — **besieging the city** and cutting its supply line, but he did not destroy the trapped army. [NGE, WP-Chatt]

## 6. The Cause — Slavery (SHIP-BLOCKING — must appear in the section's own prose)

- The war Rosecrans's and Bragg's armies fought was, at its root, **a war over slavery.** The Confederate states had seceded to protect and extend the institution; the campaign for Chattanooga was a campaign to crack open the **Deep South** — the heart of the slave economy — and Chattanooga was the **rail gateway** into it (to Atlanta and beyond). [WP-Chatt, NGE; standing NAME-THE-CAUSE rule in `war-content-pipeline.md`]
- **Local throughline:** Georgia's plantation agriculture **depended on enslaved labor**; slaveholding had grown explosively after the cotton gin, and the South fought to keep that system while the North's war aim had, by 1863 (post-Emancipation Proclamation), become its destruction. [NGE]
- **A pointed, documented irony to consider (verify before using):** the Union's own hero of the battle, **George H. Thomas, was a Virginian and a former slaveholder** who chose the Union over his native South — a man who fought against the order he had been born into. [WP-Thomas] `[VERIFY]` exact framing of Thomas's enslaver past before putting it in prose.
- **The section MUST state, in its own words, that slavery was the cause** — omission (not euphemism) is the default Lost Cause failure mode the framing gate fails sections for. A reader landing on this battle alone must be told what the killing was ultimately about.

## 7. Human Cost (named people, documented detail, the toll)

- **~34,000 men fell in two days** — second only to Gettysburg. The Confederates **won and yet lost more men** than the army they drove off. [WP, ABT, NGE]
- **Maj. Gen. John Bell Hood (South)** — a Minié ball **shattered his right leg**; it was **amputated** on the field. (Hood had already lost the use of his **left arm at Gettysburg** ten weeks earlier — Chickamauga cost him the leg.) [HISTORY, WP]
- **Brig. Gen. Ben Hardin Helm (South)** — Confederate general **killed** at Chickamauga; he was **married to Mary Todd Lincoln's half-sister (Emilie)** — i.e., **President Lincoln's brother-in-law.** Lincoln reportedly mourned him personally. [HISTORY] `[VERIFY]` the exact relation wording ("half-sister's husband").
- **Brig. Gen. William H. Lytle (North)** — Union general and published poet, **killed** leading his brigade. [HISTORY]
- Confederate generals **James Deshler** and **Preston Smith** also **killed.** **Roughly ten Confederate generals were killed or wounded** at Chickamauga. [HISTORY] `[VERIFY]` the exact general-officer-casualty count.
- The **dense woods** turned the fight savage and close-range: units fired blind into the trees, friendly fire and confusion were rampant, and the wounded lay in burning underbrush. (Use the terrain to make the cost felt; keep specifics to what sources support.) [WP, NGE]

## 8. Key Quotes (exact attribution + source; shaky ones flagged)

- **"standing like a rock"** — **Brig. Gen. James A. Garfield (North)**, Rosecrans's chief of staff, reporting to Rosecrans that **Thomas** was holding. This is the origin of the **"Rock of Chickamauga"** nickname. The most-cited rendering is **"Thomas is standing like a rock."** [WP-Thomas, CWM]
  - **PROVENANCE CAUTION (`[VERIFY]` — important):** Civil War Monitor's research found **no newspaper use of "Rock of Chickamauga" before 1869**, and Garfield's famous 1870 memorial oration ("He was, indeed, the 'Rock of Chickamauga'") *presumes* the phrase was already familiar. So: the **nickname is real and securely attached to Thomas's Snodgrass Hill stand**, but the clean "Garfield coined it on the spot and it stuck immediately" story is **folklore-tinged.** Safe to write: *Thomas's stand earned him the name "the Rock of Chickamauga."* Be cautious asserting Garfield invented it in the moment. [CWM]
- **"Close up on Reynolds as fast as possible, and support him"** — the substance of **Rosecrans's order to Wood** (delivered via an aide, Maj. Bond) that triggered the gap. Wording is paraphrased across sources; treat as **the gist, not a sacred verbatim quote** unless the fact-checker pins the exact wording. [WP] `[VERIFY]` exact wording.
- **"The élan of the Southern soldier was never seen after Chickamauga"** — attributed to Confederate **Maj. Gen. D. H. Hill (South)**, reflecting on the costliness of the "victory." [WP] `[VERIFY]` exact phrasing/punctuation.

## 9. Significance

- **A Confederate tactical victory they failed to exploit.** Bragg won the field and inflicted the war's second-worst casualties — **but did not pursue aggressively, did not destroy the broken Army of the Cumberland, and let it reach the safety of Chattanooga.** Longstreet and others urged a vigorous pursuit; Bragg besieged instead. [WP, ABT, NGE]
- **The only major Confederate victory in the Western Theater** — and **the most significant Union defeat in the West.** [WP, NGE]
- **It led directly to the Siege of Chattanooga:** Bragg's army held Missionary Ridge and Lookout Mountain and nearly starved the Union army out (the supply crisis broken only by the **"Cracker Line"** opened via Brown's Ferry, Oct 27). [NGE, WP-Chatt]
- **It brought Grant.** The crisis prompted Lincoln to consolidate the western armies under **Maj. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant**; **Rosecrans was relieved** and replaced (Thomas took command of the Army of the Cumberland). [WP-Chatt, NGE]
- **The Union breakout (Nov 23–25, 1863).** Grant broke the siege at the **Battles for Chattanooga** — **Lookout Mountain (Nov 24)** and the dramatic spontaneous charge up **Missionary Ridge (Nov 25)** — routing Bragg's army back into Georgia. So Chickamauga's tactical defeat became a **strategic Union triumph within two months**, opening the gateway to Atlanta and **Sherman's 1864 campaign / March to the Sea.** [WP-Chatt, ABT]
- **Net verdict for the Outcome card:** *Confederate tactical win, strategic failure.* The South won the bloodiest day of the western war and threw it away; Thomas's stand saved the Union army, the siege that followed brought Grant, and Grant turned a defeat into the breakthrough that cracked the Deep South open.
