# Mansfield — LOST CAUSE / FRAMING GATE

**Verdict: FAIR (PASS). No MUST-FIX. Slavery is named as the cause in the section's own prose, repeatedly and concretely. This is one of the strongest cause-framings in the war corpus.**

---

## FIRST CHECK (ship-blocking): is slavery named as the cause in the section's OWN prose?

**YES — explicitly, in the section's own voice, not deferred to a quote or a sidebar.** Locations:
- §the-river-and-the-cotton: *"The Confederacy was a republic founded to keep four million human beings in bondage and to defend the slave economy that produced exactly that cotton; the wealth Banks was reaching for **was** the wealth of slavery."* And: *"every boll of it — by **enslaved people**, on those river plantations, under the lash."*
- §what-it-cost: Mouton died *"defending a Louisiana slave society his planter family belonged to."*
- Closing MEANWHILE: *"the congealed labor of enslaved people … the slave society the Confederacy existed to defend … The cotton is not a footnote … It is what the battle was for."*

The NAME-THE-CAUSE gate is satisfied concretely and four times over. PASS on the hard gate.

---

## Is the cotton-as-slavery hook honest and non-tokenizing?

**Honest.** This is not a tacked-on "and slavery was bad" sentence to clear the gate. The cause is **load-bearing structure**: the section's spine is that the object two armies maneuvered over was the slave economy's signature crop, and that point opens the section, recurs at the central death, and closes it. The slavery framing is the *thesis*, not a token.

**Non-tokenizing checks:**
- It names the enslaved as **people who grew the cotton**, not an abstraction ("congealed labor of enslaved people," "under the lash," "four million human beings in bondage"). Good.
- It supplies the **counter-image** — the Corps d'Afrique freedmen-in-blue on the same campaign — so the enslaved/freedpeople are agents, not just victims or backdrop. This is the opposite of tokenism.
- It does NOT reach for the Poison Spring atrocity to manufacture racial stakes (which would have been off-battle and chronologically wrong) — it carries the racial dimension honestly and in-place via the cotton + the Corps d'Afrique. Disciplined.

---

## Lost Cause / soft-pedaling hunt

- **No "states' rights" euphemism.** Absent. Slavery is named as the cause directly.
- **No noble-defender romanticization of the Confederate win.** Taylor's skill is credited (the cigar, the road, the local concentration) but the victory is explicitly tied to *defending* a slave society. Mouton's death is given pathos — BUT the draft immediately frames it as dying *"defending a Louisiana slave society his planter family belonged to … The cause that opened this story closed over his body."* That is the correct move: humanize without absolving. Not romanticized.
  - **Strengthening fact available (SHOULD-FIX, optional):** web-verified that Mouton personally **owned enslaved people**, was a sugarcane planter, and led a Lafayette "vigilante committee" that drove free Black people out of the parish. The draft's "slave society his planter family belonged to" is true but slightly *softer* than the record — Mouton wasn't merely from a planter family, he was himself an enslaver. Tightening "his planter family belonged to" → "he himself belonged to / profited from" would make the framing more honest, not less. Not blocking; the current line is accurate.
  - Same available strengthener for **Taylor**: he was one of Louisiana's wealthiest sugar planters and an enslaver. The draft doesn't tag him as such. Optional add; mirrors Mouton and removes any residual "gallant general" softness.
- **No false balance / "both sides" wash.** The draft does note the Union's cotton greed was real and ugly (speculators, Porter profiteering, the scandal). This is NOT false balance — it's honest about Union venality WITHOUT equating it with the Confederate cause. Crucially, it keeps the moral axis straight: *"We don't need to settle that debate to make the simpler point: the object two armies maneuvered over … was the slave-grown crop itself."* The Union's profit motive is depicted as grubby; the Confederacy's purpose is depicted as defending slavery. The asymmetry is preserved. PASS.
- **No euphemism for slavery** ("servants," "the peculiar institution" dodge, "workers"). Uses "enslaved people," "bondage," "the lash," "slave economy." Clean.
- **No presentism problem.** The moral judgments are anchored in documented period fact (the Confederacy's own founding purpose, the cotton economy's labor basis), not anachronistic editorializing. The "republic founded to keep four million human beings in bondage" is a statement of the secessionists' own stated purpose, not a presentist imposition.
- **The cotton-vs-military-aims debate is named, not resolved.** The draft cites Ludwell Johnson by title and explicitly declines to adjudicate ("We don't need to settle that debate") — exactly the fact-pack directive. No overclaim.

---

## One thing to watch (SHOULD-FIX, not blocking)
The Union side is portrayed as cotton-greedy (true and well-sourced). Ensure a reader can't walk away thinking "both armies were just after money, so the cause was a wash." The draft already guards against this with the "simpler point" pivot and the slave-society framing of the Confederate purpose — but the closing MEANWHILE could land the asymmetry one notch harder (the Union chased the *wealth* of slavery; the Confederacy fought to keep slavery *itself*). The Mouton/Taylor enslaver facts above would do this cleanly. Optional polish; the gate already passes.

---

## Disposition
- **MUST-FIX: none.**
- **SHOULD-FIX (optional, all strengtheners):**
  1. Mouton was himself an enslaver/planter (and ran free Black people out of his parish) — tighten "his planter family belonged to" to name his own complicity.
  2. Tag Taylor (South) as a slaveholding sugar planter to mirror Mouton and remove residual gallant-general softness.
  3. Land the Union-greed-vs-Confederate-purpose asymmetry one notch harder in the close so no "both sides were just greedy" misread is possible.

**FRAMING GATE: PASS (FAIR). Slavery named as the cause; hook honest and non-tokenizing; no Lost Cause, no false balance, no euphemism.**
