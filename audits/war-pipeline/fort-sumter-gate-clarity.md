### Leaps & comprehension issues

1. **"The state's reading... Washington had promised not to change the military situation"** — No prior context establishes this promise. A newcomer wonders: when was this promise made, and who made it? Not a MUST-FIX (it's a secondary detail, and the move's political impact is still clear), but a brief clause like "South Carolina's governor believed Washington had given informal assurances not to alter the harbor's military balance" would help. SHOULD-FIX.

2. **"the formal name for that walkout was 'secession'"** — The inline gloss is excellent, but the sentence also uses "Union" before defining it. **Passage:** *"South Carolina had become the first state to walk out of the Union."* For a zero-knowledge reader, "the Union" means nothing yet — it's the United States, but that should be stated once, here. SHOULD-FIX (context almost closes the gap, but "walk out of the United States" would be clearer for a true newcomer).

3. **"Star of the West" framing** — Very well done. No flags.

4. **"Confederate States of America" / "Confederacy"** — Introduced clearly in "The ring closes." No flag.

## Overall verdict

The draft reads remarkably well for a newcomer. The writer defined "secession," "mortar," "casemate," and "lanyard" inline — all correctly. The narrative voice is clear and the pacing is strong. The 8 MUST-FIX items are real comprehension blockers, concentrated in three spots: (1) "battery" never glossed on first use despite being the most-repeated military term in the piece, (2) "Upper South" dropped without context at the exact moment it most needs it (the four-state secession cascade), and (3) "parapet" and "gun ports" left bare in the bombardment section. Fix those eight and the section is newcomer-ready.
