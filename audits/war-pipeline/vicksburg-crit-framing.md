## FINDINGS

### FAIR — Black agency / self-emancipation (the brief's central concern)
**Strongly FAIR — best-in-corpus.** The draft explicitly REFUSES the "freed by Grant"
framing and corrects it in its own prose: *"It did not come apart because Grant freed
people, exactly. It came apart because enslaved people freed themselves"* (line 77). It
foregrounds self-emancipation as the active verb ("young men slipping away," "families
walking off the plantations toward the soldiers… rather than waiting to be liberated"),
and the people "doing the splitting were, in large part, the enslaved themselves." This
is agency shown, not asserted. Milliken's Bend treats the formerly enslaved soldiers as
the subjects of their own valor ("They had stood, and fought hand-to-hand, and held").
No notes.

### FAIR — contraband camps NOT sanitized
The camps are given as "both a lifeline and a hard place" — disease, prejudice, violence,
and coerced wage labor on leased plantations are named (line 79). This is the right
move: it avoids a triumphalist "the army welcomed them to freedom" gloss without tipping
into gratuitous suffering. FAIR.

### FAIR — human reality (starving garrison, cave-dwellers) neither sanitized nor gratuitous
The mule/horse/rat/shoe-leather diet, scurvy, the 500 caves, Loughborough's diary — all
rendered with weight but not wallowing. The civilian suffering is attached to a clear
cause (the siege Grant chose over another bloody storm), not floated as free-standing
pathos. FAIR.

### FAIR — assault casualties / McClernand
The two failed storms (May 19/22) report Union losses honestly against far smaller
Confederate ones, and McClernand's self-promoting false breakthrough is named as feeding
men "into the killing ground" (line 59). No glory-washing of Union command. FAIR.

### TILTED (minor) — Pemberton sympathy in §the-surrender
The Pemberton material (lines 95–97) is mostly fine, but two touches lean faintly
sympathetic to a Confederate commander and should be watched:
- *"Pemberton was out of road"* and the close attention to his July-4 calculation give
  him a near-protagonist beat in the surrender section.
- **Bowen** (South) is twice rendered as a tragic figure — "his best division commander,"
  carried the flag of truce, "already dying of dysentery… dead within days… one more
  casualty of the disease" (lines 95, 97). One line is reportorial; the cumulative effect
  edges toward a sympathetic Confederate-officer vignette.

This is TILTED, not DISTORTED — it does not romanticize the Confederate CAUSE, and the
section's own OUTCOME re-anchors to "ended slavery on the Fourth of July." But per the
gate's "unfair sympathy" axis, the Bowen death-beat is the one place a Confederate
officer gets uncomplicated pathos. **SHOULD-FIX:** trim the Bowen pathos to one factual
mention, or add a half-clause keeping the human note tethered to the cause (these were
the men defending the slaveholding fortress). Do NOT cut Pemberton's Northern-birth /
July-4 detail — that is historically substantive, not romantic.

### FAIR (with one watch) — "Confederacy existed to defend" framing
The draft repeatedly names the Confederacy as the thing that "existed to defend" slavery
(lines 87, 105) — correct and load-bearing. No euphemism ("states' rights," "Southern way
of life," "the peculiar institution" as a dodge) anywhere. The one euphemism that DOES
appear — "contraband" — is explicitly flagged in-prose as "the awkward legal language of
the time" (line 79), which is the correct way to use it. FAIR.

### FAIR — no false both-sides balance
The "Meanwhile" sidebars give the Confederate interior view (Pemberton's winter
confidence, Johnston's helplessness) as STRATEGIC context, not moral equivalence. None of
them frame the two sides as morally symmetric; the §emancipation Meanwhile (line 89)
explicitly frames the war as "the war to end slavery." FAIR. No presentism: emancipation
is grounded in the actors' own period framing (NPS, Conkling letter, Stanton), not
imposed retroactively.

### NOTE (not a framing defect) — Grant-drinking rumor
The §the-failed-approaches whiskey aside (line 31) is correctly framed as "rumor and
political knife-work… not fact." This is a fact/sourcing call already in the ledger, not
a Lost Cause issue. No framing action needed.
