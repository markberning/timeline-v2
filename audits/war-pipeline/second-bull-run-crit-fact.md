## ❌ MUST-FIX (wrong fact + correction + source)

**1. Hennessy's *Return to Bull Run* is dated 1992 — it was published in 1993.**
- Draft (both the road-to-antietam prose, line ~114, and the closing Meanwhile): "it took a major **1992** study, John Hennessy's *Return to Bull Run*…" and "a major **1992** study."
- **Correct:** *Return to Bull Run: The Campaign and Battle of Second Manassas*, John J. Hennessy, Simon & Schuster, **1993** (607 pp.).
- Source: American Historical Review review (academic.oup.com/ahr/article/99/1/302/76755) cites "New York: Simon and Schuster. 1993"; Amazon/Goodreads/Open Library all list 1993.
- **Fix:** change "1992" → "1993" in both places.

(Note: the fact pack §10 also says "1992" — the pack is the source of the error, not just the draft. The draft inherited a wrong year. It is a single, clean, ship-blocking factual error: wrong publication year of a named book.)

## ⚠️ SHOULD-FIX (unsupported as worded / tighten before ship)

**A. Looted-foods specifics — supported in substance, but tighten two items.**
- Draft: men "gorging on **canned lobster** and **tinned beef**," and the Meanwhile lists "oysters, lobster, coffee, new boots."
- **Verified real items at Manassas Junction (Aug 27):** the famous eyewitness line is "To see a starving man eating **lobster salad** & drinking **rhine wine**, barefooted & in tatters was curious"; depot stores included **pickled/canned oysters, corned beef, salt pork, bacon, flour, coffee**; men replaced worn shoes with **new brogans (boots)**; Jackson had the **whiskey** casks poured out. (Sources: civilwarsong.com "Feast at Manassas Junction"; Emerging Civil War "War Picnic at Second Manassas"; NPS "August 28, 1862.")
- So "lobster," "oysters," "coffee," "new boots," and "whiskey" are all solidly attested. **Two wordings are slightly off:** the lobster is documented as **lobster salad**, not "canned lobster"; and "**tinned beef**" is better given as **corned beef** (the documented term). Recommend: "gorging on lobster salad and corned beef." Substance is fine; just match the attested terms. NOT ship-blocking.

**B. "Smoke visible for miles" — reasonable but unsourced color.**
- The burning of the depot/supply trains is fully documented (the trains and warehouses were torched). "A column of smoke that Pope's men could see for miles" is a plausible flourish but I found no source stating a specific visible distance. Keep only as clear authorial color (it reads as such), or soften ("a column of smoke rising over his own rear"). Low priority.

**C. "Church spires of Richmond" — atmospheric, not literally sourced.**
- McClellan's army did advance to within a few miles of Richmond in the Peninsula Campaign (his pickets were close enough to hear the city's church bells, per common accounts). "Within sight of the Confederate capital's church spires" is acceptable popular-history color; not a hard claim. No change required.

**D. Longstreet's force "25,000 to 28,000" — verified as a legitimate range; consider noting the spread.**
- Wikipedia: "Longstreet's command of 25,000 men in five divisions … the largest simultaneous mass assault of the war." Other sources (Warfare History Network; multiple narratives) put it at **28,000**. The draft's "somewhere between 25,000 and 28,000 men, five divisions" is correctly handled as a range. ✅ No fix needed — flagged only to confirm it's deliberately a range, which it is.

## ✅ VERIFIED — flags cleared, NO change needed

1. **Pope quote** — wording is accurate. History.com gives verbatim: *"I come to you from the West, where we have always seen the backs of our enemies… I desire you to dismiss from your minds certain phrases… [like] 'lines of retreat.'"* The draft's quote-callout matches exactly. Safe to print verbatim with the ellipses as written. (Source: history.com sesquicentennial article.) The draft correctly does NOT attach the separate "headquarters in the saddle" jab.
2. **"Foot cavalry"** — verified, real nickname for Jackson's infantry, coined 1862 for their rapid marches (Wikipedia "Foot cavalry"; Wikipedia "Stonewall Jackson"). Keep.
3. **A.P. Hill's men "out of ammunition and throwing rocks"** — verified real at the railroad cut, Aug 30 (and Aug 29). Units in Hill's/Jackson's line (1st Louisiana, Stonewall Brigade, Johnson's & Stafford's brigades) ran out of cartridges and hurled rocks at the 24th NY and other attackers. (Emerging Civil War "Rocks or Bullets?"; ABT "Railroad Cut" series.) The draft's attribution to "A.P. Hill's division … sometimes out of ammunition and throwing rocks" is sound — Hill anchored Jackson's left where this happened. Keep.
4. **Porter court-martial dates** — verified exactly. Proceedings opened **Nov 25, 1862**; found guilty **Jan 10, 1863**; Lincoln approved/Porter cashiered **Jan 21, 1863**. (Wikipedia "Court-martial of Fitz John Porter"; Encyclopedia.com.) The draft prints only "**early 1863**," which is correct and conservative — fine to keep, OR upgrade to the exact dates now that they're confirmed. Either is accurate.
5. **Pope's army folded into McClellan's** — verified. Pope reassigned to the Dept. of the Northwest **Sept 7, 1862**; **General Orders No. 129 (Sept 12, 1862)** dissolved the Army of Virginia and merged its three corps into McClellan's Army of the Potomac. (Wikipedia "Army of Virginia"; "Army of the Potomac.") The draft's "his army was folded back into McClellan's" is correct.
6. **"Four million enslaved people"** (drawer Meanwhile) — verified and acceptable. ~4 million people were enslaved nationally in 1862–63. (History.com; National Archives.) Note: the **Proclamation itself** applied to ~3.5 million in the rebelling states; but the draft uses "four million" for the total enslaved population whose "fate … was tied to" the war, which is the correct national figure. Keep "four million." (The ledger's worry was needless — the pack's "millions" was just more conservative; "four million" is factually right.)
7. **Casualties — given as ranges, no false single number.** Verified: Union ~14,462 (1,747 K / 8,452 W / 4,263 captured-missing) [WP/NPS], Britannica ~13,824–14,500 → draft's "about 14,500." Confederate range ~7,298 [WP] to ~8,353–9,500 [Britannica/Hist] → draft's "7,300 to as high as 9,500," with the consistent core 1,096 K / 6,202 W. Combined ~22,000. All correctly presented as ranges; no single Confederate total asserted as fact. ✅
8. **Strength** — draft avoids hard single numbers (says only "much larger" Union, "half the army" splits); pack range (~62–77k Union / ~50–55k Confederate) is not contradicted. ✅
9. **5th New York / Duryée's Zouaves** — verified verbatim: 500 men, "almost 300 casualties" within "the first 10 minutes," "the largest loss of life of any infantry regiment in a single battle during the entire war." Draft's "about 500 men … nearly 300 … in roughly ten minutes … single greatest loss of life" matches exactly. ✅
10. **Brawner's Farm "one of every three men engaged was shot"** — verified verbatim (Wikipedia). ✅
11. **Jackson ~20,000 men in ~3,000-yard line** — verified verbatim (Wikipedia: "20,000 men in a 3,000-yard line … south of Stony Ridge"). ✅
12. **Longstreet "largest simultaneous mass assault of the war"** — verified as Wikipedia's characterization; draft correctly uses it as analysis, never as a person-quote. ✅
13. **Chinn Ridge** — verified verbatim: McDowell's shift left "only 2,200 Union troops … face ten times their number," possibly "the worst tactical decision of the day." Draft matches. ✅
14. **Dates/place/result** — Aug 28–30, 1862; Prince William County, VA; Eastern Theatre; Confederate victory — all confirmed. ✅
15. **Ranks** — Pope/McClellan/Sigel/McDowell/Porter = Maj. Gen.; Lee = Gen.; Jackson/Longstreet = Maj. Gen. (correct for this date, pre-Oct-1862 Lt. Gen. promotion); Hood = Maj. Gen.; Gibbon/Reynolds = Brig. Gen. — all consistent with WP order of battle. ✅
16. **Antietam Sept 17, 1862 "bloodiest single day"; Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation Sept 22, 1862** — both correct. ✅
17. **Jackson flank march ~50–55 mi in ~2 days, Aug 25; struck O&A RR at Bristoe Station Aug 26; took Manassas Junction before dawn Aug 27; Longstreet through Thoroughfare Gap ~noon Aug 29; retreat east over the Stone Bridge** — all confirmed. ✅
