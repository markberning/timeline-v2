# Perryville Draft — COMPREHENSIVENESS Critic Gate (war pipeline step 4)

**Subject:** `audits/war-pipeline/perryville-draft.md` (5-section narrative).
**Method:** Independently rebuilt the must-cover checklist from web sources (Wikipedia, American Battlefield Trust, Kentucky Historical Society, HistoryNet, Essential Civil War Curriculum, Emerging Civil War, Warfare History Network, Army CGSC staff ride) — NOT from the fact pack — then graded the draft COVERED / THIN / MISSING.
**Verdict:** **PASS with 2 MUST-ADDs.** The draft is strong on the locked essentials (strategic context, acoustic shadow, win-but-retreat paradox, human cost, slavery throughline). Two genuinely essential pieces of strategic context are absent and are also fact-pack blind spots: **(A) Buell's Frankfort feint that fooled Bragg into dispersing his army**, and **(B) the recruitment failure — Kentucky did not rise, and Bragg's wagons of unclaimed rifles.** Both are causal to the outcome the draft is built around. Everything else is SHOULD-CONSIDER or fine as-is.

## MUST-ADD (genuinely essential gaps — these block)

### MUST-ADD #1 — Buell's Frankfort feint, and why Bragg fought outnumbered
Independently corroborated by ABT, Essential Civil War Curriculum, Wikipedia, History of War. Buell sent a diversion (Sill's divisions) toward Frankfort while his main body moved on Perryville/Bardstown. Bragg, reinforced by Kirby Smith's cavalry reports, mistook the Frankfort feint for Buell's *main* thrust, ordered Polk north to concentrate against it, and so had **roughly half his army facing the wrong way** when Buell's real weight arrived at Perryville. That is the structural reason Bragg attacked with ~16,000: not only did the Union under-commit (acoustic shadow), the Confederacy was *also* dispersed by a deception. The draft currently presents the asymmetry as one-sided (Buell's army stayed out) and only vaguely notes Bragg "misread" the enemy. Naming the feint completes the cause and sharpens the irony — **both** commanders were fighting half-blind, in opposite directions. ~2–3 sentences, best placed in §1 (close) or §2.

### MUST-ADD #2 — Kentucky did not rise; the unclaimed rifles
This is the campaign's defining failure in the sources (ABT/HistoryNet "Bragg's Impossible Dream," Confederate Heartland Offensive entries). Bragg invaded expecting 25,000+ Kentuckians to flock to him — promised as many as 50,000 — and hauled ~15,000–20,000 spare rifles in his wagons to arm them. Almost no one came; the rifles stayed on the wagons. Kentucky's early neutrality had already let its hottest secessionists leave south, and with Union armies present and no Confederate victory to point to, sympathizers stayed home. **This is the heart of the "bid for a slave state" story the draft is built on:** Bragg came to take a slave state and the state declined to be taken — by its own white population, not just by Buell's army. Without it, §1's premise pays off only on the battlefield, not in the recruiting failure that actually doomed the offensive. The wagons-of-rifles image is also vivid human/material detail of exactly the kind §7 of the fact pack calls for. ~2–3 sentences; natural home is §1 (setup) with a callback in §5 (reckoning).

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (strengthens; not blocking)

- **S1 — Low ammunition/supplies in the retreat (item #15).** Add a clause to §4: Bragg withdrew not only because he was exposed and outnumbered but because he was running low on ammunition and supplies, with no Kentucky base materializing to resupply from. One clause; ties directly to MUST-ADD #2.
- **S2 — Green troops on Open Knob (item #16).** A line that Parsons' guns and the regiments around them (21st Wisconsin formed under a month earlier, 123rd Illinois, 105th Ohio) were near-raw recruits would deepen §3's horror and explain why the line cracked. Optional but high-value for the "human cost" mandate.
- **S3 — The Antietam pairing / autumn-'62 hinge (item #17).** §5 could note in one sentence that Perryville was the western half of a twin repulse — Antietam in the East three weeks earlier — and that throwing back *both* invasions in Sept–Oct 1862 is what historians (McPherson) call the turning point that forestalled European recognition and cleared the way for emancipation. The draft already invokes "high tide in the West"; this connects it to the war-wide hinge and reinforces the slavery throughline with a documented payoff. Strongly recommended.
- **S4 — Kirby Smith's army earlier (item #18).** A single early mention that the invasion was two Confederate armies (Bragg's + Kirby Smith's, who'd already won at Richmond, KY) would set up both the dispersion (MUST-ADD #1) and the retreat-to-join (§4) without a cold introduction at the end.

## FACT-PACK BLIND SPOTS (flag to the pack-builder; not the author's fault)

1. **The Frankfort feint is absent from the fact pack.** §5/§6 cover the Hawes inauguration but never mention Sill's diversion or that Bragg dispersed his army chasing it. This is a primary cause of the engaged-force asymmetry the pack itself calls "the story" (§2) — a real omission. Add it to the sequence/strategic sections.
2. **The recruitment failure / unclaimed rifles is absent.** The pack states the *goal* (25–30k recruits, §1/§6) but never that it failed or how badly — no mention that almost no Kentuckians enlisted or of the rifles in the wagons. This is the most-cited reason the campaign collapsed and belongs in §6 (the cause) and §9 (significance).
3. **Green-troops dimension absent** (§2/§7 list the units/casualties but not that McCook's regiments were weeks-old recruits). Minor.
4. **The Antietam/McPherson turning-point frame is absent** from §9 significance. The pack's "high tide in the West receded" is good but stops short of the documented twin-invasion hinge.
5. **Low-ammunition/supply retreat factor under-stated** in pack §5 (gives exposure/numbers, not supply). Minor.

Pack strengths to keep: the engaged-vs-present discipline, casualty-range + 4,421-typo flag, Polk/Hardee rank-dating caution, and the slavery framing guidance are all exactly right and the draft inherited them cleanly.
