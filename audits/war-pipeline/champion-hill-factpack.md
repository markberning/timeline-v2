### Sizes — READ THIS, the headline number is misleading
- **Total available:** Grant ~**32,000** (some sources frame McPherson + McClernand together as ~**33,000**); Pemberton ~**22,000–23,000**. Source: WP; ABT-facts; WHN. **[contested]** — use the range.
- **Actually ENGAGED ≈ even, ~the same on each side.** Grant's numerical edge **evaporated** on the day: Sherman was out of range and **McClernand sat idle** on the southern roads, so the fighting fell on **McPherson's corps plus Hovey** — a force **"about equal in strength to Pemberton's,"** which **"would have to bear almost all of the fighting for the Federals."** Source: WHN (direct paraphrase). **Author hook:** don't write "32,000 vs. 22,000" as if Grant steamrolled by mass — frame it as a near-even, savage fight that Grant won by where the blow landed (the Jackson-Road flank), not by weight.

## 4. Geography (for MAP accuracy — relative positions, north up)

- **Champion Hill** — a **bald, ~75-ft crest** on the **Champion family's plantation land** (the Champion house sat near the base; the family name is where the battle's name comes from). It was **"one of the highest points in that section, and commanded all the ground"** (Grant). It dominated the road junction below it. Source: Grant *Memoirs*; ABT-art; WP.
- **The three roads (this is the whole tactical key — get them right on the map):** Pemberton's army was posted along a **~3-mile ridge** covering **three roughly parallel east–west roads** that all ran from the **Jackson/Edwards area back toward Vicksburg**:
  1. **Jackson Road** — the **northernmost**; ran past Champion Hill's crest. The Union main effort (McPherson's corps, Hovey + Logan) came in on this road. **This is the flank that decided the battle.**
  2. **Middle Road** — the center road.
  3. **Raymond Road** — the **southernmost**; Loring's Confederate division held this end; McClernand's idle column faced it.
  Source: WP; ABT-art; WHN; search.
- **The crossroads** — where the **Jackson Road and Middle Road met** (just south/southwest of the crest, ~⅓ mi below it). **This junction WAS the prize:** it sat astride **Pemberton's only good line of retreat to Vicksburg.** Whoever held the crossroads held the road home. It changed hands with the hill. Source: ABT-art; search; WP.
- **Baker's Creek (Bakers Creek)** — the creek running roughly **north–south behind (west of) the Confederate position**, between the battlefield and Vicksburg. Pemberton's retreat had to **cross it.** Source: WP; ABT-art; VFP §4.
- **The bridge and the ford:** Pemberton's army escaped over the **Baker's Creek bridge on the Raymond Road crossing** (and a ford), covered by **Tilghman's rearguard.** **Loring's division was the problem:** by the time it tried to pull back, the **usable crossing was cut off** (Union troops + the loss of the bridge route), so Loring could **not** follow Pemberton across — he had to peel away to the south/east and march clear around to **join Johnston near Jackson** instead. **Late afternoon Grant's troops seized the Baker's Creek bridge; by midnight they held Edwards.** Source: WP; ABT-art; VFP §5.
- **Relative positions summary for the map:** **Vicksburg = far west (off-map or far left); Jackson/Edwards = east (right).** Confederate line runs along the ridge facing **east** (toward Grant's advance); **Champion Hill + Jackson Road = the NORTH (top) end** of the line; **Raymond Road + Loring = the SOUTH (bottom) end**; **Baker's Creek = behind the line, to the WEST (left)**, the thing the Confederates must cross to escape. The Union blow falls on the **north/top** flank and rolls the line up.
- **Map labels (spell EXACTLY as prose will):** **Champion Hill**, **Champion house**, **Jackson Road**, **Middle Road**, **Raymond Road**, **the crossroads**, **Baker's Creek**, **Edwards** (or **Edwards Station**), **Vicksburg** (west, for orientation), **Jackson** (east, for orientation). North up, east right.

## 5. Sequence & Turning Points

> **Scene-set (KEEP SHORT — → see Vicksburg for the campaign):** By mid-May 1863 Grant was loose in the Mississippi interior, having crossed the river at Bruinsburg, cut from his supply line, and won at Port Gibson, Raymond, and Jackson. He'd just wrecked the state capital (Jackson) and scattered Johnston; now he turned **west** toward Vicksburg. Pemberton, ordered confusingly both to defend Vicksburg and to strike Grant's supply line, had marched **out** of the city and was caught in the open at Champion Hill. **The author should compress all of this to a few sentences and link to the campaign page — the battle is the focus.**

1. **The converging columns (dawn–morning).** Grant advanced on Pemberton along **all three roads** (Jackson / Middle / Raymond). Skirmishing opened at dawn. Stephen D. Lee (South), posted on Champion Hill, **spotted the Union flanking column on the Jackson Road** and warned it would cut the army off from Vicksburg. Source: WP; ABT-art; VFP §5.
2. **Grant arrives, the assault goes in (~10:00 a.m.–1:00 p.m.).** Grant reached the field ~10 a.m. Hovey's (North) and Logan's (North) men pushed down the **Jackson Road**; by ~11:30 a.m. they hit the Confederate main line; **around 1:00 p.m. they seized the crest of Champion Hill**, breaking Stevenson's (South) left, which fell back **in disorder.** The Federals swept on to take **the crossroads** — **closing Pemberton's Jackson-Road escape route.** Source: WP; ABT-facts; search.
3. **THE HILL CHANGES HANDS — Bowen's counterattack (~2:00 p.m.).** Bowen's (South) Missourians and Arkansans **slammed into Hovey's men**, **surged back over the crest**, and drove the Federals nearly to the **Champion house** — momentarily **retaking the hill and the crossroads.** It was the most savage fighting of the day. **But Bowen was too few to hold it.** Source: WP; ABT-art; search; VFP §2.
4. **Grant feeds in the reserve — the hill changes hands AGAIN.** Grant committed **Crocker's (North) division.** The Federals stopped Bowen's surge and **retook the crest and the crossroads for good.** **Net: the hill / crossroads changed hands ~three times** (Union takes it → Bowen retakes it → Union takes it back). Source: ABT-art; search.
5. **Pemberton's defeat & retreat.** With the crossroads lost and McClernand finally pressuring the south, Pemberton **ordered the army off the field**, retreating **southwest across Baker's Creek** toward Vicksburg. **Tilghman's (South) brigade held the rearguard at the crossing and Tilghman was killed by an artillery shell.** By late afternoon Grant's troops took the **Baker's Creek bridge**; by midnight they held **Edwards.** Source: WP; ABT-art.
6. **TURNING POINT — Loring's division lost.** **Loring's (South) division could not get across Baker's Creek** with the rest of the army (the crossing was cut off). Rather than fight through, Loring **marched away to the south and east and joined Johnston** near Jackson — **he never rejoined Pemberton and never entered Vicksburg.** Pemberton thus walked into the Vicksburg works having **lost an entire division he'd need for the siege.** Source: WP; ABT-art; VFP §5.
7. **The hinge of the campaign.** Champion Hill **sealed Vicksburg's fate.** Grant stayed wedged **between Johnston and Pemberton** so the two Confederate armies could never unite. The next day (**Big Black River Bridge, May 17** → see Vicksburg) routed Pemberton's rearguard, and on **May 18** the beaten army was shut inside Vicksburg — **the 47-day siege and the July 4 surrender followed** (→ see Vicksburg). Source: WP; ABT; VFP §5.

> **Friction to note (real, sourced):** the **Pemberton–Loring feud.** Pemberton **repeatedly ordered Loring to reinforce the collapsing left;** Loring **insisted he faced a strong Federal force on the Raymond Road and could not move,** then moved too late to matter — and was then the division that got cut off. The blame-game between them is part of the story. Source: ABT-art; NPS-via-ABT.

## 6. THE CAUSE — SLAVERY (SHIP-BLOCKING — must appear in the section's OWN prose)

**Standing rule (`feedback_name_the_cause`): this section FAILS the framing gate if the word "slavery" never appears in its own prose.** A reader can land on Champion Hill cold; it must stand on its own and name what the war — and the campaign to split the Confederacy — was fought over. The Vicksburg page carries the full emancipation throughline (→ see Vicksburg §6); echo it, don't re-document it. Hooks:

- **The whole point of taking Vicksburg was to split the Confederacy in two** — and what the Confederacy was, the thing it had seceded to protect, was a **slave society**. Vicksburg held the **last Confederate stretch of the Mississippi**; cutting it severed the slaveholding Trans-Mississippi from the rest. Name it plainly: the war was about slavery, and this campaign was about breaking the slaveholders' republic in half. Source: VFP §6.
- **Self-emancipation along Grant's march (the on-the-ground hook).** As the Army of the Tennessee moved through the Mississippi Valley in 1862–63, **thousands of enslaved people freed themselves** — walking off plantations to Union lines. NPS stresses they **"more often already had taken steps to secure their own emancipation"** rather than waiting to be freed. **Champion Hill was fought on a slaveholding plantation** (the Champion family's land) in a county worked by enslaved people — emancipation was happening on the very ground the armies fought over. Source: NPS via VFP §6. **[VERIFY]** the Champion family's specific slaveholding status if the author wants to assert it directly — the general valley pattern is solid; the specific plantation detail should be confirmed before stating it as fact.
- **The reckoning came weeks later, on the same campaign:** Vicksburg's surrender **ended slavery in the city** (NPS article title: *"July 4, 1863: Slavery Ends in Vicksburg"*), and **Milliken's Bend (June 7)** put newly freed Black soldiers into combat. Both belong to the Vicksburg page — **reference, don't re-tell** → (see Vicksburg §6). Source: VFP §6.

> **Author hook (light, one or two sentences where the section sets its stakes):** every mile Grant fought west — Port Gibson, Raymond, Jackson, **Champion Hill** — the institution the South seceded to defend dissolved behind his army. The battle for the crossroads was, at bottom, a battle to break a slaveholding republic in half.

## 8. Human Cost (named + documented)

- **Hovey's division (North)** took the bulk of the **~2,457 Union casualties** — the men who fought up and were thrown off and fought back up the crest. Their name for it — **"the hill of death"** — is the human core of the Union side. Source: WHN; ABT-facts.
- **Brig. Gen. Lloyd Tilghman (South)** — killed by a Union artillery shell holding the **Baker's Creek rearguard** so the rest of the army could escape. A concrete, named death at a concrete place (the crossing). Source: WP; ABT-art.
- **The broken Confederate army** — ~3,840 casualties, of which **~2,441 were missing/captured**: the cost of a defeated army streaming back over one creek crossing. Source: WP; ABT-facts.
- **Loring's lost division (South)** — thousands of men who survived the battle but were **cut off, marched away, and were simply gone** from Pemberton's army when the siege closed — a loss without a body count. Source: WP; ABT-art.
- **Maj. Gen. John S. Bowen (South)** — led the counterattack that nearly won the hill back; would carry the Vicksburg surrender flag seven weeks later and **die of dysentery July 13** → (see Vicksburg). A human thread that runs from this hill to the surrender. Source: VFP §8.

## 9. Significance (for the "what it meant" close)

- **The decisive field battle of the Vicksburg Campaign.** Of the five inland battles, Champion Hill is the one that **decided the campaign in the open** — beat Pemberton here and Vicksburg's fall became a matter of time. Source: WP; ABT-art; VFP §5.
- **"The hill of death" / the bloodiest day of the campaign** — frequently cited as the single bloodiest day of the entire Vicksburg operation. Source: WHN.
- **It cost Pemberton a whole division** (Loring) before the siege even began, and **kept Johnston and Pemberton permanently apart** — Grant's central aim. Source: WP; VFP §7.
- **It led directly to the siege and the July 4 surrender** — Big Black River the next day, the works on May 18, then 47 days and ~29,000 men surrendered → (see Vicksburg §5/§9). The river opened, the Confederacy was split, Gettysburg fell the day before — the twin turning point. **Reference the campaign's payoff; the battle's own meaning is: this is where it was decided.** Source: VFP §9.

## 11. Blind spots / flags for the fact-checker

- **The "32,000 vs. 22,000" trap.** Total available ≠ engaged. The fight was **near-even** because McClernand idled and Sherman was absent (McPherson + Hovey ≈ Pemberton's size). If the author writes "Grant's much larger army," the fact-checker should push back — frame it as numbers Grant **failed to bring to bear**. Source: WHN. **Exact "engaged" headcounts are not cleanly sourced** — I could not pin a hard "X engaged" number; present it qualitatively ("roughly even on the day"), not as a false-precise figure.
- **"Changed hands" count.** Consensus = the crest/crossroads changed hands ~**three times** (Union → Bowen → Union). Some sources say "twice." Author should say "changed hands several times" or "three times" and the fact-checker can confirm against the Bowen-counterattack sequence. Source: WP / search / ABT-art.
- **"The hill of death"** is a **paraphrased attribution to Hovey's men**, not a single verbatim quote from a named source — treat as such. **[VERIFY]** if a named first-person source is wanted.
- **Grant "between Johnston and Pemberton" quote — [VERIFY] exact wording** (secondary attribution).
- **The Champion plantation's specific slaveholding detail — [VERIFY]** before asserting as fact (the valley-wide self-emancipation pattern is solid; the specific plantation is an inference until confirmed).
- **Casualty/strength numbers are [contested]** Civil War figures — the WP/ABT/NPS set used here is consensus; note the range.
- **Ford vs. bridge at Baker's Creek:** sources clearly name a **bridge** (Raymond Road crossing) and Grant's late-day seizure of it; a **ford** is implied/secondary. Don't over-specify the ford as a named feature unless confirmed.
- **Don't import campaign material** the Vicksburg page owns (siege, surrender, Milliken's Bend, caves, batteries, Bruinsburg). Every such reference should be a **link, not a re-telling** → (see Vicksburg). This is the main structural risk for this section: bloating into a second campaign page.
- **Loring's later war** (he survived, kept commanding, later served the Khedive of Egypt — "Loring Pasha") is **out of scope** — interesting but off the battle; cut it.
