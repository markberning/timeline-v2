## Fort William Henry
- South tip Lake George, Province of NY; northern anchor of Albany corridor; French held Fort Carillon (later Ticonderoga) ~15 mi north → §6 Background.
- Largest force yet in N. American war: 6,200 regulars+militia + ~1,800 Native from ~33 nations; Great Lakes + St. Lawrence coalition (Odawa, Abenaki, Potawatomi, Nipissing, Ojibwa, Menominee, Caughnawaga, others); some traveled hundreds of miles → §6 Siege.
- Garrison ~2,500: ~200 regulars + 800 militia (many smallpox) under Lt. Col. George Monro (British), 35th Foot career officer → §6.
- **[R2 ADD fix #7] Native war aims established BEFORE the massacre,** at the point the 1757 coalition first gathers: pays d'en haut (upper Great Lakes) warriors made up the largest part of the ~1,800; came for their OWN reasons (war honors, plunder, captives to adopt/ransom/hold for ritual), served without pay (asked only rations/ammunition/gifts), drawn by the Oswego victory story, inside the French trade-and-gift alliance that supplied them → §C (Anderson, *Crucible of War* ch.21).
- Fort Edward 17 mi south: ~1,500 militia + ~150 Mohawk, never sent → §6.
- Webb (Maj. Gen., British, full rank first per R2 fix #14) at Fort Edward → §6, §9 cast.
- **[R2 ADD fix #14] Mohawk made their OWN calculation,** not merely sharing Webb's funk: they weighed an unchosen fight against a French force whose size/momentum they could read better than Webb; "the two refusals were not the same refusal" → §6 / §8 (Iroquois conditional-alliance agency).
- Siege Aug 3; trenches/guns Aug 5; walls breached + guns disabled Aug 7; Monro's repeated pleas → §6 Siege Operations.
- Webb deceived (French prisoner) into believing 11,000–12,000; refused; "negotiate the best terms" message intercepted, given to Montcalm first; Webb recalled → §6 Webb's Refusal.
- Johnson: Webb "the only Englishman [I] ever knew who was a coward" → §6 (passed thru later accounts).
- Monro surrendered Aug 9 → §6.
- Terms: march to Fort Edward, full honors, keep colours/muskets/1 symbolic cannon, parole 18 months → §6 Surrender terms.
- **[R2 GLOSS fix #12] parole defined on first use:** a sworn promise (by the surrendered garrison) not to take up arms against France again for a set time; explicitly "not a prison term" → §6.
- Native aims at surrender now refer BACK to the upfront establishment (R2 fix #7); massacre paragraph trimmed to avoid re-introducing the aims as if new; "the French had conspired with their enemies against their friends" framing → §C (Anderson via Steele citation).
- Montcalm warning re: "a mob of Indians" → §6 (ABT source quote).
- Native aims: captives (ransom/adopt/ritual), scalps as war honours; terms gave them nothing, no compensation/alternative; chiefs limited coercive authority → §6 Massacre / Native agency.
- Aug 9 morning attack before/during withdrawal; killed/scalped wounded, seized women+children, chaos → §6.
- Montcalm + officers intervened, recovered ~500 captives, physical courage; limited not prevented → §6 Montcalm's response.
- Toll: British contemporary ~1,500 (inflated outrage); French minimized; Steele *Betrayals* (1990) standard; "at least 69, could not possibly exceed 184," ~2.8–7.5% → §6 Casualty debate, §10 #5.
- ~200 more captives carried off; ~300 missing year's end; ≥40 refused return (adopted) → §6.
- "1,500" from Cooper *Last of the Mohicans* (1826), 19th-c racial lens; amplified by popular historians → §6 LOTM Myth.
- Warriors exhumed cemetery bodies for scalps; smallpox carried home; epidemic; Potawatomi nearly wiped out → §6 Smallpox Aftermath.
- Monro survived march to Albany; died Nov 3 1757, stroke; some say rage at Webb + trauma → §6 Monro's Fate.
