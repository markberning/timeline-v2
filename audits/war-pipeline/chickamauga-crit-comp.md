## Must-cover checklist

### Strategic frame & campaign
- **Chattanooga = rail gateway to the Deep South / "gateway to the lower South"** — COVERED (opening + closing Meanwhiles, repeatedly and well).
- **Tullahoma campaign maneuvered Bragg out of Middle TN, nearly bloodless** — COVERED.
- **Rosecrans crosses the Tennessee, advances in 3 separated columns, levers Bragg out of Chattanooga Sept 9 without a fight** — COVERED; "danger hidden inside a bloodless victory" framing is excellent.
- **Bragg reinforced (Longstreet from Virginia by long rail detour + troops from Mississippi); rare CS numerical edge** — COVERED.
- **First major battle fought in Georgia** — THIN/MISSING. Fact pack §1 flags it; the draft never states it. Minor but it's a clean, true "why this place matters" beat. SHOULD-CONSIDER.

### The battle itself
- **Sept 18 — fight for the creek bridges; Wilder's Spencer-armed Lightning Brigade at Alexander's, Minty at Reed's** — COVERED (Wilder named; Minty alluded to as "other Union horsemen").
- **Sept 19 — confused meeting battle across ~4 mi of timber, fed in piecemeal, no decision; Cleburne's twilight attack at Winfrey field** — COVERED. (Draft says "twilight attack near Winfrey field" without naming Cleburne — acceptable compression; SHOULD-CONSIDER naming him since he's a marquee CS division commander.)
- **The dense-woods terrain as the controlling fact ("a battle nobody could see")** — COVERED, and it's the draft's strongest structural choice.
- **How the battle actually *opened* — accidental, over a reported trapped CS brigade; Croxton/Brannan vs Forrest's cavalry at ~7:30 a.m.** — MISSING. The draft jumps from "the bridges" to "the real battle came on Sept 19 … as a confused brawl." The accidental, water-seeking-pickets origin is a vivid and true detail, but it's a SHOULD-CONSIDER, not a blocker — the "groping blind" frame already conveys the meeting-engagement nature.
- **Sept 20 — Polk's late dawn attack (~9:30) on Thomas's fortified Kelly Field left, limited gains** — COVERED.
- **The fatal Wood order / the gap / Longstreet's ~8-brigade column hitting Brotherton by coincidence (~11:10)** — COVERED, and superbly told. This is the heart of the battle and the draft lands it.
- **Union right/center routs; Rosecrans, McCook, Crittenden, Garfield swept off; Rosecrans rides to Chattanooga believing all lost** — COVERED.
- **Thomas's stand on Snodgrass Hill / Horseshoe Ridge ~1 p.m.–dark; Granger marches to the guns** — COVERED, well weighted.
- **Organized withdrawal into Chattanooga, not annihilation** — COVERED ("a rescue inside a defeat").

### Consequence (the part that makes the battle matter)
- **Siege of Chattanooga: Bragg takes Missionary Ridge + Lookout Mountain, cuts supply, near-starvation** — COVERED. The starvation detail is light (one clause) — SHOULD-CONSIDER adding the human texture (men and animals starving) the sources stress, since "human cost" is a weighted axis for this tier.
- **Cracker Line / Brown's Ferry Oct 27 breaks the supply crisis** — COVERED.
- **Grant consolidates the western armies; Rosecrans relieved; Thomas takes the Army of the Cumberland** — COVERED.
- **Nov breakout — Lookout Mtn Nov 24, Missionary Ridge spontaneous charge Nov 25 — routs Bragg into Georgia** — COVERED.
- **Net: tactical CS win → strategic disaster; opens gateway to Atlanta / Sherman 1864 / March to the Sea** — COVERED, and tied back to slavery-as-cause in the closer.

### Why the victory was thrown away  ← the one real gap
- **Bragg fails to pursue / destroy the beaten army** — COVERED (stated plainly).
- **Named pressure to pursue — Longstreet AND Forrest urged a hard chase; Bragg refused** — **THIN → MUST-ADD.** Draft says only "Longstreet and others pressed him." Forrest is the iconic name here (the "What does he fight battles for?" confrontation) and Longstreet's push is documented. Naming the pressure is what makes Bragg's choice land as a *squandered* victory rather than a tired one.
- **The Confederate command meltdown that followed (the deeper "why it curdled")** — MISSING → part of MUST-ADD. After the battle Polk, D.H. Hill, Buckner, and others openly revolted; Longstreet was urged to write Richmond about Bragg's "mismanagement"; Bragg relieved Polk and had him court-martialed (Davis later dismissed it), relieved D.H. Hill, and Longstreet was sent off to the Knoxville campaign — fatally weakening the army that was supposed to be holding Chattanooga. This is the standard historiographic explanation for why a smashing tactical win produced nothing, and it directly sets up *why* Grant could break the siege so easily. The draft attributes the squandering almost entirely to Bragg's personal passivity; the army-tore-itself-apart story is the missing causal half.

### Human cost (weighted for this tier)
- **~34,000 in two days; second-bloodiest of the war after Gettysburg; victor bled more than the vanquished** — COVERED, and the "bitter arithmetic" framing is exactly right. (Independent check: 16,170 US / 18,454 CS confirmed; CS lost ~20% of force.)
- **Hood's leg amputated (already lost left-arm use at Gettysburg)** — COVERED.
- **Helm killed — Lincoln's brother-in-law (Mary Todd's half-sister Emilie's husband); Lincoln mourned** — COVERED.
- **Lytle (Union poet-general) killed; Deshler + Preston Smith killed; ~10 CS generals killed/wounded** — COVERED. (Independent check confirms the "ten Confederate generals" figure — VERIFY flag can be cleared; sources do not give a clean Union-general count, so keep the prose CS-side.)
- **The wounded in burning underbrush, friendly fire in the blind timber** — COVERED.

### Cause (ship-blocking framing gate)
- **Slavery named as the cause in the section's own prose** — COVERED, twice (opening + closing), explicit and uneuphemized. Framing gate satisfied.
- **Thomas the Virginian/former-slaveholder irony** — COVERED (with an appropriate VERIFY flag on exact framing).

### Naming / etymology
- **"River of death" handled as folklore, not literal translation** — COVERED, correctly cautious.
- **"Rock of Chickamauga" provenance — earned by the stand, not asserted as spot-coinage** — COVERED, exactly to the fact-pack caution.

## MUST-ADD (blocks ship)

1. **The squandered-victory mechanism: name the pressure to pursue AND the command
   meltdown.** Currently the draft explains the thrown-away victory as Bragg simply
   not pursuing ("Longstreet and others pressed him"). The essential, standard account
   has two named load-bearing pieces the draft omits:
   - **Forrest** (with Longstreet) explicitly urged a hard pursuit to destroy the army
     before it reached Chattanooga; Bragg refused with his eye on his own losses.
     Forrest is the marquee name and belongs here.
   - **The post-battle Confederate command revolt** — Polk relieved + court-martialed,
     D.H. Hill relieved, the generals' petition to Richmond against Bragg, and
     Longstreet detached to Knoxville — which is *why* the army that won couldn't hold
     what it won, and *why* Grant broke the siege so fast. This is the causal bridge
     between "tactical win" and "strategic catastrophe" the draft already asserts but
     doesn't fully earn. A compact 2–3 sentence beat in **the-reckoning** closes the gap;
     it does not require a new section.

   (Note: the Forrest "What does he fight battles for?" / threatened-Bragg's-life
   anecdote is single-source and post-war — per HistoryNet, treat as legend or omit the
   verbatim, exactly as the draft already does with "river of death" and the nickname.
   The *fact* that Forrest urged pursuit is solid; the dramatic quote is not.)

## Fact-pack blind spots (for the fact-checker)

1. **No mention of the Confederate command revolt / Bragg-subordinate war** anywhere in
   the fact pack §§5/9. This is the single biggest omission in the pack itself — it is
   the consensus explanation for the squandered victory and feeds the MUST-ADD above.
   The pack should add a "Why Bragg failed to exploit" line (Forrest+Longstreet urged
   pursuit; Polk/Hill relieved; Longstreet sent to Knoxville).
2. **The battle's accidental opening** (Croxton/Brannan vs Forrest over a reported
   trapped brigade) is absent from §5; §5 jumps straight from the bridges to "the
   meeting battle." Not blocking, but the pack should at least note it so the author
   isn't inventing how Sept 19 began.
3. **Ten-CS-generals figure** is independently confirmed (WP/HISTORY) — the §7 VERIFY
   flag can be downgraded; the pack is right that there's no clean Union-general count.
4. **CS losing ~20% of its force** (a standard ABT framing of the bleed-more point) is
   not in the pack; would strengthen the "victor bled harder" beat with a percentage.
5. The pack's geography (§4) is thorough and matches independent sources; no blind spots
   there. Distance ~12 mi vs ~20 mi remains the one open numeric VERIFY (already flagged).
