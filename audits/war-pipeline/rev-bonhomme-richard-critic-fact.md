## BLOCKERS

**B1 · Richard Dale stated as "twenty-three at the battle" — he was twenty-two; appears twice**

> (commander bio, dossier): "Virginia-born, **twenty-three** at the battle, and the most quietly important officer aboard…"
>
> (fact ledger, line ~255): "Dale: VA-born, **23**; escaped from an English prison…"

**The math:** Richard Dale was born November 6, 1756. The battle was September 23, 1779. He did not turn 23 until six weeks after the battle. He was **22** at Flamborough Head.

**Source of the error:** The fact pack (§3 bio) gives "23 at the battle" without a birth date, and the draft faithfully copies it. The pack is wrong; the draft inherited it. Both need correction.

**Web verification:** Wikipedia, "Richard Dale (naval officer)" — born November 6, 1756, confirmed. September 23, 1779 minus November 6, 1756 = 22 years, 10 months, 17 days.

**Fix:** Change "twenty-three at the battle" to "twenty-two at the battle" in the commander bio, and "23" to "22" in the fact ledger entry. Also flag this as a pack error to be corrected in `rev-bonhomme-richard-factpack.md` §3.

**SF2 · London Observer quote truncated in a way that changes its character**

> (Section 3, prose): "A London paper carried another version, that he was 'determined to make you strike.'"

The fact pack (§7a, item 2) gives the full London Observer (letter from Amsterdam), Oct 12, 1779 form as: "**I have not as yet thought of it, but am determined to make you strike.**" The draft paraphrases only the second half, dropping "I have not as yet thought of it" entirely. This matters because the first half is linguistically very close to Jones's own memoir version ("I haven't as yet thought of surrendering") — the parallel actually strengthens the evidence chain that the London Observer version is independently reliable. By stripping the first clause, the draft makes the quote read as a bare threat rather than a rebuff-and-counter, losing the connection to the memoir.

The draft also describes this as one of "four early sources, four different lines" — accurate as a count of the sources presented, but slightly undercounting: the pack also has the garbled London Evening Post (Sept 30) version. That omission is reasonable compression; this note is for completeness.

**Fix:** Render the London Observer quote in full: "I have not as yet thought of it, but am determined to make you strike." This also tightens the evidence chain the paragraph is building.

**SF3 · "as France's answer to Lafayette" for Landais — pack gives this as describing how he was handed the Alliance, not as a characterization of Landais himself**

> (commander bio, Landais): "made an honorary citizen of Massachusetts, and handed the Continental frigate Alliance **as France's answer to Lafayette**."

The pack (§3) says Landais was "made an honorary citizen of Massachusetts and given the Continental frigate *Alliance* (June 1778) — **France's answer to Lafayette, on paper**." The pack's framing is hedged by "on paper" — it acknowledges this was the official presentation, not the reality. The draft's bio drops "on paper," making it sound like an unhedged characterization of what Landais actually was. Given that the pack's entire Landais entry is about the gap between his official status and his actual conduct, the hedge is load-bearing.

**Fix:** Add "on paper" (or equivalent framing: "as France billed him") to convey the pack's intended irony: "handed the Continental frigate Alliance — France's answer to Lafayette, on paper."

**N2 · Jones's crypt: draft says "an American ambassador found his lead coffin in 1905"**

> (Section 3, prose): "an American ambassador spent six years searching for it, found his lead coffin in 1905."

The pack (§6) gives this as "U.S. Ambassador Horace Porter's six-year search found his lead coffin." Web-verified: Porter was ambassador to France 1897–1905, personal search began ~1899, coffin found April 1905. The draft omits Porter's name (acceptable compression) and says "ambassador" rather than specifying ex-ambassador or former ambassador (Porter had left his post when the search concluded in 1905). This is extremely minor and the pack itself uses "Ambassador Horace Porter" without flagging his departure from office. No fix required; noting the minor imprecision.
