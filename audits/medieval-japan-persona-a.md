## Chapter 1 — Two Capitals, Two Powers

1. **"the polish of a thousand years of borrowed Chinese refinement"** — empty claim; "polish" and "refinement" assert sophistication without one concrete thing — what does borrowed Chinese refinement actually look like in Kyoto? — Give me one example: what did they borrow, a script, a building style, a poetry form?
2. **"the perfumed courtiers of the west"** — "perfumed" is atmosphere, not a concrete handle on what made courtiers different from warriors — Did they literally wear perfume, or is this a figure of speech for soft/refined?
3. **"the polish of a thousand years"** — "a thousand years" of what — when did the imperial line actually begin in the written record versus in legend? — How far back does the verifiable record go, as opposed to the descent-from-the-sun-goddess claim?
4. **"He went under holding the legend that his grandmother told him a capital waited beneath the waves."** — pronoun/attribution: is this a real recorded detail or part of the Tale of the Heike the text just warned is literature? — Is this story fact or from the epic the chapter says invents speeches?
5. **"a relationship not unlike the lord-and-vassal bond taking shape in Europe in the same centuries, though it grew from entirely separate roots"** — European feudalism is invoked as a comparison the reader is assumed to know — What is the lord-and-vassal bond in Europe? The newcomer may not actually know how European feudalism worked.
6. **"a grand old military title once given to generals sent to fight the indigenous peoples of the north"** — who were the indigenous peoples of the north? named and undefined-by-omission — Who lived in the north that Japan was fighting — were these a distinct people, and what happened to them?
7. **"the late Roman emperors propped up by their generals, the Frankish kings reduced to 'do-nothing' puppets while the mayors of the palace ran the kingdom, the Abbasid caliph in Baghdad reduced to a sacred figurehead"** — three rapid-fire historical parallels (Roman, Frankish, Abbasid) each assuming the reader knows the story — Who were the Frankish kings and the "mayors of the palace"? Who was the Abbasid caliph and why was he a figurehead? These are dropped without a one-line setup.
8. **"In a twist that tells you how slippery power in Kyoto had become, even reigning emperors found a way to escape the job"** — the *insei* system: it says emperors got "more freedom" by abdicating but never explains the mechanism — *why* did a retired, vow-taking ex-emperor have more real power than a sitting one?
