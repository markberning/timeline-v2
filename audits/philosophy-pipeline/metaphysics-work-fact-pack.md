**THE ARISTOTLE-CORPUS RULE (carried from the thinker-pack — this work's #1 structural fact):**
The *Metaphysics* is **not a single composed book**. It is a **compilation of separate
treatises** — lecture notes, research drafts, and in-house records — that a later editor
(almost certainly **Andronicus of Rhodes**, c. 70 BCE) bundled, ordered, and shelved
*after* the *Physics* on the library roll. There is no continuous authorial through-line
the way the *Republic* has one; the books were composed at different times, some overlap or
repeat, Book II (little-alpha, *α elatton*) is widely held to be a later insertion, and the
fourteen-book unity is editorial, not Aristotelian. The author must never imply Aristotle
"sat down and wrote the *Metaphysics*"; write "the treatises collected as the *Metaphysics*,"
"in the book later editors placed twelfth (Lambda)," etc. This is the headline framing fact
of the whole read. `DOCUMENTED` / `FRAMING-TRAP` (see §0 and Trap 1).

## 1. The argument-arc — the whole treatise, book by book

This is the spine the author walks. Unlike the *Republic*, it is **not one continuous
composed argument** — it is a sequence of treatises with a recurring center of gravity:
*what is being, and what is the primary being (substance), and is there an eternal substance?*
The famous set-pieces (the opening on knowledge, the four causes, being qua being,
non-contradiction, substance, potentiality/actuality, the unmoved mover) are the load-bearing
moves. Books are given by Greek letter + Roman numeral.

### Book I — Alpha (Α): why we seek wisdom; the four causes; the survey and critique of predecessors
- **The opening (I.1, 980a):** the most famous sentence in the corpus — **"All men by nature
  desire to know"** (Q-A). The proof Aristotle offers is everyday: the delight we take in our
  senses "even apart from their usefulness," above all **sight**. He then climbs a ladder —
  sensation → memory → experience → art/*technē* → and finally **wisdom** (*sophia*), the
  knowledge of **first causes and principles** (Q-B). Wisdom is knowing not just *that*
  something is so but *why* — the universal, the cause.
- **Philosophy begins in wonder (I.2, 982b):** "it is owing to their wonder that men both now
  begin and at first began to philosophize" (Q-C). Wisdom is sought for its own sake, not for
  any further use — the most free of the sciences.
- **The four causes (I.3, 983a):** Aristotle's master tool, introduced here as the four senses
  of "cause" a complete explanation must give (Q-D): the **substance/essence** (the *formal*
  cause — "the 'why' is reducible finally to the definition"), the **matter or substratum**
  (*material*), the **source of the change** (*efficient*), and **the purpose and the good**
  (*final*). He then reads the whole prior tradition (the *Physics*-style "natural
  philosophers" — Thales' water, Anaximenes' air, Heraclitus' fire, Empedocles' four roots,
  Anaxagoras' Mind, the atomists, the Pythagoreans on number) as each having groped at one or
  more of these four causes without seeing them clearly. The history is told as a slow,
  partial discovery of the four-cause scheme he is about to systematize.
- **The critique of Plato's Forms (I.6 and I.9):** Aristotle reports the Platonic theory of
  Forms (Ideas) sympathetically — he was in the Academy twenty years — then dismantles it.
  The Forms are **causally idle**: "what on earth the Forms contribute to sensible things" he
  cannot see (Q-E); and to call sensible things "copies" that "share in" Forms is "to use
  **empty words and poetical metaphors**" (Q-F). He also gestures at the **"third man"**
  regress (the move the thinker read develops on the example of *man*: the Form plus its
  instances need a further Form to explain what they share, and so on forever). The Forms, he
  argues, double the number of things to explain and explain none of the change they were
  posited to ground. (Present fairly — this is a respectful, twenty-years-earned correction of
  his own teacher, not a sneer. See FRAMING-TRAP — Forms.) `VERIFIED` Q-E/Q-F.

### Book II — little-alpha (α elatton, = II): a short methodological preface
A brief book (widely judged a later insertion) on the difficulty of truth, the impossibility
of an infinite regress of causes, and how to listen to a lecture. The author can note it
exists and that scholars treat it as an inserted preface; no signature quote needed.
`DOCUMENTED` / `PARAPHRASE-ONLY`.

### Book III — Beta (Β): the aporiai (the catalogue of problems)
A list of the hard puzzles ("difficulties," *aporiai*) first philosophy must face — is there
one science of all causes? do the principles of perishable and imperishable things differ? are
genera or individuals the principles? etc. Aristotle's method shown live: lay out the knots
before cutting them. `PARAPHRASE-ONLY` (survey; no headline quote required).

### Book IV — Gamma (Γ): the science of being qua being, and non-contradiction (the firmest principle)
The book where the *Metaphysics* names what it IS.
- **Being qua being (IV.1, 1003a):** **"THERE is a science which investigates being as being
  and the attributes which belong to this in virtue of its own nature"** (Q-G). Other sciences
  cut off a *part* of being (the mathematician studies quantity, the physicist studies things
  that change); first philosophy studies being *itself*, as such — the most general and most
  fundamental inquiry there is. This is the closest the treatise comes to a definition of its
  own subject.
- **The principle of non-contradiction (IV.3, 1005b):** the firmest of all principles. Ross:
  **"the same attribute cannot at the same time belong and not belong to the same subject and
  in the same respect"** (Q-H) — and Aristotle calls this **"the most certain principle of
  all"** (Q-I). It is *non-hypothetical* (presupposed by all reasoning, so it cannot itself be
  proved without circularity) and *best known* (anyone who understands anything already has
  it). To deny it is to say nothing at all. Aristotle's defense is famously by *elenchus* — he
  refuses to prove it (that would concede it needs proof) and instead shows the denier cannot
  even speak coherently.

### Book V — Delta (Δ): the philosophical lexicon
A standalone dictionary — about thirty key terms defined in their several senses (*cause,
substance, nature, necessity, one, being, potency, quality, prior/posterior,* etc.). Reference
material the rest of the corpus leans on; a treatise serving as Aristotle's glossary.
`DOCUMENTED` / `PARAPHRASE-ONLY`.

### Book VI — Epsilon (Ε): the three theoretical sciences; being "in the strict sense"
Divides theoretical knowledge into **physics** (study of things that move and have matter),
**mathematics** (things that don't change but aren't separate), and **first philosophy /
"theology"** (what is *both* unchanging *and* separate — the divine, if such a being exists).
First philosophy is the highest because its objects are the highest. Sets up that the science
of being qua being and the science of the divine substance are, in the end, the same science.
`DOCUMENTED` / `PARAPHRASE-ONLY`.

### Books VII–VIII — Zeta (Ζ) & Eta (Η): SUBSTANCE — the heart of the work
The densest, most-studied books in the treatise. The question "what is being?" is collapsed
into "what is **substance** (*ousia*)?":
- **The central question (VII.1, 1028b):** "There are several senses in which a thing may be
  said to 'be'" — and substance is primary among them (first in definition, in knowledge, in
  time). So **"the question which was raised of old and is raised now and always... namely,
  what being is, is just the question, what is substance?"** (Q-J). This is the hinge of the
  whole *Metaphysics*: the grand question of being reduces to the question of substance.
- **Candidates for "what substance is":** Aristotle canvasses four — the **essence** (the
  *what-it-is-to-be* a thing, *to ti ēn einai*), the **universal**, the **genus**, and the
  **substratum** (matter). He argues the **essence/form** is substance in the primary sense —
  not the bare matter (a heap of bronze isn't *anything* in particular), and not the Platonic
  universal (universals can't be substances; substance is what is "this" and separable). The
  form (the organizing principle *in* the individual) is what makes a thing the thing it is.
  This IS hylomorphism doing metaphysical work (matter + form = the composite individual; see
  thinker read Ch.3). `PARAPHRASE-ONLY` on the four candidates; `VERIFIED` Q-J for the framing.
- **Book VIII (Eta)** ties off: sensible substances are compounds of matter and form; the form
  is the actuality, matter the potentiality, and the unity of a definition mirrors the unity of
  matter-taken-into-form. Bridges directly into Book IX. `PARAPHRASE-ONLY`.

### Book IX — Theta (Θ): potentiality and actuality (*dynamis* / *energeia*)
The pair that runs through Aristotle's whole system, here given its own treatise.
- **The turn (IX.1, 1045b–1046a):** having treated substance, he turns to being as divided "in
  respect of **potency** and **complete reality**" — "let us now add a discussion of potency
  and complete reality" (Q-K). *Dynamis* = potentiality, what a thing *can* be (the bronze that
  could become a sphere; the boy who could become a builder). *Energeia / entelecheia* =
  actuality / complete reality, what it *is* in full exercise (the sphere; the builder
  building). Change is matter passing from potency to actuality.
- **Actuality is prior to potentiality (IX.8, 1049b):** "actuality is prior to potency"
  (Q-L) — prior in definition, in substance, and in time (the actual man is needed to make the
  potential man actual; the seed comes from a prior tree). This priority is the metaphysical
  engine that *forces* the next book: if actuality is prior, the ultimate principle must be
  **pure actuality** with no unrealized potency at all. `VERIFIED` Q-K, Q-L.

### Books X–XI — Iota (Ι) & Kappa (Κ): unity, and a recap
- **Book X (Iota):** on the **one and the many**, identity, difference, contrariety — what it
  is for something to be "one," and how unity and being run parallel.
- **Book XI (Kappa):** a recapitulation — partly a digest of III, IV, VI and partly excerpts
  from the *Physics*; widely thought to be compiled from other material, not freshly composed.
  `DOCUMENTED` / `PARAPHRASE-ONLY`.

### Book XII — Lambda (Λ): THE CULMINATION — the Unmoved Mover and thought thinking itself
The most famous and self-standing book — a complete short treatise on substance that climbs to
the divine. This is where the whole inquiry into being lands.
- **The set-up (XII.1–6):** there are three kinds of substance — perishable sensible
  (plants, animals), eternal sensible (the heavenly bodies), and **unmovable** (immaterial).
  Things move; motion is eternal; an eternal motion needs an eternal mover that is itself
  **unmoved** — for if the first mover were itself in motion it would just be one more moved
  link needing a mover behind it. So the first principle must be **pure actuality**, with no
  matter and no unrealized potential. "There must, then, be such a principle, whose very
  essence is actuality... these substances must be without matter; for they must be eternal"
  (Q-M).
- **HOW it moves — the crucial mechanism (XII.7, 1072b):** it does **not push**. "The object of
  desire and the object of thought move in this way; **they move without being moved**" (Q-N);
  and **"The final cause... produces motion as being loved, but all other things move by being
  moved"** (Q-O). The unmoved mover is the **final cause** of all motion: the world moves
  because it is *drawn toward* the mover, the way a beloved object draws a lover or a goal draws
  an agent — it "moves as being loved." Everything strains toward the completeness it imitates
  by moving. **GET THIS RIGHT** — see PRECISION-NOTE — mechanism.
- **What the cosmos hangs on (XII.7, 1072b):** **"On such a principle, then, depend the heavens
  and the world of nature"** (Q-P) — the single line the whole treatise has been climbing
  toward.
- **What the mover DOES — thought thinking itself (XII.9, 1074b):** it is a living, eternal,
  perfect actuality, and its one activity is **thinking**. But it cannot think about anything
  lesser than itself without depending on it, so it thinks **the only object worthy of it —
  itself**: "it must be of itself that the divine thought thinks (since it is the most excellent
  of things), and **its thinking is a thinking on thinking**" (Q-Q); and since in things without
  matter thinker and thought are one, "the divine thought and its object will be the same, i.e.
  the thinking will be one with the object of its thought" (Q-R). The Greek tag is *noēsis
  noēseōs* — "thought thinking itself" / "thinking on thinking" (`PARAPHRASE-ONLY` for the
  Greek; quote Ross's English). The summit of the *Metaphysics* is an eternal mind eternally
  contemplating its own perfect activity.
- **The guardrail (carry from the thinker read):** this is **NOT the personal God of later
  religion.** It does not create the world, does not know you exist, answers no prayers,
  intervenes in nothing; it is the eternal terminus where explanation stops. Aquinas later
  seized this argument and fused it with the God of the Bible — but that re-pointing was
  Aquinas's move, not Aristotle's. `PRECISION-NOTE` (see Trap 2).

### Books XIII–XIV — Mu (Μ) & Nu (Ν): against mathematical Platonism
The closing two books return to attack the **Platonists' and Pythagoreans' separate numbers
and Forms** — the view that numbers and Forms exist as independent substances. Aristotle
argues mathematical objects are not separate substances; they exist *in* sensible things, as
abstractions. A sustained final demolition of the Academy's mathematical metaphysics. The
treatise thus ends, as it began (Book I), in dispute with Plato. `DOCUMENTED` /
`PARAPHRASE-ONLY`.

### Book XII (Lambda) — the unmoved mover & thought thinking itself

**Q-M · The first principle is pure actuality, eternal, immaterial · XII.6–7 1071b–1072a:**

**PRECISION-NOTE — mechanism (load-bearing):** The unmoved mover moves the cosmos **as the
object of desire/love — i.e. as a FINAL cause — NOT by pushing or efficient force.** "Produces
motion as being loved" is Ross's exact wording; "moves as being loved" is the standard short
paraphrase of it (acceptable unquoted). The world moves because it is drawn *toward* the mover's
perfection, straining to imitate its completeness. Never write that the unmoved mover "pushes,"
"sets in motion by contact," or "starts the chain with a shove" — that is the precise error this
note exists to block.

## 3. FRAMING-TRAPS — the places this work is most often misread

**TRAP — the title.** *"Metaphysics means 'beyond the physical' / the study of what transcends
nature."* **Wrong about the origin.** *Ta meta ta physika* literally means **"the [books] after
the *Physics*"** — an **editor's shelving/ordering label** (Andronicus of Rhodes, c. 70 BCE)
for the treatises catalogued *after* the *Physics* on the roll, very possibly also a hint to
study them *after* mastering the *Physics*. The "science of what is **beyond** the physical"
sense is a **later Latin-tradition misreading** of *meta* ("after") as "beyond/above." The
author MUST flag this: Aristotle never called the work "Metaphysics" (he says "first philosophy"
and "wisdom"); the title is a librarian's accident that fossilized into a whole branch of
philosophy. (It is *fair* to add that the discipline the accident named did grow into the study
of being, the ultimate, the immaterial — so the misreading turned out weirdly apt — but the
*etymology* is "after the Physics," full stop.) `FRAMING-TRAP` / `PRECISION-NOTE`.

**TRAP — the unmoved mover is "God."** The unmoved mover is an **impersonal final cause**: pure
actuality, thought thinking itself, that moves the world **by being loved/desired**, not by
creating, willing, knowing, or intervening. It is NOT the personal creator-God of Judaism,
Christianity, or Islam — it didn't make the world, doesn't know individuals exist, answers no
prayers. The fusion with the biblical God is **Aquinas's thirteenth-century synthesis**, read
back onto Aristotle. State Aristotle's actual claim; flag the later religious adoption as a
later move. (Aristotle does call it "god"/divine and "the most excellent of things" — so it is a
*divine* principle, but in his own, impersonal sense.) `FRAMING-TRAP` (carry from thinker read
Ch.3/Ch.6).

**TRAP — the *Metaphysics* is a single composed book with a master argument.** It is a
**compilation of separate treatises** of different dates and overlapping content, assembled and
ordered by a later editor (Andronicus). Book II (little-alpha) is likely inserted; Book XI is
largely a digest; Book XII (Lambda) is a self-standing treatise; the books don't form one
continuous deductive chain the way the *Republic* does. Write "the treatises collected as the
*Metaphysics*" and "the book editors placed twelfth," never "as the *Metaphysics* unfolds,
Aristotle next proves…" as if it were one composed argument. `FRAMING-TRAP` / `DOCUMENTED`.

**TRAP — Aristotle "rejected" or "sneered at" Plato's Forms.** Present the critique **fairly and
as a respectful correction.** Aristotle spent ~20 years in Plato's Academy and treats the Forms
as a serious theory worth refuting at length (Books I, XIII–XIV). His objections are
*philosophical*: the Forms are causally idle (Q-E), "share in / pattern" is empty metaphor
(Q-F), and the theory generates a regress (the "third man"). He is dismantling his own teacher's
central doctrine — the thinker read's "*amicus Plato*" tension (he honors truth above friends,
but is visibly torn) is the right register. Don't flatten it into a put-down. `FRAMING-TRAP`.

**Bonus — "being qua being" vs "being as being."** Ross's text says **"being as being"** (Q-G).
The ubiquitous classroom tag is **"being qua being"** (from Latin *qua*, "in so far as it is").
They mean the same thing. Use "being qua being" in narration if you like, but quote Ross's "being
as being" inside quote-marks; never present "being qua being" as a *quotation* of Ross.
`PRECISION-NOTE`.

### (a) Cast — adapted: the cast-of-CONCEPTS (a treatise has no characters)
**Literal cast is a WEAK fit and should be flagged as such.** The *Metaphysics* has no
interlocutors, no narrator, no dramatic setting — it is theoretical prose. Recommend a
**cast-of-concepts** instead (the load-bearing ideas, each with a one-line role):

| Concept | One-line role |
|---|---|
| **Being qua being** | The subject of the science — being *as such*, what it is for anything to *be* at all (Q-G). |
| **The four causes** (material / formal / efficient / final) | The four-part answer to every "why" — Aristotle's master tool of explanation (Q-D). |
| **Substance (*ousia*)** | The primary kind of being; the protagonist — "what is being?" reduces to "what is substance?" (Q-J). |
| **Potentiality & actuality** (*dynamis* / *energeia*) | The pair that explains all change — matter passing from what-it-could-be to what-it-is; actuality is prior (Q-K, Q-L). |
| **The unmoved mover** | The culmination — the eternal, immaterial, pure-actuality first cause that moves the world *as the object of love*, and is *thought thinking itself* (Q-O, Q-Q). |

### (b) Famous bits — with Bekker loci + hooks
1. **"All men by nature desire to know."** (I.1 980a, Q-A) — the most famous opening in
   philosophy; the hook is that he *proves* it from something as ordinary as our love of looking.
2. **The four causes** (material/formal/efficient/final). (I.3 983a, Q-D) — hook: the one little
   word "why?" is secretly four questions, and you haven't explained anything until you've
   answered all four (esp. *what it's for*).
3. **Being qua being** — "a science which investigates being as being." (IV.1 1003a, Q-G) —
   hook: every other science studies a *slice* of reality; this one studies *existence itself*.
4. **"What is being?" = "what is substance?"** (VII.1 1028b, Q-J) — hook: the biggest question
   there is, the one "raised of old and now and always," turns out to be a question about *which
   things are the real ones*.
5. **The unmoved mover — "moves as being loved" / "thought thinking itself."** (XII.7 1072b,
   Q-O / XII.9 1074b, Q-Q) — hook: the thing that makes the whole universe move never moves; it
   moves everything the way a beloved draws you across a room, and all it does, forever, is think
   about thinking.

moves the cosmos by being loved and spends eternity thinking about thinking.**

## 7. One-paragraph summary of the arc + verification cautions

**The arc.** The *Metaphysics* (Greek *ta meta ta physika*, "the [books] after the *Physics*" —
an editor's shelving label, NOT "beyond the physical," and not Aristotle's own title: he calls
the inquiry "first philosophy") is **not one composed book** but **fourteen treatises**
assembled by a later editor, almost certainly Andronicus of Rhodes around 70 BCE. They circle a
single question. Book I opens with the most famous line in philosophy — "All men by nature desire
to know" — climbs from sensation to wisdom (knowledge of first causes), lays out the **four
causes** (material, formal, efficient, final), and dismantles Plato's Forms as causally idle
"empty words and poetical metaphors." Book IV names the science: it studies **being qua being**,
being *as such*, and rests on the firmest of all principles, **non-contradiction**. Books
VII–VIII drive to the heart — "what is being?" just *is* the question "what is **substance**?" —
and answer that the **form/essence** of the individual, not bare matter and not a separate
Platonic universal, is substance in the primary sense. Book IX explains all change as
**potentiality** becoming **actuality**, and proves actuality is *prior* — which forces the
treatise's summit. Book XII (Lambda) reaches the **unmoved mover**: an eternal, immaterial, pure
actuality that moves the whole cosmos **not by pushing but "as being loved"** (as the final cause
all things desire), on which "depend the heavens and the world of nature," and whose sole eternal
activity is **thought thinking itself** ("a thinking on thinking") — a divine principle, but
emphatically *not* the personal creator-God that Aquinas would later fuse it with. Books XIII–XIV
close as Book I began, refuting the Platonists' separate numbers and Forms.

**Verification cautions.** Every quotation in §2/§6 was fetched today from W. D. Ross's 1908
translation (Internet Classics Archive) and matched word-for-word — all are `VERIFIED`, all PD
(pre-1929). The cautions: (1) the Greek tags (*ousia*, *dynamis/energeia*, *noēsis noēseōs*,
*to on hēi on*) are `PARAPHRASE-ONLY` — Ross uses English, so gloss them, never quote the Greek
as Ross (§5.1); (2) the bronze-**statue** four-causes example is from the *Physics* II.3, not

says **"being as being"** — the popular "being qua being" is the standard tag, not a Ross
quotation (Trap — qua); (4) the dense substance argument (Books VII–VIII candidates) is recorded
as summary — fetch before quoting any candidate-by-candidate reasoning (§5.4); (5) Books II, III,
V, VI, X, XI, XIII–XIV are `PARAPHRASE-ONLY` (no signature quote fetched) — safe as paraphrase,
