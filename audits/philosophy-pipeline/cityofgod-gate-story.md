## What works (keep — do not sand these down)

- **The opening lands the stakes cleanly.** "On 24 August 410 the unthinkable happened" → the eight-hundred-year fact → the accusation that "wrote itself." Real stakes, no manufactured drama. This is exactly the bar's mode (Republic's "a question that sounds like dinner-party small talk and turns out to have no floor under it").
- **The through-line is stated, tracked, and protected.** "two *loves* build two cities, tangled until the end" is set in the throughline, restated in the hook's closing rule, hammered in chapters 4, 5, 7, and 8, and the not-Church-vs-State guardrail is repeated at every place a reader could go wrong. The single most important interpretive claim of the book is never left implicit. This is the draft's biggest strength.
- **The set-pieces are all landed and in order:** the sack + pagan charge (ch1), "great robberies" + the pirate/Alexander retort (ch2), the Platonists "approach nearest" + the Incarnation limit (ch3), the two loves XIV.28 (ch4), Cain's city + *permixtae* (ch5), "tranquillity of order" + the peace-ladder + the Cicero/res publica deflation (ch6), Last Judgment + the two endings (ch7), the afterlife/irony (ch8). Nothing famous is dropped or left as a bare quotation; each is shown as "a move inside that one long answer," which the hook promises and delivers.
- **The demolition/construction symmetry (10 books + 12 books) is set up in the hook and paid off structurally** — the reader always knows where they are in the 22-book arc.
- **The Lucretia / rape-and-suicide passage (ch1 block 5)** is handled with care and turned into the book's load-bearing point (virtue is interior, in the will), which is genuinely good narrative economy.
- **The ch8 irony** ("the book that said 'Rome is not the City of God' outlasted Rome by more than a thousand years") is a real, earned closing beat, not a manufactured one.
- Pronunciation glosses ARE present on most hard names: Alaric, Marcellinus, libido dominandi, Varro, permixtae, summum bonum. This is the habit the sibling Plato gates kept flagging as missing; here it is mostly done.

## BLOCKERS

### B1. Missing pronunciation gloss: **Visigoths** — and the term is never inline-defined on first use
**Location:** hook[0] ("plundered for three days by the Visigoths, a Germanic people…"). Actually the Visigoths ARE glossed-by-meaning here ("a Germanic people who had pushed into the empire"). The gloss-by-meaning is fine. BUT the prompt flags Visigoths for a *pronunciation* gloss specifically, and the bar (Republic/Augustine) gives phonetic glosses on hard proper names. "Visigoths" (VIZ-ih-goths) is a name a zero-knowledge reader may not know how to say. Add the phonetic on first use in hook[0].
**Rewrite:** "…plundered for three days by the Visigoths (VIZ-ih-goths), a Germanic people who had pushed into the empire…"
(Severity is BLOCKER only because the sibling gates made hard-name pronunciation glosses a standing ship-block against this bar; the meaning-gloss itself is already present and good.)

### B2. **caritas / cupiditas are never named** — the prompt's named term pair, and the Latin the two loves actually rest on, is absent
**Location:** chapter 4 (the two-loves chapter) and chapter 3/5 where disordered vs. ordered love is discussed.
**Problem:** The draft defines the two loves beautifully in English ("love of self even to contempt of God" vs. "love of God even to contempt of self") but never gives the reader Augustine's own paired terms **caritas** (rightly-ordered love, love of God) and **cupiditas** (disordered love, grasping love of self/world). The prompt explicitly lists "caritas/cupiditas" among the terms to define-on-first-use AND among the hard names needing a pronunciation gloss. The Augustine thinker-read leans hard on "disordered love" as the engine of the whole system; the work-read should name the technical pair at least once so the reader meets the vocabulary the tradition uses. Right now a reader who encounters "caritas/cupiditas" anywhere else has never been introduced to it here.
**Rewrite (add to ch4, after the XIV.28 definition is stated, e.g. end of block 4 or in block 6):** "Augustine has names for the two loves: **caritas** (KAR-ih-tahs), rightly-ordered love that reaches out to God and loves everything else in its proper place, and **cupiditas** (koo-PID-ih-tahs), grasping love that curves back on the self and loves lower things out of order. The whole book is, underneath, a contest between those two loves." (Keep it once; do not over-gloss.)
**Note for the fact gate:** confirm Dods/Augustine's own usage of caritas/cupiditas as the standard pairing before it ships; the storytelling case for naming them is what this gate asserts.

### B3. **"Arian" is dropped undefined into chapter 1** — a zero-knowledge reader stalls
**Location:** ch1, block 4: "The Visigoths, Arian Christians themselves, spared those who fled to them…"
**Problem:** "Arian" is a loaded technical term (a Christian who held the non-Nicene view that the Son is subordinate to the Father) with zero prior introduction. A reader with no background reads "Arian Christians" and has no idea what distinguishes them or why it matters that the sackers were Christians at all. The draft's own standard (every term inline-defined on first use) is violated here, and it's a spot a newcomer gets lost.
**Rewrite:** Either (a) inline-define: "The Visigoths, who were themselves Christians (of the Arian kind — a strand of Christianity, later judged heretical, that ranked Christ below God the Father), spared those who fled to them…" OR (b) if the Arian detail isn't load-bearing for the point (which is just *that the sackers were Christians and so spared the churches*), simplify to "The Visigoths, who were Christians themselves, spared those who fled to them, Christian and pagan alike." Option (b) is cleaner and loses nothing the chapter needs.

## POLISH

### P1. Mild second-person imperative / "reader-coaching" residue — trim toward the plain-voice rule
The plain-voice memory's #1 offense is commanding the reader. The draft is mostly clean, but a few imperatives address the reader directly and could be flattened to plain narration:
- ch1 block 1: **"Start where Augustine starts, with the wound."** and **"To grasp what that meant you have to feel how long it had been coming…"** — "you have to feel" is the coached-reaction move the memory flags ("Feel the pull of that before you reject it"). Recommend: "It begins where Augustine begins, with the wound." and "What that meant only registers against how long it had been coming and how impossible it had seemed…"
- ch2 block 1: "His first line is the plainest" — fine, but borderline meta.
- ch2 block 6 / ch3 block 5: **"Quote it for its edge, but it is a knife aimed at unjust power"** and **"That is the exact thing to keep straight"** — these are mild "here's the thing people get wrong" framings. They're defensible because the not-Church-vs-State guardrail genuinely needs flagging, and the bar (Republic ch6 "Here is where almost everyone gets the Cave wrong") does exactly this. Keep the *content*, but consider dropping the imperative wrapper ("Quote it for its edge" → just state it). Low priority; the bar tolerates this register.
These are POLISH, not blockers — the draft is far cleaner on this axis than the pre-cleanup Greek-era reads the memory was written against.

### P2. "res publica" gets a meaning-gloss but no pronunciation; "civitas" likewise
**Location:** ch6 block 5 ("a *res publica*, a 'public thing,' a real political community"); ch4 break / brk ("*civitas* in his Latin").
**Problem:** Both are inline-defined by meaning (good), but neither carries a phonetic. They're easier than the names that did get glossed, so this is genuinely optional — flagging only because the bar is consistent about phonetics on Latin. If you gloss caritas/cupiditas per B2, match the treatment: a phonetic on civitas (KIH-wih-tahs / SIV-ih-tas) would be consistent. Low priority.

### P3. The Descartes aside in chapter 4 (block 2, *si fallor sum*) is good but slightly interrupts the cosmic-history flow
**Location:** ch4 block 2.
**Problem:** It's accurate and the "twelve hundred years forward to Descartes… though Augustine's purpose is his own, not a foreshadowing of anyone" caveat is exactly right (matches the Augustine thinker-read's careful "the influence is not claimed"). But it lands between the angels-fall and the human-Fall, two beats of the two-cities origin story, and momentarily pulls the reader out of the narrative spine. Consider whether it earns its place here or reads as a detour. The thinker-read already carries this parallel at length, so the work-read could compress it to a single sentence. Not blocking — it's a real and famous connection — but tighten.

### P4. Chapter 7 block 2 (eternal punishment) risks tipping toward sermon-flat
**Location:** ch7 block 2 ("Book XXI is the dark one").
**Problem:** This is the one stretch that comes closest to treatise-dry / sermon-stiff, because it summarizes a whole book of theological defense without a concrete hook to carry it. It's not a story beat; it's a précis. It's serviceable and honest ("Augustine does not soften it"), but it's the flattest paragraph in the read. Consider one concrete detail — what specific objection Augustine answered (e.g. the pagan claim that no body could burn forever, or that endless punishment for finite sin is disproportionate) — to turn the précis into an argument the reader can hold. Polish, not blocking.

### P5. "the *martyrs* being Christians killed for their faith…" — slightly over-glossed mid-sentence
**Location:** ch1 block 4.
**Problem:** The parenthetical inline-define of "martyrs" is correct doctrine (define on first use), but "martyr" is arguably within a smart zero-knowledge reader's vocabulary, and the gloss is set off with em-dash-style parenthetical stacking inside an already-long sentence. The plain-voice rule warns against over-glossing the obvious. Judgment call — if the bar's "zero prior knowledge" floor is taken strictly, keep it; if "martyr" counts as known, cut the parenthetical for flow. Lean toward keeping a *short* gloss ("the shrines of the martyrs — Christians killed for their faith, whose graves were treated as holy") since the reader floor is genuinely zero. Low priority.

Flagging only so the fact/comprehensiveness coordinator can confirm the omission is intentional and not a gap.
