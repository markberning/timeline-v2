## A. Biography (CONFIRMED — verified by research subagent vs SEP / Britannica / Wikipedia)
- Born **5 May 1818, Trier** (then in the Prussian Rhine Province / Rhineland). Died
  **14 March 1883, London**. — CONFIRMED.
- Father **Heinrich Marx**, a lawyer (advocate) of Ashkenazi Jewish descent, descended
  from a line of rabbis (his own father was rabbi of Trier; his brother became Trier's
  chief rabbi); **converted to Lutheranism** (the Prussian state church) for
  professional reasons after Prussian law barred Jews from the legal profession.
  (Conversion best dated 1816/1817; Karl himself baptized 1824 — the draft states only
  that Heinrich converted "in order to keep practicing law," no year asserted.) —
  CONFIRMED.
- Studied at **Bonn** then the **University of Berlin**; fell in with the **Young
  Hegelians**. Doctorate **1841, University of Jena**; dissertation *On the Difference
  Between the Democritean and Epicurean Philosophy of Nature* (the draft says
  "a dissertation on the natural philosophy of two ancient Greek atomists, Democritus
  and Epicurus"). — CONFIRMED.
- Editor of the **Rheinische Zeitung**, a liberal **Cologne** newspaper, **~1842–1843**;
  **suppressed by the Prussian censors** (banned early 1843). — CONFIRMED.
- Married **Jenny von Westphalen** (daughter of a Prussian baron, his childhood
  sweetheart) in **1843**. — CONFIRMED.
- Moved to **Paris (1843)**; met **Friedrich Engels there in 1844** (brief prior meeting
  late 1842); began the lifelong collaboration. **Expelled from Paris early 1845**, moved
  to **Brussels**. — CONFIRMED.
- **Engels**: son of a wealthy textile manufacturer; worked in / managed the family firm
  **Ermen & Engels in Manchester**, using the income to **support Marx financially for
  decades**; wrote ***The Condition of the Working Class in England* (1845)**. — CONFIRMED.
- Co-authored: ***The Holy Family* (1845)**; ***The German Ideology* (written 1845–46,
  unpublished in their lifetime**, first published in full 1932); ***The Communist
  Manifesto*** (commissioned by the **Communist League**, published **February 1848**, in
  German, in London). The "gnawing/gnawing criticism of the mice" remark about abandoning
  the German Ideology manuscript is Marx's own (1859 Preface), given as his remark. —
  CONFIRMED.
- After the **1848 revolutions failed**, Marx expelled from the continent; settled in
  **London in 1849** for the rest of his life, **often in genuine poverty** (Soho
  lodgings; kept afloat by Engels, journalism, pawnshops). **Several children died young**
  — the standard count is three at 28 Dean Street: **Guido (1850), Franziska (1852),
  Edgar/"Musch" (1855, age 8)** (a fourth baby died at birth in 1857). The draft says
  "several of the Marx children died young, of illnesses that better food and warmer rooms
  might have prevented" — no specific count asserted. — CONFIRMED.
- Did much of his research in the **Reading Room of the British Museum**. — CONFIRMED.
- ***Capital* (*Das Kapital*) Vol. I** published **1867** in German; **Vols. II (1885)
  and III (1894) edited and published by Engels** after Marx's death from Marx's
  manuscripts. — CONFIRMED.
- **Buried in Highgate Cemetery, London**; **Engels gave the graveside speech**
  (17 March 1883), claiming for Marx the law of development of human history and the
  inner workings of the capitalist mode of production. (The draft paraphrases the eulogy,
  no direct quote.) — CONFIRMED.

## D. PARAPHRASED-ONLY content (NO quotation marks anywhere in the draft)
- **1844 Manuscripts — the four alienations** (ch. 3): product / act of laboring /
  species-being / other people. All four are standard readings of the "Estranged Labour"
  section. Specific paraphrased claims verified against the text:
  - "The worker becomes all the poorer the more wealth he produces" → paraphrased as
    the more he produces, the poorer he becomes. (Milligan text; in copyright → paraphrase.)
  - the product "confronts it as something alien, as a power independent of the producer"
    → paraphrased. species-being = free, conscious, creative production as the human
    essence, degraded by alienated labor into a mere means of survival → standard reading,
    paraphrased. The draft explicitly states it is paraphrasing because no PD translation
    exists. — CONFIRMED standard; NO quotation marks used. NO [VERIFY] (paraphrase).
- **The German Ideology — "ruling ideas"** (ch. 5): paraphrased ("The class which has the
  means of material production at its disposal has control at the same time over the means
  of mental production…"). The draft gives the QUOTED form only from the **Manifesto**
  (Moore 1888, item 2), and paraphrases the German Ideology version with no quote marks,
  explicitly flagging that no PD English exists. — CONFIRMED standard; NO quotation marks.
- **"Religion is the opium of the people"** (ch. 5): from Marx's *Contribution to the
  Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right. Introduction* (1843–44, pub. 1844). The fuller
  passage ("the sigh of the oppressed creature, the heart of a heartless world… the soul
  of soulless conditions… the opium of the people") is given in PARAPHRASE / reported
  form, NOT as a pinned-translator quotation, because the standard English (e.g. O'Malley)
  is 20th-c. The famous five-word phrase is treated as the well-known English rendering of
  *Opium des Volkes*, given without quotation marks around a pinned translator. The
  contextualization (opium = the era's painkiller; a true expression of real misery that
  also consoles) is the standard scholarly reading. — CONFIRMED as the famous line;
  handled as the well-known rendering, contextualized, NOT mis-wielded as a flat sneer.

## E. Doctrine / interpretation (CONFIRMED, taught — PHILOSOPHY altitude)
- **Inversion of Hegel** ("standing Hegel on his head" / found the dialectic "standing on
  its head," set it "on its feet"): Marx's own image, from the Afterword to the 2nd German
  edition of *Capital* (1873). Given as Marx's report/image, paraphrased — NOT quoted with
  a pinned PD translator. Dialectic KEPT, idealism DROPPED; material conditions drive
  ideas, not the reverse. — CONFIRMED standard (and the [Hegel](/philosophy/thinker/hegel)
  read carries the source side).
- **Historical materialism / base & superstructure / mode of production / forces &
  relations of production**: standard exposition. Base (forces + relations of production)
  shapes superstructure (law, state, morality, religion, philosophy); base "sets the
  terms" rather than mechanically dictating every detail (the non-vulgar reading, stated).
  — CONFIRMED standard.
- **Class struggle** as the motor; bourgeoisie (owning/capital-holding class) broke feudal
  aristocracy; proletariat (owns no means of production, lives by selling labor) defined
  plainly. — CONFIRMED standard.
- **Alienation** (four forms, ch. 3): structural, built into the wage relation, not into a
  cruel boss — stated as the strongest version. — CONFIRMED standard.
- **Labor theory of value / socially necessary labor time**: value = congealed
  socially-necessary human labor. Taken over from classical political economy (Smith,
  Ricardo) and put to Marx's use. Given at philosophical level, linked to
  [Capital](/philosophy/work/capital). — CONFIRMED standard.
- **Commodity fetishism**: the social relations between people (their labor) appear as a
  relation between things with value as a natural property; a real appearance generated by
  a real arrangement (private producers connecting only through exchange), not a head-error
  dispelled by argument. — CONFIRMED standard (and quoted, items 3–5).
- **Ideology** ("the ruling ideas are the ideas of the ruling class"): the dominant class
  controls mental production; ideas serve interests while presenting as neutral common
  sense; depends on the ideas being SINCERELY held, NOT a conscious-conspiracy theory —
  the strongest version is stated this way. — CONFIRMED standard.
- **11th Thesis on Feuerbach** as the hinge: philosophy's task is to CHANGE the world, not
  only interpret it; a genuinely philosophical claim about what thought is for. — CONFIRMED.
- **Communism** held as a PHILOSOPHICAL idea (abolition of the owner/worker division, of
  private ownership of the means of production, and so of class struggle); Marx wrote
  little blueprint, mostly diagnosis. — CONFIRMED standard.
- **Engels** credited accurately: genuine intellectual collaborator (co-author of the
  Manifesto, Holy Family, German Ideology; editor of *Capital* II–III) AND chief financial
  supporter for decades AND self-effacing ("Marx the genius, Engels at best a talent" — his
  own line, paraphrased as "Marx was the genius and he the second fiddle"). — CONFIRMED.

## F. LANDMINE — "what was done in his name" (ch. 6), per the c19 brief
Handled two-sided and hedged to documented EFFECT, never collapsing the two:
1. The 20th-c. communist states genuinely CLAIMED Marx, used his vocabulary, read
   themselves as his fulfillment — the connection is real, not waved away.
2. The specific apparatus (vanguard party seizing power, one-party dictatorship, secret
   police, terror, the planned economy of an agrarian empire) was the work of LATER men
   (Lenin and after) in conditions Marx did not live to see, did not describe, and in
   several respects argued AGAINST — he expected revolution in the most ADVANCED
   INDUSTRIAL societies via an already-formed working class, NOT a peasant empire seized
   by a small party. — CONFIRMED standard scholarship. Lenin/Stalin named only as the
   later authors of the institutions, NOT read back into Marx's own philosophy. No claim
   about anyone's inner intent beyond documented program.
