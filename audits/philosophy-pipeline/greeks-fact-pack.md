# Fact pack — "The Greeks" (Philosophy era 1, kind = ERA)

**Scope:** Western philosophy from Thales (~585 BC) through the Hellenistic schools,
ending on the Rome/late-antiquity handoff (Justinian 529 AD → era 2).
**Built:** 2026-06-12, per `audits/philosophy-content-pipeline.md`. Every quotation
below was verified word-for-word against the linked text on this date (fetched, not
recalled); every image URL returned HTTP 200 on this date. The author writes ONLY
from this pack. Era altitude: sweep + throughline + a few signature ideas taught
vividly — do NOT pre-spend the Plato/Aristotle/Socrates deep reads.

**Status tags used throughout:**
- `VERIFIED` — exact wording confirmed against the linked source, translation named.
- `PARAPHRASE-ONLY` — idea is real, no verbatim PD English wording verified; ship as paraphrase, never in quote marks.
- `DOCUMENTED` — anecdote/fact with an ancient source close to the event (named).
- `LATE-TRADITION` — earliest source centuries later (named, gap stated); usable if framed as tradition.
- `LATE-LEGEND (DL)` — sourced only to Diogenes Laertius (3rd c. AD, i.e. 500–800 years after most of these lives); the standing legend-vector flag from the pipeline doc. Frame as "by Diogenes Laertius's much-later account" or equivalent.
- `APOCRYPHAL` — do not use except explicitly AS a debunk.

**A note on DK numbers:** pre-Socratic fragments are cited by Diels–Kranz ("DK B12")
per universal convention; the English wording cited here is verified against Burnet,
*Early Greek Philosophy* (1892, PD) on Wikisource. The DK numbers themselves are the
standard mapping — fact-checker should spot-confirm any DK number that ends up in
shipped prose.

## 0. Chapter-arc assessment (requested flag)

The proposed 7-chapter arc holds. Material argues for these adjustments, none structural:

1. **Pythagoras has no home in the proposed cuts.** Recommend a beat in **ch2**
   (the Italian wing: Pythagorean number-mysticism is the milieu Parmenides emerges
   from, and the soul/mathematics thread feeds Plato directly) — or a closing beat
   of ch1. He should not be skipped: the Plato chapter's Forms land better with the
   "mathematics as the really real" seed planted. Treat his life as almost entirely
   LATE-LEGEND (he wrote nothing; the miracle stories are centuries later).
2. **Ch2 needs the pluralist bridge** — Empedocles (four roots, Love/Strife) and
   Anaxagoras (Mind; "sun is a stone" — which detonates in Socrates' trial, a
   cross-chapter payoff) between Parmenides and the atomists. Material below.
3. **Ch6 should carry Chrysippus** (the Stoa's systematizer; also the era's best
   death legend) and the **Crates → Zeno of Citium bridge** (Cynicism is Stoicism's
   parent — the influence chain needs it). **Hipparchia** (woman Cynic, with her own
   chapter in DL — VI.96, verified below) is the framing gate's women-dropped-by-
   the-canon item for this era.
4. **Ch7 must include Lucretius** (Epicureanism survives at Rome as a poem) and
   frame **Cicero as transmitter/translator** (he coins Latin philosophical
   vocabulary), ending on the 529 closure + Boethius/Arabic-transmission teaser that
   era 2 ("Faith meets reason") picks up. The 529 "closure" itself must be told with
   the modern qualification (below) — the dramatic "lights out on ancient
   philosophy" version is itself a potted summary.
5. **Western-overclaim guard (framing gate):** the era's opening must say *Western*
   philosophy begins in Ionia — not "philosophy begins." Indian and Chinese
   traditions are older-or-contemporary and deferred by scope, not by rank.

## 2. Position summaries (with potted-summary traps pre-flagged)

Each summary is the *steelmanned* documented position. ⚠️ = the inherited caricature
the author must either avoid or explicitly frame AS caricature.

**Milesians (Thales, Anaximander, Anaximenes)** — the question: what is the world
made of, and how does it work, asked WITHOUT gods-as-explanation. Thales: the
principle (*archê*) is **water**; the earth **rests on water** like a raft —
source: Aristotle, *Metaphysics* I.3 (verified quote §3). Anaximander: the source
is the **apeiron** ("the boundless") — an indefinite stuff prior to all the
opposites; things "make reparation … to one another for their injustice according
to the ordering of time" (the oldest surviving sentence-fragment of Western
philosophy — verified §3; even its exact verbatim extent is debated — say "the
oldest surviving words"). Anaximenes: **air**, with an actual *mechanism* —
condensation and rarefaction (air thins to fire, thickens to wind→cloud→water→
earth→stone). The Milesian throughline for the break block: not their answers
(wrong) but the MOVE — natural stuff + natural process, criticizable and
improvable. ⚠️ Trap: "Thales the primitive who thought everything was literally
water" — Aristotle's report is already an interpretation; we have zero words of
Thales himself (say so — the pipeline doc requires flagging fragments-and-hostile-
reports survival).

**Pythagoras / Pythagoreans** — number as the deep structure of reality (harmony
ratios, the mathematization of the cosmos); transmigration of souls; a religious
community with rules, not just a theory. ⚠️ Trap 1: "Pythagoras proved the
Pythagorean theorem" — the attribution is conventional; the relationship was known
to Babylonian mathematics long before, and no proof by Pythagoras is documented.
⚠️ Trap 2: presenting the cult rules (beans etc.) as the whole story.

**Heraclitus** — everything is in flux, BUT ordered by **logos** (a rational
structure running through change); unity of opposites (the road up and the road
down are one); fire as emblem-stuff. SEP's correction (verified): the river
fragment's authentic point (DK B12) is subtler than "everything is chaos" — the
river stays THE SAME river *because* ever-different waters flow through it;
identity persisting through change, not change destroying identity. ⚠️ Trap: the
exact "you cannot step in the same river twice" wording is contested — of the
three river fragments (B12, B49a, B91), SEP reports only **B12** as genuinely
Heraclitean wording; the famous one-liner descends from Plato's paraphrase
(*Cratylus* 402a, verified §3 — where Jowett's Plato says "the same **water**
twice"). The pack's quotable VERIFIED translations are Burnet's (§3) — use with
the frame "as the fragments come down to us." ⚠️ Trap 2: "panta rhei / everything
flows" as a verbatim Heraclitus quote — it's the later tradition's slogan (see §9).

**Parmenides** — the era's great roadblock: what-is cannot come from what-is-not;
change and plurality, thought through rigorously, are contradictory; therefore
reality is one, unchanging, and the senses deceive. Delivered as a goddess-
revelation poem in two parts (Way of Truth / Way of Belief — note the irony: the
arch-rationalist wrote revelation verse). The "It is" line verified §3. Whether he
was answering Heraclitus *by name* is scholarly-contested — frame as "the tradition
pairs them," not "Parmenides read Heraclitus and replied."

**Zeno of Elea** — defended Parmenides by paradox: if motion/plurality are real,
absurdities follow. Four motion arguments preserved **by Aristotle** (*Physics*
VI.9, verified §3): the dichotomy, the **Achilles** (the quickest can never
overtake the slowest), the arrow, the stadium. Teach the Achilles as the worked
example — it's the era's best "feel the pull" argument. ⚠️ Trap: Zeno survives
only through his critic — Aristotle quotes him in order to refute him (say so).

**Empedocles** — four "roots" (earth, air, fire, water — his own names are divine:
"shining Zeus, life-bringing Hera, Aidoneus and Nestis," verified §3) mixed and
unmixed by two forces, **Love and Strife**. Compromise with Parmenides: the roots
themselves never come-to-be or perish; only mixtures change.

**Anaxagoras** — everything-in-everything; **Nous** (Mind) starts the cosmic
rotation and orders it; sun = a fiery/red-hot stone, moon = earth (the claim that
got him prosecuted in Athens, and which surfaces verbatim at Socrates' trial —
verified §3, Apology). Note for influence chain: Socrates (in *Phaedo*) tells of
his disappointment that Anaxagoras' Mind did no explanatory work — bridge to the
teleology hunger Plato/Aristotle answer (the *Phaedo* disappointment passage is
NOT verified in this pack — PARAPHRASE-ONLY unless fact-checker pins it).

**Leucippus & Democritus (atomists)** — the attempted resolution of ch2's problem:
concede to Parmenides that true being neither arises nor perishes; save the
appearances by making being PLURAL — unsplittable **atoms** (*atomos* = "uncuttable")
moving in **void**. All qualitative change = rearrangement. The epistemic kicker,
verified §3: "by convention sweet… but in reality atoms and void" (DK 68B9). ⚠️
Trap: do NOT present this as modern atomic theory anticipated — it's a metaphysical
solution, not an experimental one; the resemblance is the story's delight, not an
identity. ⚠️ "Laughing philosopher" = later Roman tag (caption-relevant, see §7).

**The Sophists** — STEELMAN REQUIRED (framing gate). What they actually were (SEP,
verified): professional educators selling higher education — rhetoric, civic
skill, "how best to run one's household and the city's affairs" — to a democracy
where speaking well WAS political power; no shared doctrine, no school, so blanket
condemnation is inaccurate by construction. Protagoras taught deliberation and
citizenship; Hippias taught astronomy/geometry/history; Prodicus, linguistic
precision. The "charlatan" picture descends overwhelmingly from **Plato**, who
needed to distinguish Socrates from them (SEP: "underrated by philosophers going
all the way back to Plato"). Their two scandal-doctrines, both verified §3:
Protagoras' measure doctrine ("Man is the measure of all things") and his
theological agnosticism ("As to the gods, I have no means of knowing…"). Gorgias'
*On Non-Being* (nothing exists; if it did we couldn't know it; if we could we
couldn't communicate it) survives only in later summaries — **PARAPHRASE-ONLY**,
and possibly a rhetorical showpiece rather than sincere doctrine (frame with that
possibility). ⚠️ Trap: "Sophist = swindler." The modern smear-sense of "sophistry"
is the residue of Plato's polemic — name this openly; it's a great framing beat.

**Socrates** — wrote nothing; method, not doctrine: the *elenchus* — question,
elicit a definition, find the counterexample, repeat; wisdom begins in knowing
what you don't know (his actual formulation, verified §3: "I neither know nor
think that I know" — NOT the bumper-sticker, see §9). Mission framed as divine
(the Delphi oracle story + the gadfly image, both verified §3). The turn from
nature to human questions: ethics, definition, how to live. ⚠️ Trap 1: the
"Socratic problem" — Plato's, Xenophon's, and Aristophanes' Socrateses disagree
(SEP, verified); the pipeline doc makes telling this silence mandatory (his Key
passage block is `absent: true` — the substitute source is Plato's *Apology*).
⚠️ Trap 2: don't flatten the trial into "Athens kills free speech" — the charges
(impiety + corrupting the young, verified) sat inside the post–Peloponnesian-War,
post-Thirty-Tyrants trauma (Socrates' circle included Critias and Alcibiades);
honest framing names both the principle AND the politics.

**Plato** — the **Forms**: the sensible world is "in some way defective"; the real
and perfect realm is "populated by entities (called 'forms' or 'ideas') that are
eternal, changeless" — goodness, beauty, equality themselves (SEP's wording,
verified). The Forms reconcile ch2's deadlock: Heraclitean flux is true of the
*sensible*, Parmenidean changelessness true of the *intelligible*. The cave
(Republic VII, verified §3) is the era-read's set-piece — run it fully (pipeline
"make them GET it": worked example = the cave itself; plain restatement = "what if
the things you see are shadows of the real things?"). The Academy; philosopher-
kings line verified §3. ERA ALTITUDE: cave + Forms-in-brief + Academy; the deep
read gets the rest. ⚠️ Trap: "Plato thought this world is an illusion" — defective
copy ≠ illusion.

**Aristotle** — the systematizer: invents formal **logic** (the syllogism — frame
the "first" carefully, §8); **four causes** (material/formal/efficient/final) with
teleology — nature explained by what things are FOR; ethics of **habit** (virtue
as *hexis*, verified §3: "we become just by doing just acts"), the doctrine of the
mean, eudaimonia as activity-in-accordance-with-virtue over a whole life ("one
swallow does not make a summer," verified §3); the Lyceum; tutor of Alexander
(343 BC). ⚠️ Trap 1: *eudaimonia* ≠ modern "happiness" (feeling) — it's flourishing/
living-well (the pipeline names this anachronism explicitly). ⚠️ Trap 2:
posthumous-misuse honesty (framing gate): Aristotle's *Politics* defends natural
slavery — name it plainly, neither sanitized nor turned into a lecture. ⚠️ Trap 3:
the fake habit quote, §9.

**Cynics (Diogenes)** — not an "ism" with a syllabus but a way of life: virtue is
the only good, convention (*nomos*) is the enemy, train yourself (*askêsis*) to
need almost nothing; shamelessness as philosophical practice ("dog" — *kyôn* — was
the insult they adopted as the brand). Diogenes' one-liners ARE the doctrine
(anecdotes §4 — all LATE-LEGEND-flagged but explicitly usable as the tradition).
Cosmopolitanism's birth-line verified §3 ("I am a citizen of the world"). ⚠️ Trap:
modern lowercase "cynicism" (sneering distrust) is nearly the OPPOSITE of Cynic
moral seriousness — flag the word-drift; the newcomer critic will demand it anyway.

**Stoics (Zeno of Citium → Cleanthes → Chrysippus; Rome: Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus
Aurelius)** — live in agreement with nature/cosmic reason; **virtue is the only
true good** and is sufficient for happiness; externals (wealth, health, reputation)
are "indifferents"; the **dichotomy of control** (Epictetus' opening, verified §3)
is the practical engine; emotions are not brute weather but products of judgments
you can examine (SEP). ⚠️ Trap: "Stoic = emotionless statue / gritted teeth."
The school's claim is not "feel nothing" but "false judgments produce destructive
passions" — the sage isn't numb, he's un-deceived. Use the verified Epictetus and
Marcus lines, which say exactly this.

**Epicureans** — atomist physics in the service of tranquility: the gods don't
manage the world, death is disassembly ("Death … is nothing to us," verified §3),
and the goal is **ataraxia** + absence of bodily pain. His "pleasure" is explicitly
NOT the prodigal's (verified §3: "not an unbroken succession of drinking-bouts…");
the actual diet was bread-and-water austere — the man begged a friend for "a little
pot of cheese, that, when I like, I may fare sumptuously" (DL X.11, verified §3 —
LATE-LEGEND-flag the letter's provenance but it's quotable as DL's report). The
Garden admitted women and at least one slave (names in §1). ⚠️ Trap: "Epicurean =
gourmet glutton" — the inherited caricature is almost the inverse of the documented
position; the pipeline doc names this trap explicitly. The smear is ancient (rival
schools + later Christian polemic) — naming THAT is the story.

**Skeptics (Pyrrho; the skeptical Academy: Arcesilaus, Carneades)** — for every
argument an equal opposing argument; suspend judgment (*epochê*); tranquility
(*ataraxia*) follows — SEP's wording (verified): "they find themselves unable to
decide things, give up, and experience tranquility." Pyrrho wrote nothing and
practiced it as a life; Arcesilaus turned Plato's own Academy skeptical
(rediscovering the questioning Socrates); Carneades' 156/5 BC Rome embassy (§1)
is the set-piece anecdote. ⚠️ Trap: "Skeptic = believes nothing / lazy doubter" —
it's a *practice* with a therapeutic goal, and its weapon is the strongest argument
on BOTH sides.

**Rome / the long afterlife** — Cicero translates the Greek schools into Latin
(philosophy's survival kit for the Latin West); Lucretius versifies Epicurus;
Stoicism becomes the working philosophy of the Roman elite — Seneca (the
conflicted courtier), Epictetus (the freed slave), Marcus Aurelius (the emperor):
the same school spanning slave to emperor is the chapter's spine. Then the schools
fade into Neoplatonism, and in **AD 529** Justinian's measures end public pagan
teaching in Athens — BUT (verified via search, sources below): modern scholarship
questions the cinematic version — it is "not clear that Justinian 'closed'
anything" as a single act; the 6th-c. Athenian school's continuity with Plato's
Academy is doubtful; the seven philosophers' famous exile to Persia (per Agathias)
may have been short and they returned unharmed; private teaching persisted. Frame
529 as the traditional bookend WITH the qualification. Sources: [Wikipedia:
Damascius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascius) (traditional account + Agathias),
[Alan Cameron, "The Last Days of the Academy at Athens" (OUP chapter)](https://academic.oup.com/book/10522/chapter/158433894)
(the scholarly unraveling), [History for Atheists overview](https://historyforatheists.com/2023/08/the-closing-of-the-athenian-academy/).
Handoff teaser: Boethius (executed 524, *Consolation*) and the coming Arabic
transmission of Aristotle — era 2's opening material, name-drop only.

## 5. "Why this is a break" block material (ch1 / the era's break)

**BEFORE — explanation by myth (concrete, citable):**
- Earthquakes: Poseidon is the "Earth-shaker" (*ennosigaios/enosichthon* — his standing Homeric epithet); the sea-god's anger moves the ground. The epithet is pervasive in the *Iliad*/*Odyssey* — author may cite "Homer's standing epithet for Poseidon, 'Earth-shaker'" without a pinpoint line (epithet, not a single passage). PARAPHRASE-LEVEL claim, safe.
- Origins: Hesiod's *Theogony* explains the world's existence by divine genealogy — first Chaos, then Earth, then the begettings of gods; explanation = "who was born from whom." (Standard content; if the author wants a verbatim Theogony line, it must be fetched and verified first — NOT yet verified in this pack.)
- The structure of myth-explanation: a *who* (which god, what mood), answered by ritual; not a *what/how*, answered by argument.

**AFTER — the same phenomena, natural causes:**
- Thales: the earth floats on water like a raft — Aristotle, *Metaphysics* I.3 (VERIFIED §3) and *De Caelo* II.13 (294a28 — not separately verified; the Metaphysics quote suffices). The doxographic tradition (Seneca, *Naturales Quaestiones*) extends this to earthquakes — the floating earth ROCKS, and that rocking is the quake: same phenomenon as Poseidon's anger, zero gods. **The Seneca NQ attribution is standard but NOT verified in this pack — fact-checker must pin it (Sen. NQ III.14 / VI.6) before the earthquake pairing ships as Thales-specific.** Fallback that needs no further verification: keep the break on the *archê*/water + eclipse-as-natural-event material, all verified.
- The eclipse: in Homer an eclipse is an omen; in 585 BC Thales treats it as a regular, anticipatable natural event (Herodotus VERIFIED §3) — whatever his method, the CATEGORY moved from omen to mechanism.
- Anaximander: pushes past Thales with a non-observable theoretical entity (the *apeiron*) and law-like necessity ("according to the ordering of time," VERIFIED §3) — nature governed by something like cosmic law, expressed in the era's legal metaphor (penalty, reparation).
- The fragments-and-hostile-reports caveat (REQUIRED by the pipeline for this block): the pre-Socratics survive almost entirely as quotations and summaries in later authors — often their critics (Aristotle quotes Zeno to refute him; the Sophists come to us chiefly through Plato's polemic). Say this IN the break block.

**The gated "concrete move" sentence material:** the break is not better answers but a new *kind* of answer — natural stuff + natural process, stated so it can be criticized and improved; Thales' student disagreeing with Thales (Anaximander vs. water) is itself the new institution: a tradition of correction, not a tradition of recitation.

## 6. Influence-chain skeleton (answered / answered-by pairs)

Spine (each arrow = an explicit chip; consistency across future pages is gate 7's job):

1. **Homer/Hesiod (myth)** → answered by → **Thales & the Milesians** (natural causes).
2. **Thales** (water) → answered by → **Anaximander** (apeiron: water can't beget its own opposite) → answered by → **Anaximenes** (air + a mechanism: condensation/rarefaction).
3. **Milesians** → answered by → **Heraclitus** (not the stuff but the ORDER of its changing: logos, flux).
4. **Heraclitus** ↔ **Parmenides** (flux vs. the impossibility of change) — chip with care: direct reply is contested (§2); "the tradition pairs them."
5. **Parmenides** → defended by → **Zeno of Elea** (paradoxes) → answered by → the pluralists: **Empedocles** (four roots + Love/Strife), **Anaxagoras** (Mind), and decisively the **atomists Leucippus & Democritus** (being is plural and unchanging — atoms; change is rearrangement in void).
6. **Pythagoras** → (long arc) → **Plato** (mathematics as the gateway to the really real; soul doctrine).
7. **The Sophists (Protagoras, Gorgias)** → answered by → **Socrates** (definitions and the examined life vs. persuasion for hire — while sharing their turn to human questions; the chip should carry BOTH the opposition and the inheritance).
8. **Natural philosophy (Anaxagoras)** → answered by → **Socrates** (the turn: from what the cosmos is made of to how a person should live).
9. **Socrates** → answered by → **Plato** (the Forms; the Academy; the dialogues as monument) — and separately → **Antisthenes/the Cynic line**.
10. **Heraclitus + Parmenides** → synthesized by → **Plato** (flux is true of the sensible, changelessness of the intelligible).
11. **Plato** → answered by → **Aristotle** (forms brought down into things; four causes; logic; the Lyceum vs. the Academy).
12. **Socrates → Antisthenes → Diogenes (Cynics) → Crates → Zeno of Citium (Stoa)** — the school-to-school descent the lineage chips need; then **Zeno → Cleanthes → Chrysippus** (systematization).
13. **Democritus** → answered by → **Epicurus** (atomism repurposed: physics as therapy against fear of gods and death).
14. **Socrates' profession of ignorance + Pyrrho** → → **Arcesilaus** (the Academy turns skeptical) → **Carneades**.
15. **The Greek schools** → transmitted by → **Cicero (Latin translation), Lucretius (Epicurean epic)** → carried by → **Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius (Roman Stoa)**.
16. **The schools' fade** → **Neoplatonism (Plotinus — name-drop)** → **529 closure (qualified) + Boethius** → handoff chip to **Era 2: Augustine, the Arabic transmission (al-Fārābī, Avicenna, Averroes), Aquinas**.

## 9. APOCRYPHA BLACKLIST — famous fakes the author must NOT use

(Each is widely attributed online; none is in the source texts. If one is

4. **"Only the dead have seen the end of war." — NOT Plato.** George Santayana (1922); the misattribution spread via its use as an epigraph.
5. **"Strong minds discuss ideas, average minds discuss events, weak minds discuss people." — NOT Socrates** (nor Eleanor Roosevelt verbatim). No ancient source.
6. **"When the debate is lost, slander becomes the tool of the loser." — NOT Socrates.** No ancient source.
7. **"The secret of change is to focus all of your energy not on fighting the old, but on building the new." — NOT Socrates.** It's a line by the fictional character "Socrates" in Dan Millman's *Way of the Peaceful Warrior* (1980).
8. **"Everything flows" (*panta rhei*) as a verbatim Heraclitus quote** — the slogan comes from later summarizers (Simplicius; Plato's characterization in *Cratylus*, VERIFIED §3, is "all things are in motion and nothing at rest"); no surviving fragment says "panta rhei." Usable as "the slogan later attached to him."
9. **"You cannot step in the same river twice" presented as Heraclitus' exact sentence** — contested wording; see §2/§3 for how to use it honestly.
10. **"It is the mark of an educated mind to be able to entertain a thought without accepting it." — NOT Aristotle verbatim.** A loose modern paraphrase of NE I.3; never in quote marks.
11. **"The whole is greater than the sum of its parts." — NOT Aristotle verbatim** (a modern compression of a *Metaphysics* VIII.6 point).
12. **"Let no one ignorant of geometry enter"** as a documented Academy inscription — see §4: earliest testimony ~700 years late; tell it only AS late legend.
13. **"I am not an Athenian or a Greek, but a citizen of the world" — NOT Socrates in this wording.** The expanded version is via Plutarch's essay on exile paraphrasing Socrates; the verified citizen-of-the-world line belongs to Diogenes via DL (§3). Keep them straight.
14. **Marcus Aurelius: "The happiness of your life depends upon the quality of your thoughts."** — paraphrase hardened into quotation; not Long's text. Use the verified "The universe is transformation: life is opinion" (§3).
15. **Marcus Aurelius: "Everything we hear is an opinion, not a fact. Everything we see is a perspective, not the truth."** — not in the *Meditations*; a modern composite.
16. **The "Epicurean paradox" / riddle of evil ("Is God willing to prevent evil, but not able?…") as a verbatim Epicurus quote** — transmitted by the Christian writer Lactantius ~500 years later, attribution doubted by scholars (some trace the argument to the Skeptics). Usable only as "an argument later writers put in Epicurus' mouth."
17. **"Know thyself" — NOT Socrates' line.** A Delphic maxim, far older; DL I.40 (VERIFIED) already shows antiquity arguing over who said it first (Phemonoë? Chilon?). Socrates *invoked* it.
18. **Alexander: "If I were not Alexander, I would wish to be Diogenes"** — Plutarch's much-later embroidery on the sunlight scene; never present as part of the DL anecdote (§4).
