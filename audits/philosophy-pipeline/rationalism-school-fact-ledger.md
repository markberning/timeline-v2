# Fact ledger — "Rationalism" SCHOOL read (school id `rat`, kind = TRADITION)

The author's audit trail for the Rationalism school-level "the whole tradition" read,
the analog of the shipped Platonism school read. Written ONLY from the verified era-3
fact pack (`audits/philosophy-pipeline/rationalists-empiricists-fact-pack.md`, built +
verified 2026-06-12) and the era-4 Kant fact pack
(`audits/philosophy-pipeline/kant-germans-fact-pack.md`) for the closing
fusion/handoff. No new web research; every quote below is lifted from a `VERIFIED`
pack entry with its translation and sourceUrl carried over. Where the pack tags a
claim `PARAPHRASE-ONLY`, the read paraphrases and never sets it in quotation marks.

**Scope of THIS read vs the era read.** The era-3 read ("The rationalists and the
empiricists") tells the whole century-and-a-half as one argument across SIX minds
(Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Hobbes, Locke, Berkeley, Hume). THIS school read is
narrower and goes DEEPER on one side of that argument: rationalism as a *tradition* —
the three continental rationalists (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz), their shared
conviction, where they split, and the empiricist rivalry + Kantian fusion seen from
the rationalist side. Differentiation from the era read is the nesting-gate
requirement: the era read is the survey; this is the rationalist family portrait.

## Quote ledger — every quoted line, with source carried from the verified pack

All translations PD. Each verified word-for-word in the era-3 pack §3 on 2026-06-12.

### Descartes
- **The cogito** — *Discourse on the Method*, Part IV, trans. John Veitch (1850;
  pack cites Gutenberg #59): "I think, therefore I am (COGITO ERGO SUM), was so
  certain and of such evidence that no ground of doubt … could be alleged by the
  sceptics capable of shaking it, I concluded that I might … accept it as the first
  principle of the philosophy of which I was in search." VERIFIED (pack §3, ch1).
  **LOCUS DISCIPLINE (pack §9.1 + §2):** the Latin "cogito ergo sum" as an explicit
  axiom is the *Principles of Philosophy* (1644); the *Meditations* (1641) carries
  the argument as "I am, I exist," NOT the slogan; Veitch's Discourse adds the Latin
  parenthetically as his own gloss. The read attributes the slogan to the Discourse
  (Veitch) and notes the Meditations form is "I am, I exist."
- **The evil genius** — *Meditations*, Meditation I, trans. William Molyneux (1680;
  Gutenberg #70091): "some Evil Spirit which is very Powerful and crafty has used all
  his endeavours to deceive me." VERIFIED (pack §3). Read uses "evil genius" (standard
  PD English, Veitch) with the note that the name is translation-dependent; quotes
  Molyneux's "Evil Spirit" form. Paraphrased mostly to avoid archaic spelling.
- **The wax / "I am, I exist"** — Meditation II, Molyneux. VERIFIED (pack §3).
  Paraphrased (archaic spelling), not quoted at length.

### Spinoza
- **"God or Nature"** — *Ethics*, Part IV, Preface, trans. R. H. M. Elwes (1883;
  pack: Wikisource Elwes): "the eternal and infinite Being, which we call God or
  Nature, acts by the same necessity as that whereby it exists." VERIFIED (pack §3, ch2).
- **No substance besides God** — *Ethics* Part I, Prop. XIV, Elwes: "Besides God no
  substance can be granted or conceived." VERIFIED (pack §3).
- **Definition of God** — *Ethics* Part I, Def. VI, Elwes: "By God, I mean a being
  absolutely infinite—that is, a substance consisting in infinite attributes, of which
  each expresses eternal and infinite essentiality." VERIFIED (pack §3).
- **Sub specie aeternitatis** — *Ethics* Part V, Prop. XXIX, Elwes: "under the form
  of eternity." VERIFIED (pack §3). Read uses "under the form of eternity" (Elwes) +
  glosses the Latin.

### Leibniz
- **Monad = simple substance** — *Monadology* §1, standard PD rendering (pack credits
  "standard public-domain rendering"; the brief names Latta 1898 as the PD Monadology
  translation): "The monad … is nothing else than a simple substance … by simple, we
  mean without parts." VERIFIED (pack §3, ch3). Translation credited to Robert Latta
  (1898) in the read, the named PD Monadology translation.
- **No windows** — *Monadology* §7, Latta: "The monads have no windows through which
  anything may come in or go out." VERIFIED (pack §3).
- **Sufficient reason** — *Monadology* §32, Latta: "the principle of sufficient
  reason, in virtue of which we believe that no fact can be real or existing and no
  statement true unless it has a sufficient reason why it should be thus and not
  otherwise." VERIFIED (pack §3).
- **Living mirror** — *Monadology* §56, Latta: "a perpetual living mirror of the
  universe." VERIFIED (pack §3).
- **Pre-established harmony** — *Monadology* §78, Latta: "The soul follows its own
  laws, and the body likewise follows its own laws. They are fitted to each other in
  virtue of the preestablished harmony." VERIFIED (pack §3).
- **God's choice among possible worlds** — *Monadology* §53, Latta: "there are an
  infinity of possible universes in the ideas of God, and but one of them can exist,
  there must be a sufficient reason for the choice of God which determines him to
  select one rather than another." VERIFIED (pack §3).

### Kant (closing handoff only)
- **"Dogmatic slumber"** — *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics* (1783), Preface,
  trans. Paul Carus (1902; Kant pack §3, Gutenberg #52821): "I openly confess [that]
  the suggestion of David Hume was the very thing which many years ago first interrupted
  my dogmatic slumber, and gave my investigations in the field of speculative philosophy
  quite a new direction." VERIFIED (Kant pack §3). This is Kant's line ABOUT Kant; the
  read attributes it correctly and never puts it in Hume's mouth (pack §9.8 blacklist).

## Potted-summary / apocrypha traps honored (pack §2, §8, §9)
1. **"Descartes invented mind-body dualism"** — FALSE as stated (Plato, Augustine,
   the Scholastics held dualisms; pack §2 trap). Read says what is genuinely new:
   two complete substances, mechanistic framing, the acute interaction problem.
2. **Cogito locus** — slogan = Discourse(Veitch)/Principles, not the Meditations
   (pack §9.1). Honored above.
3. **"Spinoza was an atheist" / "the first pantheist"** — pack §2 trap 1 + §8.
   "Pantheist" is Toland's 1705 coinage, post-Spinoza, and contested. Read uses "what
   later thinkers would call pantheism" / describes him as identifying God with Nature,
   never asserts atheism.
4. **The 1656 herem cause** — UNKNOWN (pack §2 trap 2, §9.11). Read states the
   document names no specific grounds; invents no reason.
5. **"Best of all possible worlds" as a Leibniz quote** — pack §9.5. Honored above.
6. **Voltaire/Pangloss as Leibniz's actual view** — pack §2 trap. Read states the
   real theodicy first, then names *Candide* (1759, post-1755 Lisbon earthquake) as
   parody.
7. **"Hume woke Kant from his dogmatic slumber" as a Hume action / Hume's line** —
   pack §9.8. The line is Kant's, about Kant. Honored above.
8. **"Rationalism vs empiricism" as two sealed camps** — pack §0 + §5: the split is
   partly retrospective; it hardens with Kant. Read frames empiricism as the rival
   POLE (senses-first) that defines the era by opposition, and is honest that the camp
   labels solidified later. Hume reads Locke; the camps are a teaching frame.
9. **Leibniz independently invented calculus** — pack §8: well-documented; notation is
   his; the Newton priority dispute is real. Used only as a one-clause bio aside.

## Dates (pack §1) — all DOCUMENTED, no legend problem
- Descartes 31 Mar 1596 – 11 Feb 1650 (b. La Haye, Touraine; d. Stockholm, pneumonia).
- Spinoza 24 Nov 1632 – 21 Feb 1677 (b. Amsterdam; herem 27 Jul 1656; d. The Hague).
- Leibniz 1 Jul 1646 – 14 Nov 1716 (b. Leipzig; d. Hannover).
- Kant 1724–1804 (Kant pack); *Critique of Pure Reason* 1781; *Prolegomena* 1783.
- Works: Descartes *Discourse* 1637, *Meditations* 1641, *Principles* 1644, *Passions*
  1649. Spinoza *Ethics* completed by 1675, published posthumously 1677. Leibniz
  *Discourse on Metaphysics* 1686, *Theodicy* 1710 (only book published in his life),
  *Monadology* 1714 (posthumous).

## Lineage chips honored (pack §6)
- Descartes ← the Scholastics (replaces substance-and-form with extension-and-motion).
- Descartes → Spinoza (geometric method pushed past God-separate-from-Nature).
- Descartes → Leibniz (the mind-body problem answered with windowless monads +
  pre-established harmony).
- Descartes → Elisabeth (interaction objection, both directions).
- Spinoza ↔ Leibniz (1676 visit; monadology partly a pluralist answer to monism).
- Locke/Hume = the empiricist rival pole (senses-first), defining the era by contrast.
- Hume → Kant ("dogmatic slumber"), and Kant fuses the two poles (era-4 handoff).
- Malebranche → occasionalism as the rival Cartesian repair (pack §11.3), one mention.
