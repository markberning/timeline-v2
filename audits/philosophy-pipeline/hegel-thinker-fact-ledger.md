## A. Biography (CONFIRMED)
- Born **27 August 1770, Stuttgart** (Duchy of Württemberg). Died **14 November
  1831, Berlin**, during a **cholera epidemic** (the standard cause given; some
  later scholarship disputes cholera specifically, so framed as the standard
  account). — CONFIRMED (Britannica, SEP, Wikipedia)
- Father a minor revenue official at the Württemberg court. — CONFIRMED
- Studied at the **Tübinger Stift** (the Protestant seminary at Tübingen), where his
  roommates/contemporaries included the poet **Friedrich Hölderlin** and the younger
  **Friedrich Wilhelm Joseph Schelling**; the three were close. Tradition: as
  students they planted a "freedom tree" in enthusiasm for the French Revolution
  (flagged as tradition). — CONFIRMED (the friendship; the tree is the standard
  anecdote)
- After the Stift he worked as a **house tutor** (Hofmeister) in Bern (1793–96) and
  then Frankfurt (1797–1800), a poorly paid private-tutor stretch. — CONFIRMED
- Joined **Schelling at Jena** (1801); habilitated; lectured as Privatdozent then
  extraordinary professor. — CONFIRMED
- **Phenomenology of Spirit** finished at Jena in **October 1806** as Napoleon's
  army entered the town (Battle of Jena, 14 Oct 1806). Hegel reportedly sent off the
  last manuscript pages around the battle. — CONFIRMED (Wikipedia "Phenomenology of
  Spirit"; Pinkard biography)
- The **"world-soul on horseback" letter** to **Friedrich Niethammer**, dated **13
  October 1806**: Hegel describes seeing the Emperor (Napoleon) ride out of the city,
  calling him "this world-soul." Given as PARAPHRASE / the famous report (no pinned
  PD translator). — CONFIRMED as the famous letter; wording paraphrased.
- After Jena: editor of a newspaper in **Bamberg** (1807–08), then **rector and
  philosophy teacher at the Gymnasium in Nuremberg** (1808–16), where he married
  Marie von Tucher (1811) and wrote the **Science of Logic** (1812–16). — CONFIRMED
- Professor at **Heidelberg** (1816–18), then the chair at the **University of
  Berlin** (1818) which he held until death — his years of greatest fame and
  influence; the **Philosophy of Right** published **1820/1821**; the lectures later
  edited into the **Philosophy of History**, **Aesthetics**, **Philosophy of
  Religion**, **History of Philosophy**. — CONFIRMED
- Died **14 November 1831, Berlin**. Standard account: during a cholera epidemic.
  — CONFIRMED as the standard account.

## D. Doctrine / interpretation (CONFIRMED, taught)
- **Geist (Spirit)** = mind/spirit; the single developing subject whose
  self-knowledge is the content of reality; "substance is also subject." — standard.
- **Idealism** (define on first use): reality is mind-like / structured by thought;
  for Hegel specifically the self-unfolding of Spirit, with NO unknowable beyond.
- **Dialectic / Aufhebung (sublation)**: determinate negation that cancels +
  preserves + lifts up. German *aufheben* genuinely carries all three senses (cancel,
  keep, raise) — Hegel exploits this. — CONFIRMED standard reading.
- **Thesis–antithesis–synthesis**: NOT Hegel's. Triad of TERMS from **Fichte**;
  applied to Hegel as a formula by **Heinrich Moritz Chalybäus (1837)**; debunked by
  **Gustav E. Mueller, "The Hegel Legend of 'Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis'"** (Journal
  of the History of Ideas, 1958) and by **Walter Kaufmann**. Hegel used the words
  rarely and never as his method's engine. — CONFIRMED (per brief + standard
  scholarship).
- **Phenomenology** = the "ladder" / education of consciousness from sense-certainty
  up to Absolute Knowing; **Lordship and Bondage** (master/slave) is in the
  Self-Consciousness section. The bondsman gains through (a) the fear of death that
  shakes the whole self loose, and (b) formative work on the object. — CONFIRMED.
- **"The real is the rational"**: handled NOT as crude quietism. Hegel's *wirklich*
  (actual) ≠ everything that merely exists; what is genuinely actual is what has
  realized its rational essence. Standard scholarly correction (the *wirklich* /
  *Existenz* distinction Hegel himself draws in the Encyclopaedia). — CONFIRMED.
- **History as progress of the consciousness of freedom**; the **world-historical
  individual** (Caesar, Napoleon) as the unwitting instrument of Spirit; the **cunning
  of reason** uses their private passion to advance the universal. — CONFIRMED.
- **Relation to Kant**: Hegel dissolves the **thing-in-itself** (the noumenon Kant
  walled off as unknowable). If we can even mark a limit, Hegel argues, we are already
  past it; there is no unknowable beyond, only Spirit not yet at home with itself.
  — CONFIRMED standard reading.
- **Fichte/Schelling**: Fichte's self-positing I and Schelling's Absolute are way
  stations; Hegel's complaint that Schelling's Absolute is "the night in which all
  cows are black" is a famous jab (Preface to the Phenomenology, widely attributed,
  aimed at Schelling). Given as the famous jab, paraphrased. — CONFIRMED standard.
- **Forward**: **Marx's inversion** ("standing Hegel on his head" / the dialectic
  found "standing on its head") — Marx's own image in the Capital afterword; given as
  Marx's report, paraphrased. **Nietzsche** as later reaction against the system /
  against progress-of-Spirit history. — CONFIRMED standard.
