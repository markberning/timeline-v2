## Framing traps honored (from the verified pack §2)
- **"tabula rasa" is NOT Locke's phrase.** Locke wrote "white paper" (II.1.2) and "empty
  cabinet" (I.2.15). *Tabula rasa* is the ancient Latin tag (Aristotle's *De Anima* via
  Aquinas) applied TO Locke by later commentators. The read uses Locke's own words for the
  doctrine and introduces "tabula rasa" only AS the later label, glossed as the Latin tag.
- **Hobbes: "war of all against all" is retroactive.** The 1651 text says "every man,
  against every man." Honored.
- **Berkeley is not a crank.** He does NOT deny tables and trees exist; he denies
  mind-independent MATTER. Steelmanned, then LABELED idealism / immaterialism (the clarity
  gate's catch on the era-3 read). Honored: both labels stated explicitly.
- **"Hume was an atheist" — he never said so.** His positioning was deliberately ambiguous
  (Dialogues are dramatized; his own view implied, never stated). Framed as careful refusal,
  not cowardice. Honored.
- **The camp model is partly retrospective.** The empiricist label solidifies with Kant;
  Hume is reading Locke, not "answering the rationalists" across a settled divide. The read
  states the tradition as a real British 17th–18th-c. line while noting the rivalry organizes
  the era only in hindsight.
- **Honest warts (carried from era-3 r1):** the read does NOT relitigate Locke's
  Royal-African-Company / Carolina-slavery facts or Hume's 1753 racist footnote at school
  altitude (those live on the thinker pages); but it does not sanitize either man into a
  hero. At school altitude the focus is the doctrine; the warts are flagged as belonging to
  the deeper reads, not erased.
- **Berkeley's Bermuda college** — DOCUMENTED (era-3 pack §4); the failed scheme is a real,
  comic-sad fact, used lightly.

## New material verified fresh 2026-06-14 (Francis Bacon — the chapter-1 forerunner)
Bacon appears in NO existing fact pack, so his facts were verified fresh:
- **Dates 1561–1626** — SEP "Francis Bacon." CONFIRMED.
- **Works:** *The Advancement of Learning* (1605); *Novum Organum* (Oct 1620, part of the
  *Instauratio Magna*); *Essays* (1597, expanded 1625); *New Atlantis* (written ~1623,
  published posthumously 1626/1627). SEP. CONFIRMED.
- **Method:** rejected Aristotelian syllogism/deduction (general→particular); championed a
  systematic, eliminative INDUCTION rising from particulars to axioms "by a gradual and
  unbroken ascent" (*Novum Organum* I.19, Wikiquote verbatim). CONFIRMED.
- **The four Idols of the mind** (Tribe / Cave / Marketplace / Theatre) — *Novum Organum*
  Book I. SEP. CONFIRMED. Used as paraphrase (the four named, briefly glossed).
- **"Knowledge is power":** the popular slogan is a LATER compression. Bacon's nearest exact
  lines: *Meditationes Sacrae* (1597) Latin "Nam et ipsa scientia potestas est" = "knowledge
  itself is power"; and *Novum Organum* I.3 "Human knowledge and human power meet in one"
  (Wikiquote, no translator credited there — so the read uses the I.3 line as PARAPHRASE of
  the idea, NOT in quote marks, and frames "knowledge is power" as the later slogan). The
  I.19 two-ways line is the ONLY Bacon line set in quote marks, attributed to *Novum
  Organum* I.19. Wording confirmed against Wikiquote 2026-06-14.
- **Royal Society:** Bacon was NOT a member (he died 1626; the Society was founded 1660). The
  1660 founders hailed him as their inspiration; *New Atlantis*'s "Salomon's House" is the
  fictional research institute later read as a blueprint for the Royal Society and the
  Académie des Sciences. SEP + Wikipedia (New Atlantis / Salomon's House). Framed exactly so:
  forerunner and posthumous patron saint, never a member.
  Bacon sources: SEP https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/francis-bacon/ ;
  Wikiquote https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Francis_Bacon ;
  Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Atlantis , Salomon's House.

## Inline-defined terms (clarity-gate floor, every term glossed on first use)
empiricism (knowledge from experience); innate ideas (concepts the mind holds by its own
nature, not learned); tabula rasa (the Latin "blank slate" tag — Locke's own image was
"white paper"); sensation (the report of the outer world) and reflection (the mind observing
its own workings); primary qualities (inseparable from the object — extension, shape,
motion, number, solidity) vs secondary qualities (powers to produce sensations in us —
color, sound, taste, smell); materialism (everything is matter in motion); idealism /
immaterialism (only minds and ideas are real, no mind-independent matter); *esse est percipi*
(to be is to be perceived); induction (reasoning from particular observations to general
conclusions) and the problem of induction (past regularities cannot guarantee future ones);
constant conjunction (two events always observed together) vs necessary connection (the
unobserved "power" the first is supposed to have over the second); custom/habit; the bundle
theory of the self; *a priori* (known by thinking alone) vs *a posteriori* (known through
experience).
