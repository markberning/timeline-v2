## MUST-FIX LIST (consolidated)

**MF-1 — Protagoras earnings: inaccurate compression**
The draft says Protagoras "reportedly earned more than the greatest sculptor of the age." The source (Plato, *Meno* 91d, via SEP sophists entry) says he earned more than *ten sculptors*, with Phidias named as the most celebrated among them — not a one-on-one Protagoras vs. Phidias comparison. The draft's phrasing misstates the claim.
Correction: "reportedly earned more in fees than ten sculptors put together — including the celebrated Phidias" (or just cut to the safe claim: "earned more than the sculptors of the age, Socrates tells us in Plato").

**MF-2 — Tortoise: framing needed, name not wrong but context missing**
The draft uses "the tortoise" as Achilles' opponent without flagging that the verified Aristotle text (*Physics* VI.9, cited in the pack) uses only "the slowest runner." The tortoise identification enters via Simplicius (~530 CE), approximately 1,000 years after Zeno. Using "tortoise" is standard shorthand and not false, but the pack's own verified Aristotle quote says "the quickest runner can never overtake the slowest," and the draft — which explicitly quotes that verified clause — then names the tortoise without noting it is the traditional name. The pack's ledger flagged this for fact-checker ruling (see ledger §"One number/name flagged"). This is a framing duty, not an error: the name should be introduced as the traditional name. Omitting the frame means the draft implicitly presents a 6th-century CE gloss as a detail in the original argument, which contradicts the draft's own careful sourcing elsewhere.
Correction: add one clause — "the tortoise, as the tradition names it" or "Achilles against the slowest runner — a tortoise, in the version that has stuck" — the first time the tortoise appears.

**MF-3 — Diogenes cup-and-child anecdote: confirmed in DL but framing slip**
The cup story is confirmed at DL VI.37 (Hicks): "One day, observing a child drinking out of his hands, he cast away the cup from his wallet with the words, 'A child has beaten me in plainness of living.'" The detail checks out. However, the pack ledger flags this as "classic DL VI material adjacent to the pack's mouse-shelter item; fact-checker confirm it's in DL VI before ship." It is confirmed. The framing issue: the draft's sentence is "owned a cup until he saw a child drinking from cupped hands and threw it away" — this is accurate but is listed alongside the jar and the lamp without an explicit DL legend-frame marker. The jar and lamp lines carry "by that tradition" and "the tradition Diogenes Laertius hands down" framings; the cup line does not. Since every Diogenes anecdote must carry that frame (pipeline requirement; pack §4 standing rule), the cup line needs it.
This is a framing gap in an already-flagged item. **MUST-FIX: attach the DL tradition marker to the cup story explicitly.** (Not a factual error — the story is confirmed.)

## SHOULD-FIX LIST

**SF-1 — Chrysippus birth year: minor inaccuracy**
The draft says "roughly 280 to 206 BC." Most standard sources give the birth year as c. 279 BC (the 280 appears in some sources but 279 is more consistently cited). The death year 206 BC is confirmed. The fact pack itself gives "c. 280–c. 206 BC" so this is within the pack's own acknowledged approximation, but as fact-checker I record the more common scholarly figure.
Correction: "roughly 279 to 206 BC" or the safe "around the turn of the third century BC" — but this is a minor precision point, not a wrong fact.

**SF-2 — "Painted Porch" vs. "Painted Stoa"**
The draft translates "Stoa Poikile" as the "Painted Porch." "Painted Porch" is a defensible rendering (the root *stoa* covers both porch and colonnade), but scholarly writing consistently uses "Painted Stoa" or "Painted Colonnade." "Porch" creates a slightly different architectural image. Not wrong, but the more standard and less visually misleading translation is preferred.
Correction: "the Stoa Poikile, the 'Painted Stoa'" (or "Painted Colonnade").

**SF-3 — Protagoras: source of the earnings claim should be made visible**
If MF-1 is fixed with the Meno 91d material, the draft should make the source explicit ("as Socrates says in Plato's *Meno*" or similar), since the pack marks this as LATE-TRADITION via a quotation in Plato. The claim is about what Socrates says about Protagoras in Plato — not a direct attestation about Protagoras. Framing it as "reportedly" alone somewhat obscures that this is a Platonic report, in a dialogue that is also partly hostile to the Sophists.

**SF-4 — Raphael fresco: one minor frame gap**
The draft says "the Renaissance painter Raphael caught it perfectly: in his fresco of the Greek philosophers." The fresco has a title (*The School of Athens*) and a date (1509–11), which the pack lists in §7. Not including the title is a minor omission rather than an error, but since the pack has it, a first-mention title would make the reference citable. The pack also explicitly requires the caption note "a RENAISSANCE imagination of the Greeks, not a document" — the draft uses it only as passing prose color, not as a titled image with caption. If this fresco appears as an actual image card, the caption rule is mandatory.

## Rulings on the three author-flagged items

### (a) The tortoise: ancient or later tradition?

**RULING: The tortoise is a Simplicius gloss, not in Aristotle's text. Frame it — MUST-FIX.**

Aristotle's *Physics* VI.9 (the pack's verified source) says "the quickest runner can never overtake the slowest" — no animal named. The tortoise enters via Simplicius's 6th-century CE commentary, approximately 1,000 years after Zeno. It is real ancient tradition but late tradition. Using "tortoise" without any frame claims more ancient specificity than the source supports, especially since the draft itself quotes the verified Aristotle clause ("the quickest runner can never overtake the slowest") two sentences later. The draft should introduce "the tortoise" as the traditional name.
