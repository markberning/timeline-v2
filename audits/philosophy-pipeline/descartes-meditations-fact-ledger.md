## Translation doctrine (HARD FLOOR)
Every quoted line in the work read is **John Veitch's 1901 public-domain
translation**, read verbatim from loadable sources and string-matched. Veitch chosen
as the single consistent translator for the whole read (Haldane & Ross 1911 is the
other licit PD option and was cross-checked for every passage; wordings agree in
substance). NO modern in-copyright translation is quoted (Cottingham/CSM, Cress,
Moriarty, Williams = BLACKLIST). The Cottingham trap "non-extended thing... really
distinct" was specifically avoided; Veitch reads "thinking and unextended thing...
entirely and truly distinct." Paraphrase (Latin tags, "trademark," "Cartesian
circle," Elisabeth's objection) never appears inside quote marks.

## VERIFIED QUOTES (Veitch 1901) — string-matched, cited by Meditation
Each ✅ CONFIRMED read verbatim from the Veitch source.

**Q1 — Med I, senses deceive** ✅
"All that I have, up to this moment, accepted as possessed of the highest truth and
certainty, I received either from or through the senses."

**Q2 — Med I, dream argument** ✅
"I perceive so clearly that there exist no certain marks by which the state of waking
can ever be distinguished from sleep, that I feel greatly astonished; and in
amazement I almost persuade myself that I am now dreaming."

**Q3 — Med I, the malignant demon** ✅
"I will suppose, then, not that Deity, who is sovereignly good and the fountain of
truth, but that some malignant demon, who is at once exceedingly potent and
deceitful, has employed all his artifice to deceive me."

**Q4 — Med II, the cogito as it appears here ("I am, I exist")** ✅
"this proposition (pronunciatum) I am, I exist, is necessarily true each time it is
expressed by me, or conceived in my mind."
(Author use clips the parenthetical: "this proposition, I am, I exist, is necessarily
true each time it is expressed by me, or conceived in my mind." — the ellipsis of
"(pronunciatum)" is marked or the clean phrase quoted; NOT altered wording.)

**Q5 — Med II, the wax (perceived by the mind alone)** ✅
"the perception of it is neither an act of sight, of touch, nor of imagination, and
never was either of these, though it might formerly seem so, but is simply an
intuition (inspectio) of the mind."

**Q6 — Med III, definition of God** ✅
"By the name God, I understand a substance infinite, [eternal, immutable],
independent, all-knowing, all-powerful, and by which I myself, and every other thing
that exists, if any such there be, were created."
(Brackets are Veitch's own — they mark the French-edition addition; reproduced as
printed.)

**Q6b — Med III, the causal principle (engine of the trademark argument)** ✅
"there must at least be as much reality in the efficient and total cause as in its
effect."

**Q6c — Med III, the idea of the infinite given by an infinite being** ✅
"I should not, however, have the idea of an infinite substance, seeing I am a finite
being, unless it were given me by some substance in reality infinite."

**Q7 — Med IV, the will wider than the understanding (source of error)** ✅
"They arise from this cause alone, that I do not restrain the will, which is of much
wider range than the understanding, within the same limits, but extend it even to
things I do not understand, and as the will is of itself indifferent to such, it
readily falls into error and sin by choosing the false in room of the true, and evil
instead of good."

**Q8 — Med V, the ontological argument** ✅
"the existence can no more be separated from the essence of God, than the idea of a
mountain from that of a valley, or the equality of its three angles to two right
angles, from the essence of a [rectilinear] triangle."
(Bracketed "[rectilinear]" reproduced as printed in Veitch.)

**Q9 — Med VI, the real distinction of mind and body** ✅
"because, on the one hand, I have a clear and distinct idea of myself, in as far as I
am only a thinking and unextended thing, and as, on the other hand, I possess a
distinct idea of body, in as far as it is only an extended and unthinking thing, it
is certain that I, [that is, my mind, by which I am what I am], is entirely and truly
distinct from my body, and may exist without it."

**Q10 — Med VI, God is no deceiver** ✅
"God is no deceiver" (verbatim phrase, appears twice in Med VI; e.g. "since God is no
deceiver, it necessarily follows that I am not herein deceived").

## CROSS-WORK locus point (the one thing the read must nail)
The Latin slogan **"Cogito, ergo sum" / "I think, therefore I am" is NOT in the
*Meditations*.** ✅ CONFIRMED.
- *Meditations* (1641, Latin): "ego sum, ego existo" → Veitch "I am, I exist" (Q4).
  No "therefore."
- *Discourse* (1637, French): "je pense, donc je suis"; the Latin "COGITO ERGO SUM"
  in English editions is **Veitch's own added gloss**, not Descartes' 1637 text.
- *Principles of Philosophy* (1644, Latin): "ego cogito, ergo sum" as a formal axiom.
The work read states this plainly in the cogito chapter (consistent with the thinker
read ch3 and era-3 read).

## DATES / FACTS (cross-checked, consistent with thinker read)
- *Meditations on First Philosophy* (*Meditationes de Prima Philosophia*) first
  published **1641** (Paris, Latin; 2nd ed. 1642 Amsterdam adds the 6th set of
  Objections). ✅
- Six Meditations; the device "six days" of meditation is Descartes' own framing in
  the Preface/Synopsis (one meditation = one day's withdrawal). The hub "Six days"
  stat reflects this framing. ✅ (Descartes, Synopsis: the work is offered as six
  meditations to be taken slowly; the "days" framing is traditional and grounded in
  the Synopsis's pacing language — framed as framing, not asserted as a literal diary.)
- Published WITH the **Objections and Replies**: Mersenne circulated the manuscript;
  the printed objections include Caterus (1st), Mersenne (2nd, who first raised the
  circularity), Hobbes (3rd), Arnauld (4th, who pressed the circle hardest), Gassendi
  (5th). The 6th set added 1642. ✅ (Consistent with thinker read ch5.)
- The **Cartesian circle**: clear-and-distinct ideas are trusted because God exists;
  God's existence is proved by clear-and-distinct ideas. Named first by Mersenne (2nd
  Objections), pressed by Arnauld (4th). Most scholars hold Descartes never escaped
  it. ✅
- The **trademark argument** (Med III): the idea of God is like the maker's mark
  stamped on the work — Descartes' own image. ✅
- The **ontological argument** (Med V): a version of Anselm's (faith-and-reason
  chapter); existence is treated as a perfection belonging to God's essence. ✅
- The **real distinction** (Med VI): mind = thinking, unextended thing (res cogitans);
  body = extended, unthinking thing (res extensa); really distinct, can exist apart;
  the external/material world recovered because a good God would not systematically
  deceive. ✅

## ANACHRONISM / FRAMING GUARDS
- Do NOT say "Descartes invented mind-body dualism" (Plato's *Phaedo*, Augustine, the
  Scholastics all precede; the potted-summary trap). The read frames what is genuinely
  new: two *complete* substances + the body as machine; the *interaction problem* he
  created. ✅ (Consistent with thinker read ch4.)
- The evil demon is a TOOL (hyperbolic, methodic doubt), not a belief; Descartes is
  anti-skeptic. ✅
- The "six days" framing flagged as Descartes' device, not a literal diary.
- Elisabeth of Bohemia's interaction objection: NO PD English of her letters
  confirmed → paraphrase only, never quoted (era-3 rule). The work read keeps her at a
  forward-pointer level (she belongs to the thinker read ch5); the work read stays on
  the text.
- "Cogito ergo sum is the Meditations' line" — the canonical error; corrected (above).
