## QUOTATIONS (all Carter 1758 unless flagged)

### Q1 — Enchiridion §1 (the dichotomy of control, the hinge of the whole read)
**Carter wording, VERBATIM (confirmed):**
> "Of Things, some are in our Power, and others not. In our Power are Opinion, Pursuit, Desire, Aversion, and in one Word, whatever are our own Actions. Not in our Power, are Body, Property, Reputation, Command, and, in one Word, whatever are not our own Actions."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim against Wikisource, *All the Works of Epictetus … / The Encheiridion* (Carter).
- URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/All_the_Works_of_Epictetus,_Which_Are_Now_Extant/The_Encheiridion  (HTTP 200)
- NOTE: Carter capitalizes nouns in 18th-c style (Power, Opinion…). The read preserves Carter's capitalization inside the quotation marks (it is a verbatim period text). Carter opens "Of Things, some are in our Power" — NOT the modern Higginson/popular "Some things are in our control"; the read uses Carter's exact opening.

### Q2 — Enchiridion §5 (men disturbed by judgments, the most-quoted Stoic line)
**Carter wording, VERBATIM (confirmed):**
> "Men are disturbed, not by Things, but by the Principles and Notions, which they form concerning Things."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim against Wikisource (Carter).
- URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/All_the_Works_of_Epictetus,_Which_Are_Now_Extant/The_Encheiridion  (HTTP 200)
- ⚠️ WORDING TRAP CAUGHT: the popular "Men are disturbed not by things, but by the **views which they take** of things" is the **Higginson** translation (PD, Gutenberg 45109), NOT Carter. The MIT Internet Classics Archive page (classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html) is **Higginson/Carter-mixed and renders this line as "principles and notions"**; it is unreliable as "Carter." This read quotes ONLY Carter's verified "Principles and Notions, which they form concerning Things." The famous shorter paraphrase "men are disturbed not by things but by their opinions/judgments about things" is carried in the read as PARAPHRASE (house voice), never inside quote marks attributed to a specific translation.
- The fuller §5 continues (Carter, available if needed): "Death, for Instance, is not terrible, else it would have appeared so to Socrates. But the Terror consists in our Notion of Death, that it is terrible." (verified, used as paraphrase in the read.)

### Q3 — Enchiridion §8 (wish events as they happen)
**Carter wording, VERBATIM (confirmed, OCR typo corrected):**
> "Require not Things to happen as you wish; but wish them to happen as they do happen; and you will go on well."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED against Wikisource (Carter). The Wikisource OCR shows "as you with … but with them" — a scanning error for "wish." The intended/printed Carter word is "wish" (confirmed by the parallel Higginson "wish them to happen as they do happen" and the sense). Read uses "wish."
- URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/All_the_Works_of_Epictetus,_Which_Are_Now_Extant/The_Encheiridion  (HTTP 200)

### Q4 — Discourses I.1 (the one faculty in our power — chapter title + key line)
**Carter chapter title, VERBATIM:** "Of the Things which are, and of those which are not, in our own Power."
**Carter key line, VERBATIM (confirmed):**
> "this most excellent and superior Faculty alone, a right Use of the Appearances of Things, the Gods have placed in our own Power"

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED against Wikisource, *All the Works … / Book 1 / Chapter 1* (Carter).
- URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/All_the_Works_of_Epictetus,_Which_Are_Now_Extant/Book_1/Chapter_1  (HTTP 200)
- Used in the key-passage block / Discourses context. "A right use of the appearances of things" = the disciplined faculty of judgment (prohairesis territory; see below).

### Q5 — "Bear and forbear" / *sustine et abstine*
- Status: 🟡 LEGEND/FRAGMENT — FRAME IT. This is NOT in the Enchiridion or surviving Discourses. It comes from **Aulus Gellius, *Attic Nights* (Noctes Atticae) XVII.19**, reporting that Epictetus said the whole of philosophy could be summed in two Greek words: **ἀνέχου καὶ ἀπέχου** (*anechou kai apechou*), Latinized as *sustine et abstine*, "bear and forbear" / "endure and abstain."
- Basis: Aulus Gellius was a near-contemporary; the saying survives as Epictetus **Fragment 10** (Schweighäuser numbering). Read attributes it explicitly to "the Roman writer Aulus Gellius, who reports…," NOT to the Discourses, and gives the Greek.
- The same two Greek words appear CARVED on the hero image's base (see below) — a nice convergence, noted in the caption.

## TECHNICAL TERMS (defined in-read on first use; each tied to the documented doctrine)

| Term | Gloss used | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **dichotomy of control** | some things are up to us (judgments, desire, will, our own acts), some are not (body, property, reputation, others, the weather); peace = wanting only what is up to us | ✅ the doctrine of Enchiridion §1 + Discourses I.1. "Dichotomy of control" is the MODERN label (popularized by 20th–21st-c Stoicism revival, e.g. William B. Irvine); the read uses it as the standard name and notes Epictetus's own framing is "what is and is not in our power." |
| **prohairesis** | the faculty of choice / moral will — the part of a person that judges and chooses, the one thing fully their own | ✅ CONFIRMED Epictetus's central technical term (IEP, SEP). Often translated "will," "volition," "moral character," "faculty of choice." Read glosses it plainly and notes translators render it variously. |
| **indifferents** (*adiaphora*) | things that are neither good nor bad in themselves — wealth, health, reputation, death — because they don't make a person good or bad | ✅ standard Stoic doctrine; matches the Marcus read's gloss (*adiaphoron* = "a thing that makes no difference"). |
| **preferred indifferents** (*proēgmena*) | indifferents it's still rational to pursue (health over sickness, wealth over poverty) — worth having, not worth staking your character on | ✅ standard Stoic doctrine (consistent with the Marcus read ch.5). |
| **the three disciplines** | desire (*orexis*), action/impulse (*hormē*), assent (*sunkatathesis*) — Epictetus's three fields of Stoic training | ✅ CONFIRMED. The three *topoi* are explicit in Epictetus (Discourses III.2); the influential modern systematization is **Pierre Hadot**'s (and A.A. Long's). Read names them as Epictetus's three fields and credits the modern emphasis to scholars where it's a framing, not a quote. |
| **role ethics / "play well the part assigned"** | the image of life as a play in which you don't choose your role but can play it well | ✅ Enchiridion §17 ("Remember that you are an actor in a play, the character of which is determined by the Author… your business is to act well the part that is given to you"). Read uses this as PARAPHRASE of §17 (the verbatim Carter §17 not block-quoted, only summarized — flagged as paraphrase). |
| **logos** | the rational order running through the universe (Stoic) | ✅ consistent with the Greeks + Marcus reads (cross-vertical coherence). |
