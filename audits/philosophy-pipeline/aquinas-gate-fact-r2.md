## Item 1 — Natural-law worked example (Ch4): first precept + three natural inclinations

**Draft claim:** The first precept is "good is to be done and pursued, evil avoided." The natural inclinations track three tiers of precepts: (1) preserve your own life (living-thing inclination); (2) care for offspring (animal inclination); (3) live in society, tell the truth (rational-animal inclination).

**Verdict: CONFIRMED — with one precision note on "tell the truth."**

ST I-II q.94 a.2 is verified (newadvent.org; SEP; multiple scholarly sources). The first precept wording, the three-tier inclination structure (substance/preserve-life → animal/procreation-and-offspring → rational-animal/know-truth-about-God-and-live-in-society), and the universality claim are all accurate to the text. The fact-pack (§4) flags q.94 a.2 as PARAPHRASE-ONLY on exact wording but confirms the SEP rendering.

**Precision note:** Aquinas's own text for the third inclination (rational animal tier) names "know the truth about God" and "live in society," with examples "to shun ignorance, to avoid offending those among whom one has to live." "Tell the truth" is not literally stated in q.94 a.2 as a discrete example; it is a reasonable gloss on the social-living inclination (not offending those one lives among), not a direct textual claim. The draft presents it as one item in a natural-prose list ("live in society, to tell the truth, to not wrong your neighbor"), which is defensible as honest paraphrase — no reader will think it is a verbatim Aquinas phrase — but the author should be aware it is an inference, not a quoted precept. The rest of the passage is accurate and not overstated.

**Source:** ST I-II q.94 a.2 at https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2094.htm; SEP "Aquinas's Moral, Political and Legal Philosophy."

## Item 2 — Essence/existence distinction (Ch4): creatures distinct, God = *ipsum esse subsistens*

**Draft claim:** In every creature, essence (what-it-is) is distinct from existence (that-it-is); existence is "borrowed." In God alone the two coincide: his essence IS to exist — *ipsum esse subsistens*, "subsistent Being itself."

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

The real distinction between essence and esse in creatures, God as ipsum esse subsistens, and the "borrowed" / received-act framing are all verified and standard Thomistic doctrine, sourced to De Ente et Essentia and ST I qq. 3–4. Fact-pack §4 confirms it DOCUMENTED with the locus; the formula *ipsum esse subsistens* is confirmed by SEP and Catholic Encyclopedia as the standard formulation. The draft's use of "borrowed" as a gloss on the received-act structure is a fair paraphrase — Aquinas uses language of participation and reception; "borrowed" captures that the creature does not possess esse by right of its own essence, which is accurate.

**Source:** *De Ente et Essentia*; ST I q.3; SEP "Thomas Aquinas"; https://ses.edu/aquinas-on-existence-and-the-essence-existence-distinction/

## Item 3 — Analogy of being (Ch4): analogical God-talk neither univocal nor equivocal; "healthy" example; Scotus rejects it

**Draft claim:** God-talk is analogical — neither univocal (identical meaning for God and creature) nor equivocal (totally unrelated). The draft uses the "healthy" proportion example. Flagged as what Scotus later rejects in favor of univocity.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

ST I q.13 a.5 establishes exactly the three-mode framework (univocal / equivocal / analogical). The "healthy" example is Aquinas's own (used in ST I q.13 and related texts — a diet is "healthy" by relation to the primary sense, a body that IS healthy). The Scotus contrast (univocity of being as his correction of Aquinas's analogy) is verified: Scotus explicitly argued that "being" must be univocal across God and creatures to make natural knowledge of God possible, directly opposing the analogia entis. The fact-pack §4 notes the term *analogia entis* is later Scholastic coinage but that Aquinas's doctrine is in the Summa — the draft does not use the Latin term in a way that overclaims, so no issue.

**Source:** ST I q.13 at https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1013.htm; SEP "Medieval Theories of Analogy"; IEP "John Duns Scotus."

## Item 4 — Scotus/Ockham one-beat characterizations (Ch6)

**Draft claim (Scotus):** Univocity of being (against Aquinas's analogy) + will-over-intellect (against Aquinas's intellectualism). **Draft claim (Ockham):** Nominalism (from Latin *nomen*, "name") — no real universals, only individuals and names we throw over them. The razor shaves the unseen metaphysical furniture Aquinas built the cathedral out of.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

Both characterizations are accurate one-beat summaries and not distortions.

**Scotus:** The univocity-of-being and voluntarism (will-over-intellect) points are the two standard signature positions in the secondary literature (SEP, IEP, Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy all lead with these). The draft's framing — "Scotus made the will free to choose regardless, so love and choice run out ahead of understanding" — is a fair gloss on voluntarism, not a caricature. The voluntarist interpretation of Scotus is the consensus view even if some scholars nuance it.

**Ockham:** The derivation of "nominalism" from *nomen* is accurate and standard. The razor's function — trimming universals and metaphysical entities beyond what observation establishes — is accurate. The draft's line "shave away exactly the kind of unseen metaphysical furniture (universal natures, the real distinction of essence and existence) that Aquinas had built the cathedral out of" correctly names the Thomistic targets of Ockham's critique. The draft also notes this is "not crude rejection" and that Ockham is in deep argument with the Summa — that's the right framing.

**Source:** SEP "John Duns Scotus"; IEP "John Duns Scotus"; IEP "William of Ockham"; Wikipedia "Duns Scotus" and "Nominalism."

## Item 5 — Faith "perfects" reason (break block): faith builds on / keeps reason's findings

**Draft claim:** "Perfects" does NOT mean faith replaces what reason found. It means faith builds on what reason got right and carries it further — reason's findings are kept, not overwritten. The break-block prose makes the telescope analogy explicit.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

This is precisely Aquinas's position as expressed in ST I q.1 a.8 ("grace does not destroy nature but perfects it") and verified against newadvent.org. The draft also correctly quotes the full sentence: "Since therefore grace does not destroy nature but perfects it, natural reason should minister to faith as the natural bent of the will ministers to charity" — which is the verified text. The interpretation that "perfects" means builds-on-and-extends rather than replaces is the standard and correct reading, confirmed across multiple secondary sources (Wikipedia, Imaginative Conservative, SEP, and the Catholicism.org article). The draft's explicit warning against the wrong direction ("the easy misreading runs the wrong way: 'perfects' does not mean faith replaces what reason found") proactively corrects a real misreading and is itself accurate to Aquinas.

**Source:** ST I q.1 a.8 at https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1001.htm; Wikipedia "Gratia non tollit naturam, sed perficit."
