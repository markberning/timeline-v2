## Complete [VERIFY] tag list (11 tags) — exact claim text for the r2 fact-checker

These are NEW factual sentences beyond what the pack carries verbatim, flagged for the r2 verification pass. None is a direct quotation; all are claims to confirm.

- **[V1]** (Ch4): German Idealism as a ~60-year movement (1780s–1840s) constituted by Kant, Fichte, Schelling, and Hegel.
- **[V2]** (Ch3): Kant's third formula / Formula of Autonomy / Kingdom of Ends — "act as a member of a community in which every rational being is both author and subject of the same laws" — confirm the Abbott *Groundwork* wording before the r2 pin.
- **[V3]** (Ch3): the third *Critique*'s bridging function (beauty/sublime/nature-as-purposive bridges the nature/freedom split) and the *sublime* as its vivid concept — standard Kant scholarship.
- **[V4]** (Ch4): Fichte's recognition problem (a self knows itself as a self only through another self-consciousness confirming it) is the conceptual source of Hegel's master-slave dialectic.
- **[V5]** (Ch4): Hegel's documented conservatism — accepted the Prussian state chair in 1818, wrote against the more radical Burschenschaft reformers, and the *Philosophy of Right* praises constitutional monarchy as a high realization of freedom.
- **[V6]** (Ch4): Hegel's Being → Nothing → Becoming as the opening triad of the *Science of Logic* (pure Being collapses into Nothing; both pass over into Becoming) — stated beyond the pack's general Aufhebung material.
- **[V7]** (Ch5): the Persian Upanishad translation was commissioned/made in the seventeenth century by the Mughal prince Dara Shukoh, eldest son of Shah Jahan, who had fifty Upanishads rendered into Persian.
- **[V8]** (Ch5): by 1820 Hegel's *Phenomenology* and *Logic* already constituted the most comprehensive philosophical system then available — an architecture covering art, religion, law, and history.
- **[V9]** (Ch3): Kant's *Observations on the Feeling of the Beautiful and Sublime* (1764) and his geography/anthropology lectures contain race-hierarchy claims in tension with "every rational being is an end in itself"; recent scholarship (Pauline Kleingeld) centers this; his views may have shifted toward fuller universalism late.
- **[V10]** (Ch5): Schopenhauer's engagement with Buddhist sources deepened in the 1840s as the first real European scholarship on Buddhism became available.
- **[V11]** (Ch5): Schopenhauer's 1851 essay "On Women," collected in the *Parerga and Paralipomena*, extends his pessimism into explicitly misogynist territory (women as intellectually and morally lesser).

Note: V5 carries two sub-claims (the 1818 chair / Burschenschaft opposition and the constitutional-monarchy praise); both are inside the single Ch4 [VERIFY] tag on the politics line.
