## BIOGRAPHY
- Born **5 May 1813**, Copenhagen. Died **11 November 1855**, Copenhagen, aged **42**.
  Sources: Britannica https://www.britannica.com/biography/Soren-Kierkegaard ;
  Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard
- Father: **Michael Pedersen Kierkegaard** (1756–1838), well-to-do wool merchant who rose from a
  poor Jutland childhood. Source: Wikipedia (as above).
- The **"great earthquake"** (Danish *det store Jordskælv*): the family tradition / Kierkegaard's
  own journal account that his father, as a starving shepherd boy on the Jutland heath, had cursed
  God, and later believed a curse / God's wrath lay over the household (also linked to begetting
  a child out of wedlock with the housemaid Ane, later his second wife). Søren feared none of the
  children would outlive their father. FLAGGED IN-READ AS the family's own account / what
  Kierkegaard recorded, not asserted as external fact. Sources: Wikipedia;
  D. Anthony Storm's commentary https://sorenkierkegaard.org/kierkegaard-biography.html ;
  Britannica.
- **Regine Olsen**: met 8 May 1837; proposed **8 September 1840**; broke the engagement
  **11 August 1841** (returned the ring; he then posed publicly as a scoundrel to make the break
  easier on her). Sources: Wikipedia; Univ. of Copenhagen SKC
  https://teol.ku.dk/skc/english/about-soeren-kierkegaard/.../regine/ ; Britannica.
  The break haunts the authorship; framed in-read as biography + the read does not over-claim a
  one-to-one decoding of the books.
- **Pseudonyms** (facts, may state): Victor Eremita (*Either/Or*, ed.), **Johannes de silentio**
  (*Fear and Trembling*), Constantin Constantius (*Repetition*), **Vigilius Haufniensis**
  (*The Concept of Anxiety*), **Johannes Climacus** (*Philosophical Fragments*; *Concluding
  Unscientific Postscript*), **Anti-Climacus** (*The Sickness unto Death*; *Practice in
  Christianity*), Hilarius Bookbinder (*Stages on Life's Way*). "Johannes de silentio" = "John of
  the Silence." Sources: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
  https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/ ; Wikipedia.
- **Indirect communication**: Kierkegaard's deliberate method — the pseudonymous authors hold
  positions he does not simply endorse, so the reader is forced to choose rather than be handed a
  doctrine; he contrasted it with direct communication. Sources: SEP; Wikipedia.
- **Corsair affair**: 1845–46. After the pseudonym (P.L. Møller / "Frater Taciturnus" exchange)
  provoked *The Corsair* (a satirical Copenhagen paper), it ran months of caricatures mocking his
  appearance (thin legs, uneven trouser-legs, stooped gait), making him a figure of street
  ridicule. Sources: Wikipedia "Corsair Affair" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsair_Affair ;
  SEP.
- **Attack upon Christendom**: 1854–55, after Bishop Mynster's death and a eulogy that called him
  a "witness to the truth." Kierkegaard attacked the Danish State Church (Folkekirke) in newspaper
  articles and in his own pamphlet **Øjeblikket** (*The Moment* / *The Instant*), of which he wrote
  ten issues; the official Church had betrayed New Testament Christianity by making it comfortable.
  He **collapsed in the street** in autumn 1855, was hospitalized ~a month, and died 11 Nov 1855,
  the tenth issue unpublished. Sources: Britannica; Wikipedia; SEP.

## CORE IDEAS

### Three stages / spheres of existence (aesthetic → ethical → religious)
- Aesthetic, ethical, religious; the later spheres presuppose and include the earlier as
  subordinate moments; movement between them is by choice/leap, not gradual maturation.
- *Either/Or* (1843, ed. Victor Eremita): the aesthete "A" (incl. the *Diary of a Seducer*) vs.
  the ethical "Judge William" (Vilhelm), who argues the aesthete never becomes a self.
- Aesthetic = living for the interesting / pleasure / mood; despair = boredom and loss of self.
  Ethical = binding oneself in commitment, duty, marriage, vocation; the universal.
  Religious = the single individual standing before God, by faith. Sources: SEP; Britannica
  https://www.britannica.com/biography/Soren-Kierkegaard/Stages-on-lifes-way ;
  Academy of Ideas summary.

### Subjectivity is truth; the critique of Hegel
- *Concluding Unscientific Postscript to Philosophical Fragments* (1846), pseud. **Johannes
  Climacus**. Thesis paraphrased in-read: subjectivity is truth / truth is subjectivity — i.e.
  for the existential, faith-bearing questions, what matters is the HOW (the passionate, inward,
  committed manner of holding) not only the WHAT; an objective approximation can never ground an
  infinite personal interest.
- Critique of **Hegel's System**: the all-encompassing speculative System abstracts away from the
  existing individual; the philosopher who claims the standpoint of the whole forgets that he is a
  particular existing human being who must still live and die and decide. Climacus's barb: the
  System is a grand palace whose builder lives in a shack beside it (PARAPHRASE only — the famous
  image is rendered without quotation). Sources: Wikipedia "Concluding Unscientific Postscript"
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concluding_Unscientific_Postscript_to_Philosophical_Fragments ;
  Storm commentary; SEP. Cross-link [Hegel](/philosophy/thinker/hegel).

### Fear and Trembling (1843, pseud. Johannes de silentio); teleological suspension of the ethical
- Genesis 22: God commands Abraham to sacrifice his son Isaac on Mount Moriah; Abraham obeys,
  raises the knife, and is stopped by the angel; a ram is provided. (Biblical fact.)
- **Teleological suspension of the ethical**: faith can require the single individual to suspend
  the ethical (the universal moral law — do not murder your son) for the sake of a higher *telos*
  (his absolute relation to God). The first Problema asks whether such a suspension exists.
- **Knight of infinite resignation** vs. **knight of faith**: the knight of resignation gives Isaac
  up completely, makes the infinite movement of renouncing the finite, and finds a sad nobility in
  the loss. The knight of faith makes that same movement of resignation AND then, on the strength
  of the absurd, believes he will get Isaac back in this life — believes the impossible. Faith as
  **the absurd** = believing by virtue of the absurd, against the understanding. The knight of
  faith is outwardly indistinguishable from an ordinary burgher (de silentio's tax-collector
  image, rendered as paraphrase). Sources: Wikipedia "Fear and Trembling"
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_and_Trembling ; SparkNotes Problema I
  https://www.sparknotes.com/philosophy/feartrembling/section6/ ; LitCharts; SEP.
  Cross-link [Fear and Trembling](/philosophy/work/feartrembling).

### The leap of faith
- The transition into the religious sphere / to faith is not reached by accumulating reasons or
  proofs; reason cannot underwrite it. It is a leap — a free act of will / passionate commitment
  across the gap that argument cannot close. (Kierkegaard's own phrase in Danish is *Spring*, a
  leap; the popular "leap of faith" formulation is a later coinage but captures his movement.)
  FLAGGED: the exact phrase "leap of faith" is not a verbatim Kierkegaard quotation; the read
  presents it as the standard name for his *Spring*/leap, paraphrased. Sources: SEP; Britannica.

### Three forms of despair (The Sickness unto Death, 1849, pseud. Anti-Climacus)
- Self = a relation that relates itself to itself, grounded (when healthy) transparently in the
  power that established it (God). Despair = the sickness unto death = a misrelation of the self
  to itself. Three forms (paraphrased): (1) despair of not being conscious of having a self / not
  knowing one is in despair (the lowest, commonest, "unconscious" despair); (2) the despair of not
  willing to be oneself / weakness — wanting to be rid of oneself; (3) the despair of willing to be
  oneself in defiance — defiantly insisting on being a self on one's own terms, refusing the power
  that grounds it. Sources: Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sickness_Unto_Death ;
  SparkNotes; Storm commentary https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html .

### Anxiety / angst (The Concept of Anxiety, 1844, pseud. Vigilius Haufniensis)
- Anxiety (Danish *Angest*) is distinct from fear: fear has a definite object; anxiety has no
  object — it is the response to possibility / freedom itself. Paraphrased image: anxiety is the
  **dizziness of freedom**, the vertigo that comes when freedom looks down into its own
  possibility (the man at the cliff edge who is gripped less by fear of falling than by the
  awareness that he is free to jump). Anxiety is tied to his reading of original sin / the leap.
  Sources: Wikipedia "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom"
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety_Is_the_Dizziness_of_Freedom ; Philosophy Now
  https://philosophynow.org/issues/145/Kierkegaard_Young_Free_and_Anxious ; SEP.
  NOTE: "anxiety is the dizziness of freedom" circulates as a quote; the standard Hannay/Thomte
  translations are in copyright, so the read renders the IDEA as paraphrase, NO quotation marks.

### Father of existentialism
- Kierkegaard's priority of the existing individual, choice, anxiety, and authentic faith over the
  System made him the acknowledged forerunner of 20th-c existentialism (Heidegger, Jaspers,
  Sartre, Camus, and the Christian existentialists). Framed in-read as influence/reception, with
  the named successors hedged to documented debt. Sources: SEP; Britannica.
  Cross-link [Nietzsche](/philosophy/thinker/nietzsche) as the other great 19th-c root of
  existentialism (parallel diagnosis, opposite conclusion: Kierkegaard leaps to God, Nietzsche
  pronounces God dead) — framed as a comparison, not a claim of mutual influence (they did not
  read each other).

## THE BREAK BLOCK
- FROM: [Hegel](/philosophy/thinker/hegel)'s all-encompassing rational System — the universal,
  world history, *Aufhebung*/mediation, reality as Spirit's self-knowing whole in which the
  particular is a vanishing moment (steelmanned; see hegel-thinker.read.ts).
- TO: the single existing individual (*den Enkelte*), choice, the leap, and a faith reason cannot
  underwrite — truth as subjectivity, the teleological suspension of the ethical, the individual
  higher than the universal in the God-relation.
- Sources: SEP (Kierkegaard's anti-Hegelianism); Wikipedia (Postscript). All paraphrase.
