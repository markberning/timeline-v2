## A. Biography (CONFIRMED unless flagged)
- Born **22 February 1788, Danzig** (then a free city; now Gdańsk, Poland). — CONFIRMED
  (SEP; Britannica).
- Died **21 September 1860, Frankfurt am Main** (peacefully, in his apartment at Schöne
  Aussicht 16), age 72. — CONFIRMED (SEP).
- Father **Heinrich Floris Schopenhauer** (1747–1805), a wealthy Danzig **merchant and
  shipowner** who groomed Arthur for a merchant's career. Died **20 April 1805**;
  **possibly by suicide** (drowning in a canal beside the family warehouse) — treated
  by SEP as uncertain conjecture, so the read frames it as "probably," "by most
  accounts," not asserted flat. — CONFIRMED (the death; the suicide is the standard but
  unproven account).
- Mother **Johanna Schopenhauer** (1766–1838), a successful **novelist** and Weimar
  **salon hostess** connected to Goethe — for a time the more famous Schopenhauer.
  Mother and son were temperamentally at war; the **final break came in 1814** after a
  bitter quarrel, and by the standard account **they never saw each other again** in
  the remaining ~24 years of her life (she died 1838). The "threw him down the stairs"
  detail is anecdotal; the read uses the documented core (the 1814 rupture, never
  reconciled) and does not assert the stairs detail. — CONFIRMED (the estrangement +
  permanent break); stairs = tradition, omitted as fact.
- Doctorate **1813**, Jena, dissertation *On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of
  Sufficient Reason* (*Über die vierfache Wurzel des Satzes vom zureichenden Grunde*).
  — CONFIRMED.
- **Hated Hegel** and, in **March 1820** as a *Privatdozent* in Berlin, **scheduled his
  own lectures at the same hour as Hegel's** popular course; **few students chose
  Schopenhauer**; the venture failed and he effectively gave up an academic career.
  — CONFIRMED (SEP: "In March of 1820, Schopenhauer self-assuredly scheduled his class
  at a time that was simultaneous with Hegel's popular lectures, and few students chose
  to hear Schopenhauer"). The "empty room" is the standard dramatization of "few
  students"; the read says the hall stayed nearly empty / he lectured to almost no one,
  consistent with the source.
- Decades of **obscurity**: the main work (1818/1819) sold almost nothing; most of the
  first edition was eventually pulped. **Late fame** arrived only after **Parerga and
  Paralipomena** (1851), helped by an admiring **1853 review in the Westminster
  Review** ("Iconoclasm in German Philosophy" by John Oxenford); his last decade in
  Frankfurt brought disciples and celebrity. — CONFIRMED (SEP).
- Kept **pet poodles** in his Frankfurt years; by tradition he called them all by the
  same name, **Atma** (after the Sanskrit *Ātman*, world-soul/self), nicknamed
  **Butz**. — CONFIRMED that he kept poodles (SEP "pet French poodles"); the names
  Atma/Butz are the well-attested tradition, flagged as such in the read ("by
  tradition," "the story goes").

## C. Verified QUOTES — all from Haldane & Kemp, *The World as Will and Idea* (1883–86, PD)

Primary source: Project Gutenberg ebook **38427** (Vol. 1), and Wikisource page-scans of
the same Haldane & Kemp text. Title as printed by the translators = *The World as Will
and **Idea***; attributions name it that way.

### Book I — The world as Idea (Representation)
1. **Opening of §1** (Gutenberg 38427):
   "The world is my idea" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (the famous opening clause; printed
   "'The world is my idea:'—this is a truth which holds good for everything that lives
   and knows").
2. **The eye that sees a sun** (§1, Gutenberg 38427):
   "what he knows is not a sun and an earth, but only an eye that sees a sun, a hand
   that feels an earth" — VERIFIED VERBATIM.
3. **No truth more certain** (§1, Gutenberg 38427):
   "No truth therefore is more certain, more independent of all others, and less in
   need of proof than this, that all that exists for knowledge, and therefore this
   whole world, is only object in relation to subject" — VERIFIED VERBATIM.

### Book II — The world as Will
4. **The world is my will** (§1, Gutenberg 38427): the parallel clause
   "the world is my will" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (Schopenhauer states the second
   first-person truth, that the world is also will).

### The veil of Mâyâ (Schopenhauer naming the Indian/Vedic idea)
5. **The veil of Mâyâ** (§3, Gutenberg 38427):
   "It is Mâyâ, the veil of deception, which blinds the eyes of mortals, and makes them
   behold a world of which they cannot say either that it is or that it is not"
   — VERIFIED VERBATIM. (Schopenhauer's own attribution to "the wisdom of the Indians,"
   the Vedas/Upanishads, is his text; the read names the Upanishads + Vedas as HIS
   acknowledged source, not orientalized — see D below.)

### Book IV — Pessimism (suffering, the pendulum)
6. **All willing arises from want** (Book IV, §57; Wikisource Vol.1 p.444 region):
   "All willing arises from want, therefore from deficiency, and therefore from
   suffering. The satisfaction of a wish ends it; yet for one wish that is satisfied
   there remain at least ten which are denied." — VERIFIED VERBATIM.
7. **The basis of all willing is need** (§57; Wikisource Vol.1 p.444):
   "But the basis of all willing is need, deficiency, and thus pain." — VERIFIED VERBATIM.
8. **The pendulum between pain and ennui** (§57; Wikisource Vol.1 p.444):
   "Thus its life swings like a pendulum backwards and forwards between pain and ennui."
   — VERIFIED VERBATIM. (*Ennui* = boredom; the read glosses it once.)
9. **Suffering essential to life / constant dying** (§57; Wikisource Vol.1 p.443):
   "how essential to all life is suffering" and "His real existence is only in the
   present, whose unchecked flight into the past is a constant transition into death, a
   constant dying." — VERIFIED VERBATIM.

### Book III — the escape through art; music
10. **Music a copy of the will itself** (Book III, §52; Gutenberg 38427 / Haldane & Kemp
    Vol. 1):
    "Music is as _direct_ an objectification and copy of the whole _will_ as the world
    itself, nay, even as the Ideas, whose multiplied manifestation constitutes the world
    of individual things. Music is thus by no means like the other arts, the copy of the
    Ideas, but the _copy of the will itself_, whose objectivity the Ideas are."
    — VERIFIED VERBATIM (Gutenberg 38427 plain text). NOTE: the ledger previously
    misquoted this as "namely a copy of the Ideas, but a copy of the will itself" —
    corrected 2026-06-15. The epigraph in the read quotes the corrected wording: "…music
    is thus by no means like the other arts, the copy of the Ideas, but the copy of the
    will itself…"

### Book IV — compassion / seeing through the principium individuationis
11. **The veil of Mâyâ lifted = the principium individuationis** (Book IV, §66;
    Wikisource Vol.1 p.531):
    "If that veil of Mâyâ, the _principium individuationis,_ is lifted from the eyes of
    a man to such an extent that he no longer makes the egotistical distinction between
    his person and that of others…" — VERIFIED VERBATIM.
12. **The good man regards others' suffering as his own** (§66; Wikisource Vol.1 p.531):
    "…such a man, who recognises in all beings his own inmost and true self, must also
    regard the infinite suffering of all suffering beings as his own…" — VERIFIED
    VERBATIM. (Used to ground compassion as the basis of ethics.)

## D. Doctrine / interpretation (CONFIRMED, taught)
- **World as Representation (Idea)** = the completion of **Kant**: the world we know is
  structured by the subject (space, time, causality / the principle of sufficient
  reason); it is *Vorstellung*, presentation-for-a-subject. — standard. Cross-link
  [Kant](/philosophy/thinker/kant), [CPR via the work hub if needed]. The read keeps
  Kant's thing-in-itself as the hinge: Schopenhauer claims to have FOUND it (the Will),
  where Kant said it was unknowable. This is Schopenhauer's own claim, taught as his
  claim (his critics, Nietzsche included, dispute the identification).
- **World as Will** = the thing-in-itself is a single, blind, aimless, ceaseless
  **striving** (*Wille*), known from the inside via one's own **body** (the act of will
  and the bodily movement are one thing known two ways), and read outward into all of
  nature — gravity, growth, instinct, animal drive, human desire — as one Will
  objectifying itself at every grade. NOT a personal/divine will, NOT purposeful: it
  wills nothing in particular, it just wills. — standard.
- **Pessimism argued, not asserted**: because the inner nature of everything is endless
  willing, and all willing springs from lack/deficiency (= pain), life is essentially
  suffering; satisfaction is merely the brief, negative cessation of a particular pain,
  immediately replaced by a new want or by boredom — the pendulum between pain and
  ennui. — CONFIRMED (quotes 6–9).
- **Three escapes / saving routes from the Will** (the soteriology):
  (a) **Aesthetic contemplation** — in disinterested perception of beauty (and the
      Platonic Ideas the artwork shows) the knower becomes a "pure will-less subject of
      knowledge," the wheel of willing stops for a moment; **music** is the highest art
      because it is not a copy of the Ideas but a direct **copy of the Will itself**.
  (b) **Compassion (*Mitleid*)** as the **basis of ethics** — the good person sees
      through the *principium individuationis* (the principle of individuation: the
      space-and-time framework that makes us appear to be separate selves), feels
      another's suffering as their own, because at bottom all beings are one Will. This
      is Schopenhauer's positive ethics (developed fully in *The Two Fundamental
      Problems of Ethics*, 1841): morality is grounded in compassion, not reason or
      duty (his explicit break from Kant's ethics). — CONFIRMED.
  (c) **Asceticism / denial of the will-to-live** — the highest and rarest route: the
      saint/ascetic, seeing the whole game of willing, turns the Will against itself and
      quiets it (voluntary poverty, chastity, resignation), approaching the "nothing"
      that is the only true release. NOT suicide, which Schopenhauer rejects as still an
      act of will (an angry affirmation of the will, not its denial). — CONFIRMED.
- **Debt to Kant** (genuine, accurately stated): Schopenhauer revered Kant, took the
  Representation/thing-in-itself frame wholesale, and called his own work the
  completion of Kant; he also savaged Kant's ethics (duty) and rejected Hegel/Fichte/
  Schelling as charlatans corrupting Kant. — CONFIRMED.
- **Debt to Hindu/Buddhist thought** (name it PRECISELY, not orientalized): Schopenhauer
  read the **Upanishads** in the Latin *Oupnek'hat* (Anquetil-Duperron's 1801–02 Latin
  rendering of a Persian version), kept it on his desk, and openly drew the **veil of
  Mâyâ** and the unity-behind-appearances from **the Vedas/Upanishads**; he also
  embraced **Buddhism** (the world as suffering; release as the quieting of craving;
  he called himself a "Buddhaist" / kept a Buddha figure). He is one of the first major
  Western philosophers to take Indian thought seriously as philosophy. The read NAMES
  the specific sources (Upanishads, the Vedanta notion of Māyā, Buddhist dukkha-analog)
  rather than gesturing vaguely at "Eastern wisdom." — CONFIRMED.
- **Influence forward** (CONFIRMED standard): **Nietzsche** (early disciple via *The
  Birth of Tragedy*, then critic — kept the Will, flipped pessimism into affirmation,
  recast will-to-live as will-to-power); **Wagner** (read WWR ~1854, saturates *Tristan
  und Isolde* and the Ring's resignation); **Freud** (the primacy of unconscious drive
  over intellect — Freud acknowledged the affinity); **Wittgenstein** (read Schopenhauer
  young; the *Tractatus*'s willing subject + ethics-as-outside-the-world owe to him).
  Cross-link [Nietzsche](/philosophy/thinker/nietzsche). — CONFIRMED.

## E. The break (era hinge)
- FROM **Hegel** ([Hegel](/philosophy/thinker/hegel)): reality as rational *Geist*
  unfolding through history toward freedom; history GOING SOMEWHERE GOOD; the real is
  the rational; suffering redeemed by the cunning of reason.
- TO **Schopenhauer**: reality as a blind, purposeless **Will** with NO goal, NO
  progress, NO redemption; existence itself is suffering; "history" is just the same
  willing repeating. Schopenhauer despised Hegel personally and philosophically and
  cast him as the great fraud of the age. — CONFIRMED as the genuine, documented
  opposition.
