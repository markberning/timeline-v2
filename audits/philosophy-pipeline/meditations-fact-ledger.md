## Dates / framing facts (CONFIRMED)
- Marcus Aurelius: 121–180 CE (Roman emperor 161–180). Matches THINKERS.aurelius `dates:'121 – 180'`.
- The *Meditations* (Greek title *Ta eis heauton*, "to himself" / "things to oneself")
  is a private notebook, 12 books, written in Greek during his reign, largely on
  military campaign on the northern frontier (the wars against the Quadi and
  Marcomanni), c.170–180 CE; never prepared for publication. CONFIRMED in the
  Gutenberg #15877 editorial introduction (it notes Book I was "written during one of
  M.['s] campaigns" / "during the war with the Quadi"; the 12-book division is Long's).
  Hub `year:'c.175'` is consistent.
- "12 books" — CONFIRMED: the text runs FIRST BOOK … TWELFTH BOOK in #15877.
- Q. Junius Rusticus — Marcus's Stoic teacher; Marcus credits him (Bk 1.7) with
  introducing him to Epictetus's discourses. CONFIRMED verbatim (see Q7).
- Epictetus (c.50–135) — the freed-slave Stoic whose *Discourses* (recorded by his
  pupil Arrian) Marcus inherited via Rusticus. The Epictetan core (some things are up
  to us, some are not; master your own judgments) is the documented backbone of the
  *Meditations*' ethics. Framed as inheritance, not as Marcus's invention.

## VERIFIED QUOTES (George Long 1862, string-matched in Gutenberg #15877)

### Q1 — the morning rehearsal (Book 2.1)
> "Begin the morning by saying to thyself, I shall meet with the busybody, the
> ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, unsocial. All these things happen to
> them by reason of their ignorance of what is good and evil."
Cite: II.1. Continues: "…the nature of him who does wrong, that it is akin to me…
For we are made for co-operation, like feet, like hands, like eyelids…"
STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

### Q2 — the impediment / "obstacle is the way" (Book 5.20)
> "the mind converts and changes every hindrance to its activity into an aid; and so
> that which is a hindrance is made a furtherance to an act; and that which is an
> obstacle on the road helps us on this road."
Cite: V.20. STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.
NOTE — the famous slogan **"The obstacle is the way" / "What stands in the way
becomes the way"** is a MODERN paraphrase (popularized by Ryan Holiday's 2014 book
of that title), NOT Marcus's wording. The read quotes Long's actual sentence and
frames the slogan AS a later paraphrase. Do not present the slogan as a quotation.

### Q3 — the view from above (Book 9.30)
> "Look down from above on the countless herds of men and their countless
> solemnities, and the infinitely varied voyagings in storms and calms, and the
> differences among those who are born, who live together, and die."
Cite: IX.30. STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

### Q4 — the elevated mind / view of all time (Book 7.35, Marcus quoting Plato)
> "The man who has an elevated mind and takes a view of all time and of all
> substance, dost thou suppose it possible for him to think that human life is
> anything great?"
Cite: VII.35 ("From Plato:" — Marcus is excerpting Plato, *Republic* VI.486;
Long's footnote marks the source). STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.
FRAME: attributed in-text to Plato (as Marcus does), not invented by Marcus.

### Q5 — memento mori: live each act as the last (Book 2.5)
> "thou doest every act of thy life as if it were the last, laying aside all
> carelessness and passionate aversion from the commands of reason"
Cite: II.5. STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

### Q6 — memento mori: depart this moment (Book 2.11)
> "Since it is possible that thou mayest depart from life this very moment, regulate
> every act and thought accordingly."
Cite: II.11. STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

### Q7 — the Epictetus inheritance via Rusticus (Book 1.7)
> "I am indebted to him for being acquainted with the discourses of Epictetus, which
> he communicated to me out of his own collection."
Cite: I.7 (the Rusticus entry). STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

### Q8 — impermanence: time is a river (Book 4.43)
> "Time is like a river made up of the events which happen, and a violent stream;
> for as soon as a thing has been seen, it is carried away, and another comes in its
> place, and this will be carried away too."
Cite: IV.43. STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

### Q9 — substance like a river (Book 5.23, supporting impermanence)
> "substance is like a river in a continual flow"
Cite: V.23 (Long). STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim (fragment; usable inline).

### Q10 — Book 1, the gratitude list opening (Book 1.1)
> "From my grandfather Verus [I learned] good morals and the government of my
> temper."
Cite: I.1. STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. (Book 1 is the famous catalogue of debts
to family and teachers — grandfather Verus, his mother, Rusticus, Antoninus Pius,
the gods — used as the structural opening of the work.)

### Q11 — what I am: flesh, breath, ruling part (Book 2.2, supporting)
> "Whatever this is that I am, it is a little flesh and breath, and the ruling part."
Cite: II.2. STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

### Q12 — loss is change / fame forgotten (Book 9.30 tail + 9.35)
> "neither a posthumous name is of any value, nor reputation, nor anything else."
Cite: IX.30 (closing the view-from-above passage). STATUS: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

## PARAPHRASE-ONLY (never inside quote marks)
- The Greek title *Ta eis heauton* / "to himself" — transliteration, paraphrased.
- The "Quadi / Marcomanni frontier, c.170–180" campaign framing — paraphrased from
  the editorial intro, not quoted.
- "Some things are up to us and some are not" — Epictetan dichotomy of control,
  summarized (it is Epictetus's *Enchiridion* 1, framed as the inheritance Marcus
  rehearses, NOT quoted as Marcus's words).
- The slogan "the obstacle is the way" — explicitly framed as a later paraphrase (Q2).
