## FINDINGS

### 1. MINOR — "charming tale" presented in quotation marks; pack does not VERIFY that exact Jowett wording
**Location:** Ch8 ¶1: `he closes the argument with a vision... and he is careful to
label it. It is, he says, a "charming tale," a likely story, not a proof`.
**Problem:** "charming tale" sits inside quotation marks and is attributed to Socrates
("he says"). The pack uses the phrase "charming tale" in its own §1 narration
("Socrates tells 'a charming tale' of the **true earth**") and in the FRAMING-TRAP 4
note, but it is NOT listed as a VERIFIED fetched quote in §2/§7 — the myth's only two
VERIFIED quotes are Q-T (bottom-of-the-sea) and Q-U (twelve-piece ball). §5.3
explicitly tags the rest of the myth (rivers, judgment) PARAPHRASE-ONLY and says
"fetch before quoting." "Charming tale" is in the same un-fetched myth region. Under
the work's own quote doctrine (a quoted phrase must be a §2-VERIFIED Jowett string
with its locus), this two-word quote is not on firm footing.
**Severity rationale:** MINOR, not BLOCKER — the phrase does appear in the pack's own
prose so it is not invented, and the surrounding framing (myth-not-proof) is correct;
the exposure is only that two words are wrapped in quote marks without a §2 ledger
entry behind them.
**Fix:** Either (a) drop the quotation marks and paraphrase ("he is careful to label
it a likely tale, not a proof"), or (b) have the coordinator fetch the exact Jowett
wording at ~107c/110b and add it to the ledger before ship. Lowest-risk: remove the
quote marks. Note Ch8 ¶2's closing "worth the risk of believing" is already correctly
un-quoted paraphrase of the same register, so (a) is consistent with the draft's own
handling two sentences later.

### 2. MINOR — Nietzsche reading attributed with a degree of specificity slightly beyond the pack
**Location:** Ch8 ¶5: `A much later and more provocative reading, from the philosopher
Nietzsche, took the line as a grim verdict that *life itself* was the sickness
Socrates was glad to be cured of.`
**Problem:** The pack (FRAMING-TRAP 2 + §8 cautions) authorizes exactly this:
"(Nietzsche's provocative reading) a verdict that *life itself* was the sickness."
So the content is in-pack. The only soft edge is that the draft adds "Socrates was
glad to be cured of," which leans the interpretation a hair past the pack's neutral
"life itself was the sickness." This is a paraphrase of the same idea, not a new
claim, and it is explicitly framed as one reading among several with the crux left
open.
**Severity rationale:** MINOR / borderline-clean — flagged only for completeness.
**Fix:** Optional. If trimming, "took the line as a grim verdict that *life itself*
was the sickness" matches the pack verbatim-in-substance. No change required for ship.

### 3. MINOR — "twenty-four centuries" arithmetic claim (not in pack, but checkable and correct)
**Location:** Ch8 ¶5: `something readers have debated for twenty-four centuries`.
**Problem:** Not a sourced pack fact; it is the author's own gloss. The dramatic date
is 399 BCE (pack §0) and composition is the 380s BCE; 399 BCE → 2026 CE ≈ 2,425 years
≈ "twenty-four centuries." The figure is arithmetically sound against the pack's own
dates and is a round-number rhetorical flourish, not a precise factual assertion.
**Severity rationale:** MINOR — accurate, in-voice, no correction needed; logged so
the coordinator knows it is author-supplied, not pack-sourced.
**Fix:** None required.

### 4. MINOR — Simmias/Cebes "from Thebes," "Pythagorean-trained" — confirm both attributions
**Location:** Ch5 ¶1: `**Simmias** and **Cebes**, two young men from Thebes trained
in Pythagorean thought`; Ch2 ¶4 and Ch3 ¶1 also name them.
**Problem:** Pack §0 persons-list calls them "Simmias and Cebes of Thebes (the two
young Pythagorean-trained men)," and §1 calls the objections "Pythagorean-flavored."
Both "of Thebes" and "Pythagorean-trained" are directly supported. CLEAN — flagged
only because the draft states them as flat fact and they should be confirmed against
the pack, which they are.
**Severity rationale:** Effectively CLEAN.
**Fix:** None.

### 5. MINOR — Theseus/Minotaur gloss in Ch1 ¶3
**Location:** Ch1 ¶3: `a religious mission commemorating the legendary hero Theseus,
who (the story went) once sailed to Crete and rescued a group of young Athenians from
the Minotaur.`
**Problem:** Pack Q-B VERIFIES the ship is "the one 'in which, according to Athenian
tradition, Theseus went to Crete'" and §0 glosses "commemorating Theseus' rescue of
the youths from the Minotaur." The draft's gloss matches the pack's own framing.
CLEAN. The draft correctly does not put the Minotaur detail in quotation marks (it is
not in the VERIFIED Q-B string, which stops at "went to Crete") — it is handled as
prose gloss. Good.
**Fix:** None.

## FRAMING TRAPS — all five confirmed honored

- **TRAP 1 (character vs. author; proofs are Plato's, voiced by Socrates).** HONORED,
  and prominently. Throughline: "these proofs are Plato's, put in the mouth of his dead
  teacher." Hook ¶2 spells out the seam explicitly ("the speaker doing the arguing is
  Socrates, but the doctrines... are Plato's own developed philosophy... Keep that seam
  visible"). Break ¶3, Ch3, Ch7 ¶3 all reinforce. The three-removes layering (absent
  Plato → Phaedo → Echecrates) is carried in Hook ¶2, Ch1 ¶1–¶2. Exemplary.
- **TRAP 2 (last words left open).** HONORED. Ch8 ¶5 lays out three+ readings
  (death-as-cure / literal recovery / Nietzsche's life-as-sickness), states "the line
  is genuinely enigmatic and the dialogue does not gloss it," and "Plato chose to leave
  it open." No single reading asserted as certain. Matches FRAMING-TRAP 2 exactly.
- **TRAP 3 (hedged, not QED; objections nearly win).** HONORED, repeatedly. Throughline
  ("honest enough to let the strongest objections... very nearly win"), break ¶3, Ch3
  ¶2/¶5 (argument 1 "weakest"; recollection proves pre- not post-existence), Ch4 ¶3
  (affinity = resemblance/likelihood, "does not strictly demonstrate"), Ch5 (Simmias'
  lyre + Cebes' weaver staged as casting "a sort of despair," "Plato armed them"), Ch7
  ¶3 ("three of the four are... less than airtight," objections staged by the author).
  The "sort of despair" phrase (Ch5 epigraph context + ¶1) tracks the pack's §1/Q
  framing. Strongly honored.
- **TRAP 4 (closing myth = likely tale, not literal cosmology).** HONORED. Ch8 ¶1
  ("a likely story, not a proof"), ¶2 ("the label stays on it the whole time: this is
  the picture Socrates offers as worth the risk of believing, not as something he
  claims to know"). The cosmology is consistently framed as myth. (See Finding 1 re:
  the "charming tale" quote marks — a quotation-integrity nit, not a framing failure;
  the framing itself is correct.)
- **TRAP 5 ("practice of death" is NOT a death-wish; text argues against suicide).**
  HONORED, and given its own paragraph. Ch2 ¶3: "not a death-wish and not an
  endorsement of suicide. The dialogue is explicit about this, because earlier in the
  very same conversation Socrates argues *against* suicide... like a soldier at his
  post." Matches FRAMING-TRAP 5. The anti-suicide passage is paraphrased, never quoted
  (correct — §5.1 flags it PARAPHRASE-ONLY).

**Additional framing checks:**
- **Narrated at Phlius (frame) vs. death in the Athens prison.** HONORED. Ch1 ¶1
  ("a man from the town of Phlius, in the Peloponnese"), ¶2 ("far from Athens"); the
  prison/Athens setting is clear throughout. Matches pack §0.
- **399 BCE dating; BCE/CE not BC/AD.** HONORED. Hook ¶1 "In 399 BCE," Hook ¶2 "the
  380s BCE." No "BC"/"AD" anywhere in the draft. CLEAN. (Note: the cross-ref
  `plato-fact-pack.md` uses "BC" internally, but that is the source pack's own
  convention; the draft correctly uses BCE per pack §0's explicit instruction.)
