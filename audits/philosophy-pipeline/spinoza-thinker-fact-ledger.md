## Verified biography (SEP, World History Encyclopedia, OLL, Tablet/NEH on the cherem)
- **Born** Bento (Hebrew **Baruch**, Latin **Benedictus** — all three mean
  "blessed") **24 November 1632, Amsterdam**, into the Portuguese-Jewish (Sephardic)
  community of merchants who had fled the Iberian Inquisition. [SEP; OLL]
- **Excommunicated (cherem / herem)** from the Talmud Torah congregation of
  Amsterdam on **27 July 1656** (6 Av 5416), at age 23. The harshest writ of herem
  the congregation ever issued; **never rescinded**. The surviving document does
  NOT itemize the charges (frame honestly); the offenses it gestures at ("abominable
  heresies," "monstrous deeds") align with what he later wrote (no providential
  personal God, Torah not literally God-authored, no personal immortality of the
  soul). [SEP; Tablet; NEH]
- **Trade: grinding optical lenses** (and making instruments); his lenses were
  praised by Christiaan Huygens. He refused a Heidelberg chair and a pension large
  enough to compromise him, living plainly. [SEP; WHE]
- **Lived**: Amsterdam → **Rijnsburg** (near Leiden, by 1661) → **Voorburg**
  (outside The Hague, after 1663) → **The Hague** (final years). [SEP]
- **Died 21 February 1677, The Hague**, age 44, of a lung disease (consumption /
  pulmonary tuberculosis), **probably aggravated by years of inhaled glass dust**
  from lens-grinding (the standard account; stated as the likely, not certain,
  cause). [SEP "1677, The Hague"; WHE]
- **Works**: *Theologico-Political Treatise* (Tractatus Theologico-Politicus)
  published **1670, anonymously** (and "to great alarm" — banned). The **Ethics**
  (*Ethica, Ordine Geometrico Demonstrata*) published **posthumously in 1677** by
  his friends in the *Opera Posthuma*, with his initials only. [SEP]

## Verified quotations (Elwes) — cited by part/prop, string-matched
ETHICS (Wikisource Ethics_(Spinoza)/Part_N; Gutenberg #3800; Elwes):
- **Pt I, Def. VI** (God) — "By God, I mean a being absolutely infinite—that is, a
  substance consisting in infinite attributes, of which each expresses eternal and
  infinite essentiality." ✅
- **Pt I, Prop. XIV** — "Besides God no substance can be granted or conceived." ✅
- **Pt I, Prop. XVI** — "From the necessity of the divine nature must follow an
  infinite number of things in infinite ways." ✅
- **Pt I, Appendix** (free-will illusion) — "Men think themselves free inasmuch as
  they are conscious of their volitions and desires, and never even dream, in their
  ignorance, of the causes which have disposed them so to wish and desire." ✅
- **Pt II, Prop. VII** — "The order and connection of ideas is the same as the order
  and connection of things." ✅ (the parallelism of the two attributes)
- **Pt III, Prop. VI** (conatus) — "Everything, in so far as it is in itself,
  endeavours to persist in its own being." ✅
- **Pt IV, Preface** (Deus sive Natura) — "For the eternal and infinite Being, which
  we call God or Nature, acts by the same necessity as that whereby it exists." ✅
- **Pt V, Prop. XXXII, Cor.** — "From the third kind of knowledge necessarily arises
  the intellectual love of God." ✅
- **Pt V, Prop. XXXVI, Note** — "...our salvation, or blessedness, or freedom,
  consists: namely, in the constant and eternal love towards God..." ✅
- **Pt V, Prop. XLII** (the last proposition) — "Blessedness is not the reward of
  virtue, but virtue itself; neither do we rejoice therein, because we control our
  lusts, but, contrariwise, because we rejoice therein, we are able to control our
  lusts." ✅

## Position summaries (paraphrase, each tied to the verified system — no quote marks)
- **Substance monism / Deus sive Natura**: exactly ONE substance, infinite, self-
  caused, which is God = Nature. Everything else (you, a stone, a thought) is a
  *mode* (a passing modification) of that one substance. God is not a person outside
  the world choosing to make it; God is the world's own infinite necessity. Tie:
  Pt I Defs/Props above. NOT pantheism-as-worship and NOT atheism-in-disguise
  flatly — frame as the contested reading it is (SEP notes both charges were
  leveled; he was called both God-intoxicated and atheist).
- **Two attributes, not two things** (the Descartes answer): mind (Thought) and
  matter (Extension) are two *attributes* — two ways the one substance is — not two
  substances. So the Cartesian "how do mind and body interact?" never arises; they
  are the same thing described two ways (Pt II Prop. VII parallelism). This is the
  read's bridge to [Descartes].
- **Geometrical order** (*ordine geometrico*): the Ethics is built like Euclid —
  Definitions, Axioms, Propositions, Demonstrations, each closed with Q.E.D. (Elwes
  prints "Q.E.D."). Frame as a deliberate method claim (truth is deduced, not
  preached), not a gimmick.
- **Strict determinism**: everything follows necessarily from God's nature; nothing
  could have been otherwise. "Free will" is an illusion — the *feeling* of freedom
  is just ignorance of the causes that in fact move us (Pt I Appendix, verbatim
  above). Worked example must be third-person.
- **Conatus**: each thing strives to persist in its own being (Pt III Prop. VI);
  this striving, in a mind, is will, and grounds the account of the affects.
- **The affects (emotions) as ideas**, and freedom as *understanding* necessity: we
  are passive ("in bondage") when we are pushed by emotions whose causes we don't
  grasp; we become active/free by forming adequate ideas of those causes. Freedom is
  not escaping the causal order; it is understanding it.
- **Amor Dei intellectualis** (the intellectual love of God) + **blessedness**:
  the highest knowledge (the "third kind") sees particular things as following from
  God's eternal nature; the joy in that, with the idea of God as its cause, is the
  intellectual love of God, in which salvation/blessedness/freedom consists (Pt V).
  Blessedness is not a reward paid out later for virtue; it IS virtue (Pt V Prop.
  XLII).
- **TPT / freedom of thought**: early historical-critical reading of Scripture (the
  Bible as a human document with a human history, to be read like any other text)
  and the case that a free state must let every person think and say what they
  think (Ch. XX). Folds in the political-philosophy duty.

## Anecdote / caricature discipline (framing)
- The cherem document's specific charges are NOT listed in the surviving text — say
  so; don't assert a tidy list of "what he was banned for" as fact.
- "Spinoza the gentle saint grinding lenses" and "Spinoza the atheist" are both
  inherited caricatures — frame the lens-trade and the God/atheist double-charge as
  contested reception, tell the documented position.
- Lens-dust-caused-his-death = the standard scholarly account, stated as *probable*
  not certain.
- No Diogenes-Laertius-class legend-vector here (he is a documented 17th-c figure),
  but the cherem-charges gap and the cause-of-death are the two "don't overclaim"
  spots.
