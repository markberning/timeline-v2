## What works (keep)

- **The throughline + hook nail the two hard framing facts and keep the seam visible.** "These proofs are Plato's, put in the mouth of his dead teacher" and "the dialogue is honest enough to let the strongest objections very nearly win" are both stated up front, then re-landed at exactly the right beats (Ch1 b2 on Plato writing himself out of the room; Ch5 b1 "Plato armed them"; Ch7 b3 "his, ambitious"). This matches the republic/plato fidelity discipline cleanly and is the single most important comprehension move in the read.
- **The four arguments genuinely build as a sequence with rising stakes — they are NOT a dry catalogue.** This is the prompt's crux test and the draft passes it well. Each argument is explicitly weighed against the last: Argument 1 is flagged "the weakest... a foundation, not the building" (Ch3 b2); recollection is shown to prove only pre-existence, deliberately bracketed with Argument 1 (Ch3 b5); affinity is honestly downgraded to "resemblance... not proof" (Ch4 b3) — and crucially that honesty is set up *as the gap the objections drive into* ("his two sharpest listeners have noticed the gap between 'more like the immortal' and 'actually immortal'"). The reader feels the case getting stronger and shakier at once. Excellent through-line engineering.
- **The objections are landed as genuinely threatening, which is the whole hinge.** Ch5 is the best chapter in the draft. The lyre (Simmias) and the weaver (Cebes) are each explained step-by-step with their own bolded headers, the "a sort of despair" beat is used to register the stakes, "A weaker author would have given Socrates' opponents weak lines. Plato armed them" earns its place, and the two objections are correctly differentiated (Simmias = soul *is* the harmony; Cebes = durability ≠ immortality). The "Durability is not immortality" line crystallizes Cebes perfectly.
- **The dread-and-calm mood lands without being milked.** Ch1 b4's "weeping and philosophy in the same breath" and the read of Socrates' calm as *the dialogue's first piece of evidence* is a real insight, not atmosphere. The death scene (Ch8 b3–b4) is told with the "clinical gentleness" it names — the cold climbing from the feet, the uncovered face, "There was no answer to this question" — and lets the material be moving on its own.
- **The set-pieces are all present and correctly weighted:** practice-for-death with the anti-suicide guard rail (Ch2, the soldier-at-his-post), recollection tied to the Forms (Ch3), the Anaxagoras disappointment with the sitting-in-prison example (Ch6), the final argument run as fire/cold + three/even → soul/death (Ch7), the closing myth correctly flagged as myth-not-doctrine (Ch8 b1–b2), and the last words handled exactly right — laid out as a genuine interpretive crux with three readings (death-as-cure / literal debt / Nietzsche) and none asserted (Ch8 b5).
- **The brk block is a real break.** before/after labels ("a wisp... that thins out and dies" vs. "the one thing in you that *can't* die, and there's an argument for it") are concrete and contrastive; the third paragraph states the historical novelty plainly (first to *argue*, not assert or sing). Strong.
- **Voice is plain, smart, and conversational throughout.** "It is a foundation, not the building." "Durability is not immortality." "The man being executed is steadying the people watching." No manufactured drama, no meta-narrator scaffolding ("keep one hand on that"-type tics the Apology gate flagged are largely absent here). Em-dash density (~15/1000 words) is *below* republic.ts (~17/1000) and the em-dashes are overwhelmingly rhetorical breaks, not definitions — so the corpus-wide em-dash-for-definition problem does NOT recur here. Good.

**Verdict-relevant:** there are no storytelling BLOCKERS. The dread/calm, the through-line, the four-arguments-as-rising-sequence, and every set-piece all land. The blockers below are all clarity/comprehension — chiefly the same systematic missing-pronunciation-gloss gap the sibling Apology and Symposium gates flagged as a blocker against this exact bar.

## BLOCKERS

### 1. BLOCKER — Pronunciation glosses are missing on most hard Greek names (the same systematic gap the Apology + Symposium gates blocked on)
**Location:** corpus-wide; first offenders Ch1, Ch2 b4, Ch3, Ch5, Ch8.
**Problem:** WRITING-RULES "include pronunciations for unfamiliar names" is a hard rule, and the WORK-read bar honors it relentlessly (republic.ts glosses Cephalus, Polemarchus, Thrasymachus, Glaucon, Adeimantus, Kallipolis, timocracy, oligarchy, aporia). The Apology and Symposium gates each declared the missing-gloss gap a BLOCKER against this bar. This draft glosses **four** names — Echecrates (eh-KEH-kra-teez), Phaedo (FEE-doh), Xanthippe (zan-TIP-ee), Anaxagoras (an-ax-AG-or-as) — and that's the right format, but it then leaves the rest of the hard roster bare. The prompt's own checklist (Cebes, Simmias, Echecrates, Phlius, Anaxagoras, Xanthippe, Asclepius) is only half-covered. A zero-knowledge reader cannot say **Cebes, Simmias, Phlius, Asclepius, Acheron, Pyriphlegethon, Cocytus, Tartarus, Cephalus**(n/a here)**, Apollodorus**, or **Theseus/Elis** (the last two are arguably familiar enough to skip).
**Rewrite:** Add a `Name (stress-CAPS)` gloss on first use for at least:
- **Cebes** (SEE-beez) — first real use is Ch2 b4 ("His friends Simmias and Cebes press...") — gloss there, at the genuine first mention, not only at the Ch5 bold intro.
- **Simmias** (SIM-ee-as) — same, Ch2 b4 first use.
- **Phlius** (FLY-us) — Ch1 b1 ("a man from the town of Phlius").
- **Asclepius** (as-KLEE-pee-us) — the epigraph attribution / Ch8 b4 last words / Ch8 b5 first prose use ("Asclepius was the Greek god of healing").
- **Acheron** (AK-er-on), **Pyriphlegethon** (pir-ih-FLEG-eth-on), **Cocytus** (koh-SY-tus), **Tartarus** (TAR-tar-us) — Ch8 b2 underworld-rivers list (Pyriphlegethon especially is unsayable cold).
- **Apollodorus** (uh-pol-oh-DOR-us) — only if you keep him; he is named in the fact pack but does not appear in the draft prose, so no action needed unless added.
Skip already-familiar names (Socrates, Plato, Athens, Crito, Homer, Delos, Theseus, Elis are defensibly skippable; Crito and Delos are short and high-frequency). Match the bar's `Name (stress-CAPS)` format, not IPA.

### 2. BLOCKER — "hemlock" is used from the hero caption onward but not defined as *poison* until Chapter 8
**Location:** hero `cap` ("about to drink the hemlock"); hook ("the hemlock was finally going to be brought"); Ch1 b4 ("face the hemlock without flinching"); Ch2 b3 ("as the hemlock now summons Socrates"). First actual gloss is Ch8 b3 ("the cup of hemlock, the poison").
**Problem:** "Hemlock" is on the prompt's required-define list and it is load-bearing from the very first surface a reader sees (the hero caption). A zero-knowledge reader who does not already know hemlock is a lethal poison cannot parse "the hemlock was finally going to be brought" as *the execution is about to happen* — the whole hook turns on it. The definition arrives seven beats too late.
**Rewrite:** Gloss at the **first prose use in the hook**: "...and the hemlock — the poison that would kill him — was finally going to be brought." (Or, if you want the cleaner first-surface fix, add it to the hero caption: "about to drink the hemlock (the poison used for his execution)". The Ch8 "the cup of hemlock, the poison" can then stay as a light re-anchor or drop "the poison.")

### 3. BLOCKER — the Delos-ship delay is *narrated* well but the reader is never told plainly **why** a religious voyage forbids executions, so the dialogue's whole single-day premise rests on an unexplained taboo
**Location:** hook ("while that ship was away the law forbade any execution"); Ch1 b3 ("during which the city is not allowed to be polluted by public executions").
**Problem:** The mechanism is *stated* (ship out → no executions → Socrates waits) but never *explained* — the reader is given the Jowett phrase "polluted by public executions" and left to infer the logic. For a zero-knowledge reader the connection between "a sacred ship is at sea" and "therefore no one may be executed" is not obvious; "pollution" here is a specific Greek religious concept (*miasma* — ritual defilement that a killing would bring on the city during a holy season), and without one clause naming it, the premise that organizes the entire dialogue reads as an arbitrary rule. This is the "explain the mechanism, not just the outcome" rule.
**Rewrite:** In Ch1 b3, after "during which the city is not allowed to be polluted by public executions," add a short gloss: "— a death during the sacred season would have counted as a religious defilement of the whole city, so the execution simply had to wait." One clause converts a quoted taboo into an understood reason.

### 4. BLOCKER — Simmias and Cebes are introduced (bolded, with Thebes + Pythagorean context) in Ch5, but they have already been *named and used* in Ch2 and Ch3 with no introduction at all
**Location:** First mention Ch2 b4 ("His friends Simmias and Cebes press exactly this point. Cebes puts the common fear bluntly..."); used again throughout Ch3 ("Then the living, whether things or persons, Cebes..."); the actual *introduction* ("**Simmias** and **Cebes**, two young men from Thebes trained in Pythagorean thought") does not arrive until Ch5 b1.
**Problem:** "Set the scene before introducing new people" — the reader meets two named interlocutors three chapters before being told who they are, why they matter, or that they are the dialogue's two key challengers. By Ch5 the bolded "introduction" reads oddly, because the reader has been living with these names for two chapters. The names also carry no pronunciation gloss at the Ch2 first use (see #1).
**Rewrite:** Move a compressed version of the Ch5 introduction to the Ch2 b4 first use: "His friends **Simmias** (SIM-ee-as) and **Cebes** (SEE-beez) — two young men from Thebes, both trained in Pythagorean thought, who become the dialogue's two main challengers — press exactly this point." Then the Ch5 b1 passage can shrink to "Socrates' two main interlocutors do not nod along," since they're already known. This also fixes the bare "interlocutors" (see #6) by giving the reader the people before the fancy word for them.

## POLISH

### 5. POLISH — "the second best mode of enquiring into the cause" / *deuteros plous* is handled correctly but at length; trim so the label-disambiguation doesn't outweigh the idea
**Location:** Ch6 b3.
**Problem:** This is the prompt's flagged term and the draft handles the Jowett-vs-"second sailing" distinction exactly right (Jowett's phrase in quotes; "second sailing" attributed to the Greek/convention, never to Jowett — matches the fact-pack PRECISION-NOTE). No accuracy problem. The only note is craft: the paragraph spends ~90 words on *which label to use* and the rower-when-the-wind-dies image, which is more meta-apparatus than a newcomer needs to grasp *what the method is* (they have to wait for b4 to find out it's "the turn to the Forms"). It reads slightly like footnote-in-prose.
**Rewrite (optional):** Keep the disambiguation but tighten it to one sentence and let the rower image land faster: "Scholars nickname this passage the *deuteros plous*, the 'second sailing' — the image of a sailor who, when the wind dies, takes to the oars, the harder second-best way home. That nickname is the Greek and later translators', not Jowett's, whose words are 'the second best mode of enquiring into the cause.'" Then go straight to b4's "What is this second-best method? It is the turn to the Forms." Lower priority — it is correct as written, just front-loaded.

### 6. POLISH — "interlocutors" is used bare; a zero-knowledge reader may not have the word
**Location:** Ch5 b1 ("Socrates' two main interlocutors — **Simmias** and **Cebes**...").
**Problem:** "Interlocutor" is a register-jump in an otherwise plain-spoken read; the surrounding sentence makes it recoverable, so this is borderline. If #4 is taken (introducing Simmias/Cebes in Ch2), this sentence gets rewritten anyway and the word can simply go.
**Rewrite:** Use "the two friends" or "his two main challengers" instead of "interlocutors," or define on first use ("his two main interlocutors — the friends doing the actual back-and-forth with him"). Lowest priority.

### 7. POLISH — "attunement" is glossed in place (good) but verify the lyre-objection's payoff term "harmony" stays anchored to it
**Location:** Ch5 b2 ("The *harmony* — the tuning, the attunement — of a lyre...").
**Problem:** Not a defect — the gloss is present and clear ("the tuning, the attunement"), and "due proportionate admixture" is unpacked as the body's hot/cold/wet/dry in balance. Flagging only that the chapter then alternates "harmony" / "attunement" / "tuning" across b2 and the answer in b4; a newcomer tracks it because the gloss is up front, but keep "harmony" as the lead term once introduced so the three synonyms don't read as three different ideas. No change required; included for the clarity record.

### 8. POLISH — the hero/caption carry "[VERIFY]" placeholders (expected at this stage), not a gate fail
**Location:** `hero.fig` and `hero.cap`.
**Problem:** Both are `[VERIFY]` stubs for the media gate (David's *Death of Socrates*), per the file's own quote-doctrine header — correct for this pipeline stage, the media/image-rights gate owns it. The caption prose itself is strong ("a death scene that is really a philosophy lesson"). No action for this gate beyond noting that the hemlock gloss (#2) should be added to the caption if the caption is the first surface a reader hits.
