## VOICE — HARD BLOCKERS

**Location:** throughout. Count: 87 em-dashes in reader-facing content (throughline + hook + brk + all blocks), of which 8 are legitimate epigraph attributions (`— Aristotle, *Politics*...`). That leaves **~79 narration em-dashes**. The standing rule (`feedback_philosophy_voice_plain` point 6) sets the target at essentially zero, "at most a tiny handful." The cleaned Republic bar runs 11 total across a comparable 8-chapter read. This draft is ~7× over the bar and is a REVISE blocker, not polish.

**Rewrite rule for the reviser:** do a mechanical pass converting every narration em-dash. A dash framing a parenthetical aside → parentheses. A dash introducing a definition/gloss → parentheses. Leave the 8 epigraph `— Author` attributions and any em-dash inside a quoted Jowett passage untouched (there are none inside the quotes here, but the rule stands).

### 2. SECOND-PERSON COACHING — BLOCKER (Ch4)
**Location:** Ch4, block 5 (line 146): "**Hold the two axes in mind — who rules, and for whose benefit — and the scheme stays clear; forget them and you will read it exactly backwards.**"
**Problem:** "Hold ... in mind" is a direct second-person imperative, and "forget them and you will read it" coaches the reader on how to read — exactly the banned move (point 1 of the voice rule). It also carries two more em-dashes.
**Rewrite:** "The two axes are the key to the scheme: who rules, and for whose benefit. Read his labels with modern meanings instead and the table inverts." (states the caution without commanding the reader).

### 3. GENERIC-YOU / MILD CHATTINESS — POLISH (verify against the bar's restraint)
- **Ch7, block 1 (line 203):** "You cannot design the best city until you know what the best city is *for* ... so you settle what a good life is first and build the city to serve it." Generic-you, not a hard imperative, but the bar avoids it. Recast impersonally: "There is no designing the best city without first knowing what it is *for*... so the good life is settled first and the city built to serve it."
- Note: "his own *picture* of the best state" (Ch7) and "**picture**" elsewhere are nouns, not imperatives — fine. No "notice/watch/consider/imagine/feel" imperatives elsewhere. Good.

### 4. SELF-LABELED FRAMING / MILD META — POLISH
The draft leans on an "honest read" / "has to be stated" framing repeatedly. It is far milder than the banned "a small honesty note" and is arguably load-bearing given the slavery content, so most can stay — but it recurs enough to feel like a tic. Trim the throat-clearing wrappers where the point can simply be stated:
- Ch2, block 2 (line 90): "**It is worth showing his actual argument rather than a caricature of it, because** the reasoning is what reveals..." → just present the argument: "His actual argument, not a caricature of it, reveals how deeply the claim is woven in."
- Ch2, block 3 (line 93): "it should be recorded **without being mistaken for a retreat**" — fine, keep.
- Ch4, block 5 (line 146): "is a trap for a modern reader, and **it has to be flagged**:" → "is a trap for a modern reader:" (the colon does the work).
- Ch7, block 3 (line 209): "**It is worth pausing on** what this does to the worry..." → "This bears on the worry, raised back in Book I, that Aristotle makes the state swallow the individual." 
These are POLISH, not blockers — but doing them tightens the draft toward the bar.

## STORYTELLING — strong, minor flags

The through-line is genuinely well carried and is the draft's biggest strength. The Plato-vs-Aristotle method contrast (imagine the ideal city vs. collect the real ones) is set in the hook and brk and then pays off in every chapter (Ch3's plurality critique, Ch5's middle class "out of the empirical temper," Ch6's "clinician's manual" vs. Plato's "parable of decline," Ch8's closing "from the ideal city to the real ones"). The slavery chapter is placed where the text places it (Book I, Ch2), given its real Q-I→Q-J→Q-K argument, and refuses to launder it. The six-constitution scheme is the dramatic centerpiece it should be. The unfinished-mid-sentence ending is a lovely close.

- **POLISH — Ch4 block 3 is the one genuinely listy stretch.** The roll-call of the three correct forms ("monarchy... aristocracy... a constitution... polity") is necessarily enumerative; it reads cleanly enough, but watch that the em-dash conversion (#1) doesn't leave it as a flat list of definitions. Keep the "rule by X, for whose benefit Y" rhythm so the two-axis logic stays felt, not just stated.
- **POLISH — the "achievement and grave error in the same hand/breath/book" image recurs** in throughline, brk ¶3, and Ch2. Three times is one too many for the same metaphor; vary or drop the brk-¶3 instance.

No flat or aimless stretches otherwise. Storytelling axis: PASS on its own.

## CLARITY / COMPREHENSION — strong, a few gaps

Inline definitions and pronunciation glosses are handled well for a zero-knowledge reader: *polis* (POH-lis), *oikos* (OY-kos), *zōon politikon*, *logos*, *oligarchy* (OL-ih-gar-kee), *stasis* (STAH-sis) all glossed on first use. The six constitutions are laid out cleanly by the two axes (who rules × for whose benefit), and the deviant-twin pairing is explicit.

- **GOOD / required item CONFIRMED:** the "Aristotle's democracy ≠ modern democracy" caution is made explicit and prominent (Ch4 block 5): "modern liberal democracy actually maps closer to Aristotle's *polity* than to his \"democracy.\"" This is the single most important clarity requirement for this read and it is met. (Once the second-person rewrite in #2 lands, keep this content intact — only the "Hold ... in mind / you will read it backwards" framing changes.)
- **POLISH — "polity" gloss timing.** "polity" first appears in the brk ("a broad-middle-class \"polity\"") with no gloss; it is properly introduced in Ch4 block 3 ("the one usually called **polity** (in Jowett's wording, \"constitutional government\")"). The brk is the reader's first encounter. Add a three-word gloss at the brk first use, e.g. "a broad-middle-class \"polity\" (his good popular regime)", so the break section stands alone.
- **POLISH — "faction" carries a lot of weight in Ch5 and is defined there** ("the splitting of a city into hostile camps that fight rather than share rule") — good. But *stasis* in Ch6 is the same concept under the Greek name; consider one back-reference ("the *stasis* — the faction of Chapter 5 —" no, avoid the dash) so the reader connects them. Minor.
- **POLISH — "the doctrine of the mean."** Ch5 defines it well inline ("virtue lies between two vices, courage between cowardice and recklessness"). It also appears in the brk ("the doctrine of the mean from his ethics") undefined. Same fix as polity: a three-word gloss at the brk, or accept that brk is a teaser.
- No undefined hard terms found that would lose a newcomer. "*Nicomachean Ethics*" (Ch7) appears once, glossed in context as "his ... Ethics spends its length describing" — adequate.

Clarity axis: PASS on its own (with the brk-gloss polish).
