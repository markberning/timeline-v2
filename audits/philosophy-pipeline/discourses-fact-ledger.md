## QUOTATIONS (all Carter 1758, verbatim, each load-checked)

### Q1 — *Enchiridion* §1 (the dichotomy of control — the work's signature)
> "Of Things, some are in our Power, and others not. In our Power are Opinion, Pursuit, Desire, Aversion, and in one Word, whatever are our own Actions. Not in our Power, are Body, Property, Reputation, Command, and, in one Word, whatever are not our own Actions."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. Source: Wikisource, *All the Works of Epictetus … / The Encheiridion* (Carter).
- URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/All_the_Works_of_Epictetus,_Which_Are_Now_Extant/The_Encheiridion — HTTP 200.
- Carter's 18th-c noun capitalization preserved inside the quotation.

### Q2 — *Enchiridion* §5 (men disturbed by judgments, not things)
> "Men are disturbed, not by Things, but by the Principles and Notions, which they form concerning Things. Death, for Instance, is not terrible, else it would have appeared so to Socrates."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim (incl. the Socrates/Death continuation). Source: Wikisource (Carter). URL as Q1 — HTTP 200.
- TRAP: the popular "views which they take" is **Higginson**, NOT Carter. This read quotes Carter's "Principles and Notions."

### Q3 — *Enchiridion* §8 (wish events as they happen)
> "Require not Things to happen as you wish; but wish them to happen as they do happen; and you will go on well."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED (Wikisource OCR shows "with" for "wish" — a scan error; the printed Carter word is "wish," confirmed by sense + parallel Higginson). URL as Q1 — HTTP 200.

### Q4 — *Enchiridion* §17 (the actor in the play — role ethics)
> "Remember that you are an Actor in a Drama, of such a Kind as the Author pleases to make it. If short, of a short one; if long, of a long one. If it be his Pleasure you should act a poor Man, a Cripple, a Governor, or a private Person, see that you act it naturally. For this is your Business, to act well the Character assigned you: to chuse it, is another's."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim (FULL section, incl. the closing "to chuse it, is another's"). Source: Wikisource (Carter). URL as Q1 — HTTP 200.
- NOTE: the prior thinker read used §17 as PARAPHRASE only; this work read quotes it in full (verified above), which is the deeper-altitude move the work read is for.

### Q5 — *Discourses* I.1 (chapter title + the one faculty in our power)
**Carter chapter title, verbatim:** "Of the Things which are, and of those which are not, in our own Power."
**Carter key line, verbatim:**
> "this most excellent and superior Faculty alone, a right Use of the Appearances of Things, the Gods have placed in our own Power"

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. Source: Wikisource, *All the Works … / Book 1 / Chapter 1* (Carter).
- URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/All_the_Works_of_Epictetus,_Which_Are_Now_Extant/Book_1/Chapter_1 — HTTP 200.

### Q6 — *Discourses* I.1 (the lame leg / the unconquerable will)
> "You will fetter my Leg; but not Jupiter himself can get the better of my Choice."

- Status: ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. Source: Wikisource, Book 1 / Chapter 1 (Carter). URL as Q5 — HTTP 200.
- This is the line spoken in the dialogue with the tyrant in *Discourses* I.1; Carter renders *prohairesis* as "Choice." The read uses it as the will-cannot-be-chained passage.

### Q7 — "Bear and forbear" (NOT in the surviving text — framed as Aulus Gellius)
- Status: 🟡 LEGEND/FRAGMENT — FRAME IT. NOT in the *Enchiridion* or surviving *Discourses*.
- Basis: **Aulus Gellius, *Attic Nights* (Noctes Atticae) XVII.19**, reporting Epictetus summed philosophy in two Greek words: **ἀνέχου καὶ ἀπέχου** (*anechou kai apechou*), Latin *sustine et abstine*, "bear and forbear." Survives as Epictetus **Fragment 10** (Schweighäuser). Read attributes it explicitly to Aulus Gellius, never to the *Discourses*.

## TECHNICAL TERMS (defined in-read on first use; tied to the documented doctrine)

| Term | Gloss | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **dichotomy of control** | some things are up to us (judgments, desire, will, our own acts), some are not (body, property, reputation, others, outcomes); peace = wanting only what is up to us | ✅ doctrine of *Ench.* §1 + *Disc.* I.1. "Dichotomy of control" is the MODERN label (20th–21st-c revival, e.g. William B. Irvine 2009); read uses it as the standard name and notes Epictetus's own framing is "what is and is not in our power." |
| **prohairesis** | the faculty of choice / moral will — the deciding part of a person that judges, assents, chooses; the real self, the one thing fully one's own | ✅ Epictetus's central technical term (IEP, SEP). Translators render it "will," "volition," "choice," "moral character." Carter renders it "Choice" in Q6. |
| **externals / indifferents** (*adiaphora*) | things neither good nor bad in themselves — wealth, health, reputation, even life and death — because none makes its owner good or bad | ✅ standard Stoic doctrine; consistent with the Marcus + Epictetus-thinker reads. |
| **preferred indifferents** (*proēgmena*) | indifferents still rational to pursue (health over sickness, wealth over poverty) — worth having, never worth one's character or peace | ✅ standard Stoic doctrine. |
| **the three disciplines** | desire (*orexis*), action/impulse (*hormē*), assent (*sunkatathesis*) — Epictetus's three fields of training | ✅ The three *topoi* are explicit in *Disc.* III.2; the influential modern systematization (the "three disciplines") is **Pierre Hadot**'s / **A. A. Long**'s. Read names them as Epictetus's three fields and credits the modern tidy numbering to scholars (framing, not quote). |
| **askesis** | training/exercise — philosophy practiced as a discipline, not learned as a body of theory | ✅ standard (Hadot, "spiritual exercises"). Read uses it plainly as "training." |
| **role ethics / "play well the part assigned"** | life as a play: you don't choose your role or its length, only how well you act it | ✅ *Ench.* §17 (Q4, now quoted in full). |
| **right use of impressions** (*phantasiai*) | an "appearance"/impression is how a situation first strikes you; the one faculty in your power is what you DO with it (endorse, question, reject) | ✅ *Disc.* I.1 (Q5); the discipline of assent. |
