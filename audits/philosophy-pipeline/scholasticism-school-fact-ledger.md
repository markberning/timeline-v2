# Fact ledger — "Scholasticism" SCHOOL read (kind = TRADITION)

The school analog of the shipped Platonism school read. Scope: the Scholastic
tradition, roughly 1100–1400 — the cathedral schools and universities, the
scholastic method, Anselm → Abelard / Peter Lombard → the recovery of Aristotle →
Aquinas → the problem of universals → Scotus and Ockham → the afterlife and
caricature. Authored ONLY from this ledger + the already-verified
`faith-reason-fact-ledger.md` (the era-2 read shares almost every figure and quote)
and `platonism-school.read.ts` as the structural template.

Quote doctrine: every quoted line is VERIFIED PD wording, keyed below to a URL that
resolves and is the right document + translation. Anselm via Sidney Norton Deane
(1903, PD). Aquinas via the Fathers of the English Dominican Province (1920, PD,
hosted at newadvent.org). Aristotle via W. D. Ross (PD, Internet Classics Archive).
Abelard / Scotus / Ockham are PARAPHRASE-ONLY where no clean PD English of the exact
line exists (the Ockham razor formula is the one verified exception, via Thorburn
1918 + SEP, used to DEBUNK the apocryphal Latin). The apocryphal "angels on the head
of a pin" is framed AS the caricature it is, never asserted as a real scholastic
question. The era-2 ledger already verified every shared quote word-for-word; this
ledger re-pins each to its URL and adds the school-altitude framing.

## Dates / biographical facts (all from era-2 ledger §1 + standard reference)
- **Anselm** 1033–1109; monk then abbot at Bec (Normandy), later Archbishop of
  Canterbury. → faith-reason §1.
- **Peter Abelard** 1079–1142; Paris dialectician; condemned Soissons 1121 and Sens
  1141; died under Cluny's protection. → faith-reason §1, Ch5.
- **Peter Lombard** c. 1096–1160; bishop of Paris; the *Sentences* (Latin
  *Sententiae*, "Four Books of Sentences"), c. 1150s, became the standard theology
  textbook every later master lectured on. → faith-reason §0.3, Ch5.
- **Averroes** (Ibn Rushd) 1126–1198, Córdoba; "the Commentator." → faith-reason Ch4.
- **Aristotle** 384–322 BC. → confirmed in `src/lib/philosophy-data.ts` (id `aristotle`).
- **Albertus Magnus** (Albert the Great) c. 1200–1280; Dominican; Aquinas's teacher
  at Cologne; commented on the whole Aristotelian corpus. → faith-reason §0.4, Ch6.
- **Thomas Aquinas** c. 1225–1274; Dominican; *Summa Theologiae* 1265/66–1273
  (unfinished); died en route to the Council of Lyon at Fossanova. → faith-reason §1, Ch6.
- **1277 condemnation:** 7 March 1277 (3 years to the day after Aquinas's death);
  Bishop Étienne Tempier of Paris; 219 propositions; some brushed Aquinas's own
  teaching. → faith-reason §2, Ch6.
- **Duns Scotus** c. 1266–1308; Franciscan; "the Subtle Doctor" (*Doctor Subtilis*);
  Oxford → Paris → Cologne. → faith-reason §1, Ch7. (Not a thinker hub — paraphrase
  only, no inbound link.)
- **William of Ockham** c. 1287–1347; English Franciscan; lectured on the *Sentences*
  at Oxford; "the Venerable Beginner" (*Venerabilis Inceptor*); summoned to Avignon
  1324, fled to Emperor Ludwig of Bavaria 1328; died April 1347 (BEFORE the Black
  Death reached Munich — never say he died of plague). → faith-reason §1, Ch7.
- **Universities:** *universitas* = a corporation/guild of masters and students.
  Bologna (law), Paris (theology + arts), Oxford. Foundation "dates" are later
  conventions (Bologna's 1088 is a 19th-c. anniversary; Paris c. 1200; Oxford
  coalesced late 1100s). → faith-reason §2, §8.

## Quotations (VERIFIED — text, translation, URL)

1. **Aristotle, *Metaphysics* I.1 (980a), trans. W. D. Ross (PD):**
   "All men by nature desire to know."
   - URL: https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.1.i.html — HTTP 200.
   - Use: epigraph / the appetite scholasticism trusted; era-2 §3 already verified.

2. **Anselm, *Proslogion* (Proslogium) ch. 1, trans. Sidney Norton Deane (1903, PD):**
   "I do not seek to understand that I may believe, but I believe in order to
   understand." (Latin *credo ut intelligam*.)
   - URL: https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/anselm-proslogium.asp — HTTP 200;
     wording confirmed verbatim on the page; translator named "Sidney Norton Deane, B.A."
   - Use: the motto of faith-seeking-understanding; the method's charter.

3. **Anselm, *Proslogion* ch. 2, trans. Deane:** the ontological argument's subject
   phrase, verbatim — "a being than which nothing greater can be conceived."
   - URL: same as above — HTTP 200; phrase confirmed verbatim.
   - Use: the worked example of the argument; framed as Anselm's, with "ontological
     argument" flagged as Kant's much-later label (era-2 §2 Trap 1, §8 anachronism).

4. **Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.2, a.3 (the Five Ways), trans. Fathers of the
   English Dominican Province (1920, PD):** the First Way's close —
   "Therefore it is necessary to arrive at a first mover, put in motion by no other;
   and this everyone understands to be God."
   And the example — "as the staff moves only because it is put in motion by the hand."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1002.htm — HTTP 200; both confirmed verbatim.
   - Use: the worked example of the scholastic method in full operation (objections →
     *sed contra* → respondeo → replies). Honest seam kept: the argument reaches "a
     first mover," not the God of any particular scripture — Aquinas knew this.

5. **Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.1, a.8, trans. Dominican Fathers (1920, PD):**
   "grace does not destroy nature but perfects it" (the standard rendering of
   *gratia non tollit naturam sed perficit*).
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1001.htm — HTTP 200 (q.1 page; era-2 §3
     verified the line against this article).
   - Use: the one-line statement of the whole synthesis (faith completes, not cancels,
     reason).

6. **Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q.2, a.3, obj. 2, trans. Dominican Fathers:** the
   parsimony objection Aquinas states against himself — "it is superfluous to suppose
   that what can be accounted for by a few principles has been produced by many."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1002.htm — HTTP 200; era-2 §3 verified.
   - Use: shows the razor pointed at God ~60 years before Ockham, by Aquinas's own
     hand, inside the method (the strongest objection always gets stated).

7. **Averroes, *Decisive Treatise*, trans. (Jamil-ur-Rehman rendering, PD-era):**
   reason "makes the observation and consideration of creation by reason obligatory,"
   and "truth cannot contradict truth."
   - era-2 §3 Ch4 (VERIFIED there); used at school altitude as the import that lit the
     crisis. (Quoted sparingly; the load-bearing Averroes point here is the
     transmission, not new wording.)

## Paraphrase-only (no clean PD English of the exact line — NEVER set in quotes)
- **Abelard, *Sic et Non* prologue** — the doubt-to-inquiry program ("by doubting we
  come to inquiry, and by inquiry we perceive the truth"). era-2 §3 Ch5 quoted this in
  the Robinson rendering; at school altitude this read PARAPHRASES it (the method's
  engine: line the authorities up for and against, withhold the easy answer) to keep
  the school read distinct from the era read and avoid leaning on a borderline-PD
  rendering. Title + the 158-questions structure are facts (era-2 §2, §3 Ch5).
- **Peter Lombard, the *Sentences*** — described, not quoted (it is a structured
  arrangement of authorities, the textbook the disputation method grew out of).
- **Duns Scotus** — univocity of being (that "being" means the same thing said of God
  and of creatures), the formal distinction, the primacy of will — all PARAPHRASED;
  no PD English leaned on. era-2 r2 fact-check confirmed the univocity one-liner as
  the link that motivates Ockham.
- **Ockham** — nominalism and the razor: PARAPHRASED, with one verified DEBUNK. The
  textbook Latin *entia non sunt multiplicanda praeter necessitatem* ("entities must
  not be multiplied beyond necessity") is NOWHERE in Ockham's texts (SEP); it first
  appears in John Punch, 1639. Ockham's actual formula, *Pluralitas non est ponenda
  sine necessitate* ("plurality is not to be posited without necessity"), via W. M.
  Thorburn, "The Myth of Occam's Razor," *Mind* 27 (1918), and the SEP entry on
  Ockham. era-2 §3 Ch7 + §9.2 verified. Used here exactly as the debunk: the razor
  bears his name the way America bears Amerigo's.

## Apocrypha framed AS apocrypha (the potted-summary / inherited-caricature traps)
- **"How many angels can dance on the head of a pin?"** — there is no scholastic text
  that poses this question; it is a post-medieval polemical invention (the earliest
  needles-pin gibes are 17th-c. Protestant jabs at scholastic subtlety, e.g. the line
  often traced to Ralph Cudworth / William Sclater and later Isaac D'Israeli's
  *Curiosities of Literature*). The read names it AS the caricature and contrasts it
  with the real questions the method asked. era-2 §9.1 (blacklist — "angels-on-a-pinhead
  not used as fact"). NEVER asserted as a real scholastic debate.
- **"Scholastic" as a put-down** — the pejorative sense ("dry, hair-splitting,
  pedantic") is a Renaissance-humanist and Enlightenment coinage thrown BACK at the
  schoolmen, not the word's medieval meaning (a *scholasticus* was simply a teacher in
  a school). Framed honestly as later contempt, then the real rigor restored.
- **"The Dark Ages" / medieval sterility** — named as the wrong frame; the
  scholastic method is precisely the rigor the caricature denies (era-2 §9.3).
- **Aquinas "official philosopher of the Church"** — that status is the OUTCOME of Leo
  XIII's 1879 *Aeterni Patris*, not a crowning in Aquinas's lifetime (era-2 §2 Trap 2).
  Used only if the afterlife chapter touches it; framed as the later canonization of
  his thought.

## Anachronism / first-claim guards (framing gate)
- "Ontological argument" = Kant's term, six centuries after Anselm — flagged.
- "Ockham's razor" as Ockham phrased it = the verified debunk above.
- "Double truth" = NOT Averroes; it is the charge in Tempier's 1277 preamble, and
  modern scholars doubt even the Latin Averroists held it as a doctrine (era-2 §9.7).
  Touched only lightly here; framed as the charge, not Averroes's view.
- The "scholastic method" is described as the genre's shared form (quaestio → videtur
  quod / objections → sed contra → respondeo → replies), not credited to one inventor;
  it grew out of Abelard's *Sic et Non* model + the *Sentences* commentary tradition +
  the university disputation. No "X invented the method" claim.
- Universities described as a 12th–13th-c. emergence (guilds of masters/students), not
  a single founding event; foundation dates flagged as later conventions.
