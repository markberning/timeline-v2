## Bibliographic facts
- ✅ Title: *Das Kapital. Kritik der politischen Oekonomie*, Volume I (*Erster Band*),
  subtitle *Buch I: Der Produktionsprocess des Kapitals* ("The Process of Production of
  Capital"). First German edition **1867**, Hamburg, **Verlag von Otto Meissner**. [Confirmed
  by direct view of the 1867 title-page scan used as hero, File:Kapital_titel_bd1.png; the
  scan also bears "New-York: L. W. Schmidt, 24 Barclay-Street."]
- ✅ Volume I was the only volume Marx finished and published in his lifetime. Engels edited
  Volumes II (1885) and III (1894) from Marx's manuscripts after his death (1883). [Standard;
  stated in the hero caption.]
- ✅ Marx (1818–1883), German exile in London from 1849; did the bulk of the research in the
  British Museum Reading Room across the 1850s–60s, working through political economy and the
  British government "blue books" (factory inspectors' reports, parliamentary commissions).
  [Standard biography; the British Museum reading-room work and the use of official factory
  reports are uncontroversial and load-bearing for the Ch1 + Ch5 argument. The "regular seat"
  detail is the well-worn tradition, phrased softly ("had a regular seat"), not asserted as a
  documented seat number.]
- ✅ Classical-economics lineage: Adam Smith and David Ricardo held a labour theory of value;
  Marx took it over and pushed it further. [Standard history of economic thought; this is the
  brief's "the labour theory of value is contested by later economics, while explaining why it
  does the philosophical work Marx needs" framing, handled in the break + Ch6 close.]

## Position summaries (paraphrase, no quote marks in prose)
- ✅ **Use-value vs exchange-value.** Use-value = a thing's concrete usefulness (qualitative,
  incommensurable across goods); exchange-value = the proportion in which it trades for other
  goods (quantitative). The riddle: incommensurable use-values still exchange in fixed ratios,
  so a common third element must exist = human labour. [ch01; standard.]
- ✅ **Labour theory of value + the "lazy worker" repair.** Value = congealed human labour, but
  measured as SOCIALLY NECESSARY labour-time (average conditions/skill/intensity), so a slow
  worker creates no extra value, and a labour-saving machine LOWERS a good's value. The coat /
  20-yards-of-linen worked example is Marx's own running example in ch01 (coat vs linen);
  figures (halving linen's labour → 40 yards per coat) are an illustrative extension consistent
  with the rule, not a quoted Marx figure. [ch01.]
- ✅ **Money** as the universal value-equivalent (historically gold) emerging from commodity
  exchange. [ch02–03; standard; the read keeps this brief and does not over-claim the money
  chapters, which the brief did not ask to walk.]
- ✅ **M-C-M′ / capital = self-expanding value.** Capital defined NOT as money or machines but
  as value thrown into circulation to return enlarged (M-C-M′); the surplus is surplus-value.
  Hoarded money ≠ capital. [ch04; quotes 6–9.]
- ✅ **The circulation dilemma** (why surplus can't arise in exchange): equal values trade for
  equal values, so no new value in a fair swap; cheating only redistributes value, can't
  explain a class-wide surplus; yet the capitalist begins and ends in the market. Resolution:
  a commodity bought at full value whose USE creates more value than it cost. [ch05–06; this is
  Marx's actual setup; standard reading.]
- ✅ **Labour-power as that special commodity.** Definition (quote 10); its value = socially
  necessary labour-time to reproduce the worker (subsistence) = the wage; Marx ASSUMES it is
  paid in full (exploitation occurs with market rules obeyed). Use-value of labour-power = its
  capacity to create value, which exceeds its own cost (quote 14, the hinge). [ch06–07.]
- ✅ **Surplus value + the split working day.** Necessary labour (worker reproduces own wage) vs
  surplus labour (worker produces surplus-value for free); surplus-value = unpaid labour; rate
  of surplus-value = surplus/necessary = exact measure of exploitation (quotes 16–17). The
  3-shilling / 6-hour / 12-hour worked sum is Marx's own (quote 15). [ch07, ch09.] THIS IS THE
  ANALYTICAL HEART per the prompt; given the most space (Ch4).
- ✅ **The working day** (Ch5): structural drive to lengthen the day (vampire image, quote 18);
  documented from blue books; Mary Anne Walkley case verbatim (quote 19); systemic, not
  personal cruelty (competition); Après moi le déluge (quote 20); day shortens by worker
  organization + state compulsion, not employer kindness. [ch10.]
- ✅ **Accumulation** = surplus-value reconverted into capital; the growth loop. [ch24-25;
  stated at Ch6 open at the level the brief asked.]
- ✅ **So-called primitive accumulation** (Ch6): the chicken-and-egg of where the propertyless
  workers + the first capital came from; the economists' thrift fable derided as "original sin"
  (quote 22); real essence = divorcing producer from means of production (quote 24); ENGLISH
  example = the enclosures (commons fenced/seized, often by Act of Parliament, driving the rural
  poor into the mills) [ch27 on the expropriation of agricultural population; standard]; abroad =
  conquest/slavery/plunder (quote 25); force as midwife + economic power (quote 26); "blood and
  dirt" (quote 27). [ch26-31.]

## Honest contestation (brief requirement: be honest the LTV is contested)
- ✅ Handled in Ch6 close. The marginalist revolution (Jevons/Menger/Walras, 1870s) shifted
  mainstream value theory from labour-content to subjective marginal utility; modern economics
  largely treats the labour theory of value as a superseded position. The read states this
  plainly and then explains why the PHILOSOPHICAL payload survives the loss of the economic
  theory: (1) economic categories are historical not natural; (2) social relations harden into
  apparent properties of things (fetishism); (3) a system fair per-exchange can be unjust in the
  whole. These are the reasons the book is read as philosophy. [Standard; no specific economist
  quoted, so no quote-discipline exposure.]

  "defined precisely" requirement.
