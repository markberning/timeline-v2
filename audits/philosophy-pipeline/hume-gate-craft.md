## Per-chapter verdicts (Gate 2 — Storytelling & Understanding)

### Throughline + hook (pre-chapter material)
**STRONG.**
The throughline ("the cheerful skeptic who followed empiricism all the way to its bitter end") is precise and not generic — "bitter end" is earned by ch3, and the "went and played cards" closer is a specific image, not a gesture. The hook builds the biographical mystery well (most dangerous thinker / most likable man) and plants the core payoff. The deathbed Boswell foreshadow at the end of ch1 is sharp.

### Break block
**STRONG.**
The before/after labels are exact and different in the right way. The steelman of Locke/Berkeley's confidence (paragraph 2) is genuine, not a strawman. The "concrete move" paragraph (paragraph 3) — demanding the experiential receipt for each grand idea — is the best summary of Hume's method in the whole draft. The "corrosive" image in paragraph 4 pays off across chapters 3–4.

### Ch1 — "The man whose book fell dead-born"
**STRONG.**
Biography is not merely biography — the chair-denial anecdote is woven into the philosophical story ("unemployable for the very conclusions his careful prose refused to actually state"), and the *Treatise* failure/recovery arc illustrates the Enquiry distinction early. The Boswell deathbed foreshadow is a hook that keeps the chapter from being pure setup.

One flag: [VERIFY] tags are still live in this chapter:
- The name-change date and reason ("came later, around 1734")
- An "insurmountable aversion" self-quote attributed to "My Own Life" — this phrase does not appear in the pack's verified MOL quotes and is flagged with [VERIFY] in the draft. The pack does NOT contain this wording. Coordinator must locate it or cut it.

### Ch2 — "Impressions, ideas, and Hume's fork"
**GOOD.** Borderline STRONG.
The opening promise ("two tools ... by the time you notice the knife, it is already at the throat of causation") is an excellent frame and it pays off. The copy-principle is properly worked — a meaning-detector, the example of gold+mountain, the blind-man-and-red test case. The missing shade of blue is handled with exactly the right honesty. Hume's fork is clear and the "two bins" metaphor is clean.

The fork chapter's ending — "walk every grand claim of philosophy and religion up to the fork and ask which bin it goes in" — is strong but slightly rushes the reader into ch3. A one-sentence plain restatement of the fork explicitly for the dinner table (the pipeline's third test) is present but buried mid-paragraph rather than sharply stated at the end.

### Ch3 — "The crack in the world: causation and induction"
**STRONG.**
This is the best chapter. It does everything the gate requires:
- Worked example: billiard balls are walked move-by-move, event by event, with the killing question placed exactly right.
- Constant conjunction labeled and defined before the copy-principle payoff.
- The induction problem is walked through both attempted escapes (pure reason → no, experience → circular) in the same paragraph, and the circularity trap is felt rather than summarized.
- Plain restatement: "we don't believe the future will be like the past because we have a reason to; we believe it because we can't help it" — this is exactly what the gate is looking for.
- Custom/habit as the answer given before the reader can ask, and the answer is framed correctly (psychologically irresistible AND rationally unjustified, both true at once).
- The al-Ghazālī coda is the best comprehension-bridge in the draft — same gap, two answers, economy of comparison.

One flag: "by an absolute and uncontroulable necessity has determined us to judge as well as to breathe and feel" — [VERIFY] tag is live. Must be cleared or the passage must be reframed as paraphrase.

### Ch4 — "The self as a bundle"
**GOOD.**
The "catch the self by introspection" move is walked well. The theatre image is used correctly, with Hume's own correction ("no building and no audience — just the play, playing to no one") delivered, which is exactly right. The Berkeley-completion structural point (removing material substance → removing mental substance) is a strong comprehension move.

The bundle theory concession in the Appendix is handled — Hume's own "labyrinth" confession is present and framed as intellectual honesty. However:
- [VERIFY] tag on "stumble on some particular perception" wording is live.
- [VERIFY] tag on "labyrinth" Appendix quote is live.
- The plain-language restatement ("you are not a person who is having a day; you are the day happening") is good and distinct. However it arrives after a long theatre-image paragraph; a reader scanning quickly might miss it. Not a MUST-FIX.

One comprehension note (gate 2 not gate 4): the "bundle theory doesn't fully work" concession is excellent but the *reason* it doesn't work (the real-connection problem: Hume denies real connections but the bundle requires something to bind it, and he can't say what without re-introducing what he denied) is slightly telescoped. The draft says "he could not say what" but does not give the reader the actual logical bind. A sharp reader will want the one-sentence statement of the aporia. SHOULD-FIX.

### Ch5 — "Reason, the slave of the passions"
**GOOD.**
The two-halves of the passions claim are cleanly separated (reason can't motivate; what opposes a passion is another passion). The job-description metaphor ("your wants set the destination; your reason figures out the route") lands well. The is/ought gap treatment is the most careful in the draft — the explicit warning that Hume does NOT triumphantly declare the impossibility of is→ought, that he demands the bridge be shown rather than declaring it unbuildable, is exactly the kind of over-read correction the gate asks for.

"Hume's guillotine" is correctly labeled as twentieth-century shorthand and never attributed to Hume. The sympathy account of moral approval is present but brief. The dinner-table restatement of is/ought is not as sharp as the one in ch3 — it is subsumed in the longer explanation rather than pulled out. Not a MUST-FIX but a note.

Flag: The chapter's opening line calls the famous line "understood the least." Gate 2 asks us not to flag "vague praise" — calling it "one of the most precise and influential claims about human action ever made" is accurate here (the chapter earns it). Keep.

### Ch6 — "Religion at arm's length"
**GOOD.**
The atheist-trap correction (opening paragraph) is the single most important piece of philosophy-honesty in the draft and it is handled precisely and at the right length. The miracles maxim is walked correctly — definition → law-of-nature as massive evidence → testimony as weighable evidence → the balance. The Dialogues section is strong, with Cleanthes/Philo named and the watchmaker analogy dismantled properly.

The problem-of-evil Epicurus questions are included and sourced to Philo.

[VERIFY] tag on Part XII ironic-concession dominant-scholarly-view is live. The draft already hedges correctly ("almost certainly not," "dominant scholarly reading") but the coordinator must confirm this before ship.

One storytelling note: the chapter ends well ("the honest answer was that we do not and cannot know, and he refused to pretend otherwise in either direction, even from beyond the grave") but the transition from the miracles argument to the Dialogues is slightly abrupt — there is no connecting beat explaining why Hume published the miracles argument in his lifetime but withheld the Dialogues for decades. That gap is interesting and the reader will wonder. One sentence would close it. SHOULD-FIX (it is a story beat, not a comprehension gap).

### Ch7 — "The afterlife of a cheerful skeptic"
**STRONG.**
The Boswell deathbed scene is excellent: the specific detail ("placid and even cheerful"), Boswell's own disturbed aftermath, and the frame ("the skeptic's calm had done what the skeptic's arguments never quite managed — it got under the believer's skin") are strong storytelling. The Adam Smith eulogy, with the "ten times more abuse" line, is good narrative escalation.

The Kant paragraph is clean — the specific stakes are stated (if necessary connection is just habit, Newtonian physics has no rational foundation), the attribution of "dogmatic slumber" to Kant-about-himself is correct and the misquotation danger is named.

The racist-footnote section is handled with full precision: 1748 original had no footnote, added 1753, 1777 narrowed but did not retract, motivation unknown, the explicit "do not say he sharpened it" note delivered. The framing line — "the fork that he ran so mercilessly over religion and metaphysics, he never once ran over this" — is the right moral without being a lecture.

[VERIFY] tag on "ten times more abuse" exact wording is live.

The closing paragraph is the strongest passage in the draft. It integrates the whole arc — empiricism → dissolution → passions → is/ought → miracles → deathbed calm → Kant — and the last image ("the rest of philosophy is still standing where Hume left it, looking down") is earned.

## Gate 4 — Clarity (cold-read as sharp 15-year-old)

### Terms introduced without definition or with insufficient gloss

**F1 (MUST-FIX) — "empiricism" in the hook/break block used before it is defined.**
The hook says "followed empiricism all the way to its bitter end" and the break block gives it a functional definition in paragraph 1. But the hook is the reader's first contact, and a 15-year-old stopping there will ask "what is empiricism?" The fix is trivial: one parenthetical in the throughline or the first hook paragraph. Example: "the view that everything you know comes from experience." The break block's definition is good once reached; the gap is just the sequencing.

**F2 (MUST-FIX) — "rationalists" in break block paragraph 1 ("the rationalists-and-empiricists era") with no gloss.**
The reader has not yet reached era-3 and the phrase "rationalists-and-empiricists era" is an internal cross-reference that goes nowhere. Replace with a one-phrase gloss: "the rationalists, who held that pure reason alone could yield knowledge of the world." This is especially important because the break block sets up the contrast.

**F3 (MUST-FIX) — "impressions" and "ideas" as Hume's technical terms: first use is in ch2, defined there — CLEAR on arrival. But the throughline uses "experience" as a lay near-synonym and the hook uses "perceptions" (ch1 para 4: "a rush of passing perceptions") before ch2 defines impressions/ideas.**
A 15-year-old reading ch1 will not understand "passing perceptions" as a precise term. Either remove it from ch1 or add a gloss in ch1 that the full treatment is in ch2. SHOULD-FIX (the reader can infer, and the term is later defined, but the jump is a stumble).

**F4 (MUST-FIX) — "constant conjunction" in ch3 paragraph 4.**
The term is introduced and used correctly but the parenthetical example follows rather than leads. Current: "what Hume calls constant conjunction: A followed by B, over and over and over." The colon-gloss approach works. CLEAR.

Wait — actually reading more carefully: "what Hume calls **constant conjunction**: A followed by B, over and over and over." The definition is present. This is CLEAR. Withdraw F4.

**F4 (actual) (MUST-FIX) — "induction" in ch3 paragraph 5.**
The term is introduced as "the everyday engine of all reasoning beyond the present moment: you observe particular cases and draw a general expectation." This is a good definition. CLEAR. But the term "induction" is used in the break block ("the problem of induction") before this definition. A reader hitting the break block without the ch3 context will not know what "induction" means. The break block should add a one-phrase parenthetical: "induction — the practice of predicting the future from past patterns." SHOULD-FIX (the break block is a preview, the reader will get it in ch3, but the forward-reference is currently naked).

**F5 (MUST-FIX) — "necessary connection" first introduced in break block paragraph 3 ("the idea of necessary connection") without a gloss, before ch3 defines it.**
The break block is a preview unit that a reader may encounter without the chapter's fuller explanation. Add a parenthetical: "(the force that supposedly *makes* a cause produce its effect)." MUST-FIX in the break block specifically; ch3 is fine.

**F6 (MUST-FIX) — "the bundle theory" in ch4 — defined and walked through adequately, CLEAR.**

**F6 (actual) (MUST-FIX) — "is/ought gap" in ch5: not named until it is about to be presented.**
The term is introduced as "the is/ought gap" in the Enquiry chapter heading area and then the passage is quoted. The passage itself is immediately followed by the careful explanation. CLEAR overall. No fix needed.

**F7 (MUST-FIX) — "matter of fact vs relation of ideas" — handled clearly in ch2 as Hume's fork. CLEAR.**

**F8 (MUST-FIX) — "the design argument" in ch6 paragraph 3.**
The term appears as "the design argument — then the most persuasive case for God going — which runs by analogy: the world is full of intricate, fitted order..." The gloss is present inline. CLEAR.

**F9 (MUST-FIX) — "the design argument" is followed immediately by the watch analogy, which is the standard comparison and the reader gets it. CLEAR.**

**F10 (MUST-FIX) — "mitigated scepticism" — used in the break block context and mentioned in ch6.**
The draft mentions Hume's position as "suspension" and "ignorance" in ch6 but does not use or define "mitigated scepticism" as a term. The fact pack's §2.10 says Hume explicitly endorsed "mitigated scepticism or academical philosophy" (Enquiry §12). ### Skipped argument steps

**S1 (MUST-FIX) — The copy principle as a "meaning-detector" and the move from "all simple ideas come from impressions" to "terms with no traceable impression are empty."**
This logic step — from the psychological claim (ideas come from impressions) to the normative claim (terms with no source impression are meaningless) — is in the draft ("whenever a philosopher waves around some grand abstract term... you put it to the test") and is well stated. CLEAR.

**S2 (MUST-FIX) — The circular-experience argument for induction.**
This is the hardest step for a newcomer: why can't you use past experience to prove that future experience will resemble the past? The draft walks it: "to argue 'the future has always resembled the past before, therefore it will resemble the past this time' is to assume the very thing you are trying to prove." The argument bites its own tail is the phrase used. This is CLEAR but borderline — a sharp 15-year-old might resist: "but it HAS always worked!" The draft does not proactively address that resistance ("but it has worked!" is itself the circular move — you're already assuming the future will resemble the past when you say that). SHOULD-FIX: one sentence that names the counter-move and shows why it is still circular.

**S3 (MUST-FIX) — Why the passions argument does not lead to ethical chaos.**
This is explained in ch5: sympathy → felt approval → moral judgment. The reader tracking the "passions control reason" claim will need exactly this reassurance and it is present. CLEAR.

**S4 (SHOULD-FIX) — The bundle theory's internal problem.**
The logical bind (separate perceptions + no real connections = nothing to unify them, but we are unified = Hume can't explain unity without either real connections or a self, both of which he denied) is gestured at but not stated step-by-step. See gate 2 note above. The draft says "he could not reconcile" but not why. One sentence of explicit logical aporia here. SHOULD-FIX.

### Gaps and thin spots

**G3-1 (SHOULD-ADD) — Moral sentimentalism / sympathy is THIN.**
Ch5 mentions sympathy briefly ("a natural human capacity he calls sympathy — the ability to catch and share other people's feelings") and the mechanism of moral approval ("we feel the pleasure it spreads and the pain it prevents"). This is the correct account. But for a thinker page that goes "deeper than era-3," the positive moral theory is given only two sentences. Hume's moral philosophy — sentiment + sympathy + utility + the two Enquiries distinction — is the other half of "reason is the slave of the passions," and it is thin here. The fact pack has §3.6 (moral sentimentalism), §3.5 (is/ought), and a clear note that Hume considered the 1751 *Enquiry Concerning Principles of Morals* his best work. A thinker page that makes Hume primarily an epistemologist shortchanges him. This is SHOULD-ADD not MUST-ADD because the gate requires comprehensiveness to be selective — but a thinker whose best book was his ethics book deserves more than two sentences on ethics.

Specifically missing: the distinction between "natural virtues" (compassion, generosity) and "artificial virtues" (justice, fidelity — useful conventions), and the link from artificial virtues → Bentham's utility concept. Three sentences in ch5 would cover it.

**G3-2 (SHOULD-ADD) — Utilitarianism influence chain is present but the link is thin.**
Ch7 lists Kant, Ayer, and Popper but the Bentham/Mill influence is absent. The fact pack §8 ("What Hume generates") explicitly notes: Hume's account of social utility of "artificial virtues" influenced Bentham; his moral sentimentalism influenced Mill. Both channels are documented. If the moral sentimentalism in ch5 is expanded (G3-1), this becomes a natural addition. One sentence in ch7 connects the dots. SHOULD-ADD.

**G3-3 (SHOULD-CONSIDER) — "My Own Life" as a philosophical act.**
The autobiography is used for biographical quotes but never framed as what it is: a document Hume wrote knowing he was dying, a kind of philosophical self-testimony, a demonstration of the equanimity he had argued for. The fact pack has the relevant quotes. This is not a gap — the deathbed scene covers the same ground — but the framing of the autobiography as itself a philosophical statement could enrich ch7.

**G3-4 (MUST-ADD) — The two Enquiries distinction is present but the fact that Hume considered the *second* (ethics) his best work is buried.**
Ch1 paragraph 4 mentions it: "(Note the easy mistake here... Hume thought the *second*, the ethics one, was his best work, not the famous first.)" The note is in a parenthetical mid-paragraph, in a chapter that is primarily biography. The thinker page should give this more weight — ideally connected to the thin moral philosophy in ch5. The emphasis is backwards: the epistemology Enquiry is the famous one, but the ethics Enquiry was his own favorite, and that fact should frame the moral philosophy chapter. MUST-ADD is too strong — call it SHOULD-ADD: restructure the "his best work" note to appear at the opening of ch5, not buried in ch1.

**G3-5 (SHOULD-CONSIDER) — Hume's aesthetic philosophy.**
The *Four Dissertations* (1757) includes "Of the Standard of Taste," arguably the most influential text in modern aesthetics. The fact pack lists the work. The thinker page has no trace of Hume on beauty or taste. This is a genuine gap — he is not just an epistemologist — but given the seven-chapter scope, it would require its own space. Flag as SHOULD-CONSIDER for a future expansion but not a ship-blocking gap.

**G3-6 (SHOULD-CONSIDER) — The Newton analogy is present in ch1 (Treatise subtitle + "wanted to be the Newton of the human mind") but not returned to.**
The Treatise subtitle's "experimental method" framing is a structuring ambition: Hume thought he was doing for the human mind what Newton did for physics. Kant's crisis (ch7) was specifically that Hume undermined the rational foundation of Newtonian physics — so Newton bookends the whole story (Newton as Hume's model → Hume dismantles Newton's foundation → Kant has to rebuild it). This circularity is implicit but never stated. A brief connecting sentence in ch7 would complete the arc. SHOULD-ADD.

**G3-7 (SHOULD-CONSIDER) — The Rousseau quarrel.**
Ch1 mentions it ("one of the great quarrels of the century, but that is another story") and moves on. The quarrel does not belong in a thinker page — it is correctly set aside. No gap.

**G3-8 — Silent influence chain links: any missing?**
The pipeline requires that influence chains have no silent missing links. Check:
- Locke → Hume: Covered. Locke's anti-innatism, simple/complex ideas, limits of knowledge are in the break block and the fact pack's §8.
- Berkeley → Hume: Covered. The "Berkeley's move, completed" passage in ch4 is explicit.
- Hume → Kant: Covered. The causal-necessity → Newtonian physics stakes are stated.
- Hume → Bentham/Mill: THIN (see G3-2).
- Hume → Ayer: Brief but present.
- Hume → Popper: Brief but present.
No silent missing links. The thinness of the Bentham/Mill chain is the only issue, and it is SHOULD-ADD.
