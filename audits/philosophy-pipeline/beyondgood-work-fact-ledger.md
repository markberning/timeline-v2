### Quote-discipline notes
- The Molière "dormitive virtue of opium" line (ch.1, §11) is presented as an allusion Nietzsche
  echoes, explicitly NOT attributed to Nietzsche as his wording. Not a fabricated Nietzsche quote.
- The §16 "a thought comes when it will, not when I will it" formula is rendered without quotation
  marks as Nietzsche's suggestion (it is his own gloss in the §16–17 region). No false verbatim claim.
- The Kaufmann-translation phrasing "It was the Jews who, with awe-inspiring consistency, dared to
  invert the aristocratic value-equations" was CHECKED and REJECTED — that is the Genealogy/Kaufmann,
  not Zimmern's BGE. The read uses the verified Zimmern §195 wording instead.

## Landmine handling (per brief)
- **Will to power** defined plainly as life's self-discharging, self-expanding striving (§13, §36),
  explicitly NOT the "domination cartoon"; domination named as one degraded form, not the essence. ✔
- **Master/slave morality** presented as Nietzsche's DESCRIPTIVE genealogy (good/bad vs good/evil,
  *ressentiment*, transvaluation), with the explicit caution that it is descriptive not a battle cry,
  and that the *Genealogy* (1887) systematizes it → linked [Genealogy](/philosophy/work/genealogy). ✔
- **Posthumous misuse** named (Elisabeth, the compiled *Will to Power*, Nazi appropriation), motive
  hedged to documented EFFECT; the 1889+ afterlife kept OUT of the 1886 book's argument. ✔
- **Women passages** (§§231–239, esp. §238) named honestly, neither hidden nor endorsed; paraphrased
  not quoted (no slur reproduced as showcase quote). ✔

## Critic-pass corrections (c19 gate, web-verified vs Gutenberg #4363 / Wikisource)
Four wording fixes applied so quoted material is verbatim Zimmern:
- **§21** — quoted fragment was "the best self-contradiction that has *hitherto* been conceived";
  Zimmern reads "that has *yet* been conceived." Fixed to "yet."
- **§6** — in-prose rendering of Nietzsche's words spelled "autobiography"; Zimmern hyphenates
  "auto-biography." Fixed to match source.
- **Preface epigraph** — opening word capitalized to "SUPPOSING" to match Zimmern (the original
  small-caps/caps opening word).
- **§238** — the women paraphrase read "a possession to be kept under lock and key" (an editorial
  gloss not in Nietzsche's text). Re-paraphrased to track Zimmern's actual image: "a possession, as
  confinable property, a being made for service." Still a paraphrase (no quote marks), now faithful.

Checked-and-fine: §5 is rendered in-prose with NO quote marks ("which they dub truths"), so the
source's double-quote style on "truths" does not apply. §44 epigraph ends "...higher, greater..."
with a trailing ellipsis, which honestly signals the omitted "and fundamentally different"; the
quoted words are verbatim.
