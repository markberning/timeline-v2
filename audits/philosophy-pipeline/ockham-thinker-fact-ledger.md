## 2. Quotations — what may be quoted vs paraphrased

**Rule applied (per brief + pipeline gate 1):** Ockham wrote in Latin; clean PD English
translations are scarce, so his *positions* are PARAPHRASED without quotation marks. The
SHORT Latin razor/nominalism forms (PD, in Thorburn's appendix / SEP) are quotable WITH
their English given as standard rendering, not as a named copyrighted translation.

| Line | Quote or paraphrase? | Status / source |
|---|---|---|

| **"Frustra fit per plura quod potest fieri per pauciora"** ("it is futile to do with more what can be done with fewer") | QUOTABLE (Latin + standard English) | ✅ Thorburn: *Summa Totius Logicae* I, ch. 12; *In Sent.* I, d. 31, q. 1; *Phil. Nat.* IV, ch. 1. era-2 pack §3. |

## 3. Positions (all paraphrased; each tied to a source)

| Position | Status / source |
|---|---|
| **Nominalism**: universals ("humanity," "redness") are not real entities in the world or in the divine mind; only individuals exist; universals are concepts/names (signs) the mind uses to group resembling individuals | ✅ SEP: no universal entity of "felinity," only singular cats; universals are mental signs, universal terms derive from concepts. era-2 pack §3. |
| **The razor** governs *explanations* (parsimony in positing entities/principles), is genuinely associated with Ockham, but the *famous wording* is later (Punch 1639) and the *principle* is older + shared (Scotus used the formulas before him; Aquinas states it as an objection) | ✅ SEP + Thorburn; era-2 pack §3, §9. |
| **The razor's theological safety-catch**: for Ockham parsimony never "shaves God." He was a Franciscan theologian, not a proto-atheist. Parsimony governs human explanation, not what God may freely create | ✅ era-2 pack §3 ("the razor never shaves God"); SEP (he allowed reason, experience, AND Scripture as grounds for positing). |
| **Voluntarism**: God is absolutely free, bound by nothing but non-contradiction; could have made the world otherwise and made different acts good or evil; morality grounds in God's will. The human will too is radically free — it "can knowingly act directly against [its] ultimate good" | ✅ SEP (the will quote); era-2 pack §3. |
| **God's freedom shortens reason's reach**: if no created connection is necessary (God could have wired the world any way), reason cannot deduce how the world *must* be by pure thought; it can only observe how it *happens* to be. Theology and natural knowledge begin to separate | ✅ era-2 pack §3 (the close); framed as the consequence, not as Ockham's stated program. |
| **The faith/reason wedge**: theological truths (Trinity, Incarnation) are matters of faith, not rational demonstration — narrowing the Aquinas synthesis where reason reached the "preambles" | ✅ Consistent with SEP's account of his more restrictive view of natural theology; framed against the Aquinas read (era-2 Ch6 / aquinas.ts Ch2–3). |

## 5. Caricature / trap watch (pipeline gate 5)

| Trap | Handling |
|---|---|
| "Ockham invented Ockham's razor / the tag is his sentence" | REFUSED. The tag (*entia non sunt multiplicanda…*) is **not** in his works (SEP); first in Punch 1639; the principle is older + shared (Scotus, and Aquinas-as-objection). "It bears his name the way America bears Amerigo's." |
| "Ockham the proto-atheist sharpening a blade against God" | REFUSED. Franciscan theologian; the razor never shaves God; voluntarism is a theology of God's *freedom*, not a denial. |
| "Medieval scholasticism = sterile logic-chopping (angels on a pinhead)" | The pinhead debate is a **17th-c. Protestant jibe**, not medieval (era-2 / aquinas.ts Ch5). Read does not lean on it; treats Ockham's logic as serious. |
| Anachronism: phrasing "Ockham's razor" as if he coined the slogan | The popular slogan is a later tidy-up; stated explicitly. |
| Western-overclaim / lone-genius | Scotus credited as the real source of the formulas + the will-primacy; the principle named as communal scholastic property. |
