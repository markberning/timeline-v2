## Chapter-count decision: 6 chapters (the political-bridge ch5 was FOLDED, not written)

The pack's §0 proposes 7 chapters and explicitly flags ch5 ("the political philosophers:
context, not a detour") as cuttable if the comprehensiveness critic finds the democratic
stakes legible without it. **Decision: drop the standalone bridge, ship 6 chapters.**
Reasoning:
- Mill → Kierkegaard → Marx → Nietzsche flows as a clean four-revolt structure off one
  shared setup (climbing out of Hegel's system). A dedicated Tocqueville/socialism chapter
  sitting between Mill and Kierkegaard would interrupt that momentum without carrying a
  distinct major thinker.
- The democratic stakes the bridge was meant to supply are already delivered where they do
  load-bearing work: **tyranny of the majority + Tocqueville** live inside the Mill chapter
  (Ch2), where the harm principle gives them a target; the **base/superstructure and
  "change the world" political stakes** live inside the Marx chapter (Ch4). Splitting these
  into a separate chapter would duplicate, not deepen.
- The pack's own note: "if it judges the stakes clear from ch2 alone, drop it and keep 6."
  They are clear from Ch2 (Tocqueville credited in-text, majority-tyranny run as the
  democratic danger) plus Ch4. So: 6.

Final structure:
1. Ch1 — Climbing out of the system (Hegel inheritance + Bentham ramp)
2. Ch2 — Mill, Harriet Taylor, and the free individual
3. Ch3 — Kierkegaard and the individual before God
4. Ch4 — Marx, the philosopher
5. Ch5 — Nietzsche and the death of God
6. Ch6 — The reckoning (closes the five-era arc)

   Pack §10 PINNED + §9 blacklist ("God is dead as triumph/boast = WRONG FRAMING") satisfied.

4. **Marx as philosopher not prophet — PRESENT** (Ch4 opening): 20th-c state communism
