#### F1 — GS §108 quote attributed to "Common" — WRONG TRANSLATION ❌ MUST-FIX

**Draft text (Ch2 §4):**
> "God is dead; but given the way of men, there may still be caves for thousands of years
> in which his shadow will be shown — and we — we still have to vanquish his shadow, too" (Common).

**Verified Thomas Common wording (Gutenberg #52124, line 4033–4040, curl-verified):**
> "God is dead:--but as the human race is constituted, there will perhaps be caves for
> millenniums yet, in which people will show his shadow.--And we--we have still to overcome

**Discrepancies:**
- "given the way of men" ≠ "as the human race is constituted"
- "may still be caves for thousands of years" ≠ "will perhaps be caves for millenniums yet"
- "his shadow will be shown" ≠ "people will show his shadow"
- "vanquish his shadow" ≠ "overcome his shadow"

The draft's §108 version is NOT Thomas Common's wording. It appears to be a paraphrase or a different translation (possibly Kaufmann/Hollingdale-influenced — the "given the way of men" register is Kaufmann-flavored). The fact pack's era-5 §108 entry claims this as VERIFIED Common; the verification was incorrect.

**Fix:** Replace with the exact Common wording, OR if the author prefers the smoother modern phrasing, remove the "(Common)" attribution and ship as narrative paraphrase without quotation marks. Do NOT leave an incorrect translation attributed to Common.

**What is present and correct:**

**One nuance to watch (not a current error — a guard against future revision):** The draft says Elisabeth "assembled *The Will to Power* out of scraps from his discarded notebooks — selecting and ordering the fragments to support a nationalist, proto-fascist reading." The word "discarded" appears — scholars debate whether all the notebook fragments were "discarded" vs. simply not yet prepared for publication. Some of Nietzsche's most important concepts appear in the Nachlass in developed form. The framing "scraps from his discarded notebooks" is defensible but slightly loaded; "unpublished notebooks" (the pack's preferred term) is more accurate and less editorially weighted. 🟡 SHOULD-FIX.

### Slave revolt — steelmanned before the provocation ✅ EXEMPLARY

This is the framing gate's hardest requirement for Nietzsche, and the draft handles it well. The sequence:
1. Master morality explained as *first-person affirmative* — no resentment, not a boast, not a scorecard
2. Slave morality presented as a *genuine moral achievement*: "Sit with the steelman a moment longer, because it is a genuine moral achievement and Nietzsche half-knows it"
3. The slave revolt is said to have *succeeded* — Christianity and democratic equality are its children, stated plainly
4. *Ressentiment* explained as a technical term, not ordinary resentment
5. The potted-summary trap ("weak=bad") explicitly named and disarmed: "That is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that matters"
6. The real target identified: "life-hating morality — any value system that secretly says 'no' to existence" — available to powerful and powerless alike
7. The closing question is directed at the reader's OWN moral situation, not at any outgroup

This is a clean, fair presentation that steelmans the idea before the provocation and names the misreading that must be refused. ✅
