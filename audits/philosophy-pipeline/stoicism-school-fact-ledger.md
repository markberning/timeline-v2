# Stoicism SCHOOL read — Fact ledger (AUTHOR step)

Kind: **school / TRADITION** (school id `stoa`). Analog of the shipped Platonism school read.
Scope: Stoicism as a tradition across ~500 years — founding (Zeno) → systematizers (Cleanthes,
Chrysippus) → core doctrines → the Roman Stoa (Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius) → decline and
long afterlife (Christianity, the English word, the modern revival).

Quote doctrine: only verified PUBLIC-DOMAIN translations are set in quotation marks; the early
Stoa survives in fragments/testimonia and is paraphrased (no continuous PD work to quote). Every
quote below was string-checked against a load-checked URL on the dates of this build.

## QUOTES (verified PD, in quotation marks in the read)

### Q1 — Zeno / the Painted Porch (paraphrase + framed testimonium)
- **Source**: Diogenes Laërtius, *Lives of Eminent Philosophers*, Book VII (Zeno), §5, trans.
  R. D. Hicks (Loeb, 1925; PD).
- **Verified wording (Hicks)**: Zeno "used … to discourse, pacing up and down in the painted
  colonnade … which received its name from the painting of Polygnotus … Hither, then, people came
  henceforth to hear Zeno, and this is why they were known as men of the Stoa, or Stoics."
- **URL (load-checked 200)**: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/Book_VII
  ; also Perseus https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0258:book%3D7:chapter%3D1
- **Framing**: Diogenes Laërtius is a 3rd-century-CE biographer writing ~500 years after Zeno and is
  the corpus's single biggest legend-vector (CLAUDE pipeline flag). The shipwreck story and the
  "prosperous voyage when I suffered shipwreck" line are told AS Diogenes' much-later tradition,
  never asserted flat. ✅ CONFIRMED (wording) / 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME-IT (biography).
- **Shipwreck/oracle detail (DL VII.2–5)**: Zeno of Citium (Cyprus), a merchant in purple dye,
  shipwrecked near Athens c. 312–300 BC; read Xenophon's *Memorabilia* in a bookseller's shop, asked
  where such men were found, was pointed to the Cynic Crates. All DL tradition — framed.

### Q2 — Zeno's telos: "life in agreement with nature" (verified PD)
- **Source**: Diogenes Laërtius VII.87, trans. Hicks.
- **Verified wording**: Zeno "designate[d] as the end 'life in agreement with nature' (or living
  agreeably to nature), which is the same as a virtuous life."
- **URL (200)**: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/Book_VII
- ✅ CONFIRMED. Set in quotes as the phrase "life in agreement with nature."

### Q3 — Chrysippus, "the second founder" (verified PD proverb)
- **Source**: Diogenes Laërtius VII.183, trans. Hicks.
- **Verified wording**: "But for Chrysippus, there had been no Porch."
- **URL (200)**: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0258:book%3D7:chapter%3D7
  (Perseus 7.7 Chrysippus); cross-checked Loeb 7.183.
- ✅ CONFIRMED. "Second founder" is the standard scholarly epithet (he systematized Stoic logic +
  doctrine); attributed in the read as the proverb DL records, not asserted as Chrysippus' self-claim.

### Q4 — Seneca: virtue is the only good (verified PD)
- **Source**: Seneca, *Moral Letters to Lucilius* (Epistulae Morales), Letter 76, §21, trans.
  Richard M. Gummere (Loeb, 1917–25; PD in the US).
- **Verified verbatim**: "Virtue itself is therefore the only good; she marches proudly between the
  two extremes of fortune, with great scorn for both."
- **URL (200)**: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_76
- ✅ CONFIRMED (exact, string-matched).

### Q5 — Epictetus: the dichotomy of control (verified PD)
- **Source**: Epictetus, *Enchiridion* (Manual), ch. 1 opening, trans. Elizabeth Carter (1758; PD).
- **Verified verbatim**: "Some things are in our control and others not. Things in our control are
  opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions."
- **URL (200)**: https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html (Carter)
  ; Wikisource https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Enchiridion_(Epictetus)
- ✅ CONFIRMED (exact). NOTE: Gutenberg #45109 is the HIGGINSON translation ("There are things which
  are within our power…"), NOT Carter — do not source Carter from there. Carter sourced from MIT
  Classics / Wikisource.

### Q6 — Epictetus: disturbance is in the judgment (verified PD)
- **Source**: Epictetus, *Enchiridion*, ch. 5, trans. Elizabeth Carter.
- **Verified verbatim (Carter)**: "Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and
  notions which they form concerning things."
- **URL (200)**: https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html
- ✅ CONFIRMED. NOTE: the commonly seen "Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they
  take of things" is HIGGINSON's wording — different translator; the read uses the Carter wording and
  credits Carter.

### Q7 — Marcus Aurelius: begin the morning expecting difficult people (verified PD)
- **Source**: Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*, Book II.1, trans. George Long (1862; PD).
- **Verified verbatim**: "Begin the morning by saying to thyself, I shall meet with the busybody, the
  ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, unsocial."
- **URL (200)**: https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/marcus-aurelius/meditations/george-long/text/single-page
  (Standard Ebooks transcription of the Long PD text). Also Gutenberg #2680 (Long).
- ✅ CONFIRMED (exact). NOTE: Gutenberg #15877 ("The Thoughts of the Emperor…") is Long's biographical
  INTRODUCTION/essay, not the running translation — quote the translation from #2680 / Standard Ebooks.

### Q8 — Marcus Aurelius: retire into your own soul (verified PD)
- **Source**: Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*, Book IV.3, trans. George Long.
- **Verified verbatim**: "Men seek retreats for themselves, houses in the country, seashores, and
  mountains … but this is altogether a mark of the most common sort of men, for it is in thy power
  whenever thou shalt choose to retire into thyself. For nowhere either with more quiet or more
  freedom from trouble does a man retire than into his own soul."
- **URL (200)**: https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/marcus-aurelius/meditations/george-long/text/book-4
- ✅ CONFIRMED (first clause + the "nowhere … than into his own soul" clause string-checked;
  the elided middle is rendered with "…").

## PARAPHRASE-ONLY (no PD continuous text; framed as paraphrase, never quoted)
- **Cleanthes** (c. 330–230 BC), Zeno's successor, a former water-carrier/boxer; wrote the *Hymn to
  Zeus* (survives, but translations vary; the read does not set it in quotes). Led the school ~262–230.
- **Chrysippus** (c. 279–206 BC) systematized Stoic logic + the three-part curriculum; the bulk of his
  ~700 books is lost (survives in fragments + hostile reports). The read describes his role, does not
  quote him.
- **The three parts** (logic, physics, ethics) and the garden/egg/animal similes for their unity are
  reported by Diogenes Laërtius VII and later doxographers — paraphrased.
- **apatheia** = freedom from *pathē* (the destructive passions, themselves analyzed by the Stoics as
  mistaken value-judgments), NOT absence of feeling; the Stoics admitted *eupatheiai* (good, rational
  feelings). Standard scholarly account (SEP). The read corrects the "stoic = emotionless" caricature
  explicitly. ✅ position-accurate (SEP, *Stoicism*).
- **prohairesis** (Epictetus' term) = the faculty of moral choice / volition / "the will" — the one
  thing wholly "up to us." Standard (Epictetus *Discourses*; SEP). Paraphrased/defined, not quoted.
- **logos / providence / the cosmopolis** = the Stoic cosmos as a single rational, providentially
  ordered whole pervaded by *logos* (reason/divine fire); humans as fragments of it, hence
  *cosmopolites* (citizens of the world). Standard (SEP). Paraphrased.
- **indifferents** (*adiaphora*) = everything other than virtue/vice (health, wealth, reputation,
  life itself) — neither good nor bad in itself; subdivided into "preferred" and "dispreferred."
  Standard (SEP; DL VII.101–105). Paraphrased/defined.

## CLAIMS / DATES (basis)
- Zeno of Citium dates **c. 334–262 BC**; school founded early 3rd c. BC at the Stoa Poikile. (matches
  `philosophy-data.ts` zeno entry "c.334 – 262 BC"; SEP.) ✅
- Cleanthes c. 330–230 BC; Chrysippus c. 279–206 BC. (SEP / standard.) ✅
- Seneca c. 4 BC – AD 65, statesman + tutor to Nero, forced suicide AD 65. (standard.) ✅
- Epictetus c. AD 50 – c. 135, born enslaved at Hierapolis, freed, taught at Nicopolis; his pupil
  Arrian recorded the *Discourses* + *Enchiridion*. (matches `philosophy-data.ts` "c.50 – 135"; SEP.) ✅
- Marcus Aurelius AD 121–180, emperor 161–180; *Meditations* a private Greek notebook, not written for
  publication. (matches `philosophy-data.ts` "121 – 180"; standard.) ✅
- **English word "stoic"** (lower-case, = impassive/unmoved by pain) derives from the school; the
  popular sense narrows Stoic *apatheia* into mere emotionlessness — the caricature the read corrects.
  (OED / standard etymology.) ✅
- **Christian absorption**: Stoic ethics (natural law, conscience, *logos*, cosmopolitan brotherhood)
  fed early Christian thought; the apocryphal *Seneca–Paul correspondence* (4th c., spurious) shows the
  pull — framed AS spurious, not asserted. (standard.) ✅
- **Modern revival**: Stoic cognitive theory of the emotions (judgments cause feelings) is an
  acknowledged ancestor of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (Albert Ellis cited Epictetus' Ench. ch.5
  idea); and the 21st-c. online "Stoicism" movement (Stoic Week, popular books). Framed as influence/
  revival, not as identity. ✅
- **Decline**: no continuous Stoic school after the 2nd–3rd c. CE; Marcus Aurelius is the last major
  Stoic; the doctrines disperse into Christianity + later ethics rather than ending on a fixed date.
  Framed as dispersal, not a dated "death." ✅
