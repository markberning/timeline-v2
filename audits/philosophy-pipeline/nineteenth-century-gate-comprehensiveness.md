## MUST-ADD LIST (priority order)

**MA-1 — Mill's epistemology / philosophy of logic entirely absent**
Web research (SEP) flags this: Mill's *System of Logic* (1843) and his anti-apriorism —
the claim that even mathematical truths are inductive generalizations from experience —
occupy roughly 40% of his full philosophical profile. He is not only a utilitarian and
liberal; he is the era's major British empiricist philosopher, arguing that all knowledge
derives from experience including the laws of thought. At era altitude this need not be
deep, but the omission creates a distorted portrait: the draft covers the practical Mill
(utilitarianism, *On Liberty*, *Subjection of Women*) and entirely skips the
philosopher-of-science Mill who anchors the empiricist leg of the era's story. The
influence chain runs Hume (era 3) → Mill's inductive logic → the Victorian turn toward
science as the model of knowledge — and this connection is silently missing. A single
paragraph in Ch 2 is sufficient: Mill's quarrel with the rationalists (a priori truth
vs. experiential induction) is the live continuation of the Hume-vs-Kant debate that
the draft otherwise treats as settled. **New verification needed: none — SEP carries
this clearly; no new fact-finding required, prose addition only.**

**MA-2 — The Schopenhauer→Nietzsche link is named but not explained; the reader
cannot follow it**
The draft (Ch 5, "Why this is a break" block, and the fact pack influence chain) names
Schopenhauer as Nietzsche's starting point repeatedly, and Ch 5 gives a correct
one-sentence contrast (will-to-live → will-to-power). But Schopenhauer himself is
never introduced: the reader is told Nietzsche "had started as a disciple of
Schopenhauer, the gloomy heir of Kant who taught that a blind 'will to live' drives
everything" — a parenthetical — and nothing more. The spec requires that the influence
chain have NO silent missing links. Schopenhauer is the immediate predecessor in the
German tradition and the direct foil for will-to-power, eternal recurrence (as
life-affirmation against Schopenhauer's denial), and amor fati. At era altitude he
does not need his own chapter, but the reader needs two or three sentences: what
Schopenhauer held (the will-to-live as the world's blind, striving core; the right
response is resignation and denial; this is Kant's thing-in-itself identified with
will), and why Nietzsche found the diagnosis right but the prescription wrong. The
current parenthetical leaves a gap the influence chain cannot carry. **No new
verification needed; the fact pack §6 has the chain; this is a prose expansion.**

**MA-3 — The "dialectical materialism" trap: the draft does not use the term but
the fact pack's influence chain uses "historical materialism" inconsistently**
This is a flag for the narrator's framing accuracy, confirmed by web research: "dialectical
materialism" is a retronym coined by Engels and systematized by Soviet Marxist-Leninist
philosophy. Marx's own term is "historical materialism." The draft (Ch 4) correctly
uses "historical materialism" throughout — this is a PASS on the main prose — but the
fact pack §6 influence chain entry for Marx says "Marx as Hegel-heir → the Arabic
transmission and the Aristotle question (Marx's materialism is in dialogue with the
whole history of matter-vs-form)" in a way that gestures at a connection the era
narrative never makes. Flag to the author: do NOT introduce "dialectical materialism"
as Marx's own term anywhere in the narrative. The draft is clean; flag is a
watchlist item rather than a current error. **No fix needed in current draft; note
for reviser.**

**MA-4 — Perspectivism: present by name but its content and significance are absent**
Ch 5 names perspectivism in a single sentence — "His name for the deep drive in
everything is the will to power" — and then moves on; perspectivism as such is only
named in a sub-clause ("His most provocative analysis, the genealogy of our morals...
where he asks not whether good and evil are true but where they came from"). Actually,
the word "perspectivism" does not appear in the draft body at all. The concept does
appear in the fact pack (§2: "all knowledge is knowledge from a particular standpoint;
there is no 'view from nowhere'") but is absent from the draft. Web research confirms
perspectivism as one of the four or five essential Nietzsche concepts with its own SEP
subsection treatment. At era altitude it needs a minimum of two sentences: the
no-view-from-nowhere claim, and what it does to the notion of objective truth (sets up
20th-century poststructuralism and relativism debates). The published locus is *Beyond
Good and Evil* §22 (Zimmern translation, PD-verified). The Nachlass "only interpretations"
fragment can be used IF flagged as unpublished notebooks. **The fact pack has this;
it needs prose.**

**MA-5 — Kierkegaard's proto-existentialist label requires the caveat that
scholars resist it — currently the draft asserts it without qualification**
The draft does not actually use the word "existentialist" in the chapter text (Ch 3 uses
"first-person" and "subjectivity" throughout, which is correct). The era hook and
throughline in the draft header do not use the label either. However, the fact pack
§2 labels Kierkegaard "the first existentialist" in its section header without the
caveats the pack itself recommends. More importantly, web research (SEP) is explicit:
the "proto-existentialist" label "obscures at least as much as it reveals." The
Kierkegaard SEP entry resists this framing because his concern is with the individual
BEFORE GOD (a theistic structure Sartre rejected), and because "existentialism" as a
category postdates him by a century. The draft's actual chapter prose largely avoids
this trap, which is good. But wherever the label appears — in intros, chips, headers —
it needs the one-sentence caveat. **Flag for wherever the label appears in rendered
UI; draft prose is largely clean.**

**MA-6 — Feuerbach: entirely absent from the Marx chapter, leaving the
Hegel→Marx inversion chain unmotivated**
Ch 4 walks directly from Hegel to Marx's inversion. Web research and SEP confirm that
Ludwig Feuerbach is the essential link: Marx's own intellectual formation goes
Hegel → Young Hegelians → Feuerbach's *Essence of Christianity* (1841, which
"inverted" Hegel first by reducing theology to anthropology — God is a projection of
human nature) → Marx's further inversion (it is not ideas or theology but material
conditions that drive history). The term "species-being" (*Gattungswesen*) is
Feuerbach's before it is Marx's. Marx's 1844 Manuscripts explicitly engage Feuerbach,
and the Theses on Feuerbach (the source of Thesis XI) is named AFTER Feuerbach — the
whole document is Marx's response to Feuerbach. Without Feuerbach, the reader cannot
understand why "Theses on Feuerbach" is the title, why species-being is the
conceptual frame, or why the Hegel inversion went through a prior materialist step. At
era altitude, one sentence — "Feuerbach had already tried to invert Hegel, reducing
theology to anthropology (God is a projection of human needs); Marx said Feuerbach
hadn't gone far enough, because material conditions drive the projection as well" —
would satisfy the gate. **The fact pack names Feuerbach in the influence chain entry
for Marx but not in §2 (the position summaries); prose is absent from the draft.**

### G. The 6-chapter fold: did dropping Ch 5 (the political-bridge chapter) leave a real gap?

The fact pack §0 proposed a 7th chapter ("The political philosophers: context, not a
detour") to place Tocqueville, the democracy/socialism stakes, and the wider political
economy context. The pack explicitly delegated this decision to the comprehensiveness
critic: "if the comprehensiveness critic finds this redundant with ch2, fold back."

**Verdict: the fold is correct; no essential gap resulted.**

Reasons:
1. Tocqueville is named and credited in Ch 2, exactly where he belongs — as the source
   Mill borrows from, not as a subject in his own right.
2. The "tyranny of the majority" framing (the era's democratic anxiety) is present in Ch 2
   and in the Ch 6 reckoning.
3. The socialism/political-economy stakes are not philosophy in this vertical's sense; they
   belong to a different genre and would have imported content the era narrative should not
   own.
4. Web research confirms: Tocqueville is a political theorist, not a philosopher in the
   sense the pipeline defines it. An era-altitude philosophy article mentioning him in
   context (as the draft does) is the correct treatment. A dedicated chapter would dilute
   the arc.

**Gap check:** Is there anything the proposed Ch 5 would have covered that is now
absent and philosophically essential? Running the checklist: the socialism tradition
(Owen, Proudhon, Saint-Simon) is correctly absent — it is not philosophy at this
vertical's altitude. Mill's political economy (*Principles of Political Economy*) is
correctly absent — it is economics, not philosophy, and the philosophically relevant
parts of Mill are in Ch 2. The only thing the fold *might* have covered that is mildly
thin is the connection between Mill's harm principle and the democratic-legitimacy debate
(when does a democracy have the right to coerce?), but this is present in Ch 2 at
sufficient depth for era altitude.

**Conclusion: the 6-chapter structure is the right call; the fold left no essential
philosophical content behind.**

## SHOULD-CONSIDER LIST

**SC-1 — Kierkegaard's Religiousness A vs. B (the religious sphere's internal split)**
The fact pack does not flag this. SEP explicitly names this as a philosophically significant
distinction: Religiousness A is the immanent mode (available to any serious person — think
Socrates' dialectical ignorance as a religious stance); Religiousness B is the specifically
paradoxical, Christian mode (the eternal enters the temporal in a single historical person).
The Fear and Trembling analysis lives in Religiousness B. At era altitude, one sentence
would prevent the religious stage from looking monolithic. Example: "The religious sphere
itself splits: there is a universal, non-denominational version (what Kierkegaard calls
Religiousness A — any deeply serious commitment to the infinite) and the specifically
Christian paradox, where the eternal became a particular man in history, which no amount
of argument can metabolize." This has no factual risk; the fact-checker should confirm the
A/B framing against SEP before it ships.

**SC-2 — "That which does not kill me" deserves a sentence if perspectivism is added**
If MA-4 (perspectivism) is added, the *Twilight of the Idols* context is already open.
The "military school of life" tag is PD-verified and available; a sentence tying this
aphorism to the will-to-power as training (not a cosmic reassurance) would show the width
of Nietzsche's aphoristic mode alongside the longer analyses.

**SC-3 — The Young Hegelians as Marx's peer group**
Marx spent the late 1830s–early 1840s with the Young Hegelians (Bruno Bauer, Arnold Ruge,
Moses Hess — and Feuerbach on the materialist wing). They are the air Marx breathed as he
developed the inversion. A single clause — "along with a circle of Hegel's younger critics
who called themselves the Young Hegelians" — would prevent the portrait of Marx developing
the Hegel inversion in intellectual isolation. Feuerbach (MA-6) is the essential one; the
others are SHOULD-CONSIDER.

**SC-4 — The analytic/existentialist split in Ch 6 could name its shared Kantian ancestor**
The Ch 6 close names two 20th-century heirs but does not say they share a common
problem — the Kantian question about the limits of reason. One clause would tighten the
arc: both the analytics (pursuing clarity about what can be said) and the existentialists
(pursuing what to do when language and reason hit their limits) are still working Kant's
problem from different sides. This is the spine of the whole arc and naming it in the
close would make the "snake has reached its tail" image earn its weight.
