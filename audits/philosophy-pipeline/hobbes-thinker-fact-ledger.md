## A. VERIFIED VERBATIM QUOTATIONS (Gutenberg #3207, original spelling)

### Introduction — the body as machine; the commonwealth as Artificiall Man
> "For seeing life is but a motion of Limbs, the begining whereof is in some
> principall part within; why may we not say, that all Automata (Engines that move
> themselves by springs and wheeles as doth a watch) have an artificiall life? For
> what is the Heart, but a Spring; and the Nerves, but so many Strings; and the
> Joynts, but so many Wheeles, giving motion to the whole Body…"
— *Leviathan*, Introduction. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~312–317).

> "by Art is created that great LEVIATHAN called a COMMON-WEALTH, or STATE, (in
> latine CIVITAS) which is but an Artificiall Man; though of greater stature and
> strength than the Naturall…"
— *Leviathan*, Introduction. ✅ VERIFIED.

### Ch. 1 (Of Sense) — sensation is matter in motion
> "All which qualities called Sensible, are in the object that causeth them, but so
> many several motions of the matter, by which it presseth our organs diversly.
> Neither in us that are pressed, are they anything els, but divers motions; (for
> motion, produceth nothing but motion.)"
— *Leviathan*, I.1. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~427–431).

> "Sense in all cases, is nothing els but originall fancy, caused… by the pressure,
> that is, by the motion, of externall things upon our Eyes, Eares, and other
> organs…"
— *Leviathan*, I.1. ✅ VERIFIED.

### Ch. 5 (Of Reason, and Science) — reason is reckoning
> "When a man Reasoneth, hee does nothing els but conceive a summe totall, from
> Addition of parcels; or conceive a Remainder, from Substraction of one summe from
> another…"
— *Leviathan*, I.5. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~1239–1241).

> "For Reason, in this sense, is nothing but Reckoning (that is, Adding and
> Substracting) of the Consequences of generall names agreed upon, for the Marking
> and Signifying of our thoughts…"
— *Leviathan*, I.5. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~1271–1273).

### Ch. 11 — the restless desire of power
> "So that in the first place, I put for a generall inclination of all mankind, a
> perpetuall and restlesse desire of Power after power, that ceaseth onely in Death."
— *Leviathan*, I.11. ✅ VERIFIED (line ~3093). (Heading in text: "A Restlesse
Desire Of Power, In All Men.")

> "the Felicity of this life, consisteth not in the repose of a mind satisfied. For
> there is no such Finis Ultimus, (utmost ayme,) nor Summum Bonum, (greatest good,)
> as is spoken of in the Books of the old Morall Philosophers."
— *Leviathan*, I.11. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~3074–3077). (Hobbes' break with the
ancient/Aristotelian "highest good"; felicity is "a continuall progresse of the
desire, from one object to another.")

### Ch. 13 (Of the Naturall Condition of Mankind) — the state of nature
> "during the time men live without a common Power to keep them all in awe, they are
> in that condition which is called Warre; and such a warre, as is of every man,
> against every man."
— *Leviathan*, I.13. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~4014–4017).
**NOTE (potted-summary / apocryphal-tag trap):** the neat Latin "bellum omnium
contra omnes" ("war of all against all") is NOT Hobbes' English phrasing here.
Hobbes wrote "every man, against every man." A near-Latin form ("bellum omnium in
omnes") appears in his earlier *De Cive*, but the famous tag as usually quoted is a
later gloss. The era-3 read already flags this; this read frames it the same way.
USE the English; flag the Latin as a later tag.

> "In such condition, there is no place for Industry; because the fruit thereof is
> uncertain… no Knowledge of the face of the Earth; no account of Time; no Arts; no
> Letters; no Society; and which is worst of all, continuall feare, and danger of
> violent death; And the life of man, solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short."
— *Leviathan*, I.13. ✅ VERIFIED VERBATIM (lines ~4035–4044). The canonical line
ends "solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short." (Original spelling "poore.")

> "To this warre of every man against every man, this also is consequent; that
> nothing can be Unjust. The notions of Right and Wrong, Justice and Injustice have
> there no place. Where there is no common Power, there is no Law: where no Law, no
> Injustice. Force, and Fraud, are in warre the two Cardinall vertues."
— *Leviathan*, I.13. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~4092–4095).

### Ch. 17 (Of the Causes, Generation, and Definition of a Common-wealth)
> "And Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words, and of no strength to secure a
> man at all."
— *Leviathan*, II.17. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~5530–5531). (The reason a sovereign must
be overwhelming, not merely agreed.)

> "the Multitude so united in one Person, is called a COMMON-WEALTH, in latine
> CIVITAS. This is the Generation of that great LEVIATHAN, or rather (to speake more
> reverently) of that Mortall God, to which wee owe under the Immortall God, our
> peace and defence."
— *Leviathan*, II.17. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~5678–5682).

> The covenant each subject makes (Hobbes' own words, the words "every man should
> say to every man"): "I Authorise and give up my Right of Governing my selfe, to
> this Man, or to this Assembly of men, on this condition, that thou give up thy
> Right to him, and Authorise all his Actions in like manner."
— *Leviathan*, II.17. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~5675–5678). This is the contract: each
subject with each OTHER subject; the sovereign is not a party to it.

> The definition: the Common-wealth is "One Person, of whose Acts a great Multitude,
> by mutuall Covenants one with another, have made themselves every one the Author,
> to the end he may use the strength and means of them all, as he shall think
> expedient, for their Peace and Common Defence."
— *Leviathan*, II.17. ✅ VERIFIED (lines ~5694–5697).

## B. PARAPHRASE-ONLY positions (no quote needed; tie each to the text)

- **Materialism / no incorporeal substance.** Hobbes holds that whatever exists is
  body (matter in motion); "incorporeal substance" he treats as a contradiction
  (like "round quadrangle"). Thought, sensation, imagination, even the will are
  motions in the body. *Leviathan* I.1–6; the rejection of "Abstract Essences" and
  "Separated Essences" is hammered in IV.46 ("Of Darknesse from Vain Philosophy").
  Paraphrase the position; the I.1 sense-quote above is the textual anchor.
- **Sovereignty is absolute, undivided, perpetual.** The sovereign's power cannot be
  forfeited, divided, or held to account by the subjects; dividing sovereignty
  (Hobbes thought) is what destroys a commonwealth. *Leviathan* II.18 ("Of the
  Rights of Soveraignes by Institution") enumerates the rights. Paraphrase.
- **The one reservation — inalienable self-preservation.** The single thing a
  subject can never be bound to surrender, and may always resist, is a direct,
  immediate threat to his own life (and the means of preserving it). A man cannot
  covenant away the right to defend his own life, because the whole point of the
  covenant was self-preservation. *Leviathan* II.21 ("Of the Liberty of Subjects").
  This is the precise, narrow exception to "almost no right of rebellion." Paraphrase.
- **Erastianism — church under the sovereign.** The sovereign is head of the church;
  there is no independent ecclesiastical or papal power above the civil power.
  *Leviathan* Part III ("Of a Christian Common-wealth"). Part IV ("Of the Kingdome
  of Darknesse," ch. 44–47) attacks the Roman Church and Aristotelian scholasticism
  as the source of political confusion. Paraphrase.

## C. BIOGRAPHICAL / CONTEXTUAL FACTS  [filled from verification agent — see §D]

(Verified web-enabled against SEP, Britannica, Routledge, Royal Society, Shapin &
Schaffer, Aubrey, Jesseph. Author uses ONLY confirmed items; legend-flagged
anecdotes are framed AS legend.)

- **Born 5 April 1588, Westport (near Malmesbury), Wiltshire.** ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **The Armada/fear quip.** 🟡 The quip IS Hobbes' own (in his 1672 Latin verse
  autobiography), usually rendered "fear and I were born twins" / "my mother gave
  birth to twins, myself and fear." The CAUSAL claim (premature labor literally
  triggered by news of the Armada) is the playful legend. FRAME: Hobbes' own joke
  on himself, not a medical fact.
- **Magdalen Hall, Oxford** (matriculated c.1602/03, BA c.1608), then entered the
  **Cavendish household** (Earls of Devonshire) as tutor/companion, later secretary;
  the tie lasted essentially his whole life. ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **The Euclid discovery.** 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME: source is **John Aubrey's *Brief
  Lives***. Aubrey's story — Hobbes, around 40, finds Euclid's *Elements* lying open
  in a gentleman's library at Proposition 47 (Pythagoras), exclaims "By God, this is
  impossible!", then reads the proof backward chain by chain until convinced. Frame
  AS Aubrey's anecdote, not documented fact. (It anchors the "geometry = the only
  certain method" theme.)
- **Met Galileo c.1636** near Florence on the third Continental tour (1634–36, with
  the 3rd Earl); in contact with **Mersenne's Paris circle**. ✅ CONFIRMED (well
  attested; the Galileo meeting rests largely on Hobbes' own later report — "is
  reported to have met"). The Mersenne connection is solid.
- **Fled to Paris 1640** (after circulating *The Elements of Law*, amid the
  Strafford/Laud arrests), ~11-year exile, **returned to England winter 1651/52**.
  ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **Tutored the exiled Prince of Wales (future Charles II) in mathematics** in Paris
  — standard dating **c.1646–1648** (not the whole exile). ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **Works + dates.** ✅ *Leviathan* 1651 (London, the "Head" edition). *De Cive*
  1642 (Paris, Latin), wider edition 1647 (Amsterdam). *De Corpore* 1655. *De
  Homine* 1658. ✅ CONFIRMED. **Behemoth** (his history of the Civil War): written
  c.1668; Charles II refused it a license, so it circulated in PIRATED editions in
  the 1670s (in Hobbes' lifetime); the authorized edition came 1681, posthumously.
  Say "suppressed 1668, pirated 1670s, authorized 1681." ⚠️ precision noted.
- **Boyle air-pump dispute (early 1660s).** ✅ CONFIRMED. Hobbes was a **plenist**
  (denied a true vacuum), attacked Boyle's experimental program in *Dialogus
  Physicus de Natura Aeris* (1661); Boyle replied (1662). Modern landmark account:
  **Shapin & Schaffer, *Leviathan and the Air-Pump* (1985)**. Hobbes held that real
  philosophy must be deductive/geometric (demonstrative), so experiment could never
  yield the certainty he wanted — the core of the methodological clash.
- **Atheism scare + book burnings.** ✅ Oct 1666: a Commons heresy bill, its
  committee empowered to examine *Leviathan*; context = Plague + Great Fire; Hobbes
  burned some papers fearing prosecution. CAVEAT: the parliamentary action FIZZLED
  (nothing came of it) — don't overstate. **1683 Oxford** Convocation condemned and
  burned *Leviathan* and *De Cive*. ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **The Wallis feud.** ✅ CONFIRMED, and Hobbes was WRONG. **John Wallis** (Savilian
  Professor of Geometry, Oxford; Royal Society founder) was the antagonist. From
  1655 Hobbes claimed to square the circle / duplicate the cube / trisect the angle;
  Wallis demolished him over ~18 years. Those constructions are provably impossible
  with Euclidean tools. (The man who worshipped geometry as the one certain science
  embarrassed himself inside it — frame honestly, not gloated.)
- **Died 4 December 1679, Hardwick Hall (Derbyshire), age 91** (in his 92nd year),
  after a bladder disorder then a paralytic stroke. ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **"A great leap in the dark."** 🟡 CONTESTED/ATTRIBUTED. The reported last words
  ("Now I am about to take my last voyage, a great leap in the dark"). It is
  *attributed*, not a contemporaneous transcript; its early currency is reinforced
  by Vanbrugh's *The Provok'd Wife* (1697) joking about "Hobbes's voyage — a great
  leap in the dark." FRAME as a famous attributed deathbed line, never as sourced
  verbatim.
- **Materialism + corporeal God.** ✅ CONFIRMED. Only bodies/matter-in-motion exist;
  "incorporeal substance" is for Hobbes a self-contradiction (words that "destroy
  one another," like "an incorporeal body"). In his LATER theology (clearest in the
  Latin appendix and the Bramhall exchange) he affirmed God is **corporeal** — a
  most pure, subtle material spirit. Both halves are genuinely his; the corporeal-
  God claim is from the later works, so attribute it there.
- **Translations.** ✅ CONFIRMED. **Thucydides** (English, 1629 — his first
  published work). **Homer's *Iliad* and *Odyssey*** into English verse in the 1670s
  (in his eighties, c.1675–77) — his last major work.
- **Erastianism.** ✅ CONFIRMED. *Leviathan* Parts III–IV subordinate the church
  wholly to the civil sovereign (sovereign = supreme church authority; no
  independent priestly/papal jurisdiction). Part IV "Of the Kingdome of Darknesse"
  attacks the Roman Church and Aristotelian/scholastic "vain philosophy." ("Erastian"
  is the historians' label; Hobbes doesn't use it — fine as a category.)

### Social-contract framing — ✅ CONFIRMED on all three points
- Hobbes is a foundational **social-contract** theorist, the FIRST of the trio
  (Hobbes/Locke/Rousseau), who then diverge sharply on conclusions.
- The covenant is **"of every man with every man"** (Hobbes' phrasing) and the
  **sovereign is NOT a party to it** — subjects covenant with one another and
  authorize the sovereign, so the sovereign cannot breach a contract he never
  signed. This deliberately blocks any rebellion-for-breach argument.
- **The one reservation — inalienable self-preservation.** ✅ Precise edges: (a) it
  is a right to *resist/defend one's own life and body*, not a right to lead
  rebellion or defend others or a cause; (b) it covers immediate, direct threat to
  one's own life (Hobbes extends it to refusing to be one's own executioner — e.g.
  declining to confess or to fight when ordered against one's own life), and it does
  NOT license organized revolt. *Leviathan* II.21 ("Of the Liberty of Subjects").

## D. Potted-summary / anachronism traps to refuse (framing gate)
- "War of all against all" (*bellum omnium contra omnes*) — a later Latin tag, not
  Hobbes' English. USE "every man, against every man"; flag the Latin as a later
  gloss. (Hobbes' own near-Latin is "bellum omnium in omnes" in *De Cive*.)
- "Hobbes defended the divine right of kings" — FALSE. His sovereign can be a man OR
  an assembly; the argument is structural (any stable common power beats none), not
  a brief for a particular monarch or for divine right.
- "Hobbes was an atheist" — handle carefully. He was repeatedly ACCUSED of atheism
  and his materialism + corporeal God were heterodox, but he professed Christianity
  and an Erastian theology. Report the accusations as accusations; don't assert the
  label as fact (the Hume-rule from era-3/era-5).
- "Hobbes invented the social contract" — overclaim. Contract/consent ideas predate
  him (and the Greek sophists, the monarchomachs). He gave it its first systematic
  absolutist form. Avoid "first to."
