## Bibliographic facts
- ✅ Title: *Ethics* (Latin *Ethica, Ordine Geometrico Demonstrata* = "Ethics,
  demonstrated in geometrical order"). Elwes/Gutenberg gives the Latin subtitle as
  "(Ethica Ordine Geometrico Demonstrata)".
- ✅ Published **1677**, posthumously, in the *Opera Posthuma*, the year Spinoza died
  (1632–1677). It was not printed in his lifetime. [Standard; Opera Posthuma title
  page dated 1677 on Commons.]
- ✅ Structure: **five Parts**, each built as definitions → axioms → propositions,
  each proposition with a Proof and closing **Q.E.D.** (Latin *quod erat
  demonstrandum*, "which was to be demonstrated"). Part titles, verbatim from the
  Elwes text:
  - Part I — "Concerning God" (Elwes heading "PART I. CONCERNING GOD").
  - Part II — "Of the Nature and Origin of the Mind."
  - Part III — "On the Origin and Nature of the Emotions."
  - Part IV — "Of Human Bondage, or the Strength of the Emotions" (text line 6113).
  - Part V — "Of the Power of the Understanding, or of Human Freedom" (text line 8561).

2. ✅ **I, Def. 4 (attribute)** — "By attribute, I mean that which the intellect

6. ✅ **"God or Nature" (Deus sive Natura)** — "For the eternal and infinite Being,

7. ✅ **Geometric method** (III Preface) — "I shall consider human actions and desires

10. ✅ **Mind as idea of the body** (II, Prop. 13) — "The object of the idea

## Position summaries (paraphrase, tied to the text — no quote marks in prose)
- 🟡→✅ **One infinite substance.** Part I argues there is only one substance, God,
  which is infinite, necessary, self-caused (I Def. 1, "that of which the essence
  involves existence"), and identical with Nature. Everything else (minds, bodies,
  this rock) is a *mode*, a modification of that one substance, not a thing in its own
  right. [I Props 1–15; confirmed by the substance/mode/God definitions above.]
- ✅ **Thought and extension are attributes.** The one substance has infinitely many
  attributes; the human intellect grasps two of them, **thought** and **extension**
  (II Props 1–2 head Part II: "Thought is an attribute of God," "Extension is an
  attribute of God"). A mind and its body are the SAME single thing expressed under
  two attributes, which is why there is no interaction problem (this is the dissolution
  the descartes.ts read already names). [II Prop 7: "The order and connection of ideas
  is the same as the order and connection of things."] ✅ string-checked II Prop 7
  exists in text.
- ✅ **The mind is the idea of the body** (II Prop 13, quoted above) — the mind is not
  a separate thinking substance (contra Descartes' *res cogitans*) but the *idea of*
  an actually existing body.
- ✅ **Emotions defined geometrically** (Part III). Spinoza derives the affects
  (emotions) from three primary ones: desire, pleasure (joy), and pain (sorrow), all
  flowing from the *conatus*. [III Definitions of the Emotions; the conatus props
  above.] He treats love, hate, envy, etc. like theorems.
- ✅ **Determinism throughout.** Free will is an illusion (quotes 8 above); the will is
  not free, only the appearance of freedom arising from ignorance of causes. A "free"
  thing for Spinoza is one that acts from the necessity of its own nature alone (I Def.
  7: "That thing is called free, which exists solely by the necessity of its own
  nature"), which strictly applies only to God; human freedom is a matter of degree,
  achieved through understanding.
- ✅ **Human bondage** (Part IV) — to be ruled by the passive emotions (passions), the
  ones caused from outside, is bondage; the more the mind is moved by external causes
  it does not understand, the less power it has over itself.
- ✅ **Human freedom / blessedness** (Part V) — freedom is the mind's power over the
  emotions, gained by understanding them and their causes (turning passive emotions
  into active ones). The highest knowledge (the "third kind of knowledge," intuition)
  yields the **intellectual love of God** (quotes 12) and **blessedness** (quote 13),
  which is not a reward for virtue but virtue itself. The book closes on quote 14.

## Three kinds of knowledge (paraphrase, II Prop 40 Note 2)
✅ string-checked the passage exists. (1) imagination / opinion (from the senses,
hearsay), the only source of error; (2) reason (common notions, adequate ideas); (3)
intuition (*scientia intuitiva*), which grasps things through God's essence. The third
is the seed of the intellectual love of God in Part V.

## Apocrypha / caricature guards (the two traps)
- **"Deus sive Natura" as a slogan.** Real and Spinoza's, but the exact Latin tag is
  not in the Elwes English; the English is "God or Nature." Frame the Latin as the tag
  it became, quote the English.
- **"Spinoza was a pantheist / atheist."** The potted summary. Contested in scholarship
  (panentheism vs pantheism vs "atheist" as his enemies charged). The read states what
  the text says (God = the one substance = Nature) and FLAGS the label dispute rather
  than asserting "pantheist" flat. He was excommunicated (*cherem*) from the Amsterdam
  Jewish community in 1656; the Ethics was condemned and banned after publication. [Hold
  biography light at the WORK altitude; the SPINOZA thinker read carries it.]
- **"He proved God exists like a geometry problem, so it's just wordplay."** Caricature
  of the method. The geometric form is real (Def→Ax→Prop→Proof→Q.E.D.) and load-bearing
  to his claim that ethics is as necessary as mathematics; present it as the method it is.
- **Descartes as foil.** Descartes: two substances (mind + body) that must interact, and
  a free will. Spinoza: ONE substance, mind and body as two attributes of it (no
  interaction to explain away), and NO free will. This contrast is the work's engine and
  is already consistent with the shipped descartes.ts read. Do not anachronistically read
  later "pantheism controversy" framings back into 1677.
