## [VERIFY] tags — claims asserted beyond the pack's verified set

The pack is the only source; these are points where the draft states something the pack
does not give in exactly that form, or where the pack itself flagged a fetch-before-ship.
None are quotations in quote marks beyond §3. Listed for the coordinator's fact-checker.

1. **[VERIFY-1] "Plato ~28 at Socrates' execution."** Pack §1 says "Plato was ~29" at
   Socrates' death (399 BC) given birth c.429. Draft says "about twenty-eight." 28 vs 29
   depends on the birth-year choice (429 vs 428/7) and exact months, both unknown. Safe
   to soften to "in his late twenties" if the fact-checker prefers; pack's own number is 29.

2. **[VERIFY-2] Third Man quote splice.** Draft's epigraph-free in-body quote joins two
   verified Parmenides clauses from pack §3 Ch2 with an ellipsis ("…over and above all
   these…"). Both halves are verbatim in the pack; confirm the ellipsis doesn't drop a
   clause that changes the sense. Pack §10.3 also flags the Parmenides sourceUrl
   (Wikisource Jowett) as needing a load-check before ship.

3. **[VERIFY-3] Who poses Meno's paradox.** Pack §2 says "Glaucon poses the 'Meno's
   paradox'." That looks like a pack slip (the paradox is posed by Meno himself in the
   *Meno*; Glaucon is a *Republic* character). Draft attributes it neutrally to "the
   *Meno*" / "Meno" to avoid repeating a likely error. Fact-checker confirm the speaker.

4. **[VERIFY-4] Slave-boy geometric specifics.** Pack §2 says only "the slave-boy knows
   how to bisect a square… led through the right questions." The draft supplies the
   standard worked numbers (2-ft side / 4 sq ft → double-sides give 16 → 3-ft give 9 →
   diagonal gives 8). These are the actual Meno demonstration but the specific figures are
   not spelled out in the pack; confirm against Jowett's Meno (pack §3 Ch3 sourceUrl).

5. **[VERIFY-5] Gyges chasm contents.** Pack §2 gives the full apparatus (bronze horse
   with hollow interior, dead body inside, golden ring). Draft compresses to "among other
   wonders… a golden ring on a corpse" for pace; no false detail added, but if the
   fact-checker wants the bronze-horse detail restored it's available in the pack.

6. **[VERIFY-6] The conflict-of-desire argument for the three parts.** The draft uses the
   "want and refuse to drink at once → different parts" reasoning (Republic IV, the
   principle of opposites). The pack §2 names the three parts and their functions but does
   not spell out this specific proof. It is the canonical Republic IV argument; confirm
   wording isn't overstated.

7. **[VERIFY-7] The corpses-at-execution example (Leontius).** Republic IV's Leontius
   anecdote (the man ashamed of wanting to look at corpses, furious at his own eyes) is
   the textbook illustration of spirit siding with reason against appetite. The pack does
   not include it. It is genuine Plato but should be confirmed against the Republic IV
   text before ship; the draft does not put any of it in quotation marks.

8. **[VERIFY-8] "Same staircase, two engines" (ladder = Cave/Line).** The structural
   parallel between the love-ladder and the reason-ascent is the author's synthesis, not
   stated as such in the pack. It is sound (both terminate at a Form, both are step-by-step
   ascents) and asserted as interpretation, not fact; flag only as an interpretive claim.

9. **[VERIFY-9] Aristotle's actual NE 1096a wording.** Pack §3 Ch7 + §10.1 flag this as
   PARAPHRASE-ONLY: the Ross wording was NOT fetched. The draft therefore does NOT put the
   NE line in quotation marks — it paraphrases ("piety requires us to honour truth above
   our friends" appears with quote marks in the *pack*, but the draft renders the idea as
   reported speech without asserting it as a verified Ross quotation). **Fact-checker must
   fetch classics.mit.edu NE 1096a11–17 and confirm the exact Ross wording before any of
   this ships in quotation marks.** The draft keeps the NE material entirely as
   paraphrase/reported speech with NO quotation marks ("both the friends and the truth are
   dear to us, but our duty is to prefer the truth"), which is pack-compliant. If the
   fact-checker later confirms exact Ross wording, a verified quote could be added; nothing
   currently asserts a verbatim NE quotation.
