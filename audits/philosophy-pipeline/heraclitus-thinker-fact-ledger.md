## A. Biography / dates / status

| Claim | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Heraclitus of Ephesus, c.535 – c.475 BC | ✅ standard estimate | The conventional floruit. Diogenes Laertius (IX.1) puts his *akme* (peak, ~age 40) at the 69th Olympiad = **504–501 BC**, from which the c.535 birth is back-figured. Dates are estimates; SEP says only "active around 500 BCE." ## B. Fragments quoted in the read — VERBATIM Burnet (1892), with Bywater + DK numbers

> Burnet's translations as printed. Bywater number = Burnet's "(n.)"; DK = Diels–Kranz.

1. **Logos / the Word holds always (DK B1; Bywater/Burnet 2).** Burnet, fr. (2):
   > "Though this discourse is true evermore, yet men are as unable to understand it when they hear it for the first time as before they have heard it at all. For, although all things happen in accordance with the account I give, men seem as if they had no experience of them, when they make trial of words and works such as I set forth, dividing each thing according to its nature and explaining how it truly is. But other men know not what they are doing when you wake them up, just as they forget what they do when asleep."
   - Note: Burnet renders *logos* here as "discourse"/"the account I give" and **deliberately rejects** the Stoic "Reason" reading (his fn. 13). The read flags this: the grander "Logos = cosmic Reason/Law" is partly the *Stoics'* later development of Heraclitus, not unambiguously his own word-sense. Use DK B1.

2. **"All things are one" (DK B50; Bywater/Burnet 1).** Burnet, fr. (1):
   > "It is wise to hearken, not to me but my argument, and to confess that all things are one."

3. **Wisdom = the thought that steers all (DK B41; Bywater/Burnet 19).** Burnet, fr. (19):
   > "Wisdom is one thing. It is to know the thought by which all things are steered through all things."

4. **The world-order is an ever-living Fire (DK B30; Bywater/Burnet 20).** Burnet, fr. (20):
   > "This order, which is the same in all things, no one of gods or men has made; but it was ever, is now, and ever shall be an everliving Fire, fixed measures of it kindling and fixed measures going out."
   - ("overliving" in the OCR is a scan error for "everliving"; Burnet's word is **everliving**. Burnet fn.: "world-order, not world.") This is the single most important fire fragment.

5. **Fire exchanged for all things, like gold for wares (DK B90; Bywater/Burnet 22).** Burnet, fr. (22):
   > "All things are exchanged for Fire, and Fire for all things, as wares are exchanged for gold and gold for wares."

6. **The river fragment — GENUINE form (DK B12; Bywater/Burnet 41,42).** Burnet, fr. (41, 42):
   > "You cannot step twice into the same rivers; for fresh waters are ever flowing in upon you."
   - ⚠️ ATTRIBUTION TRAP, see §C. Burnet/Bywater fuse it into the "cannot step twice" wording. The **DK B12** Greek that scholars accept as verbatim is literally "**upon those who step into the same rivers, different and again different waters flow**" — the change is in the *waters*, not a "can't do it twice" impossibility. The read quotes Burnet's wording but immediately gives the literal sense and flags the difference.

7. **The river fragment — paraphrase form (DK B49a; Bywater/Burnet 81).** Burnet, fr. (81):
   > "We step and do not step into the same rivers; we are and are not."
   - ⚠️ DK **B49a** is rejected by the consensus as later/Attic-dialect paraphrase, not verbatim Heraclitus (see §C). Used in the read ONLY as the "stronger, later" form, explicitly flagged.

8. **War is the father of all (DK B53; Bywater/Burnet 44).** Burnet, fr. (44):
   > "War is the father of all and the king of all; and some he has made gods and some men, some bond and some free."

9. **Strife/opposite tension; bow and lyre (DK B51; Bywater/Burnet 45).** Burnet, fr. (45):
   > "Men do not know how that which is drawn in different directions harmonises with itself. The harmonious structure of the world depends upon opposite tension, like that of the bow and the lyre."

10. **The hidden attunement (DK B54; Bywater/Burnet 47).** Burnet, fr. (47):
    > "The hidden harmony is better than the open."

11. **The way up and the way down (DK B60; Bywater/Burnet 69).** Burnet, fr. (69):
    > "The way up and the way down is one and the same."

12. **Good and ill are the same (DK B58, context; Bywater/Burnet 57).** Burnet, fr. (57):
    > "Good and ill are the same."

13. **God is day and night… (DK B67; Bywater/Burnet 36).** Burnet, fr. (36):
    > "God is day and night, winter and summer, war and peace, satiety and hunger; but he takes various shapes, just as fire, when it is mingled with different incenses, is named according to the savour of each."

14. **Nature loves to hide (DK B123; Bywater/Burnet 10).** Burnet, fr. (10):
    > "Nature loves to hide."

15. **The learning of many things (polymathy) ≠ understanding (DK B40; Bywater/Burnet 16).** Burnet, fr. (16):
    > "The learning of many things teacheth not understanding, else would it have taught Hesiod and Pythagoras, and again Xenophanes and Hekataios."

16. **The waking share one world (DK B89; Bywater/Burnet 95).** Burnet, fr. (95):
    > "The waking have one and the same world, but the sleeping turn aside each into a world of his own."

17. **The common / hold fast to the common as a city to its law (DK B114 + B2; Bywater/Burnet 91, 92).** Burnet, fr. (91):
    > "Wisdom is common to all things. Those who speak with intelligence must hold fast to the common as a city holds fast to its law, and even more strongly. For all human laws are fed by one thing, the divine. It prevails as much as it will, and suffices for all things with something to spare."
    > fr. (92): "Though wisdom is common, yet the many live as if they had a wisdom of their own."

18. **One man is as ten thousand, if he be the best (DK B49; Bywater/Burnet 113).** Burnet, fr. (113):
    > "One is as ten thousand to me, if he be the best."

19. **The sun is new every day (DK B6; Bywater/Burnet 32).** Burnet, fr. (32):
    > "The sun is new every day."

20. **If you do not expect the unexpected (DK B18; Bywater/Burnet 7).** Burnet, fr. (7):
    > "If you do not expect the unexpected, you will not find it; for it is hard to be sought out and difficult."

(Epigraph reserve also from this set: B30 fire, B12/B49a rivers, B53 war, B60 up/down, B123 nature hides, B114 the common.)

## C. The central attribution trap — "everything flows" / "you cannot step twice"

**This is the load-bearing fact-discipline call of the whole read.** SEP (and Kirk 1954, Marcovich 1967, back to Reinhardt 1916) consensus:

- **"Panta rhei" ("everything flows") is NOT a verbatim Heraclitus fragment.** It is **Plato's characterization** of Heraclitus in the *Cratylus* (402a): "*panta chōrei kai ouden menei*" — "all things move/give way and nothing remains" — later compressed by the doxographers (Simplicius etc.) into the slogan "panta rhei." It appears nowhere in the genuine fragments. The read says so explicitly.
  - Source: SEP "Heraclitus"; Plato, *Cratylus* 402a (Fowler/Jowett PD trans. available on Perseus).
- **DK B12** ("upon those who step into the same rivers, different and again different waters flow") is "the **sole** [river fragment] with the linguistic density characteristic of Heraclitus' words" (SEP). It is the **best-attested genuine** river fragment (preserved via Arius Didymus/Cleanthes).
- **DK B91** ("you cannot step twice into the same river") is "**patently a paraphrase** by an author [Plutarch] famous for quoting from memory." **DK B49a** ("we step and do not step…") is in **Attic, not Heraclitus' Ionic**, and "the second clause has no grammatical connection to the first." Both are **later, stronger reformulations**, not verbatim Heraclitus, and likely already Platonizing.
- **Burnet/Bywater (1892)** predate this distinction, so Burnet prints the "cannot step twice" wording (fr. 41,42) and "we step and do not step" (fr. 81) as fragments. The read **uses Burnet's exact wording but corrects the attribution in the narration**: B12 is the genuine one and says only that the *waters* change; the famous "can't step twice" punchline is the later, harder form that may already be Plato's Heraclitus, not Heraclitus'.

**House rule applied:** quote Burnet verbatim (he's PD and named), but the *claim about what Heraclitus said* tracks the modern consensus, not Bywater's 1892 arrangement.
