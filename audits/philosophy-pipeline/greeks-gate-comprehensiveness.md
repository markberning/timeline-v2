**Critic:** Sonnet (web-enabled), running independently.

## MUST-ADD LIST (blocking — reader would be misled or lost without these)

### M1 — Neoplatonism and Plotinus: the culmination and handoff are silent
**What is missing:** The draft's Ch 7 goes from Marcus Aurelius → schools "slowly
faded" → Neoplatonism (one word, no content) → 529. Plotinus is never named.
The era's final major philosophical development — the synthesis that shaped Augustine,
medieval theology, and Islamic philosophy — is a complete gap. This is not a
peripheral detail: Plotinus is the arc's culmination AND its transmission vehicle.
The phrase "philosophical energy… channeled into a mystical, religious reworking of
Plato that scholars call Neoplatonism" is the entire coverage. The reader gets the
label but nothing of the idea or the stakes.
**Why blocking:** Without even a sentence on what Neoplatonism actually was (the One,
the soul's ascent, emanation), the handoff to Era 2 is meaningless — "faith meets
reason" presupposes Plotinus because Augustine was a Platonist before he was a
Christian. The influence chain has a silent missing link.
**Fact-pack status:** The fact pack COVERS this in §6 (influence chain point 16) with
"Plotinus — name-drop" level instruction, but the author ran below even that floor.
The pack also names the handoff chain (Neoplatonism → Boethius → Arabic transmission).
New verification NOT needed — the pack has the material. The author needs to write 3–5
sentences minimum on what Neoplatonism was, name Plotinus, and make the Era 2 handoff
legible.

### M2 — The influence chain has a broken link: Democritus → Epicurus (atomism as therapy)
**What is missing:** The draft explains Epicurean physics in Ch 6 ("If everything,
including your soul, is just atoms in the void…") but never names Democritus as the
source. Ch 2 explains atomism at length. Ch 6 uses it without completing the chain.
The words "Democritus" or "atomists" do not appear in Ch 6.
**Why blocking:** The pipeline spec is explicit: "the influence chain may not have
silent missing links." Epicurus' physics is borrowed wholesale from Democritus. A
reader following the draft can't know where the atoms-and-void argument came from;
they might reasonably assume Epicurus invented it. The single sentence "He defused
both with the old atomism of Democritus (Chapter 2)" is all that is needed, but it
is currently absent.
**Fact-pack status:** The pack covers this explicitly in §2 ("Democritus → Epicurus:
atomism repurposed," §6 influence chain point 13). No new verification needed.

### M3 — The Stoic school genealogy: Cynics → Zeno of Citium is stated, but Cleanthes is invisible
**What is missing:** The draft says Zeno founded the Stoa and names Chrysippus as
the "great systematizer." But the school had three canonical heads: Zeno →
Cleanthes → Chrysippus. The draft skips Cleanthes entirely. More importantly, the
phrase "the school went" attributed directly to Chrysippus without identifying the
intermediate generation creates a gap. This is minor for Cleanthes himself (genuinely
peripheral), BUT the draft states that without Chrysippus "there would have been no
Stoa" without explaining what Chrysippus actually did to earn that — it reads as
name-dropping rather than explanation.
**Recalibration:** This is THIN not a full gap at era altitude. The Chrysippus beat
is there but needs one sentence of content (he turned the Stoa's practical ethics into
a rigorous logical and physical system). Cleanthes is legitimately omittable. Flag as
THIN, borderline MUST-ADD on the Chrysippus content.
**Decision: MUST-ADD (THIN — needs 1 sentence).**
**Fact-pack status:** Covered in §1 and §2 of the pack. No new verification needed.

### M4 — Academic Skeptics (Arcesilaus and Carneades): named in fact pack, absent in draft
**What is missing:** The draft's Ch 6 covers Cynics, Stoics, and Epicureans. The
Skeptics — the fourth great Hellenistic school — are completely absent. Not a single
sentence. The Academic Skeptics in particular have a consequence the draft needs: they
turned Plato's own Academy skeptical for ~200 years, and Carneades' 155 BC embassy to
Rome (arguing for AND against justice on successive days) is both the best story of
Skeptical philosophy and the founding scene of Greek philosophy entering the Roman
world.
**Why blocking:** At era altitude the Skeptics are not optional — they were one of
the four major Hellenistic prescriptions (the "three rival gyms" framing in Ch 6 is
accurate only if you count three, but there were four). More critically: the Skeptics
are WHY Cicero writes what he writes — his "probabilism" (we can't know for certain
but can act on the most probable) is the Academic Skeptic legacy. Omitting them makes
Cicero's philosophical role in Ch 7 harder to understand. Also: Pyrrho of Elis,
founder of the more radical Pyrrhonist tradition, is a genuinely significant figure
who earns at least a sentence — he appears only in the fact pack, never in the draft.
**Fact-pack status:** Fully covered in §1 (Pyrrho, Arcesilaus, Carneades), §2
(Skeptics section), §3 (Pyrrho absent: true noted), §4 (Pyrrho/Carneades anecdotes),
§6 (influence chain points 14). The Carneades Rome embassy story is explicitly flagged
as a "set-piece anecdote." No new verification needed — all material exists in the pack.

### M5 — "First philosopher" framing: Aristotle's designation is mentioned but the scope qualifier is buried
**What is missing:** The draft correctly handles the "first philosopher" attribution
("Tradition, following the philosopher Aristotle… calls Thales the first philosopher
of the Western tradition"). However, the Western-scope qualifier appears *after* the
attribution, and in a slightly passive form. The pipeline spec specifies this as a
framing gate requirement ("the era's opening must say Western philosophy begins in
Ionia — not 'philosophy begins'"). The draft mostly gets this right, but the first
sentence of Ch 1 says "the first philosopher of the Western tradition" — which is
fine. The ERA hook and "Why this is a break" block are clean on this.
**Decision: COVERED (with a note)** — this is fine as written. The pipeline framing
gate (Gate 5) may note the sequencing could be tighter, but it is not a
comprehensiveness gap.

### M6 — 529 AD ending: qualified version present, but the Boethius handoff is underweight
**What is missing:** The draft ends with a Boethius sentence and the Arabic
transmission as a one-clause mention. The fact pack notes Boethius is the "torch
passer" and should be substantive. The draft's current treatment ("a Roman official
named Boethius, awaiting execution in 524, wrote a book imagining philosophy itself
as a woman who comes to console him in his cell, and so passed the torch one more
time") is actually competent. The *Consolation* is described in a way that conveys
its nature.
**The 529 qualified version:** The draft covers this correctly and precisely, noting

stroke" and the continuity questions. This is well done.
**Decision:** COVERED for the 529 ending and Boethius. The Era 2 handoff (Arabic
transmission named) is present as a name-drop. THIN but appropriate at era altitude.

## SHOULD-CONSIDER (top 3 most valuable)

### SC1 — Gorgias: the Sophists' philosophical arm
The draft covers the Sophists almost entirely through Protagoras. Gorgias of Leontini
("nothing exists; if it did we couldn't know it; if we could, we couldn't communicate
it") is the more philosophically radical Sophist and the one whose influence on
rhetoric and language theory runs directly into Plato's *Gorgias* dialogue. At era
altitude one sentence would suffice: something like "Gorgias pushed further, arguing
not just that truth is relative but that it may not exist at all — and if it did, we
couldn't tell each other." This would make the Sophist-section feel like a school with
range rather than a one-man show. Fact-pack note: Gorgias' *On Non-Being* survives
only in summaries (fact pack §2, tagged PARAPHRASE-ONLY) — any sentence about him
should be paraphrase, no quotation marks.

### SC2 — The Platonic tripartite soul / divided line (LIGHT touch)
The draft's Plato chapter covers the Forms and the cave thoroughly, but the soul
doctrine and the epistemological ladder (which structures the Republic's political
argument — the different soul-types correspond to classes in the ideal city) are
absent. At era altitude a single sentence connecting the Forms to the soul-types
("Plato divided the soul into reason, spirit, and appetite, and the philosopher is
the person in whom reason rules — which is why only the philosopher should be king")
would make the philosopher-kings argument feel grounded rather than asserted. The
fact pack has material for this but it isn't deep-verified — fact-checker would need
to pin the tripartite-soul primary source before it ships as content.

### SC3 — The Carneades Rome embassy (155 BC) as the founding scene of Greek philosophy entering Rome
Currently the Skeptics are entirely absent and Cicero appears in Ch 7 without an
introduction to the intellectual world he was trained in (Academic Skepticism). Adding
even two sentences on the Carneades embassy — the three philosopher-ambassadors sent
to negotiate a fine, Carneades arguing FOR justice one day and AGAINST it the next,
Cato demanding they be expelled before the youth were corrupted — would accomplish
three things at once: introduce the Skeptics (M4 partially addressed), give Ch 7's
Rome section a founding scene, and explain why Cicero's probabilism ("we can't know
for certain, but we can act on what seems most probable") makes sense. The fact pack
covers this at §1 (Carneades), §2 (Skeptics steelman), §4 (embassy anecdote). This
is the highest-value addition because it addresses both M4 and enriches Ch 7.

   Already written in the fact pack brief ("old atomism of Democritus (Chapter 2)") —
   the author simply needs to insert it in Ch 6.
4. **M3 (Chrysippus THIN)** — borderline; one sentence explaining what he did to
   earn the "no Chrysippus, no Stoa" tag.

None of these require new verification. All material exists in the fact pack.
