## INDEPENDENT CHECKLIST (built before reading the draft)

Based on web research and standard scholarship on the era, a comprehensive treatment of "The rationalists and the empiricists" (Descartes through Hume, ~1637–1776) at era altitude must address the following:

### A. Era-scope framing
- Why start at Descartes, not Bacon or Galileo (the scientific revolution as the pressure the era is answering)
- Why end at Hume, not Kant (the crisis handed forward)
- The rationalist/empiricist split named and demythologized — a retroactive map, not a wartime alliance system
- The era's central question stated: what can reason establish on its own, starting from scratch?
- Scientific revolution context (Galileo, Newton, the new mechanical universe) as the thing every thinker here is responding to

### B. Descartes (the founding move)
- Method of doubt — three levels (senses, dreams, evil genius) walked, not just named
- The cogito — precise form, correct source attribution (*Discourse* vs. *Meditations* vs. *Principles*)
- Mind-body dualism (*res cogitans* / *res extensa*) — the specific sharpness of Descartes' version vs. Plato/Scholastics
- God as guarantor — the rebuild from the cogito, the Cartesian circle problem
- Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia — the interaction problem as a structural critique, Descartes' failed responses
- The wax argument — the rationalist epistemology worked as an example

### C. Spinoza
- Substance monism — one substance = God = Nature, proved geometrically; the *Ethics* structure
- The herem of 1656 — what we know and what the document does NOT say
- The two attributes humans know (thought and extension) — as the answer to the interaction problem
- Determinism and Spinoza's freedom (acting from own nature, not external compulsion)
- *Sub specie aeternitatis* and the intellectual love of God — the closing vision
- The pantheism/atheism debate: neither label fits cleanly; accurate description

### D. Leibniz
- Monads — no extended building blocks, sealed, mirror the universe
- Pre-established harmony — God synchronized the clocks at creation; no actual interaction
- Principle of sufficient reason — the crowbar on all questions including "why something rather than nothing"
- The best of all possible worlds — the argument (theodicy), not just the Voltaire punchline
- The Voltaire/Candide parody clearly distinguished from Leibniz's actual position
- Spinoza vs. Leibniz contrast (same inheritance, opposite universes) — structurally essential

### E. Hobbes
- Materialism — thoroughgoing, including thought; no ghost in machine
- State of nature — the war of every man against every man (exact phrasing, not the Latin retronym)
- Social contract — subjects covenant with each other, sovereign makes no promise, near-absolute authority
- The English Civil War as the biographical matrix

### F. Locke
- Tabula rasa epistemology — Locke's actual words ("white paper," "empty cabinet"), not the later Latin tag
- Sensation and reflection as the two doors
- Primary and secondary qualities — with the key implication: secondary qualities exist only in perception
- Two Treatises — state of nature as lawful freedom (not Hobbes' war), consent, natural rights (life/liberty/estate), right of revolution
- Toleration — magistrate has no jurisdiction over souls, coercion cannot produce genuine faith
- Jefferson debt — acknowledged with the correct distinction (estate vs. pursuit of happiness)

### G. Berkeley
- The steelman: NOT denying tables and chairs; denying mind-independent matter
- The empiricist logic: if secondary qualities exist only in minds, why stop there?
- *Esse est percipi* — with Berkeley's actual phrasing (mixed Latin/English "Their ESSE is PERCIPI") vs. the later standardized form
- God's continuous perception as the solution to unperceived existence
- Berkeley as Locke's most rigorous student, not a crank

### H. Hume
- Hume's fork — relations of ideas vs. matters of fact; exhaustive; no third category
- Causation — constant conjunction only, never the necessity itself; custom/habit as the source of belief
- Induction problem — past experience cannot prove future resembles past; the argument bites its own tail
- Bundle self — never find the perceiver, only perceptions; the theatre analogy
- Is/ought gap — the illicit crossing every moral system makes
- "Reason is slave of passions" — correctly read as a claim about ends vs. means, not a celebration of unreason
- Religion — the design argument taken apart in the *Dialogues*, mitigated skepticism rather than atheism
- The *Dialogues* postponed until after death — deliberate, and why
- Hume's composure at death — Adam Smith letter as documented record; why it was philosophically charged

### I. Émilie du Châtelet
- *Institutions de Physique* (1740) — Newton's mechanics + Leibnizian metaphysical foundations (principle of sufficient reason)
- Her Newton translation as the standard French edition, with her own commentary
- The vis viva debate and her specific contribution (mv² confirmed empirically; cited by young Kant in 1747)
- Her placement at the era's close: bridge to German Enlightenment, not a footnote to the men
- The women's-erasure story told honestly

### J. Political philosophy thread
- Social contract tradition as its own through-line: Hobbes absolutist → Locke liberal → (Rousseau deferred to later eras or noted briefly)
- Natural rights language established here (Locke) as foundation of the Enlightenment political settlement
- The Jefferson/American founders debt named concretely

### K. Scientific revolution context
- The new Galilean/Newtonian mechanical universe as the pressure every thinker here is responding to
- Why the old Scholastic metaphysics needed replacing: the new science had already dissolved the Aristotelian forms-and-substances framework
- Descartes and Leibniz as scientists as well as philosophers (calculus; the vortex theory)
- Newton named: his mechanics is the fact everyone has to synthesize with metaphysics

### L. Influence chain — no silent links
- Era 2 → Descartes: Avicenna's flying man as structural parallel to the cogito; Ockham's voluntarism as ancestor of the demon thought experiment
- Descartes → Princess Elisabeth → the interaction problem (both directions)
- Descartes → Spinoza (the geometric method taken all the way)
- Descartes → Leibniz (interaction problem taken differently)
- Spinoza → Leibniz (the 1676 visit; the monadology as the pluralist answer)
- Hobbes → Locke (the liberal rebuttal to the absolutist contract)
- Locke → Berkeley (empiricism taken to its logical conclusion)
- Berkeley → Hume (bundle self applies Berkeley's move on matter to the self)
- Locke → Hume (the framework inherited and radicalized)
- Du Châtelet → German Enlightenment/Kant (vis viva debate cited by young Kant 1747)
- Hume → Kant (the dogmatic slumber handoff; Kant's own words, not a claim about Hume)

### M. Era bookends
- Opening: earns starting at Descartes from era 2 (Ockham's question handed forward)
- Close: the crisis Hume hands to Kant — a knife sharpened and handed forward, not a resolution; Kant's name and the new architecture named

## MUST-ADD LIST (in priority order)

**MA-1 — Scientific revolution context: the collapse of the Aristotelian framework is the era's missing backdrop**

The draft never tells the reader what had changed about *nature* between Aquinas and Descartes. Every thinker in this era — Descartes, Hobbes, Locke, Leibniz, Hume — was responding to a revolution in natural philosophy that had already overturned the Aristotelian physics and cosmology on which the Scholastic synthesis rested. Galileo showed the earth moves; the new mechanical universe had no place for substantial forms or final causes; the question "what can reason establish on its own?" is urgent precisely because the old framework had collapsed and a new one was needed. Hobbes was in correspondence with Galileo; Locke explicitly described himself as an "underlabourer" clearing ground for Newton's science; du Châtelet spent her career integrating Newton with Leibniz.

The draft opens on Descartes in the heated room asking a founding question, but the reader has no idea what had made the old answers inadequate. At era altitude, a short passage in the break block or the hook (four to six sentences) naming the scientific revolution as the context — Galileo's earth moves, Newton's mechanical universe, Aristotelian physics overthrown — would close this gap without becoming a history-of-science chapter. The fact pack does not flag this as required (it focuses on internal philosophical chain), but at era altitude it is genuinely essential: the era makes no sense without it.

**New verification needed:** none — this is standard context; no new fact-finding required.

**MA-2 — Rousseau: absent without acknowledgment**

Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1712–1778) is the third major voice in the social-contract tradition that Ch 4 establishes. His *Social Contract* (1762) falls within this era's chronological scope and is the direct transformation of the Hobbes/Locke tradition into the political theory that will power the French Revolution. Rousseau's absence from the draft is not automatically a defect — "selective is correct" — but the draft closes the political philosophy thread with Locke in Ch 4 and never returns to it, leaving the reader with no sense that the tradition continued or that a third position existed. A one-sentence acknowledgment — "The tradition Hobbes and Locke founded would be taken in a third direction by Jean-Jacques Rousseau (*The Social Contract*, 1762), who grounded legitimate authority in the 'general will' rather than individual consent, pointing toward the French Revolution rather than the English one; that is the next chapter's story" — would close the gap without expanding Ch 4's scope.

**New verification needed:** none for a one-sentence forward pointer; a fact-checker pin on Rousseau dates and *Social Contract* date if it becomes prose.

**MA-3 — Ockham's God-can-do-anything skepticism as the demon's medieval ancestor: the era-2-in chain link is dropped**

The era 2 draft carried al-Ghazālī's occasionalism → Ockham → Descartes' demon as one of its two cross-tradition through-lines, and the spec flags this influence chain as required reading for era 3's opening. The era 3 draft names Avicenna's flying man as the era-2 parallel (correct) but does not pick up the Ockham link. The fact pack chain item 1 explicitly names "Ockham's via moderna → the God-can-do-anything skepticism about necessary connections" as the demon's "medieval grandparents." A reader who starts at era 3 without having read era 2 has no way to know this ancestry. One sentence, framed as ancestry/parallel rather than documented direct influence, would close the link.

**New verification needed:** none — pack has the framing; the fact-checker confirmed the via moderna link in the era 2 gate.

## SHOULD-CONSIDER LIST

**SC-1 — Du Châtelet's vis viva contribution: her specific scientific move is absent**

The draft names the *Institutions de Physique* and her bridge role but does not state her specific contribution: she confirmed Leibniz's *vis viva* (kinetic energy = mv²) against the Newtonian mv through experiment (building on Willem 's Gravesande's work), and the debate this sparked was cited by the young Kant in his very first publication (1747). This is what makes her a thinker contributing a live result, not a synthesizer of other people's work. One sentence on the mv² result and the Kant citation would close the gap and give concrete weight to the "original contribution" framing.

**New verification needed:** the mv² claim and the Kant-1747 citation are confirmed by web research (Project Vox, History of Women Philosophers); a fact-checker confirmation against the SEP entry for du Châtelet before the sentence ships.

**SC-2 — Malebranche: the third answer to the interaction problem, currently invisible**

The draft gives two solutions to the interaction problem Descartes left open: Spinoza's (dissolve it — one substance) and Leibniz's (avoid it — pre-established harmony). Nicolas Malebranche's occasionalism is the third major solution of the era — God directly mediates every interaction between mind and body — and it is in the fact pack chain item 5. At era altitude a single clause would suffice. The omission is not fatal, but naming all three solutions gives the reader a genuine sense of the era's philosophical richness and avoids the impression that Spinoza and Leibniz were the only responses.

**New verification needed:** none — standard scholarship; the fact pack has the chain.

**SC-3 — Francis Bacon: the empiricist tradition has a grandfather who goes unmentioned**

Francis Bacon (1561–1626) is the systematic founder of empiricist method in natural philosophy — the Baconian inductive method — and is routinely named alongside Hobbes and Locke as the tradition's origin point. His absence from the draft means the empiricist tradition appears to start with Hobbes, when the standard account places Bacon first. At era altitude, one sentence — "The empiricists inherited a method Francis Bacon had already named: start with observation, not with first principles" — would close the gap. Whether this belongs in the break block or in Ch 4's opening is an editorial choice.

**New verification needed:** none — standard scholarship.
