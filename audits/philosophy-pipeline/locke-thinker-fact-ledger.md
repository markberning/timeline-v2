## Quote discipline (HARD floor)
- Locke wrote in **English**; all his works are long public domain. There is no
  translation question (the *Letter Concerning Toleration* is quoted in **William
  Popple's 1689 English translation**, itself PD, since the original *Epistola* is
  Latin; that is the only translated surface).
- Every quotation below was **string-matched against a PD source** (Project
  Gutenberg #7370 for the *Two Treatises*; Wikisource for the *Essay* and the
  *Letter*). Cited by book/chapter/section.
- **"tabula rasa" is NOT Locke's phrase.** He wrote "white paper." The Latin tag is
  the traditional label later commentators pinned on him (it traces through Aquinas
  to Aristotle's *De Anima*, where the mind is likened to a writing-tablet with
  nothing actually written on it). The read SAYS this — never puts "tabula rasa" in
  Locke's mouth. (Era-3 read already flags this; held consistent.)

## Verified quotations — cited, string-matched

### *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689, title-page 1690) — Wikisource, Book II
- **II.1.2** (the blank-mind image) — "Let us then suppose the Mind to be, as we
  say, white Paper, void of all Characters, without any Ideas"; the question "How
  comes it to be furnished?"; the answer "in one word, From Experience." ✅
- **II.1.3** (sensation) — "This great Source, of most of the Ideas we have,
  depending wholly upon our Senses, and derived by them to our Understanding, I call
  SENSATION." ✅
- **II.1.4** (reflection) — "I call this REFLECTION, the Ideas it affords being such
  only, as the Mind gets by reflecting on its own Operations within it self"; also
  "that notice which the Mind takes of its own Operations, and the manner of them."
  ✅
- **II.8.9** (primary qualities list) — primary qualities are "Solidity, Extension,
  Motion or Rest, Number and Figure." ✅
- **II.8.10/14** (secondary qualities) — secondary qualities are "Powers to produce
  various Sensations in us, and depend on those primary Qualities, viz. Bulk, Figure,
  Texture, and Motion of Parts." ✅
- **II.8.15** (no resemblance) — "The Ideas, produced in us by these Secundary
  Qualities, have no resemblance of them at all. There is nothing like our Ideas,
  existing in the Bodies themselves." ✅
  (Spelling/capitalization is the period source's; in the read these are quoted in
  modernized but verbatim-faithful form, capitalization normalized, NO word changed.
  The capital-S "Secundary" and "Ideas" are the original; the read renders the
  no-resemblance line as the standard "have no resemblance of them at all. There is
  nothing like our ideas, existing in the bodies themselves" — wording identical,
  casing modernized, which the era-3 read already does the same way.)

### *Two Treatises of Government* (1689, title-page 1690), Second Treatise — Gutenberg #7370, string-matched
- **§4** (state of nature, freedom) — "a state of perfect freedom to order their
  actions, and dispose of their possessions and persons, as they think fit, within
  the bounds of the law of nature, without asking leave, or depending upon the will
  of any other man." ✅ (Continues: "A state also of equality, wherein all the power
  and jurisdiction is reciprocal, no one having more than another" — the equality
  half, §4.) ✅
- **§6** (law of nature) — "no one ought to harm another in his life, health,
  liberty, or possessions"; and that "reason, which is that law, teaches all
  mankind, who will but consult it." ✅
- **§27** (labour theory of property) — "every man has a property in his own
  person… The labour of his body, and the work of his hands, we may say, are
  properly his." Whatever he removes from nature, "he hath mixed his labour with,
  and joined to it something that is his own, and thereby makes it his property." ✅
- **§95** (consent) — "MEN being, as has been said, by nature, all free, equal, and
  independent, no one can be put out of this estate, and subjected to the political
  power of another, without his own consent." ✅
- **§123** (property as the general name; the three protected things) — men join in
  society "for the mutual preservation of their lives, liberties and estates, which
  I call by the general name, property." ✅ (NB: Locke's triad is **life, liberty,
  estate**; Jefferson's "pursuit of happiness" and "consent of the governed" are
  Jefferson's rewordings, not Locke's — era-3 read already notes this; held
  consistent, NOT repeated at length.)
- **§222** (the right of revolution / dissolution) — when "the legislators endeavour
  to take away, and destroy the property of the people, or to reduce them to slavery
  under arbitrary power, they put themselves into a state of war with the people, who
  are thereupon absolved from any farther obedience." It then "devolves to the
  people, who have a right to resume their original liberty, and, by the
  establishment of a new legislative… provide for their own safety and security." ✅
- **§19–21** ("appeal to heaven") — where there is "no judge on earth," and force is
  used without authority to decide, the only remedy is "an appeal to heaven." ✅
  (Used as Locke's own phrase for the last-resort right against a tyrant who has put
  himself in a state of war; framed as Locke's phrasing, not asserted as a modern
  doctrine.)

### *A Letter Concerning Toleration* (1689, Popple's English translation) — Wikisource
- (commonwealth's scope) — "The commonwealth seems to me to be a society of men
  constituted only for the procuring, preserving, and advancing their own civil
  interests." ✅
- (belief cannot be forced) — "such is the nature of the understanding, that it
  cannot be compelled to the belief of anything by outward force." ✅

## Verified biography (SEP "John Locke"; Britannica; standard scholarship)
- **Born 29 August 1632, Wrington, Somerset, England.** Father a country
  lawyer/attorney who served as a cavalry captain on the **Parliamentary** side in
  the English Civil War. [SEP; Britannica]
- **Westminster School**, then **Christ Church, Oxford** (matriculated 1652).
  Trained in **medicine**; practiced as a physician (never took a medical degree in
  the ordinary track but was licensed/qualified). [SEP]
- **1666: met Anthony Ashley Cooper** (created **1st Earl of Shaftesbury** 1672);
  became his physician, secretary, and political adviser, joining his household.
  **1668: Locke oversaw an operation** to drain a dangerous liver cyst/abscess from
  Shaftesbury via an inserted **silver tube/cannula**, credited with saving his
  life. [SEP; Britannica]
- **1683: fled to the Dutch Republic (Netherlands)** amid the fallout of the **Rye
  House Plot** and Shaftesbury's fall; lived in exile, partly under assumed names.
  **Returned to England February 1689**, after the **Glorious Revolution (1688)**,
  in the convoy that carried Mary (wife of William of Orange). [SEP; Britannica]
- **Works + first publication:** the *Essay* (Dec 1689, dated 1690); the *Two
  Treatises* (1689, dated 1690), **published anonymously**; the *Letter Concerning
  Toleration* (Latin *Epistola de Tolerantia* written 1685, Latin printed 1689,
  Popple's English translation 1689), **also anonymous**; *Some Thoughts Concerning
  Education* (1693); *The Reasonableness of Christianity* (1695). Locke **never
  publicly acknowledged authorship of the *Two Treatises* in his lifetime** —
  acknowledged only in a codicil to his will. [SEP; Britannica]
- **Died 28 October 1704, at Oates**, the Masham family house in Essex, where he
  had lived his last years; **Lady Damaris Masham** (herself a philosopher) was
  reading the Psalms to him as he died. He **never married**. [SEP; Britannica]

## Honest-warts (named honestly; era-3 read carries the headline, this read goes deeper)
- **Royal African Company**: Locke was an **investor/shareholder** in the Royal
  African Company, the English crown's monopoly on the Atlantic slave trade (he held
  stock in the 1670s–1680s, and also invested in the Bahama Adventurers). The
  philosopher of natural liberty profited from the trade in enslaved people. [SEP
  "Locke on slavery"; standard scholarship]
- **Fundamental Constitutions of Carolina (1669)**: Locke, as **secretary to the
  Lords Proprietors of Carolina**, had a hand in drafting this colonial charter,
  which entrenched hereditary slavery: every freeman "shall have absolute power and
  authority over his negro slaves, of what opinion or religion soever." [the
  document; SEP] The exact authorship share is debated (he was secretary and
  copied/edited the document; whether he composed the slavery clause is contested),
  so the read says he **had a hand in drafting / was its scribe-and-editor**, not
  "wrote the clause," to stay precise. The tension between "no one may harm another
  in his life, liberty, or possessions" and an investment in, and legal
  architecture of, chattel slavery is part of the record, stated flat, neither
  sanitized nor turned into a lecture (per the pipeline framing gate + the era-3
  read's existing treatment, which this read does NOT restate but goes one level
  deeper on: the **selective reach** of "all men," and how the American founders
  borrowed the frame at the moment slavery was expanding).

## Position summaries (paraphrase, each tied to the verified text — make-them-GET-it)
- **No innate ideas** (*Essay* Book I): the polemical target is the rationalist /
  common-belief view that some ideas or principles are stamped on the mind at birth
  (the law of non-contradiction, the idea of God). Locke's argument: if a principle
  were innate it would be universally assented to, yet children and "idiots" assent
  to no such principles, and whole peoples lack the supposedly innate idea of God;
  "universal consent" fails, so the innateness hypothesis is unneeded. Worked in
  third person.
- **White paper + the two fountains** (*Essay* II.1): the mind starts empty; all its
  content arrives by **sensation** (the five senses reporting the outer world) and
  **reflection** (the mind noticing its own operations — perceiving, doubting,
  willing). Simple ideas come in only by these two doors; the mind then combines,
  compares, abstracts to build everything else, but invents no simple idea from
  nothing. The blind man has no idea of scarlet. (Goes DEEPER than the era read by
  walking the build-from-simples machinery and the limit it implies.)
- **Primary vs secondary qualities** (*Essay* II.8): primary qualities (solidity,
  extension, figure, motion/rest, number) are really in the body whether observed or
  not; secondary qualities (color, sound, taste, smell, warmth) are only **powers**
  in the body, grounded in its primary qualities, to produce sensations in us, and
  the ideas they cause "have no resemblance" to anything in the body. Worked example
  (third person): the same tepid water feels warm to one hand and cool to the other
  (Locke's own example, II.8.21), so warmth is in the perceiving, not the water.
  This is the crack Berkeley walks through — the read points forward, does not
  restate Berkeley.
- **The limits of knowledge** (*Essay* Book IV): Locke does not claim experience
  yields certainty about everything. Knowledge proper is "the perception of the
  connexion and agreement, or disagreement and repugnancy, of any of our ideas"
  (IV.1.2 — paraphrased, since the read does not quote Book IV verbatim), so it
  reaches only as far as our ideas reach. We have no real knowledge of the inner
  **substance** of things (he calls substance a "something, I know not what" that we
  suppose to underlie qualities — paraphrased), and our knowledge of the external
  world and of other minds is at best probable, not demonstrative. The man who
  founded empiricism set its **modest ceiling** himself. (NEW depth vs era read,
  which does not cover Book IV; paraphrase-only, no Book IV quotation.)
- **State of nature, free and equal — against Hobbes** (*2nd Treatise* §4–6): strip
  away government and you do not get Hobbes's war but a state of "perfect freedom"
  and "equality" governed by a **law of nature** knowable by reason, whose core is
  that no one ought to harm another in life, health, liberty, or possessions.
  Rights exist before the state. The era read states this; the thinker read walks
  WHY the law of nature binds without a policeman (reason + each person as God's
  workmanship, §6) and what the state of nature actually lacks (a common judge),
  which is the engine of the consent argument.
- **Property by labour** (*2nd Treatise* §27): the earth is given in common, but a
  man owns his own person and so his **labour**; mixing labour with a common thing
  (picking the apple, tilling the field) makes it his. Property is therefore
  pre-political and not a grant of the sovereign — the basis for limiting what
  government may take. (NEW vs era read, which does not cover the labour theory.)
- **Government by consent + the trustee model** (*2nd Treatise* §95, §123–124):
  because all are free and equal, legitimate political power arises only by
  **consent**; people quit the state of nature for the safety of a common law,
  judge, and enforcer, uniting "for the mutual preservation of their lives,
  liberties and estates, which I call by the general name, property." Government is
  a **trust/trustee**, hired to protect the rights people already had, bound by the
  terms of the job — NOT (as in Hobbes) a sovereign who makes no promise.
- **The right of revolution** (*2nd Treatise* §222, §19–21): if government turns
  predator — taking life, liberty, property; ruling by arbitrary power — it breaks
  the trust, puts itself "into a state of war with the people," who are "absolved
  from any farther obedience" and may dissolve it and set up a new legislative.
  Locke's name for the last resort against a tyrant is the **"appeal to heaven."**
  This is the explosive part Jefferson lifted (era read notes the borrowing; this
  read walks the mechanism).
- **Toleration** (*Letter*, 1689): the commonwealth is "a society of men constituted
  only for the procuring, preserving, and advancing their own civil interests"
  (life, liberty, property, health), and has no jurisdiction over souls, because
  belief "cannot be compelled… by outward force." Forced belief is not belief, so
  coercion cannot serve the religious end it claims; the magistrate's sword and the
  care of souls are different businesses. (Era read has the one-liner; this read
  walks the actual argument and notes the limits Locke set — he did not extend
  toleration to atheists, who he thought could not be trusted to keep oaths, nor
  fully to Catholics, framed as a state-allegiance worry — NEW honest qualification.)

## Caricature / framing discipline
- **"Locke invented the blank slate"** — frame: the blank-mind idea is older
  (Aristotle's writing-tablet, the Stoics); Locke gave it a full theory of how the
  blank fills (sensation + reflection) and weaponized it against innatism. Don't
  overclaim originality of the metaphor.
- **"tabula rasa is Locke's phrase"** — FALSE; he wrote "white paper." Held from era
  read.
- **"Locke = pure common sense, the unproblematic empiricist"** — he set hard limits
  on knowledge (Book IV) and left "substance" a mystery; he was not naïve about
  experience's reach.
- **Toleration was universal** — it was NOT: atheists and (in effect) Catholics
  excluded. Say so.
- **The slavery facts** — neither sanitized nor inflated; the Carolina-clause
  authorship share is contested (he was secretary/editor), so "had a hand in
  drafting" not "wrote it."
- No Diogenes-Laertius-class legend vector (Locke is a documented 17th-c figure);
  the silver-tube operation and the deathbed-Psalms detail are well-attested
  biography, not legend, but stated as the standard account.
