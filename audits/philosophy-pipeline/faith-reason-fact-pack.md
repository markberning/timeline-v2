# Fact pack — "Faith meets reason" (Philosophy era 2, kind = ERA)

**Scope:** Western philosophy from late antiquity (Hypatia's Alexandria, Augustine)
through Ockham (~AD 380–1350) — the thousand years philosophy worked inside (and
across) the revealed religions: Christian, Islamic, and Jewish. Era altitude: the
sweep, not thinker-depth. The Arabic transmission and the Jewish thread are INSIDE
this story (pipeline doc framing gate), not detours.
**Built:** 2026-06-12, per `audits/philosophy-content-pipeline.md`. Every quotation
below was verified word-for-word against the linked text on this date (fetched, not
recalled — Gutenberg/archive.org texts downloaded whole and grepped; web pages
fetched); every image URL was checked on this date (HTTP status noted per file —
two files were API-verified but HEAD-throttled, flagged in §7). The author writes
ONLY from this pack.

**Status tags (same scheme as era 1):**
- `VERIFIED` — exact wording confirmed against the linked source, translation named.
- `PARAPHRASE-ONLY` — idea is real, no verbatim PD English wording verified; ship as paraphrase, never in quote marks.
- `DOCUMENTED` — fact/anecdote with a source close to the event (named).
- `LATE-TRADITION` — earliest source generations later (named, gap stated); usable framed as tradition.
- `LATE-LEGEND (HAGIO)` — **this era's Diogenes Laertius is the saint's Life**: Vitae, canonization testimony, and chroniclers writing 50–300 years later (William of Tocco on Aquinas, William of Malmesbury on Eriugena, the Vita Hildegardis). Frame as "by his biographer's account," "as the canonization witnesses told it."
- `APOCRYPHAL` — do not use except explicitly AS a debunk.

**PD-English landscape (stated up front, per brief):** the Latin canon is richly
covered (Augustine, Boethius, Anselm, Abelard, Aquinas all VERIFIED below). For the

## 0. Chapter-arc assessment (requested flag)

The proposed 7-chapter arc holds. The material argues for these adjustments, none
structural:

1. **Ch2 needs Eriugena as the exception that proves the quiet centuries.** John
   Scotus Eriugena (Irish, at Charles the Bald's court, c. 800–c. 877) is the one
   genuinely original Latin philosopher between Boethius and Anselm — including him
   makes the "thin centuries" claim honest instead of absolute (and his two legends
   are ledger gold, §4). Also give the copying-machine beat a named anchor:
   Cassiodorus' Vivarium and the Carolingian schools (Alcuin) — both safe at
   paraphrase level (see §10).
2. **Ch3 must name the actual translators, not just "the House of Wisdom."** The
   romanticized-institution caveat checks out HARD (§2: Gutas — the "academy" picture
   is a modern myth; the verified story is a translation *movement*, with Hunayn ibn
   Isḥāq as its master translator and al-Kindī's circle as philosophy's wing). This
   is a better story than the myth: patrons paying for Aristotle by the book.
3. **Ch5 should plant Peter Lombard's *Sentences*** (the textbook every later
   scholastic — Aquinas, Scotus, Ockham — lectured on; Ockham's razor phrasings
   literally live in his Sentences commentary, §3). One sentence suffices; the
   influence chain needs the hook. Bernard of Chartres' "dwarfs on the shoulders of
   giants" (via John of Salisbury, 1159 — §3) is ch5's epigraph-grade line.
4. **Ch6 needs Albertus Magnus** (Aquinas' teacher, the dumb-ox anecdote's other
   character) and **ch6→7 needs a Duns Scotus beat** — without Scotus the
   Aquinas→Ockham jump has a silent missing link (the comprehensiveness critic will
   flag it; Ockham's razor formulas are *Scotus'* formulas sharpened, §3/§8). A
   paragraph, not a section.
5. **Ch7's close**: Ockham dies April 1347 (NOT of the Black Death — the 1349
   plague-death story is outdated, §1), and the era's fraying hands TWO things to
   era 3: the via moderna's God-can-do-anything skepticism about necessary
   connections (Descartes' demon has medieval grandparents) and the
   universities-vs-new-science setup. Name Descartes once, as the handoff.
6. **Hypatia's role is the handoff scene, not a doctrine.** Her teaching
   (Neoplatonism, mathematics) is real but thin in sources; the death is the era's
   single biggest legend magnet (§4 — the verified 5th-c. account vs. Gibbon's and
   Sagan's embellishments). Pair her with the era-1 handoff (she "succeeded to the
   school of Plato and Plotinus" — the ancient source's own phrase, §3).
7. **Framing-gate scope line:** this era the guard is the inverse of era 1's — not
   "Western philosophy begins" but **"the thread is one story across three
   religions"**: Baghdad and Córdoba are not a detour from the Western story, they
   are the road. Maimonides is required, not optional (brief).

## 2. Position summaries (steelmanned; ⚠️ = potted-summary trap pre-flagged)

**Augustine** — the absorption of Neoplatonism into Christian theology (the era-1
Plotinus handoff): the "books of the Platonists" taught him to conceive
incorporeal reality and evil-as-privation, and then he says they could not teach
him humility (VERIFIED §3 — he names what he found there and what he didn't). The
*Confessions* invents the inward genre: God sought through memory and
self-examination ("Thou madest us for Thyself..." §3). On **time** (Conf. XI): the
famous honesty ("If no one asks me, I know" §3) plus the actual position — past
and future exist only in the mind (memory, expectation); time is a distension of
the soul — at era altitude this is THE late-antique worked example: run the
"measure a sound while it's still sounding" puzzle. *City of God*: history as
**two cities "formed by two loves"** (VERIFIED §3) — not church-vs-state but two
orientations of desire cutting through both. ⚠️ Trap 1: "Augustine the
sex-obsessed killjoy" — the man of "give me chastity, only not yet" (§3) wrote
the era's frankest account of desire; tell him as the analyst of divided will,
not the censor. ⚠️ Trap 2: the *Confessions* is not a modern autobiography — it's
a prayer with an audience. ⚠️ Trap 3: don't credit the tolle-lege conversion as
cinema vérité — it's HIS literary reconstruction a decade later (§4).

**Boethius** — the bridge, in both directions. The *Consolation*: Lady Philosophy
visits the condemned man; Fortune speaks in her own voice ("I turn the wheel that
spins" §3 — the medieval imagination's single most durable image); the cure runs
from "you never owned what Fortune lent" up to the eternity solution — God's
eternity is "the possession of endless life whole and perfect at a single moment"
(§3), so foreknowledge doesn't force the future: God doesn't FOREsee, he SEES.
Project: to translate and comment on ALL of Plato and Aristotle (SEP) — he was
executed a few logic-texts in, and **those few translations were most of what the
Latin West had of Aristotle for 500 years**: the thinness of ch2 is downstream of
one execution. His Porphyry commentary poses the **problem of universals**
(whether genera and species really exist or are only in the mind — Porphyry had
listed the questions and declined to answer; PARAPHRASE-ONLY, no PD English
verified for the Isagoge wording). ⚠️ Trap: the *Consolation* never names Christ —
resist both the "secretly pagan" and the "obviously Christian" overreadings; the
silence is famous and openly debated (he also wrote theological tractates).
⚠️ Trap 2: "last of the Romans, first of the scholastics" is a modern historians'
tag — usable as description, never as a quote (no stable source; §9).

**The quiet centuries (ch2's honesty block)** — stated plainly: after Boethius the
Latin West produces little original philosophy for ~500 years, and the reasons are
material, not mental: the school system collapsed with the western empire; books
survive only where someone copies them; the copying machine was the monastery
(Cassiodorus' Vivarium, Benedictine scriptoria, then the Carolingian
court schools — Alcuin — standardizing texts and script). Philosophy didn't die;
it waited in the warehouses. Eriugena is the honest exception (one genuinely
original system, c. 860s, promptly too strange for everyone). ⚠️ Trap: "Dark
Ages" sneering is blacklisted (§9) — and so is the inverse over-correction;
the genuine thinness is itself the load-bearing fact that makes the Arabic
chapters matter.

**The translation movement (ch3 setup)** — the verified story: Abbasid Baghdad,
late 8th–10th c., paid serious money (caliphs, viziers, families of patrons) to
turn Greek science and philosophy into Arabic; Hunayn ibn Isḥāq and his workshop
set the standard for method; al-Kindī's circle produced philosophy's wing
(Aristotle, Plotinus — including the *Theology of Aristotle*, actually Plotinus
misattributed, a mislabel with centuries of consequences). ⚠️ Trap (the brief's
caveat, CONFIRMED): the **"House of Wisdom" as a giant academy/university is a
romanticized modern image** — Gutas and others argue the Bayt al-Ḥikma was
probably a palace library/bureau (possibly mainly for Persian material), the
sources naming it are thin, and the major translators don't mention working
there. Tell the movement, not the campus. Sources: SEP "al-Kindi" (fetched);
Gutas' position as reported in the History-of-the-House-of-Wisdom literature
([Wikipedia: House of Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wisdom),
[Renaissance Mathematicus overview](https://thonyc.wordpress.com/2019/05/29/the-house-of-wisdom-is-a-myth/)).

**al-Kindī** — the manifesto of the whole movement: truth has no passport.
SEP's rendering of his *On First Philosophy* (quotable as-quoted-in-SEP, §3):
"We must not be ashamed to admire the truth or to acquire it, from wherever it
comes." First system-builder in Arabic; Greek philosophy shown compatible with
monotheism (creation, divine unity). ⚠️ Trap: don't inflate him into Avicenna's
equal — his role is the door-opener.

**al-Fārābī** — "the Second Teacher" (after Aristotle): logic naturalized in
Arabic; philosophy organized as a system of sciences; *The Virtuous City* — an
emanationist cosmos with a Platonic political philosophy on top (the excellent
city led by a philosopher-prophet). The Avicenna hook: Avicenna says he read
Aristotle's *Metaphysics* forty times uncomprehending until a Fārābī commentary
unlocked it — the anecdote is from Avicenna's own autobiography
(LATE-ish but first-person; PARAPHRASE-ONLY, no PD English verified). ⚠️ Trap:
biography — SEP: "we know little that is really reliable"; don't decorate his life.

**Avicenna (Ibn Sīnā)** — the era's first big worked example, run in two moves
(both PARAPHRASE-ONLY — no PD English; the pack supplies content, not quotes):
(1) **The flying man** (*Shifāʾ*, Psychology/De Anima — opening of Book I, revisited
in Book V): imagine yourself created mid-air, adult, blindfolded, limbs splayed so
nothing touches nothing — no sight, no sound, no body-feel, no memory. Would you
know you exist? Avicenna says yes — you'd affirm your SELF while unable to affirm
your body. So self-awareness doesn't come through the senses; the soul is not just
the body. (Often read as a forerunner of Descartes' cogito — frame the comparison
as the era-3 handoff it is, with the difference: Avicenna isn't doubting,
he's isolating.) (2) **Essence vs. existence**: WHAT a thing is never tells you
THAT it is — horses and unicorns have equally tidy essences; existence has to be
added. Everything whose existence is borrowed points up a chain to something whose
essence IS its existence — the **Necessary Existent** — Avicenna's God, arrived at
by metaphysics, not scripture. This distinction runs straight into Aquinas (§6).
⚠️ Trap: "Avicenna = Aristotle in Arabic" — he corrects and rebuilds Aristotle into
his own system (the era-1 r2 lesson: commentary ≠ translation, and Avicenna is
neither — he's an original).

**al-Ghazālī** — the era's great internal critic, in his own voice (VERIFIED §3,
Field): the professor with 300 students who examined his knowledge and found
nothing certain but "sense-perceptions and necessary principles" — then watched
the dream argument eat sense-perception too; two months "a thoroughgoing sceptic";
cured "not to a concatenation of proofs and arguments, but to the light which God
caused to penetrate into my heart"; the 1095 breakdown ("God caused an impediment
to chain my tongue... my mouth became dumb"). The *Incoherence of the
Philosophers*: NOT a bonfire — a demonstration that the philosophers' twenty key
teachings fail their OWN standard of demonstrative proof; only three are tagged
as unbelief (the world's pre-eternity; God knowing only universals; no bodily
resurrection) (SEP). His occasionalism beat: fire doesn't NECESSITATE burning —
God creates the burning alongside the fire (the cotton example is from the
*Incoherence*, 17th discussion — PARAPHRASE-ONLY); the deep point Hume will
re-discover: necessity is never OBSERVED. ⚠️ THE trap (pre-flagged by the brief,
CONFIRMED by SEP): **"al-Ghazālī killed Islamic philosophy" is a rejected potted
summary** — SEP: the *Incoherence* was "long... misunderstood in Western
literature as an anti-philosophical pamphlet"; it refutes "a *particular kind* of
philosophy," philosophy continued in the Islamic world after him (Averroes is
LITERALLY the next beat), and al-Ghazālī himself imported logic into theology. If
the caricature is used, frame it AS the caricature and tell the truth.

**Averroes (Ibn Rushd)** — Córdoba judge and court physician; "the Commentator":
three tiers of commentary across virtually the whole Aristotelian corpus (epitome /
middle / long — with SEP's caveat that the neat threefold scheme is partly
later tidying). The *Decisive Treatise* — a legal opinion (fatwā-form) on whether
philosophy is permitted: the Law itself commands rational reflection, and
**"truth cannot contradict truth"** (VERIFIED §3) — when demonstration and the
text's surface conflict, the text bears interpretation. *Incoherence of the
Incoherence*: the point-by-point answer to al-Ghazālī. Fell from favor 1195
(banished to Lucena, reasons unclear — SEP), rehabilitated, died Marrakesh 1198.
His Latin afterlife dwarfs his Arabic one — "the Commentator" beside "the
Philosopher" in every scholastic margin. ⚠️ THE trap (brief's caveat, CONFIRMED):
**"double truth" — the doctrine that something can be true in philosophy and false
in religion — is NOT Averroes' position**; the *Decisive Treatise*'s whole point
is ONE truth. The label descends from the 1277 condemnation's preamble (Tempier
accused Paris arts masters of talking "as if there were two contrary truths," §2
below) and from the "Latin Averroists" — and modern scholarship doubts even THEY
held it as stated. Attribute the caricature to its makers.

**Maimonides (Moses ben Maimon)** — the Jewish thread, required: born Córdoba
1138; fled the Almohads (convert-or-leave, 1148); Fez by 1160, Egypt 1166,
settled in Fustat (Cairo); court physician; the *Mishneh Torah* made him "the
leading rabbinic authority of his time" (SEP); the **Guide of the Perplexed**
(completed 1190, in Judeo-Arabic) — addressed precisely to the era's reader: the
religious man trained in the Law AND in philosophy, "lost in perplexity and
anxiety" (VERIFIED §3 — the Guide's own mission statement IS the era's mission
statement; use it). **Negative theology**: every positive description of God
smuggles in a creaturely comparison; we know God by stripping predicates —
"our knowledge consists in knowing that we are unable truly to comprehend Him,"
and the best praise is the Psalmist's: "Silence is praise to Thee" (both VERIFIED
§3, Guide I.59). Influence: Aquinas cites him (respectfully) as "Rabbi Moses";
SEP lists Aquinas, Spinoza, Leibniz, Newton among his readers. ⚠️ Trap:
don't soften the radicalism — Maimonides is not saying "God is mysterious";
he's saying positive God-talk is strictly false, and the religious payoff is
discipline, not despair.

**Anselm** — **the era's worked-example goldmine; run the argument honestly.**
Setting: the *Proslogion* (1077–78, at Bec) opens as a PRAYER — "I do not seek to
understand that I may believe, but I believe in order to understand" (VERIFIED §3)
— SEP's two corrections to the slogan: faith here is volitional (love and drive,
not mere opinion), and the arguments are nonetheless "intended to be convincing
even to unbelievers." The argument (ch. 2, VERIFIED §3): God is "a being than
which nothing greater can be conceived"; even the fool understands the phrase, so
it "exists in the understanding"; if it existed ONLY in the understanding, a
greater could be conceived (the same thing existing in reality too); contradiction;
therefore it exists in reality. Then **Gaunilo** — a fellow monk, answering "in
behalf of the fool": by that logic my perfect **lost island** ("more excellent
than all other countries," VERIFIED §3) must exist too. Anselm's reply: the logic
works for NOTHING except that-than-which-nothing-greater-can-be-conceived — an
island can always be out-islanded; only the maximal being's non-existence is
inconceivable. Era altitude: run argument + objection + reply as the chapter's
centerpiece and let the reader feel both the pull and the suspicion. ⚠️ Trap 1:
**"the ontological argument" is Kant's label, six centuries later** — anachronism
gate; call it Anselm's argument and mention the later name once. ⚠️ Trap 2: don't
ship the strawman version ("existence is a perfection, QED") — Anselm's own reply
to Gaunilo explicitly disowns the general principle (SEP).

**Abelard and Héloïse** — Abelard: the era's logic celebrity ("the pre-eminent
philosopher and theologian of the twelfth century," SEP). On **universals**: the
era's standing fight, seeded by Boethius' Porphyry (ch2 payoff) — Abelard's
position is that "universality is merely linguistic, not a feature of the world"
(SEP's wording — what's shared is the *name*, with a semantic story about how
words signify; SEP calls his overall stance "irrealism"). ***Sic et Non***: 158
theological questions, authorities stacked PRO and CON, no answers supplied —
the prologue's method-manifesto rendered by Robinson (VERIFIED §3): "The master
key of knowledge is, indeed, a persistent and frequent questioning... By doubting
we come to examine, and by examining we reach the truth." (Robinson's text is a
partly summarized rendering — say "as his prologue argues" and the verified lines
in quotes.) Sample questions VERIFIED §3 ("Is God the author of evil, or no?").
His **ethics of intention**: "the agent's intention alone determines the moral
worth of an action" (SEP). **Héloïse as a thinker (framing gate, verifiable
core):** her learning predates him; the *Historia*'s wedlock argument (which
Abelard reports as HERS) is a philosophical position — the scholar's life and
domestic life are incompatible (VERIFIED §3: "between books or tablets and
distaffs"); her letters argue **disinterested love** — she wanted "love alone,"
not the marriage bond, and the famous line that she'd rather be his mistress than
anyone's wife is VERIFIED in Bellows' Historia (§3 — Abelard's report; her own
letter's "empress" version exists only in non-PD or embellished translations —
see §9 trap). Letters: authenticity now largely settled FOR her authorship (one
honest sentence on the debate; §1). ⚠️ Trap: the love-story-prop framing — the
gate requires her treated as a thinker; the verified material above is sufficient
to do it without invention.

**The universities (ch5)** — the institutional invention: guilds of masters and
students (universitas = "corporation"), with charters, curricula, degrees;
Bologna (law), Paris (theology/arts), Oxford. **All three founding dates are
conventions, not events** — Bologna's "1088" is a 19th-century anniversary
convention; Paris coalesces around 1200 (royal privilege of Philip II); Oxford
grows through the late 12th c. Phrase as "by the late 1100s..." The lecture
format IS Lombard's *Sentences* + disputation — Abelard's sic-et-non method
institutionalized (with answers now required). Bernard of Chartres' giants line
(§3) is the self-image of the whole enterprise.

**Aquinas** — the grand synthesis, at era altitude: Aristotle (arriving via
Toledo, with Averroes in the margins) baptized into Christian theology. The
architecture: **faith and reason are two roads to one truth** — "grace does not
destroy nature but perfects it" (VERIFIED §3); some truths (God's existence)
reason can reach; others (Trinity) only revelation gives; none are contradictory.
**Teach the First Way as THE worked example** (VERIFIED §3, full text): things
are in motion; whatever moves is moved by another ("as the staff moves only
because it is put in motion by the hand"); no infinite regress of movers;
"therefore it is necessary to arrive at a first mover, put in motion by no other;
and this everyone understands to be God." Run it honestly: it's an argument about
dependence-right-now (the hand-staff chain), not about a long-ago first domino —
and flag what it doesn't prove (that the first mover is the God of any scripture
— Aquinas knows; "this everyone understands to be God" is doing announced work).
Bonus beat (VERIFIED §3): in the SAME article Aquinas states the parsimony
objection — "it is superfluous to suppose that what can be accounted for by a few
principles has been produced by many" — i.e., **the Summa's own Objection 2 is
Ockham's razor pointed at God, stated by Aquinas, sixty years before Ockham** —
a gift for ch7's setup. ⚠️ Trap 1: "Five Ways = five knock-down proofs / God in
five easy steps" — they're five compressed "ways" (viae) inside a beginners'
textbook, each leaning on a worked-out metaphysics; blacklist framing §9.
⚠️ Trap 2: the 1277 condemnation (below) post-dates and bruises his school —
don't write him as instantly triumphant; "official philosopher of the Church" is
a 19th-century outcome (Aeterni Patris, 1879), not a 13th-century one.

**The 1277 condemnations (ch6's institutional pushback)** — **7 March 1277**
(three years to the day after Aquinas died): Bishop **Stephen (Étienne) Tempier**
of Paris prohibits **219 propositions** on pain of excommunication; traditional
targets: the arts-faculty "Latin Averroists" **Siger of Brabant and Boethius of
Dacia** (named by tradition, not in the document — and only ~79 of 219 propositions
have been traced to known works, per Hissette's research via SEP). The preamble
accuses masters of saying things "true according to philosophy" but false
"according to the Catholic faith" — **the birth certificate of the "double truth"
caricature** (SEP: modern scholarship rejects that anyone actually held it).
Some articles touched Aquinas' own teaching (SEP). Significance, told with the
brake on: Duhem's thesis (the condemnation freed physics from Aristotle and
midwifed modern science) is "problematic" per contemporary historians — usable
only as a flagged claim, not a fact.

**Ockham and the fraying (ch7)** — **Nominalism landed plainly**: the realist
says "humanity" names a shared something; Ockham says **"everything that exists
in the created universe is a singular substance or a singular quality"** (SEP's
wording) — generality lives in concepts and words, not in the world. **The razor,
documented honestly (the era's tortoise-class catch — see §3 for the receipts):**
the textbook tag "entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity" (*entia non
sunt multiplicanda praeter necessitatem*) is "nowhere to be found in his texts"
(SEP). What Ockham actually wrote, repeatedly: **"Pluralitas non est ponenda sine
necessitate"** ("plurality is not to be posited without necessity") and **"frustra
fit per plura quod potest fieri per pauciora"** ("it is futile to do with more
what can be done with fewer") — and the same formulas are in **Duns Scotus before
him**; the *entia* wording first surfaces in **John Punch (Ponce of Cork), 1639**,
calling it a COMMON scholastic axiom. So the razor is real, medieval, and communal
— just not Ockham's sentence. And it has a theological safety-catch: for Ockham,
God's free choice means you can't deduce what exists from neatness — parsimony
governs explanations, not God. **Voluntarism**: the will (God's and ours) is
radically free — the human will "can knowingly act directly against [its]
ultimate good" (SEP); morality grounds in God's will, not in necessities God must
obey. **The Avignon fight** (§1 dates): poverty, papal heresy, excommunication,
Munich — philosophy's premier logician ends as a political pamphleteer against
the pope. **Why the synthesis cracked (the close):** Ockham's God is too free for
Aquinas' tidy two-roads architecture — if no created connection is necessary,
reason's reach shortens; theology and natural knowledge begin to file for
separation. Add the 14th century's material catastrophes (Avignon schism, the
Black Death striking the very next year, 1348) and the handoff to era 3 writes
itself: a Europe holding the era's question — what CAN reason establish alone? —
that a soldier in a stove-heated room will pick up in 1619. ⚠️ Trap: "Ockham the
proto-atheist with a razor" — he was a Franciscan theologian; the razor never
shaves God; blacklist framing §9.

## 5. "Why this is a break" block material

**The break: revelation arrives as both constraint and fuel.** The Greek
philosopher answered to argument alone; the medieval philosopher answers to
argument AND a text God wrote — and then the surprise that powers the whole era:
the text turns out to NEED philosophy (What is God's eternity? Is the world
eternal? What are the soul, evil, free will? The scriptures raise the questions
and do not hand over definitions).

**BEFORE — reason from anywhere (citable anchors, both already era-1-verified):**
- "ALL men by nature desire to know." — Aristotle, *Metaphysics* I.1 (980a), trans. W. D. Ross — VERIFIED in era-1 pack §3 (re-verified link: classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.1.i.html). Philosophy's permission slip is human nature itself; no scripture is consulted.
- Supporting before-color: Socrates following the argument wherever it leads (*Apology* material, era-1 verified).

**AFTER — reason inside faith, stated by the era itself (both VERIFIED §3):**
- "For I do not seek to understand that I may believe, but I believe in order to understand." — Anselm, *Proslogion* 1, trans. Deane. The starting point is given by faith; the WORK is still argument (and Anselm then argues for God's existence with no premise from scripture — the era's paradox in one chapter).
- "Thou madest us for Thyself, and our heart is restless, until it repose in Thee." — Augustine, *Conf.* I.1: the inquiry's destination is fixed before the inquiry starts.

**The twist (scripture needs philosophy — the fuel half):**
- Averroes: "the Law makes the observation and consideration of creation by reason obligatory" and "truth cannot contradict truth" (VERIFIED §3) — revelation COMMANDS philosophy.
- Maimonides' Guide exists precisely for the believer "lost in perplexity and anxiety" between the Law and philosophy (VERIFIED §3).
- Aquinas: "grace does not destroy nature but perfects it" (VERIFIED §3) — the constraint reframed as architecture.

**The gated "concrete move" sentence material:** the move is not that thinkers got
more pious — it's that the **rules of the game changed and the game got, in some
ways, harder**: you now had to reconcile two authorities (text and reason) instead
of satisfying one, across THREE religions running the same experiment on the same
Greek inheritance — and the era's signature products (Anselm's argument, Avicenna's
Necessary Existent, Maimonides' negative theology, Aquinas' two-roads architecture)
are exactly the kind of thing you only build under that double load. State the
honest cost too: questions the text settled were closed (the era polices its own
borders — Soissons, Sens, 1277, Avignon), and the era ends when Ockham's too-free
God starts prying the two authorities apart again.

## 9. APOCRYPHA BLACKLIST — famous fakes the author must NOT use

(Each widely repeated; none is in the sources. If one is load-bearing as a debunk,
frame explicitly as fake.)

1. **"How many angels can dance on the head of a pin?" as a medieval debate — APOCRYPHAL, THE era-defining fake.** No medieval source poses it. The canard enters with 17th-century Protestant polemic — William Chillingworth (1638) sneering that Catholic schoolmen debated "whether a Million of Angels may not sit upon a needles point," amplified by Cudworth (1678) and later D'Israeli. Aquinas DOES discuss serious angel-questions (e.g., "whether several angels can be at the same time in the same place," ST I q. 52 a. 3) — which is about whether incorporeal beings occupy place at all, i.e., a real metaphysics problem in costume. Tell THAT story: the fake was engineered to make scholasticism look silly, and the real question is better than the parody. ([Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_many_angels_can_dance_on_the_head_of_a_pin%3F))
2. **"Entities must not be multiplied beyond necessity" as Ockham's sentence** — NOT in his works (SEP; Thorburn 1918 VERIFIED §3). First in John Punch, 1639. The real phrasings are in §3 — this is the era's tortoise-class catch; use it exactly as era 1 used Zeno's tortoise.
3. **"The Dark Ages" as a scholarly frame for the whole era** — blacklisted as framing (the term is Petrarch-descended polemic + Enlightenment self-congratulation). The honest version is ch2's thin-centuries block: real thinness, material causes, no sneer.
4. **The medieval flat earth** — educated medievals knew the earth was a sphere (Sacrobosco's *De Sphaera* was a standard university text; Aquinas uses the round earth as a stock example). The Columbus-vs-the-flat-earth-church tale was invented by Washington Irving (1828) and propagated later (Letronne 1834; demolished by Jeffrey Burton Russell, *Inventing the Flat Earth*). Usable only AS the myth-origin story.
5. **"Aquinas proved God in five easy steps"** — the Five Ways are compressed summaries in a beginner's textbook, not free-standing knockdowns; Aquinas himself stocks the same article with the era's two best counter-arguments (VERIFIED §3). Never frame the Ways as checkmate.
6. **"al-Ghazālī killed Islamic philosophy"** — rejected potted summary (SEP: the *Incoherence* was "long... misunderstood... as an anti-philosophical pamphlet"; it attacks "a *particular kind* of philosophy"). Averroes answers him a generation later; philosophy continues East under other names. Frame only as the caricature it is.
7. **Averroes' "double truth"** — Averroes taught ONE truth ("truth cannot contradict truth," VERIFIED §3); the two-truths charge is Tempier's 1277 preamble caricaturing Paris arts masters, and modern scholarship doubts even the Latin Averroists held it (SEP "condemnation" entry). Attribute the caricature to its makers, never to Averroes.
8. **The Hubbard Hypatia quotes** — "Reserve your right to think, for even to think wrongly is better than not to think at all," "All formal dogmatic religions are fallacious...," "To teach superstitions as truth is a most terrible thing." ALL from Elbert Hubbard's 1908 *Little Journeys* essay — invented dialogue (the first is even given to Theon, her father, in Hubbard's own text), with an invented portrait to match (§7). NOTHING quotable from Hypatia survives. Her key-passage block is `absent: true` — the silence is the content, and Hubbard is the cautionary tale inside it.
9. **Fake Augustine, a cluster:** "The truth is like a lion; let it loose and it will defend itself" (no source in Augustine; modern); "He who sings prays twice" (not found in his works in this form); "The Church is a whore, but she is my mother" (no Augustine source; 20th-century provenance). The REAL line these orbit — "Love, and do what you will" — IS verified (§3, Homily 7 on 1 John); use it and say the fakes are fakes if needed.
10. **"Credo quia absurdum" ("I believe because it is absurd") as the medieval motto** — it's (mis)quoted Tertullian (3rd c., and he didn't write quite that either — his phrase plays on *ineptum*/unlikelihood as evidential, not a celebration of absurdity); routinely smuggled into this era to make faith-seeking-understanding look like its opposite. The era's verified mottos are Anselm's credo-ut-intelligam and Aquinas' grace-perfects-nature (§3).
11. **"Ora et labora" as St. Benedict's own motto** — the slogan in this form is much later (the Rule mandates work and prayer; the bumper-sticker isn't in it). If ch2 uses the monastic beat, describe the practice, don't quote the slogan as the Rule's.
12. **Duns Scotus was buried alive** — centuries-later legend; do not use except as a legend exhibit (and at era altitude, better skipped).
13. **The Hughes "Letters of Abelard and Heloise" (1722) quoted as Héloïse** — the Gutenberg *Letters of Abelard and Heloise* (#35977, trans. John Hughes, packaged with Pope's poem) descends from a free French EMBELLISHMENT, not the Latin; its famous purple passages ("...infinitely preferable to me to live with Abelard as his mistress than with any other as empress of the world" — present in the fetched text) are 18th-century romance writing. Héloïse's own-letter quotes ship ONLY from a faithful translation (Scott Moncrieff 1925, PD but not online-verified — §10) or not at all; the PD-verified form of the thought is Abelard's report (§3).
14. **"Hypatia's murder destroyed the Library of Alexandria / ended ancient science"** — the Sagan-popularized fusion; the Serapeum fell in 391, the Library's decline was long and multi-causal, and the Athens schools outlived her by a century (era-1's qualified 529). Keep the murder's documented horror and drop the borrowed apocalypse.
15. **Ockham died of the Black Death (1349)** — outdated; SEP: 9/10 April 1347 (§1, §4).
16. **The Maimonides decision-quote** — "The risk of a wrong decision is preferable to the terror of indecision" — widely attributed, no locus in his works; not in Friedländer. Do not use.
17. **"Hildegard was canonized in the Middle Ages"** — her medieval canonization process stalled repeatedly; the equivalent canonization is 2012 (with Doctor of the Church the same year). Phrase her medieval status as "venerated as a saint for centuries before Rome made it official."

## §11 — r2 additions (verified)

*Added 2026-06-12 by the r2 fact-checker. Every claim below was confirmed
adversarially against web sources (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Wikipedia,
reputable secondary literature) on this date. These supplement §§1–10; no
conflicts found.*

**12th-century Jewish communities in Latin Christendom — legal exclusion and
periodic violence (CONFIRMED).**
Sources: Wikipedia — Rhineland massacres (1096); Third Lateran Council (1179,
residential/occupational restrictions); Norwich blood-libel (1144). Safe to
assert at era altitude as written. Watch-point: compulsory Jewish badge is Fourth
Lateran (1215) — do not attribute to the 12th century.

**The Maimonidean controversy — 13th-century internal Jewish dispute over
Maimonides' rationalism (CONFIRMED).**
Sources: Wikipedia — Maimonidean controversy; SEP — Maimonides. Acute phase:
1230s (Provence), with reciprocal bans and the 1232 book-burning by the Dominican
Inquisition. Full arc: late 12th – early 14th century. The "as sharp as anything
in the Latin schools" comparison is defensible. "A generation after his death"
(Maimonides d. 1204) is loose but acceptable — the crisis peaks c. 30 years on.

**Bernard of Clairvaux — affective mysticism; Trinity charge; Sens 1141
(CONFIRMED in full).**
Sources: SEP — Bernard of Clairvaux; Wikipedia — Council of Sens. (a) Affective
mysticism: standard label; center of *De diligendo Deo* and 86 *Sermons on the
Song of Songs*. (b) Trinity charge against Abelard: his flagship indictment
(Arianism/Sabellianism), with secondary Pelagianism and Nestorianism charges also
leveled. (c) Drove the case at Sens 1141: confirmed; some sources give 1140 due
to calendar reckoning; 1141 is the conventional date and correct. Ship as confirmed.

**Franciscan vs. Dominican intellectual traditions; Scotus and Ockham as
Franciscans (CONFIRMED).**
Sources: SEP — Duns Scotus; SEP — Ockham; Wikipedia — Dominican Order; Wikipedia
— Franciscan Order. Dominicans as Order of Preachers, intellectualist by
foundation: confirmed. Franciscan voluntarist/love-and-will tendency: confirmed
as a philosophical characterization (pre-existing in Bonaventure, sharpened by
Scotus). Scotus as Franciscan Friar Minor: confirmed. Ockham as Franciscan Friar
Minor: confirmed.

**1277 Paris condemnation — eternity of the world (Prop. 87) and celestial
determinism among the 219 propositions (CONFIRMED).**
Sources: Wikipedia — Condemnations of 1277; SEP — Condemnations at Paris. Both
condemned items trace to Aristotle's *Physics* / *De Caelo* and Arabic
commentators.

**1277 document does NOT name individuals — CORRECTION TO PACK §2.**
The condemnation document names no individuals; Tempier's preamble refers only to
"certain scholars at the arts faculty." Siger of Brabant and Boethius of Dacia
appear in marginal manuscript rubrics and are identified by modern historians
(Hissette's research: ~79 of 219 propositions traced to known works), but were
NOT named in the original document. The pack's §2 already flagged "named by
tradition, not in the document." R2 draft dropped that hedge for one clause —
this is the MF-R2-1 must-fix. Write: "identified by later historians though named
in no surviving document."

**1277 condemnations and Scotus's less-necessitarian metaphysics — causal link
(CONFIRMED; "cautious" framing REJECTED).**
Sources: Wikipedia — Condemnations of 1210–1277; SEP — Duns Scotus. The link is
made by historians and is defensible. However, "more cautious" is a misleading
word for Scotus's voluntarism: his emphasis on God's radical freedom was bolder,
not more restrained, than Aquinas's position. What the condemnations endangered
was Aristotelian necessitarianism. Correct framing: "will-centered, less
necessitarian metaphysics." Franciscan voluntarism also predates 1277 (Bonaventure)
— the condemnations accelerated a pre-existing tendency.

**Scotus — univocity of being; Aquinas — analogical predication (CONFIRMED).**
Sources: SEP — Duns Scotus (§3 Univocity); SEP — Analogy in Medieval Philosophy.
Scotus's univocity: "being" has exactly the same sense said of God and creatures —
central metaphysical innovation, confirmed. Aquinas's analogy: terms applied to
God are neither univocal nor equivocal but analogical — confirmed. Scotus→Ockham
link: directional claim confirmed; note that Ockham transformed (not simply adopted)
the doctrine by dissolving Scotus's realism about common natures, but the r2
framing ("lets the next man run the same ruthless logic on God") is directionally
accurate at era altitude.

**Jean Buridan — leading Parisian nominalist; via moderna (CONFIRMED — but
generational framing is WRONG).**
Sources: SEP — Jean Buridan; Wikipedia — Jean Buridan; Wikipedia — Via moderna.
Buridan (c. 1301 – c. 1360) was a leading nominalist and a genuine via moderna
figure. WRONG: he was Ockham's near-exact contemporary (only ~14 years younger,
entirely overlapping in working life), not a transmitter of the next generation.
Do NOT write "carried by logicians like Buridan" as if he came after Ockham. Write
"Ockham's Parisian contemporary Buridan" or omit his name. This is MF-R2-2.

**Luther at Erfurt under nominalist masters (CONFIRMED).**
Sources: SEP — Luther; Wikipedia — Martin Luther (education); Wikipedia — Erfurt
University. Professors Jodocus Trutfetter and Bartholomaeus Arnoldi of Usingen:
confirmed via moderna followers. Erfurt was a key Ockhamist center. The relationship
was formative but ambivalent (Luther later called them "hoggish theologians") — the
r2 text does not overstate the debt.

**Marsilio Ficino — Renaissance Platonic revival (CONFIRMED; "Platonic Academy"
framing NEEDS-REFRAME if used).**
Sources: SEP — Marsilio Ficino; Wikipedia — Marsilio Ficino; Wikipedia — Platonic
Academy (Florence). Dates: 1433–1499 (exact). Translated all 36 Platonic dialogues
(draft completed 1468–69, published 1484), Plotinus's Enneads, Hermetic Corpus.
Medici patronage (Cosimo de' Medici, from 1462–63). "Trying to do for Plato what
Aquinas did for Aristotle" is fair. REFRAME: "founded the Platonic Academy" is
contested — modern scholarship (SEP) says the sources "do not permit us to
understand the Platonic Academy as a formal school"; Wikipedia calls it an "informal
discussion group" with no statutes or membership records; the name is a later
historiographical label. Safe wording: "led an informal circle of Platonic
scholarship" or "what later historians would call the Platonic Academy."

**Augustine's privation-of-evil position (CONFIRMED — no pack entry existed).**
Sources: Augustine *Confessions* Book VII (Platonists → incorporeal truth →
privation of evil); *Enchiridion* chapters 10–14 (sustained privation argument).
R2 account accurate: evil as privation of good, not a substance or rival power;
the Manichaean (evil as dark co-equal substance) correctly named as the rival view
Augustine held for nine years. The "dent, not the car" illustration is the author's
own, not attributed to Augustine.

**Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism undercuts Avicenna's Necessary Existent argument
(CONFIRMED — philosophically).**
Avicenna's contingency argument to the Necessary Existent requires causally
necessary connections; al-Ghazālī's occasionalism (no created causal links are
necessary — only God's reliable habit) removes the necessity the chain-of-causes
argument relies on. This is a recognized point in secondary literature on the
al-Ghazālī/Avicenna relationship. The r2 "loosened the bolts on Avicenna's own
road to God" framing is accurate.

**Hildegard of Bingen — vision-command wording in quotation marks (SHOULD-FIX).**
The r2 text quotes "write down that which you see and hear" in quotation marks.
The pack's §4 entry uses the same wording but did not pin a public-domain
translation source. The Hart/Bishop translation of *Scivias* (Paulist Press, 1990)
is under copyright. If no PD source can be confirmed, convert to paraphrase:
"a voice commanded her to set down what she saw and heard." The pack's standing
paraphrase-only doctrine applies to Hildegard.
