## Must-cover checklist

### 1. The Prologue — full arc

**The descent from the mountain (Zarathustra coming down, the overflowing-cup framing,
"going under" as structural term)**
COVERED — Chapter 1 walks this in full, including the *Untergang* translation note. STRONG.

**The old saint in the forest ("God is dead" as aside, not argument)**
COVERED — Chapter 1 handles this exactly right: the casual deployment vs. Gay Science §125
distinction is explicit, the cross-link to the thinker read is placed correctly. STRONG.

**The Übermensch proclaimed to the market crowd**
COVERED — Chapter 1 quotes Q2 and explains the figure; the Common "Superman" → overman
gloss is present. The "Man is a rope" image (Q3/Q4) is covered in the same chapter.

**The crowd misunderstanding (mistaking Zarathustra for a warm-up act)**
COVERED — Chapter 1's final block makes this the book's "tone-setting failure." STRONG.

**The last man (full portrait, the blink, the crowd wanting him)**
COVERED — Chapter 2 devotes three full blocks to the last man: the anatomy, the anti-ideal
framing, and the crowd's damning reversal. STRONG. This is one of the draft's best sections.

**The tightrope walker (death scene, Zarathustra carrying the corpse)**
COVERED — Chapter 2's final block does this well; the walker as the "first image of the
Übermensch's risk made flesh" is a clean editorial choice. STRONG.

**"Remain true to the earth" (Prologue §3, counter to otherworldliness)**
COVERED — Present in the "break" block (afterLabel and first paragraph) and the Q11 epigraph
anchors Chapter 7. It is not walked as a free-standing set-piece within the chapter blocks,
but it lands across the draft's structure. ADEQUATE.

COVERED — Chapter 3 is the cleanest and most thorough chapter in the draft. Each stage is

### 3. The great speeches

**Will to power / self-surpassing (Part II, "On Self-Surpassing")**
COVERED — Chapter 4 handles this well: the Schopenhauer contrast is present, the distinction
between domination and self-surpassing is drawn clearly, the "Life speaks to Zarathustra"
prophetic register is named and contrasted with the BGE analytic mode, the cross-link is in
place. STRONG.

**Despisers of the body (Part I, "On the Despisers of the Body")**
MISSING. The "remain true to the earth" directive is present in the break block, but
"On the Despisers of the Body" — the speech that directly names the body as the greater
reason, treats the soul as a tool of the body, and is the most explicit counter to Platonic
and Christian body-contempt — is entirely absent from the chapter blocks. This is a
significant gap for a WORK read. The idea is implied in the break block's second paragraph
("the despisers of the body, the people who treat the flesh and the earth as a prison to
escape, are among Zarathustra's named enemies"), but "implied in the break block" is not a
worked set-piece. The speech is Part I's most direct statement of the anti-Platonic body
doctrine and should appear in at least a paragraph in Chapter 3 or as an addition to the
break block's chapter treatment.
**MUST-ADD.**

**Other major Part I speeches (On Reading and Writing, On War and Warriors, On the Friend,
On the Thousand and One Goals, On Love of the Neighbour)**
SHOULD-CONSIDER for one of these selectively — the "thousand and one goals" speech
introduces the cultural-relativism-of-values idea that motivates why the Übermensch must
CREATE values rather than merely choose among inherited ones. Omitting all of the major
Part I speeches beyond the metamorphoses is defensible for a seven-chapter read, but the
values-as-cultural-products move that "Thousand Goals" makes is load-bearing for the rest
of the argument and currently unspoken. If Chapter 3 is where the positive teaching lives,
one block on this would earn its place.
**SHOULD-CONSIDER (add to Chapter 3 or as a beat in Chapter 4).**

### 4. The central ordeals

**"The Vision and the Enigma" — the gateway, the dwarf, the shepherd**
COVERED — Chapter 5 is the strongest long chapter in the draft. The gateway / *Augenblick*
image lands, the dwarf's lazy answer vs. Zarathustra's harder version is drawn, the shepherd
vision is walked clearly, and the deliberate non-resolution ("a riddle Zarathustra poses
without solving") is named. STRONG.

**The Convalescent — the seven-day illness, the animals' hurdy-gurdy song, the first-person
affirmation**
COVERED — Chapter 6 handles all three beats: the collapse, the distinction between reciting
the doctrine and earning it through sickness, and Zarathustra's own affirmation. The
"animals get the words right but miss the weight" insight is well-drawn. STRONG.

**Eternal recurrence as the heaviest weight — the existential test framing, amor fati**
COVERED — The end of Chapter 6 ties eternal recurrence to *amor fati* explicitly and
restates the test in plain terms. STRONG. The cosmology / thought-experiment distinction is
handled correctly in Chapter 5's opening paragraph (matching ledger L4).

**The three-stage staging of recurrence across the book (Vision → Convalescent → Drunken
Song)**
THIN. The ledger identifies this three-stage arc as the unique structural contribution of
the Zarathustra work read over the thinker read. Chapters 5 and 6 cover stages 1 and 2 in
full. Stage 3, "The Drunken Song" (Part IV) and its "All joy wants eternity" lyric
affirmation, appears only as an unverified item in the ledger (P3) and is entirely absent
from the draft. Part IV gets Chapter 7, but Chapter 7 focuses on the higher men and the
closing sign, not on the Drunken Song as the third and culminating moment of the recurrence
arc. The read currently presents eternal recurrence as a two-stage drama (crisis + acceptance
in Convalescent) rather than the three-stage arc the ledger identifies as essential.
**MUST-ADD — at minimum a paragraph in Chapter 7 on "The Drunken Song" as the lyrical
completion of the recurrence arc; the P3 string-match must be resolved before quoting, but
a paraphrase treatment is deliverable now.**

### 5. The closing affirmation / "remain true to the earth"

**The closing "going-down" and the sign**
COVERED — Chapter 7's final block handles the lion sign, the dismissal of the higher men,
and Zarathustra's final descent. The "held breath, work undone" ending is read correctly
as non-triumphant. STRONG.

**The affirmative closing vs. the incomplete gospel**
COVERED — Chapter 7 holds the tension between the Convalescent's great Yes and the Part IV
recognition that the type hasn't arrived. This is the draft's best structural judgment.
STRONG.

**"Remain true to the earth" as the governing directive (not just a Prologue line)**
THIN. The phrase anchors Chapter 7's epigraph and appears in the break block, but the WORK
read never returns to it as a summary of what the whole book has been building. The closing
of Chapter 7 gives the lion sign as the ending image; a sentence or two connecting the
final going-down back to the Prologue's "remain true to the earth" directive would close
the book's structural loop for the reader. Low weight — the break block carries it — but
worth a sentence in Chapter 7's close.
**SHOULD-CONSIDER.**

## Silent gaps (not in the original checklist)

**"On the Gift-Giving Virtue" (Part I conclusion)**
MISSING as a set-piece. This is the chapter that closes Part I with the concept of the
*schenkende Tugend* (gift-giving virtue) — the idea that the higher person gives their
surplus to humanity rather than keeping it. It is structurally important as the reason
Zarathustra keeps going back down despite constant failure. The throughline gestures at it
("like a cup that wants to overflow"), but the speech itself is never named or walked.
SHOULD-CONSIDER for a beat in Chapter 1 or 3; it closes the loop on why the prophet
descends rather than stays on the mountain.

**The shadow (Part IV) and the other Parts II–III speeches**
OMITTED — acceptable. A WORK read of seven chapters cannot be exhaustive across a 240-page
book in four parts. The gap is defensible. No must-add.

**The "despisers of the body" speech — see above — is the one truly essential Part I
omission.**

## Duplication check

No duplication of the thinker read or BGE work read detected. The draft correctly:
- Takes "God is dead" as a given and does not re-walk the Gay Science §125 madman parable.
- Handles will to power in the prophetic register and cross-links for the analytic treatment.
- Keeps the posthumous-misuse block brief and defers the full account to BGE ch6.
- Handles the Übermensch gloss without re-running the thinker read's version.

The one risk of implicit duplication is the *amor fati* concept at the end of Chapter 6.
*Amor fati* is likely covered in the thinker read (era-5). The Zarathustra read's treatment
is earned through the book's dramatization (seven-day sickness, the bite, the affirmation),
so it is NOT duplicative — it arrives through the story, not as a standalone definition.
CLEAN.
