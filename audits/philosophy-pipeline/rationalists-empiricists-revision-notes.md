## Complete [VERIFY] tag list (exact claim text for the r2 fact-checker)

1. **[Hook — scientific revolution]** "Copernicus moved the earth from the center;
   Galileo's telescopic observations and law of falling bodies broke Aristotelian
   physics, replacing it with a mathematical mechanics." (Standard history of science;
   the comprehensiveness gate said no new fact-finding required, but tagged because the
   specifics go beyond the pack.)

2. **[Break block — Ockham→demon]** "Ockham's voluntarism (God can do anything not
   self-contradictory, so no necessary connection in the world is guaranteed) is the
   named medieval ancestor/parallel of Descartes' deceiving-God thought experiment."
   (Pack chain item 1 frames this as ancestry/parallel; confirm wording does not assert
   documented direct influence.)

3. **[Ch3 — Malebranche]** "Nicolas Malebranche's occasionalism holds that God is the
   direct cause of every apparent interaction between mind and body." (Pack chain item 5;
   standard scholarship.)

4. **[Ch4 — Locke slavery]** "Locke was an investor/shareholder in the Royal African
   Company; he helped draft the Fundamental Constitutions of Carolina (1669), which
   entrenched hereditary slavery, including the 'absolute power and authority over his
   negro slaves' clause." (Framing gate MUST-FIX 1; the quoted Carolina clause should be
   confirmed verbatim against the 1669 text before it ships in quotation marks — if it
   cannot be confirmed exactly, drop to paraphrase.)

5. **[Ch5 — Bacon]** "Francis Bacon (1561–1626) is conventionally named as the founder
   of the empiricist inductive method, 'start from observation, not from first
   principles.'" (Comprehensiveness SC-3; standard scholarship.)

6. **[Ch6 — Hume racism]** "Hume's footnote to 'Of National Characters' (1748,
   revised/sharpened 1753–54) asserts that he is 'apt to suspect the negroes, and in
   general all the other species of men... to be naturally inferior to the whites';
   published under his own name." (Framing gate MUST-FIX 2; confirm the quoted wording
   verbatim and the revision-direction claim — that the later edition sharpened rather
   than softened the language.)

7. **[Ch7 — du Châtelet vis viva]** "In the *vis viva* dispute, the Newtonian/Cartesian
   camp held the conserved quantity was mv (mass times velocity) and the Leibnizian camp
   held it was mv² (mass times velocity squared); du Châtelet sided with the Leibnizian
   mv² and was independently correct, the quantity now recognized as kinetic energy."
   (Framing gate MUST-FIX 3 / comprehensiveness SC-1; confirm against the du Châtelet SEP
   entry and that she built on 's Gravesande's experimental work, if that detail is
   later added.)

8. **[Ch4 close — Rousseau, IF the pointer is added]** Not in r2; flagged. If the
   coordinator adds it: "Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1712–1778), *The Social Contract* (1762),
   grounded legitimate authority in the 'general will' and carried the social-contract
   tradition toward the French Revolution; that is the next era's story."
