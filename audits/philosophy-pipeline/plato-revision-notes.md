## [VERIFY] tag list — exact claim text for the r2 fact-checker

Every NEW factual sentence not drawn verbatim from the pack carries an inline [VERIFY]
tag. Five tags, four topics (Berengar, Theaetetus, Antiochus, Phaedrus myth) plus the
Timaeus should-consider:

1. **[VERIFY: Berengar of Poitiers, c. 1150, earliest Latin version of the "amicus"
   formula, using Socrates]** — Ch 7. Claim: the earliest known Latin version of "Amicus
   Plato, sed magis amica veritas," using Socrates rather than Plato, appears in the
   12th-century writer Berengar of Poitiers (~1150), about a century before Roger Bacon's
   c. 1267 Socrates version. (Coordinator's reconciled correction; the fact gate sourced
   this to Wikipedia Berengar of Poitiers / Amicus Plato entries.)

2. **[VERIFY: Theaetetus, three definitions of knowledge each examined and rejected,
   dialogue ends in aporia]** — Ch 7. Claim: in the *Theaetetus* Plato has Socrates ask
   "what is knowledge?" and test three answers — (a) knowledge is perception, (b)
   knowledge is true belief, (c) knowledge is true belief plus an account — each of which
   is found wanting, the dialogue ending in aporia with no definition standing. (MA-1.)

3. **[VERIFY: Antiochus of Ascalon, c. 1st century BC, rejected the Skeptical Academy's
   suspension of judgment and restored positive Platonic doctrine; Cicero studied under
   him]** — Ch 7. Claim: Antiochus of Ascalon (1st c. BC) broke with the Skeptical
   Academy, declared its suspended judgment a wrong turn, reasserted a positive doctrinal
   Plato as the tradition's authentic core, and taught Cicero; it is his dogmatic Plato
   the Roman world inherited. (MA-3.)

4. **[VERIFY: Phaedrus, the Theuth/Thamus myth, 274b-277a, the king Thamus's objection
   that writing produces forgetting and the appearance of wisdom]** — Ch 7. Claim: the
   *Phaedrus* tells of the Egyptian god Theuth presenting writing to king Thamus as a gift
   to improve memory and wisdom; Thamus refuses it, saying writing will instead produce
   forgetfulness (readers stop exercising memory) and the mere appearance of wisdom
   (readers mistake having read for having understood). (MA-2.)

5. **[VERIFY: Timaeus, a divine craftsman shapes the visible world using the Forms as
   blueprints; the dialogue traveled separately into the Latin Middle Ages]** — Ch 7.
   Claim: in the *Timaeus* a divine craftsman shapes the visible cosmos using the Forms as
   blueprints, connecting the Forms to a story of creation; the *Timaeus* traveled
   separately into the Latin Middle Ages and made Platonism congenial to Christian
   cosmology. (Should-consider, taken as one sentence.)

Note for the fact-checker: all five sit in Ch 7. None is in quotation marks (all are
paraphrase). The pack flags the Phaedrus URL (#1636) and Parmenides Wikisource URL as
coordinator load-checks (FACT SF-3/SF-4) — unchanged, those are the coordinator's items,
not mine.
