## 0. The governing fact-discipline note
- Pythagoras left **no securely attributed writing**. Diogenes Laërtius VIII.6 records that
  "some who insist, absurdly enough, that Pythagoras left no writings whatever," and lists
  three treatises (*On Education*, *On Statesmanship*, *On Nature*) — but these are widely
  regarded by scholars as later forgeries / Pythagorean-school pseudepigrapha (the school
  routinely back-attributed its own work to the founder; DL VIII.7 itself notes works "really
  by" Lysis of Tarentum etc.). **Read ships this honestly: he wrote nothing we can trust as
  his.** Basis: DL VIII.6–7 (Hicks, PD); SEP "Pythagoras" (Carl Huffman) on the spurious corpus.
- Our richest narrative sources — **Porphyry**, *Life of Pythagoras*, and **Iamblichus**,
  *On the Pythagorean Way of Life* — are **3rd–4th c. AD** (≈ 700–900 years after Pythagoras)
  and openly hagiographic (golden thigh, bilocation, talking to a river). They are flagged in
  the read as late legend, never asserted flat.
- **Diogenes Laërtius** (3rd c. AD) is the single biggest legend-vector and is always framed
  as late tradition, per the pipeline's standing DL flag.
- The earliest and most reliable witness to the Pythagorean *idea* (number) is **Aristotle**
  (4th c. BC), who reports the Pythagoreans (a school, plural) rather than Pythagoras himself.

## 2. The society / brotherhood at Croton
| Claim | Basis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A communal society/brotherhood, with members pooling property | DL VIII.10 ("his disciples… put all their possessions into one common stock") (Hicks, PD) | ✅ CONFIRMED as tradition; the *idealized* communal portrait is heavily Iamblichus → framed as tradition |
| Five years' silence for novices, listening only | DL VIII.10 ("For five whole years they had to keep silence, merely listening to his discourses without seeing him, until they passed an examination") | ✅ CONFIRMED as the ancient tradition (DL); flagged as tradition |
| Two grades: **akousmatikoi** ("listeners," who took the precepts on authority) vs **mathēmatikoi** ("learners," who got the reasoning/mathematics) | Iamblichus; Porphyry; this is the standard scholarly reconstruction (SEP, Burkert). | The famous **bean prohibition** | DL VIII.19 ("he enjoined abstinence from… beans"); DL VIII.24 gives a rationale ("To abstain from beans because they are flatulent and partake most of the breath of life…"). | Other taboos (don't poke fire with a knife, don't step over a yoke, etc. — the *akousmata*/*symbola*) | DL VIII.17–18 | ✅ CONFIRMED as the attested precept-tradition |

## 3. Music & whole-number ratios → harmony of the spheres
| Claim | Basis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The concordant musical intervals correspond to simple whole-number ratios: **octave 2:1, fifth 3:2, fourth 4:3** | Mathematically true; standard. Burnet, *Early Greek Philosophy* (3rd ed., 1920), p.120: **"12 : 6 is the octave, 12 : 8 the fifth, and 8 : 6 the fourth"** (PD) | ✅ CONFIRMED (the ratios) |
| Pythagoras **personally discovered** the ratios on a string | Burnet p.120: **"it can hardly be doubted that Pythagoras himself discovered these intervals"** — but the famous *discovery stories* (the blacksmith's hammers; weights on strings) are physically false (frequency ∝ √tension, not tension; pitch from hammer weight is wrong) and are late anecdote (Nicomachus, Iamblichus). DL VIII.12: "he also discovered the musical intervals on the monochord." | 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME — read states the ratios as fact, credits the *connection* to the Pythagorean tradition, and flags the discovery anecdotes as physically impossible folklore. Burnet's confidence is noted as one scholar's view, not consensus |
| **Harmony of the spheres** — the heavenly bodies, moving in proportion, produce a cosmic music | Aristotle, *De Caelo* 290b (reports + criticizes the Pythagorean doctrine that the moving heavens make harmonious sound); standard. | ✅ CONFIRMED as a genuine Pythagorean doctrine (Aristotle is the witness; he himself rejects it). Read attributes to "the Pythagoreans" per Aristotle |

## 4. The core idea: reality is number
| Claim | Basis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The Pythagoreans held that **things are numbers / the elements of number are the elements of all things** | Aristotle, *Metaphysics* I.5, 985b–986a (W. D. Ross trans., PD, Internet Classics Archive). VERBATIM: **"since all other things seemed in their whole nature to be modelled on numbers, and numbers seemed to be the first things in the whole of nature, they supposed the elements of numbers to be the elements of all things, and the whole heaven to be a musical scale and a number."** | ✅ CONFIRMED — load-bearing quote; attributed to "the Pythagoreans" (Aristotle's phrasing), NOT put in Pythagoras's own mouth (no text survives from him) |
| The **tetractys** (1+2+3+4 = 10), the triangular figure the school swore by | Burnet p.118 (tetraktys discussion, PD); standard | ✅ CONFIRMED as Pythagorean; read uses it lightly as the emblem of "number as sacred" |
| Pythagoras coined "philosopher" / "cosmos" | DL VIII (Pythagoras "the first to call the heaven *kosmos*"); the "first to call himself a philosopher" story is via Heraclides Ponticus (late, anecdotal) | 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME — read does NOT assert these firsts as fact (the "first to X" trap); mentioned only as tradition, or omitted |

## 5. Transmigration of souls (metempsychosis) + the puppy
| Claim | Basis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Pythagoras taught **metempsychosis** — the soul is reborn across bodies, including animals | Earliest + best witness is **Xenophanes** (a contemporary/near-contemporary, and a critic), fragment preserved in DL VIII.36. Also DL VIII.4–5 (the Aethalides→Euphorbus→…→Pythagoras chain) | ✅ CONFIRMED that he taught soul-rebirth (Xenophanes is near-contemporary and hostile, the gold standard for "this was really his doctrine") |
| The **beaten-puppy** story: Pythagoras tells a man to stop beating a puppy because he recognized a dead friend's soul in its yelp | **Xenophanes fragment**, in DL VIII.36 (Hicks, PD). VERBATIM: **"They say that, passing a belaboured whelp, / He, full of pity, spake these words of dole: / 'Stay, smite not! 'Tis a friend, a human soul; / I knew him straight whenas I heard him yelp!'"** | ✅ CONFIRMED — and notably Xenophanes tells it to **mock** him, which the read flags (the source is satire, but that's exactly why it's good evidence the doctrine was real) |
| Pythagoras claimed to remember his own past lives (he had been the Trojan warrior **Euphorbus**) | DL VIII.4–5 (Hicks, PD): the soul "entered into Euphorbus… and he was wounded by Menelaus… But when Pyrrhus died, he became Pythagoras, and still remembered all the facts mentioned." | 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME — read presents the Euphorbus past-life claim as the tradition (DL), tied to the genuine doctrine of metempsychosis |

## 7. The theorem
| Claim | Basis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The "Pythagorean theorem" (a² + b² = c² for right triangles) | The relation was **known to Babylonian mathematicians (Plimpton 322, c.1800 BC) and used in Egypt/India long before Pythagoras**; there is **no contemporary evidence Pythagoras personally proved it**, and the attribution rests on much-later sources (Proclus, 5th c. AD, citing the theorem to "Pythagoras"). | 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME / ⚠️ ATTRIBUTION CONTESTED — read states plainly: the math predates him by a millennium, and we cannot be sure he or his school proved it; the name is a tradition, not a record |
| Pythagoras sacrificed an ox (a hecatomb) on discovering the theorem | Late anecdote (Proclus / Plutarch citing Apollodorus), and *contradicts* his own school's reverence for life / aversion to animal sacrifice — internally implausible | 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME — flagged as a charming but self-contradicting legend |

## 13. Contested / paraphrased material summary (for the framing gate)
- **Theorem attribution** — explicitly hedged: Babylonians (Plimpton 322) knew it ~1,000 yrs
  earlier; no contemporary evidence Pythagoras proved it; the *name* is later tradition.
- **Music-ratio discovery anecdotes** (hammers/weights) — flagged physically false; the ratios
  themselves are real and the conceptual link is genuinely Pythagorean.
- **Porphyry / Iamblichus / Diogenes Laërtius** — all flagged as late (3rd–4th c. AD) and
  legend-bearing; the miracle-worker material (golden thigh, etc.) is named AS legend.
- **The society's structure** (akousmatikoi/mathēmatikoi, five-year silence, communal property)
  — presented as the ancient/late tradition, not as documented Pythagoras-era fact.
- **Metempsychosis** — the one doctrine on firm ground (Xenophanes, near-contemporary + hostile);
  the Euphorbus past-life detail is DL tradition.
- **"All things are number"** — given via Aristotle's report of *the Pythagoreans*, never as a
  Pythagoras quotation (he left no text).
