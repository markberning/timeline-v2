## 0. The PRIMARY SOURCE and its PUBLIC-DOMAIN basis (load-bearing)

The *Guide for the Perplexed* was written by Maimonides in **Judeo-Arabic** (Arabic
language in Hebrew script), titled *Dalālat al-Ḥā'irīn* (Arabic) / *Moreh Nevukim*
(Hebrew). All quotations in this read are taken from the **M. Friedländer English
translation**, first published 1881 (3 vols.) and reissued in the one-volume **second
edition, 1904**.

**PD basis:** Michael Friedländer died in 1910; his translation was published 1881 and
1904. Both are well over 95 years old and in the public domain worldwide; the
one-volume 1904 edition is hosted as a clean public-domain transcription. The text is
ALSO mirrored at sacred-texts.com (currently Cloudflare-gated to scripted access, so
NOT used for verification here) and on archive.org.

## 1. QUOTATIONS (verbatim, verified against Gutenberg #73584 / Friedländer)

All wording below was grep-confirmed in `pg73584.txt`. Friedländer uses ẓ/ḥ diacritics
and British spelling; preserved verbatim. Em-dashes inside quotes are left as-is per the
voice contract (quotes are exempt).

### Q1 — Negative theology, the core claim (Guide I.58)
> "the negative attributes of God are the true attributes: they do not include any
> incorrect notions or any deficiency whatever in reference to God, while positive
> attributes imply polytheism, and are inadequate"
- Source: Guide **I.58**, Friedländer. Gutenberg #73584 (string-matched, ~line 7857).
- Used in: Ch 3 (negative theology). VERIFIED ✅.

### Q2 — We cannot describe God except by negation (Guide I.58)
> "we cannot describe the Creator by any means except by negative attributes"
- Source: Guide **I.58**, Friedländer. #73584 (~line 7862). VERIFIED ✅. Ch 3.

### Q3 — Each negation brings you nearer (Guide I.58/59)
> "every time you establish by proof the negation of a thing in reference to God, you
> become more perfect, while with every additional positive assertion you follow your
> imagination and recede from the true knowledge of God"
- Source: Guide **I.58**, Friedländer. #73584 (~line 8021). VERIFIED ✅. Ch 3.

### Q4 — Knowledge as knowing we cannot comprehend (Guide I.59)
> "our knowledge consists in knowing that we are unable truly to comprehend Him"
- Source: Guide **I.59**, Friedländer. #73584 (~line 8043). VERIFIED ✅. Ch 3.

### Q5 — Anthropomorphism: ẓelem is form, not shape (Guide I.1)
> "The term ẓelem … signifies the specific form, viz., that which constitutes the
> essence of a thing, whereby the thing is what it is … In man the 'form' is that
> constituent which gives him human perception: and on account of this intellectual
> perception the term ẓelem is employed in the sentences 'In the ẓelem of God he
> created him' (Gen. i. 27)."
- Source: Guide **I.1**, Friedländer. #73584 (~lines 3787–3792). VERIFIED ✅.
- (Ellipses are mine to bridge; each clause is verbatim and contiguous in the source.)
- Used in: Ch 4 (war on anthropomorphism). The point: "image of God" = the intellect,
  NOT a divine face/body.

### Q6 — The audience: the perplexed believer (Guide, Introduction)
> "The object of this treatise is to enlighten a religious man who has been trained to
> believe in the truth of our holy Law, who conscientiously fulfils his moral and
> religious duties, and at the same time has been successful in his philosophical
> studies."
- Source: Guide **Introduction (Part I)**, Friedländer. #73584 (~line 3195).
  VERIFIED ✅. Used in: Ch 2 (who the book is for).

### Q7 — Apples of gold in silver filigree (Guide, Introduction)
> "Just as apples of gold in silver filigree with small apertures, so is a word fitly
> spoken."
- Source: Guide **Introduction (Part I)**, Friedländer (Maimonides' rendering of Prov.
  xxv. 11). #73584 (~line 3436). VERIFIED ✅. Used in: Ch 2 (esoteric writing —
  literal sense = silver, hidden sense = gold).

### Q8 — Creation: I do not follow the philosophers (Guide II.16)
> "I do not propose this method as though I believed in the eternity of the Universe,
> for I do not follow the philosophers on this point"
- Source: Guide **II.16** (Friedländer's Part II), where he sets up proving God on
  Aristotelian premises *without* conceding eternity. #73584 (~line 9530).
  VERIFIED ✅. Used in: Ch 5 (creation vs eternity).

### Q9 — Aristotle himself did not claim a proof (Guide II.15)
> "Aristotle himself did not claim to have proved the Eternity of the Universe."
- Source: Guide **II.15**, Friedländer. #73584 (~line 13339). VERIFIED ✅.
- Maimonides then quotes Aristotle's *Physics* viii.1 to show even Aristotle argued
  from the consent of earlier physicists, which a true proof would not need.
- Used in: Ch 5 (the key move — eternity is not demonstrated, so reason cannot settle
  the question, and revelation's creation account is not contradicted by proof).

NOTE on chapter numbers: Friedländer's Part II runs the eternity discussion across
II.13–II.29. The "I do not follow the philosophers" line sits in his II.16 setup; the
"Aristotle did not claim to have proved" line sits in II.15. Both cited at their
Friedländer location. The era-2 read already cites the Guide at this altitude;
consistent.

## 2. PARAPHRASE-ONLY POSITIONS (no quotation marks in the read)

These are accurately summarized in house voice, NOT quoted, because either the exact
wording is spread across many sentences or the source is a different work:

- **Thirteen Principles of Faith** (the *Shloshah Asar Ikkarim*). SOURCE IS NOT THE
  GUIDE: they come from Maimonides' **Commentary on the Mishnah**, introduction to
  *Sanhedrin* ch. 10 (*Pereq Ḥeleq*), c. 1168, written in Judeo-Arabic. The read
  presents them as a numbered creed Maimonides drew up (existence, unity,
  incorporeality, eternity of God, that God alone is to be worshipped, prophecy,
  Moses' supremacy, the divine origin of the Torah, its immutability, God's
  omniscience, reward and punishment, the Messiah, resurrection of the dead).
  PARAPHRASE ONLY — no Friedländer text covers these; no verbatim quote shipped.
  The famous liturgical "Ani Ma'amin" / *Yigdal* are LATER versifications, NOT
  Maimonides' own words; the read does not attribute those forms to him.
- **God's incorporeality as the second of the Principles** and as the spine of Guide
  I.1–I.49 (the lexicographic chapters explaining away every bodily term applied to
  God). Summarized, not quoted, except via Q5.
- **Why anthropomorphic scripture exists** ("the Torah speaks in the language of man,"
  a rabbinic maxim Maimonides leans on, Guide I.26, I.33, I.46): the read PARAPHRASES
  the maxim's force (scripture accommodates ordinary minds) rather than hanging a
  verbatim quote on it, because Friedländer renders the maxim several slightly
  different ways across the book (#73584 lines 5102, 5363, 6682, 7366 confirm the
  doctrine exists and is central). The maxim itself is named in italics as the
  rabbinic tag it is.
- **The Five-Ways-style influence on Aquinas:** the read states that Aquinas cites
  Maimonides as "Rabbi Moses" / "Rabbi Moses the Egyptian" and adopts the
  reason-cannot-settle-creation position. This is standard, well-documented Aquinas
  scholarship (Summa Theologiae I, q.46 on the eternity of the world explicitly
  engages "Rabbi Moses"). Stated as influence, paraphrased, with the [Aquinas] hub
  link. No fabricated quote.

## 3. DATES & BIOGRAPHY (with basis)

- **Born:** 1138, Córdoba, Almoravid/Almohad al-Andalus (some older sources say 1135;
  modern scholarship and the standard reference works settle on **1138**). The read
  says "1138." Consistent with philosophy-data.ts (`maimonides … dates:'1138 – 1204'`).
- **Died:** **1204** (20 Tevet 4965), in Fustat (Old Cairo), Egypt; buried, by
  tradition, in **Tiberias**. The read says 1204 and names Tiberias as the traditional
  burial place (flagged as tradition).
- **Almohad persecution / flight:** the **Almohads** (a strict Berber Muslim dynasty)
  took Córdoba in 1148 and ended the relative tolerance of al-Andalus, pressuring Jews
  and Christians to convert, emigrate, or die. The family left Córdoba and wandered
  (Spain, then Fez in Morocco, briefly the Holy Land), settling finally in **Fustat,
  Egypt**, c. 1166–1168. Standard biography; stated plainly.
- **Court physician:** Maimonides became a practicing and renowned physician, and
  served at the **Ayyubid court** in Egypt — tradition (and his own letters) place him
  as physician to **al-Fāḍil**, the vizier/administrator under Saladin, and to members
  of Saladin's circle. The popular "personal physician to Saladin" line is
  OVER-tight; the read says he became a court physician in Saladin's Egypt and served
  the vizier al-Fāḍil — the documented version. FLAGGED so the caricature ("Saladin's
  doctor") is not asserted flat.
- **Leader of Egyptian Jewry:** he became the recognized head (the office later called
  *Nagid*, "prince/leader") of the Egyptian Jewish community. Stated.
- **The death of his brother David:** David ben Maimon, a gem trader who supported the
  family, drowned in the Indian Ocean (c. 1169–1177) with the family's capital, which
  pushed Maimonides into medicine for a living. Well documented (Maimonides' own
  surviving letter mourns it). Stated as the documented cause of his turn to medicine.

### Major works + dates (all in the read's stats register)
- **Commentary on the Mishnah** (*Kitāb al-Sirāj*), Judeo-Arabic, completed **1168**
  (contains the Thirteen Principles, in the *Pereq Ḥeleq* introduction).
- **Mishneh Torah** (*Yad ha-Ḥazaqah*), a 14-book code of all Jewish law, written in
  **Hebrew**, completed **c. 1178** (his one major work NOT in Judeo-Arabic).
- **Guide for the Perplexed** (*Dalālat al-Ḥā'irīn* / *Moreh Nevukim*), Judeo-Arabic,
  c. **1190** (commonly 1185–1190; the read says "around 1190").
- Numerous **medical treatises** (Judeo-Arabic), incl. aphorisms drawn from Galen.

All dates above are the standard reference-work consensus and are presented with the
appropriate hedge where a range exists.

## 4. POTTED-SUMMARY / CARICATURE GUARDS (the framing-gate axes)

- **"Saladin's personal doctor."** Over-tight; corrected to court physician serving the
  vizier al-Fāḍil in Ayyubid Egypt (see §3). Not asserted as Saladin's personal doctor.
- **"Maimonides was a rationalist who explained away the whole Bible."** Caricature.
  He held God's incorporeality and unity as *foundations of faith*, not concessions to
  philosophy; the figurative reading of bodily language is in service of a purer faith,
  not against it. The read frames it as a religious purpose.
- **"He proved Aristotle right / he was just an Aristotelian."** No — the creation
  break (Ch 5) is precisely where he BREAKS with Aristotle. The eyebrow "Aristotelians"
  is a school tag, not a claim that he followed Aristotle off the cliff of eternity.
- **"Negative theology means we can know nothing about God."** Subtle miss; for
  Maimonides each *negation* is real knowledge (it rules something out), and the
  via negativa is a positive method of approach, not agnosticism. Q3/Q4 carry this.
- **The Guide as "a book of answers."** It is deliberately *esoteric* — written in
  scattered "chapter headings," contradictions planted on purpose, the truth set like
  gold apples behind silver filigree (Q7). Framed as such, not as a tidy summa.
- **Anachronism guard:** Aquinas's debt to "Rabbi Moses" is stated as documented
  influence (Aquinas cites him by name), not read backward as Maimonides "anticipating
  Thomism."
