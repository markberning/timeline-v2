## Verified quotations (Meiklejohn, string-matched in pg4280.txt; line numbers in the file)
1. ✅ **"the queen of all the sciences"** — "Time was, when she was the _queen_ of all the
   sciences… Now, it is the fashion of the time to heap contempt and scorn upon her."
   (Preface to the **First** Edition; line ~363. Used as Ch1 epigraph, attributed to the
   First Edition Preface.)
2. ✅ **"arena of these endless contests" = Metaphysic** — "The arena of these endless
   contests is called _Metaphysic_." (Preface to First Ed., line ~360. Paraphrased in prose,
   wording quoted-equivalent; used as "the arena of endless contests".)
3. ✅ **Analytic vs synthetic + "All bodies are extended"** — "when I say, 'All bodies are
   extended,' this is an analytical judgement." vs "'All bodies are heavy,' the predicate is
   something totally different… it becomes a synthetical judgement." (Introduction, lines
   ~1632–1642.) Definition: analytic = predicate "contained (though covertly) in the
   conception" / synthetic = predicate "lies completely out of the conception." (line ~1618.)
4. ✅ **7 + 5 = 12 is synthetic** — "the conception of twelve is by no means obtained by
   merely cogitating the union of seven and five" + the five-fingers passage. (Introduction,
   lines ~1731–1740.) Also "'A straight line between two points is the shortest,' is a
   synthetical proposition." (line ~1755.)
5. ✅ **The grand problem** — "How are synthetical propositions à priori possible?"
   (Transcendental Aesthetic § 10, line ~2951. Used as Ch2 epigraph.)
6. ✅ **The Copernican reversal** — "It has hitherto been assumed that our cognition must
   conform to the objects… Let us then make the experiment whether we may not be more
   successful in metaphysics, if we assume that the objects must conform to our cognition."
   (Preface to the **Second** Edition, lines ~808–814.) The Copernicus analogy (spectator
   revolves, stars at rest) follows immediately, lines ~818–825. Ch3 epigraph.
7. ✅ **Thoughts without content** — "Thoughts without content are void; intuitions without
   conceptions, blind." (Transcendental Logic, Introduction, line ~2996. Ch4 epigraph.)
8. ✅ **Being is not a real predicate** (ontological-argument refutation) — "Being is
   evidently not a real predicate, that is, a conception of something which is added to the
   conception of some other thing." (Transcendental Dialectic, line ~15137. Ch5 epigraph.)
   The "hundred dollars" illustration: "A hundred real dollars contain no more than a hundred
   possible dollars." (line ~15151.)
9. ✅ **Abolish knowledge to make room for belief** — "I must, therefore, abolish
   _knowledge_, to make room for _belief_." (Preface to the **Second** Edition, line ~1094.
   VERIFIED to sit between the 2nd-ed preface start at line 631 and its 1787 sign-off at line
   1393. Ch6 epigraph.) Meiklejohn translates *Glaube* as "belief"; the read uses "belief,"
   and once "moral faith" as a gloss-paraphrase (not quoted).
10. ✅ **First Antinomy, Thesis** — "The world has a beginning in time, and is also limited
    in regard to space." with the completed-infinite-series proof. (Transcendental Dialectic,
    Antinomy of Pure Reason, lines ~11393+.) **Antithesis** — the world is, as to time and
    space, infinite; proof from the impossibility of an empty time before the world giving any
    reason to begin at one moment. (lines ~11430+.) Both paraphrased accurately in prose.

## Position summaries (paraphrase, tied to the text — no quote marks in prose)
- ✅ **The a priori / a posteriori distinction.** A priori = known independent of experience,
  carrying necessity + universality; a posteriori = derived from experience. The brief's
  "all bachelors are unmarried" (analytic a priori) and "7+5=12" (synthetic a priori) worked
  examples are used. [Standard Kant; the necessity-as-mark-of-the-a-priori point is from the
  Introduction, "carry along with them the conception of necessity, which cannot be given by
  experience," line ~1717.]
- ✅ **The categories / pure concepts of the understanding**, esp. **causality** and
  **substance**, as a priori concepts the understanding imposes for there to be objects of
  experience. [Headings "Of the Pure Conceptions of the Understanding, or Categories" present
  in #4280 contents.] This is Kant's answer to Hume on causation: necessity is contributed by
  the knower as a condition of experience, not read off objects and not a mere habit.
- ✅ **Transcendental Deduction** = the argument that the categories legitimately apply to all
  objects of possible experience, grounded in the unity of self-consciousness (one continuous
  "I" requires its intuitions to be bound by rules = the categories). [Heading present;
  argument paraphrased at a fair, standard level. The Deduction was the most heavily REWRITTEN
  section between the 1781 A-edition and 1787 B-edition — stated in the read as reception fact;
  standard scholarship, also Wikipedia.]
- ✅ **Phenomena vs noumenon / thing-in-itself.** We know only appearances (phenomena),
  structured by space, time, and the categories; the thing-in-itself (noumenon) is unknowable
  because to know it we would have to apply forms that only ever yield appearances. The real
  in-itself exists but is sealed off. ["Phenomena and Noumena" is a chapter heading in #4280;
  "thing in itself" / "things in themselves" recur throughout, e.g. lines 884, 1007, 1026.]
  This = *transcendental idealism*; "idealism" glossed (NOT "having ideals").
- ✅ **Transcendental Dialectic** = diagnosis of reason's unavoidable illusions when it
  oversteps experience chasing the unconditioned. Three classes:
  - **Paralogisms** (the soul): from the formal "I think" the old metaphysics wrongly infers a
    simple, immortal soul-substance; the "I think" is only the formal unity of experience, not
    an observed object. [Heading "Of the Paralogisms of Pure Reason," #4280 contents.]
  - **Antinomies** (the cosmos): pairs of equally valid contradictory proofs (first antinomy
    quoted/paraphrased above); the contradiction shows the world-as-completed-whole is not a
    possible object of experience. [Heading "The Antinomy of Pure Reason."]
  - **The three proofs of God** demolished: **ontological** (existence is not a predicate —
    quote 8), **cosmological**, **physico-theological**; Kant argues the latter two ultimately
    fall back on the ontological proof and so inherit its flaw. [Standard; the ontological
    refutation is string-verified, the reduction of the other two is Kant's stated structure.]
- ✅ **Net verdict.** Old metaphysics (proving God/soul/cosmos) is impossible; what survives
  is metaphysics as *critique* — a chart of reason's limits. Science is secured (its laws are
  the mind's own a priori contribution); the beyond-experience prizes are walled off.

## Reception & afterlife (Ch6)
- ✅ **1781 first edition baffled readers; 1783 Prolegomena; 1787 rewrite.** The
  *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics* (1783) was written to explain the *Critique* more
  accessibly; the 1787 second edition substantially revised the book (esp. the Deduction) and
  added a Refutation of Idealism. [Standard scholarship; edition dates Gutenberg-confirmed;
  Refutation-of-Idealism + Deduction-rewrite standard.]
- 🟡 **Garve-Feder review (1782).** The hostile/garbled first major review: Christian Garve's
  review was cut down and altered by J.G.H. Feder, and read the book as Berkeleyan idealism;
  it stung Kant into the Prolegomena. [Well-established in Kant scholarship; WebFetch of the
  CPR Wikipedia page did NOT confirm this specific item from its truncated text, so marked 🟡
  rather than ✅. The read states it at a level standard scholarship supports; if the fact
  critic wants tighter sourcing, see the SEP entry on the Prolegomena / Kant's reception.]
- ✅ **"Dogmatic slumber" is from the Prolegomena, NOT the CPR.** The famous line that
  recalling Hume "interrupted my dogmatic slumber" appears in the *Prolegomena* (1783). The
  read PARAPHRASES it (no quote marks) and explicitly tells the reader the phrase is from the
  1783 book, not the Critique. NOTE: Wikipedia's CPR article quotes the line and (mistakenly)
  cites it to the Critique; this ledger and the read do NOT repeat that error. [Per the shared
  author brief, which also locates it as the standard story.]
- ✅ **Afterlife = the agenda for German Idealism.** Kant became the unavoidable starting
  point; [Fichte](/philosophy/thinker/fichte) cut the thing-in-itself away, Schelling reworked
  mind/nature, [Hegel](/philosophy/thinker/hegel) denied any fixed limit to knowledge. The
  thing-in-itself was the "loose end" they pulled. [Standard; consistent with the German-
  Idealism school read and the shared brief's framing.] Also raised: the internal tension that
  if the thing-in-itself *causes* sensations, Kant applies the category of cause beyond
  experience — a standard post-Kantian (Jacobi) objection; stated as the successors' worry.

## Apocrypha / caricature guards
- **"Dogmatic slumber" misattribution** — handled (see above): paraphrased, located in the
  Prolegomena, never quoted as Meiklejohn CPR.
- **"Idealism" = having ideals** — the corpus's single most misleading term; glossed on use as
  the view that the form of the known world is the mind's contribution (transcendental
  idealism), explicitly NOT "nothing is real but ideas" (which is Berkeley, the very charge
  Kant rebutted) and NOT "having ideals."
- **"Kant proved God doesn't exist"** — caricature. He demolished the theoretical *proofs* of
  God (and of the soul/immortality) but framed it as clearing room for moral *belief* (quote
  9), not as atheism. The read states both halves.
- **"The clock-by-his-walk" anecdote** — flagged as the tradition it is ("said to"), not
  asserted as documented.
- **Kant's race-hierarchy writings (Kleingeld)** — per the shared brief, a single WORK read on
  the CPR need not carry this; it belongs to the thinker/era altitude. NOT included here. [No
  claim made either way in the read.]
