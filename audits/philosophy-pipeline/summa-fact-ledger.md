### The "straw" remark — LEGEND-FRAMED (biographical tradition, NOT a Summa quote)
- After a mystical experience while saying Mass on **6 December 1273**, Aquinas stopped
  writing/dictating. When his companion **Reginald of Piperno** urged him back to work,
  he is reported to have said: *"Reginald, I cannot, because all that I have written
  seems like straw to me."*
- Status: 🟡 LEGEND-FRAME-IT. This is reported biographical tradition (via Reginald and
  the early biographers / the canonization testimony), NOT a line from the Summa. The
  draft frames it as reported ("as the story is told," "his companion reported"), never
  asserted flat and never inside a Summa citation.
- Basis: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas (the 6 Dec 1273 ecstasy; the reported
  reply to Reginald, wording "all that I have written seems like straw to me").

## B. Verified quotations (Dominican PD translation — newadvent.org/summa)

All quoted VERBATIM in the draft. Each carries its ST citation in-text.

**Q1. The Five Ways — opening line.** ST I, Q2, A3 (respondeo).
> "The existence of God can be proved in five ways. The first and more manifest way is the argument from motion."
- Source: https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1002.htm — ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.

**Q2. The Five Ways — the five closing clauses.** ST I, Q2, A3.
1. First Way (motion): *"and this everyone understands to be God."*
2. Second Way (efficient cause): *"to which everyone gives the name of God."*
3. Third Way (possibility & necessity): *"This all men speak of as God."*
4. Fourth Way (gradation): *"and this we call God."*
5. Fifth Way (governance/design): *"and this being we call God."*
- Source: https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1002.htm — ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim, all five.

**Q3. Grace perfects nature.** ST I, Q1, A8, Reply to Objection 2.
> "Since therefore grace does not destroy nature but perfects it, natural reason should minister to faith as the natural bent of the will ministers to charity."
- Source: https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1001.htm — ✅ CONFIRMED verbatim.
- Note: the popular short form "grace does not destroy nature but perfects it" is a true
  CLAUSE of this Dominican sentence (not a paraphrase), so it ships in quotation marks
  ONLY as the exact clause above; the draft quotes the full Dominican sentence.

## C. Position summaries (each tied to the text, framed honestly)

- **What the Five Ways do and don't prove.** Each Way ends only by *naming* its
  conclusion "God"; the Summa's later questions (Q3 onward) argue what that being is
  like (simple, eternal, etc.). The draft says plainly that the Five Ways argue *that*
  a first mover/cause/necessary being/standard/orderer exists and is what people mean by
  "God" — not that they deliver the full Christian God in one step. This is Aquinas's own
  procedure (Q2 establishes existence; Q3+ unfold the divine nature). Framed as the
  documented structure, not the caricature "Aquinas proved God in five knockdown proofs."
- **Faith and reason cannot contradict** because both come from God (the one author of
  truth); reason can demonstrate some truths (a creator exists), while others (the
  Trinity, the Incarnation) are known only by revelation. This is the Q1, A8 ad 2 frame
  ("grace perfects nature") generalized; ✅ consistent with the verified quote.
- **Natural law** (ST I-II, QQ 90–97, the "Treatise on Law"): law is "an ordinance of
  reason for the common good, made by him who has care of the community, and
  promulgated" (Aquinas's definition, I-II Q90 A4); natural law = the rational
  creature's participation in the eternal law. ⚠️ The Q90 A4 definition is paraphrased
  in the draft (NOT quoted) unless re-verified verbatim at the gate; flagged for the
  text/quotes gate. The first precept ("good is to be done and pursued, and evil
  avoided," I-II Q94 A2) is likewise paraphrased, not quoted, pending gate verification.
- **Happiness / the good** (ST I-II, QQ 1–5): the final end of human life is *beatitudo*
  (beatitude/happiness), and perfect happiness consists in the vision of God, not in
  wealth, honor, power, or bodily pleasure. Framed as Aquinas's Aristotelian inheritance
  (the *Nicomachean Ethics* function-and-end structure) baptized: the final end is God.
  Cross-consistent with the shipped Nicomachean read. Paraphrased, not quoted.

### Potted-summary traps refused (per the pipeline's two hallucination classes)
- NOT "medieval philosophy was sterile angels-on-a-pinhead logic-chopping" — the
  pinhead debate is itself apocryphal (no scholastic text asks it); the draft says so.
- NOT "Aquinas proved God exists beyond doubt" — the draft frames the Five Ways as
  arguments, notes they conclude only by *naming* God, and flags the honest limits.
- NOT "Aquinas invented the synthesis of faith and reason from nothing" — the Arabic
  transmission (Avicenna, Averroes = "the Commentator") and his teacher Albert the Great
  are named; this is era-2's framing duty, carried into the work read.
- The "straw" remark is legend-framed (section A), never asserted as fact or as a quote.
