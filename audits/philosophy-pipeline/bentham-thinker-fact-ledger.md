## A. Biography (CONFIRMED — Wikipedia, Britannica, UCL Bentham Project, IEP)
- Born **15 February 1748** (4 February 1747/8 Old Style), **Houndsditch /
  Spitalfields, London**. Died **6 June 1832, London**. — CONFIRMED (Wikipedia,
  UCL).
- A child prodigy; his father sent him to **The Queen's College, Oxford, at age 12**.
  Trained as a lawyer, **called to the bar in 1769**, but **never practised**; he was
  disgusted by the chaos of English law and decided to reform it rather than work it.
  — CONFIRMED (Wikipedia).
- Lived on inherited means; a reclusive, prolific writer who left tens of thousands of
  manuscript pages, much unpublished in his lifetime. — CONFIRMED (UCL Bentham
  Project; standard).
- **Panopticon**: prison design (the "inspection-house"), published **1791** as
  *Panopticon; or, the Inspection-House*. Circular building, cells around the
  rim, a central inspection tower from which a watcher can see into any cell while
  prisoners can never tell whether they are being watched. Bentham spent roughly
  **sixteen years** and his own money trying to get it built; **Parliament ultimately
  rejected the scheme** and he was eventually **compensated about £23,000**. —
  CONFIRMED (Wikipedia).
- **Michel Foucault** later analyzed the Panopticon in *Discipline and Punish* (1975)
  as the model of modern disciplinary surveillance power. — CONFIRMED (standard;
  Foucault attribution given as a later reading, his interpretation not Bentham's
  intent).
- **University College London**: Bentham is called the **"spiritual father" /
  "spiritual founder"** of UCL because his radical, secular, utilitarian circle
  inspired and founded it (1826), but **his direct involvement in the founding was
  limited** — he did NOT found it himself. — CONFIRMED (Wikipedia explicitly:
  "spiritual founder ... his direct involvement in the university's founding was
  limited").
- **Auto-Icon**: in his **will**, Bentham asked that his body be dissected (a public
  good, anatomy teaching) and then preserved as an **"auto-icon"** — his skeleton,
  dressed in his own clothes and padded with hay/straw, seated, surmounted by a
  preserved head. His friend, surgeon **Thomas Southwood Smith**, carried it out. The
  mummification of the **real head** went badly (disfigured), so a **wax head** was
  made for display; the real head was for years kept with the figure, later stored
  separately by UCL. The Auto-Icon has been at **UCL** since 1850 and is **on public
  display** (relocated 2020 to UCL's Student Centre). — CONFIRMED (UCL Museums &
  Collections; Wikipedia). The 1975 student "kidnapping" of the head for a charity
  ransom is given as the well-known anecdote (UCL/Wikipedia), flagged as anecdote.

## B. The works (CONFIRMED titles + dates)
- ***An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation*** — printed 1780,
  **published 1789** (2nd ed. 1823). The central work. Abbreviated IPML below. —
  CONFIRMED.
- ***Anarchical Fallacies*** — written **c. 1795** (against the French
  *Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen*, 1789/1791); first published
  in the Bowring *Works* (1843). The "nonsense upon stilts" text. — CONFIRMED.
- ***The Rationale of Reward*** — **1825** (English; based on Dumont's 1811 French
  redaction of Bentham MSS of the 1770s). Source of the push-pin/poetry line. —
  CONFIRMED.
- ***Panopticon; or, the Inspection-House*** — **1791**. — CONFIRMED.

## C. QUOTES — all verified PD English, located by work

1. **"Nature has placed mankind under the governance of two sovereign masters, pain
   and pleasure. It is for them alone to point out what we ought to do, as well as to
   determine what we shall do."**
   — IPML, **Chapter I, §1** (opening). VERIFIED:
   https://www.laits.utexas.edu/poltheory/bentham/ipml/ipml.c01.html — exact.

2. **"By the principle of utility is meant that principle which approves or
   disapproves of every action whatsoever, according to the tendency it appears to
   have to augment or diminish the happiness of the party whose interest is in
   question."**
   — IPML, **Chapter I, §2**. VERIFIED same URL — exact.

3. **"By utility is meant that property in any object, whereby it tends to produce
   benefit, advantage, pleasure, good, or happiness ... or ... to prevent the
   happening of mischief, pain, evil, or unhappiness to the party whose interest is
   considered."**
   — IPML, **Chapter I, §3**. VERIFIED same URL — exact (ellipsis marks an
   intentionally compressed clause; no words altered).

4. The **felicific/hedonic calculus** circumstances — IPML **Chapter IV** ("Value of
   a Lot of Pleasure or Pain, How to be Measured"). VERIFIED:
   https://www.laits.utexas.edu/poltheory/bentham/ipml/ipml.c04.html
   - Of a pleasure/pain by itself: **(1) its intensity; (2) its duration; (3) its
     certainty or uncertainty; (4) its propinquity or remoteness.** — exact wording.
   - For an act tending to produce it, add **(5) its fecundity** ("the chance it has
     of being followed by sensations of the *same* kind") and **(6) its purity** ("the
     chance it has of *not* being followed by sensations of the *opposite* kind"). —
     exact paraphrase of Bentham's definitions, quoted phrases verified.
   - For a number of persons: **(7) its extent** ("the number of persons to whom it
     extends"). — exact.

5. The **mnemonic verse** Bentham appended to the calculus:
   **"Intense, long, certain, speedy, fruitful, pure— / Such marks in pleasures and
   in pains endure. / Such pleasures seek if private be thy end: / If it be public,
   wide let them extend. / Such pains avoid, whichever be thy view: / If pains must
   come, let them extend to few."**
   — IPML, Chapter IV note ("mnemonic doggerel" / "memoriter verses"). VERIFIED:
   https://www.utilitarianism.com/bentham-poetry.html and
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felicific_calculus — exact wording (the em-dash
   inside the verse is original printed punctuation, preserved as a quoted line).

6. **"Prejudice apart, the game of push-pin is of equal value with the arts and
   sciences of music and poetry."** Surrounding: **"Everybody can play at push-pin:
   poetry and music are relished only by a few. The game of push-pin is always
   innocent..."**
   — ***The Rationale of Reward***, **Book III, ch. 1**. VERIFIED:
   https://www.laits.utexas.edu/poltheory/bentham/rr/rr.b03.c01.html — exact.
   LANDMINE: the popular **"pushpin is as good as poetry"** is **J.S. Mill's
   paraphrase**, not Bentham's wording — the read says so and never attributes the
   paraphrase to Bentham as a quote.

7. **"Natural rights is simple nonsense: natural and imprescriptible rights,
   rhetorical nonsense,—nonsense upon stilts."**
   — ***Anarchical Fallacies***, Art. II commentary. VERIFIED:
   https://www.ditext.com/bentham/bentham.html (reproducing Bowring *Works* 1843) —
   exact (source page has an OCR typo "nonscnse" in one spot; corrected to the
   standard printed reading "nonsense", confirmed against Oxford Reference and the
   Collected Works title *Nonsense upon Stilts*).

8. **"...want is not supply—hunger is not bread."**
   — ***Anarchical Fallacies***. VERIFIED same ditext URL — exact. (Used to gloss the
   "nonsense upon stilts" argument: wishing a right does not make one.)

9. The **animal-suffering footnote** — IPML, **Chapter XVII, §1**, footnote.
   VERIFIED wording (Lapham's Quarterly reproduction; CMU course text; Wikipedia
   "Sentientism" / IPML):
   - **"The day may come when the rest of the animal creation may acquire those
     rights which never could have been withholden from them but by the hand of
     tyranny."**
   - **"...a full-grown horse or dog is beyond comparison a more rational, as well as
     a more conversable animal, than an infant of a day, or a week, or even a month,
     old."**
   - **"The question is not, Can they *reason*? nor, Can they *talk*? but, Can they
     *suffer*?"**
   — exact wording; the italics on *reason/talk/suffer* are Bentham's emphasis.

## D. Interpretive framing (CONFIRMED as standard, not asserted as Bentham's words)
- "Greatest happiness of the greatest number" — Bentham's slogan for the principle of
  utility (he attributed the germ of it to **Joseph Priestley / Cesare Beccaria /
  Helvétius** reading; standard). Framed as his rallying phrase, not pinned to a
  single verified sentence of that exact form in IPML. — CONFIRMED as standard
  attribution.
- **Hedonism / psychological + ethical**: Bentham is a quantitative hedonist — all
  value reduces to pleasure/pain, and pleasures differ only in QUANTITY, not in
  kind/quality. This is exactly the point **Mill** later broke from by adding
  "higher" and "lower" pleasures (landmine handled: utilitarianism is NOT the
  "ends justify any means" cartoon; Mill built in higher pleasures and liberty). —
  CONFIRMED (standard; IEP, SEP).
- **Legal/penal reform**: Bentham's whole project was codification and humane,
  proportioned punishment (punishment as itself an evil, justified only to prevent
  greater evil); he coined/spread terms like *codification*, *international*,
  *maximize/minimize*. — CONFIRMED (standard).
- **Foucault** reading of the Panopticon = a LATER interpretation (*Discipline and
  Punish*, 1975), given as Foucault's, not Bentham's intent. — CONFIRMED.

## E. Break block
- FROM: law and rights grounded in **natural law / natural rights / tradition** (the
  rights "imprescriptible," antecedent to any government — the doctrine of the French
  *Declaration* and of Blackstone's common-law reverence).
- TO: a **single measurable standard, utility** (the greatest happiness), applied
  directly to legislation, with rights re-grounded as the *creatures of actual law*
  serving utility, and "natural rights" dismissed as "nonsense upon stilts." —
  CONFIRMED framing.
