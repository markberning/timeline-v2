## What works (keep)

- **The through-line is stated, tracked, and landed.** "The heart is restless until it
  rests in God" is planted in the throughline, the hook, Ch 1's first block, and the
  final block of Ch 8 ("the book curved back to its own first line"). The restlessness
  is genuinely *worked* — pears, theater tears, the nine Manichee years, the divided
  will — not asserted. The hinge structure (nine narrative books, then the inward turn)
  is set up in the hook and paid off. This is the spine the brief asked for and it holds.
- **The set-pieces all land.** Pears (Ch 2, the "lab isolates a single variable" framing
  is excellent and pre-empts the "great sinner" misread), "only not yet" (Ch 6, correctly
  played as self-laceration not a wink), *tolle lege* (Ch 6), Ostia (Ch 7, the *shared*
  vision is the right emphasis), "what is time?" (Ch 8). The conversion reads as *felt*,
  not stipulated.
- **The unnamed concubine block (Ch 7, block 4)** is handled with restraint and force —
  "no one should fill it in. He does not." Right call, right weight.
- **Term-definition discipline is strong.** rhetoric, dualist, incorporeal, privation,
  allegorically, "the divided will," and the problem of evil are all inline-defined on
  first use in plain language. A zero-knowledge reader is carried.
- **The two-clocks frame** (events 370s–380s / written ~397) and "as Augustine tells it"
  are kept visible without over-laboring (mostly — see B3).

## BLOCKERS

### 1. Missing pronunciation glosses on hard names (BLOCKER)
Sibling gates have repeatedly flagged this against the bar, and the draft is inconsistent
about it — it glosses Monica, Faustus, Ambrose, Ostia, Adeodatus, Alypius, but leaves
these un-glossed on first use:

- **Patricius** (Ch 1, block 4) — first use `**Patricius**`, no gloss. Add `(puh-TRISH-us)`.
- **Manichees / Manichee** (Ch 3, block 3, first body use `**Manichees**`; also the
  Ch 3 title "nine years a Manichee") — the single hardest name in the read for a
  newcomer, and it is never glossed anywhere. Add `(MAN-ih-keez)` at the first bolded
  body use, and consider it for the chapter title too.
- **Mani** (Ch 3, block 3, "a third-century Persian prophet named Mani") — add `(MAH-nee)`.
- **Plotinus** (Ch 4, block 4, "the writings of Plotinus") — add `(plo-TYE-nus)`.

The bar's own Augustine thinker read does NOT gloss Mani/Plotinus either, so there is some
latitude — but the brief names these explicitly and the WORK read's whole reason to exist
is the closer, zero-knowledge walk. Glosses are cheap insurance and the read already commits
to the pattern for six other names. Inconsistency is the defect: either gloss the hard ones
or don't, and for a zero-prior-knowledge work read the answer is gloss them.
**Rewrite:** `the **Manichees** (MAN-ih-keez) — and he stayed...`;
`a third-century Persian prophet named Mani (MAH-nee)`;
`almost certainly the writings of Plotinus (plo-TYE-nus), the great third-century Platonist`;
`His father, **Patricius** (puh-TRISH-us), was a pagan small landowner`.

### 2. *distentio* is never named, though it is the labeled idea of the time chapter (BLOCKER, clarity)
The brief lists `distentio` among the hard names to gloss, and the fact-pack header (line 18)
calls the answer "the distentio-animi answer." But Ch 8 block 4 describes the idea — "as a
stretching of the soul," "a mind stretched out across what it remembers and what it awaits" —
without ever giving it its name. The bar's Augustine thinker read DOES name it:
"a soul that is stretched out — his word is *distentio*, a distension, a being-pulled-taut."
A reader who finishes this WORK read and then reads the thinker read meets a technical term
the deeper read assumed it had introduced. Name it here, inline-glossed, exactly as the bar does.
**Rewrite (Ch 8, block 4):** after "as a stretching of the soul" add
"— Augustine's word is *distentio* (dis-TEN-tee-oh), a distension, the soul pulled taut —".

### 3. Voice: meta-framing / self-labeled "honesty" asides brush the plain-voice rule (BLOCKER, voice)
`feedback_philosophy_voice_plain` bars meta-narration, self-labeled asides ("an honesty
note"), and telling the reader how to read. Several spots cross or graze that line. The
worst offenders:

- **Ch 6, block 5:** "The scene **wants care**, because Augustine handles it with care."
  This is the narrator instructing the reader on posture — the exact move the rule kills
  ("treat the wording as...", "don't wave it away"). The *content* that follows (writing
  11 years later, the Antony model, "how he chose to tell it") is great and should stay.
  Cut the framing sentence; open straight on the substance.
  **Rewrite:** Delete "The scene wants care, because Augustine handles it with care."
  and begin: "Augustine is writing about eleven years after the fact, a trained
  rhetorician with a literary model in mind..."
- **Ch 7, block 2:** "the point that **must not be lost** is that it is *shared*."
  Self-flagged emphasis = throat-clearing. State it plainly.
  **Rewrite:** "It is the only sustained mystical experience in the entire book, and it
  is *shared*: 'she and I,' together, for one instant."
- **Ch 7, block 4:** "One thing **should be named and not smoothed over**." Self-labeled
  honesty-note opener.
  **Rewrite:** Open on the fact: "The book that makes Monica a heroine... gives Augustine's
  partner of some fourteen years no name at all."
- **Hook, paragraph 2:** "And here is **the formal fact** that shapes every sentence after"
  — the "here is the X that..." announcement the rule names. Trim to: "The *Confessions*
  is not addressed to readers at all." (The hook is load-bearing and mostly earns its
  meta, so this is the lightest-touch fix — drop the announcement clause only.)

These are localized and do not touch any quote, fact, or structure — pure voice trims.
Grouped as one BLOCKER because the pattern (narrator coaching the reader) recurs and the
rule is explicit that it is "the #1 offense."

## POLISH

### 4. "Neoplatonism / the books of the Platonists" — the label slips by undefined (POLISH, clarity)
The brief asks that Neoplatonism be inline-defined on first use. The draft introduces
"certain books of the Platonists" and "Neoplatonist texts" (Ch 4, block 4) and leans on
them heavily in Ch 5, but never tells a zero-knowledge reader what *Neoplatonism* is —
that it is the late-antique revival/development of Plato's philosophy (Plotinus its great
figure), distinct from Plato himself. A newcomer reads "Platonists" and "Neoplatonist"
as interchangeable noise. One clause fixes it.
**Rewrite (Ch 4, block 4):** "...'certain books of the Platonists, translated from Greek
into Latin' — Neoplatonist texts (the late-antique school that carried Plato's thought
forward three centuries on), almost certainly the writings of Plotinus..."

### 5. Ch 3 epigraph repeats Ch 1's epigraph verbatim (POLISH, storytelling)
Ch 1 and Ch 3 both epigraph "Thou madest us for Thyself, and our heart is restless..."
(I.1). Re-using the book's thesis line as the Carthage/Manichee chapter's epigraph is
defensible (it frames the restlessness driving him to Carthage) but it costs a fresh
quote and reads as a placeholder. Consider a Carthage/Manichee-specific line ("a cauldron
of unholy loves," already quoted in the block, or a problem-of-evil line) so each chapter's
epigraph earns its place. Not blocking — the repetition is thematically coherent.

### 6. Em-dash density (POLISH, voice)
83 em-dashes across 8 chapters. The house rule is parentheses-for-definitions, em-dashes
sparingly. The draft already uses parens for the actual term-definitions (good), so most
em-dashes are rhetorical rather than definitional and are within tolerance — but a few
blocks stack three em-dashes in one sentence (Ch 5 block 3, Ch 8 block 1). Light pass to
break the longest em-dash chains into periods would tighten the prose. Low priority.

### 7. "Antony" vs "Anthony" cross-read spelling (POLISH — flag to fact gate)
This read spells the desert monk "Antony" (Ch 6); the bar Augustine thinker read spells
him "Anthony." Not my lane to adjudicate the correct form, but flagging the cross-read
inconsistency so the fact/coherence gate harmonizes it.

## Verdict

The read is genuinely good — the drama is *felt*, the inward turn lands, the set-pieces
all hit, and the term-definition discipline is strong. The blockers are narrow and
mechanical: a handful of missing pronunciation glosses (the bar-consistency issue sibling
gates keep catching), the unnamed *distentio*, and a small cluster of narrator-coaching
phrases the plain-voice rule explicitly bans. All are surgical fixes that touch no quote,
fact, or structure.
