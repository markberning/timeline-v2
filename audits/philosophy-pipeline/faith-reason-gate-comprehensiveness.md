## MUST-ADD LIST (in priority order)

**MA-1 — Augustine's privation theodicy and the problem of evil AS A WORKED POSITION (not just as Aquinas's self-objection)**
Pack has it: YES — §2 explicitly flags evil-as-privation (darkness = absence of light) and the Aquinas article carries the full "if God existed there would be no evil" objection text. Draft coverage: the privation doctrine is introduced in passing in Ch 1 ("evil as not a thing in itself but a lack, a privation, the way darkness is just the absence of light"), but it is never worked as a position in its own right. The problem of evil at era altitude is a NAMED gate checklist item per the spec; Augustine's is the era's founding answer and it must be *run*, not just mentioned. The Summa article Obj-1 text (VERIFIED in pack) handles the problem from Aquinas's side in Ch 6 and does get a sentence, but that is sixty years after the foundational move. **New verification needed: none — pack carries the privation material; the fix is prose depth, not new fact-finding.**

**MA-2 — The 1277 condemnations need a sentence naming what Boethius of Dacia actually held and why it mattered philosophically (not just as institution)**
Pack has it: YES — §2 gives Boethius of Dacia as named co-target alongside Siger, and the Duhem-thesis caveat. Draft coverage: Ch 6 names "Siger of Brabant" and "arts-faculty masters" but drops Boethius of Dacia entirely. More importantly, the philosophical *point* — that some of the 219 propositions condemned the necessary eternity of the world and the determinism of celestial influence, ideas that came from Aristotle straight — is absent. This is what made 1277 a live debate: it was Aristotle on trial, not just Averroes. The draft frames it purely as institutional friction. The gate spec requires the era's era-altitude *institutional* story to be told honestly, and 1277 is the era's most important institutional event. **New verification needed: none — pack §2 (the 1277 block) carries the content; one or two sentences needed in Ch 6.**

**MA-3 — The gap ~1347–1600 is not acknowledged; the close actively misleads**
Pack has it: YES — §2 Ch-7 close notes "Add the 14th century's material catastrophes (Avignon schism, the Black Death striking the very next year, 1348)." Draft coverage: Ch 7 close mentions "the century's catastrophes, a papacy split and discredited, and the Black Death arriving the year after Ockham died" — then cuts straight to "In 1619 a young Frenchman named Descartes, shut in a warm room against the winter, will pick it up." That jump silently skips ~250 years. The via moderna (Buridan, Peter of Ailly, Gabriel Biel) is not named; the nominalist thread into Luther is not gestured at; the Renaissance Platonism revival (Ficino) is not acknowledged. The spec requires the Ockham→Renaissance/Reformation gap to be *acknowledged honestly rather than silently skipped*. A single short paragraph (3–4 sentences) flagging what happens in those 250 years — the via moderna spreads, Luther trains under nominalists, Renaissance Platonism revives — would satisfy the gate without becoming era-3. The current close gives the false impression Descartes picked the question up directly from Ockham. **New verification needed: none for a gap-acknowledgment; the facts (Buridan, via moderna, Luther's Erfurt training) are standard and the pack's §2/Ch-7 close prepares them.**

**MA-4 — Duns Scotus is present but his two core ideas are underexplained; the Aquinas→Ockham link is thin**
Pack has it: YES — §2 devotes a dedicated beat to Scotus (will over intellect; parsimony formulas before Ockham). Draft coverage: Ch 7 gives Scotus a paragraph and names both contributions ("will," "razor"). That is structurally correct. What is missing is the one Scotus doctrine that makes the Aquinas→Ockham split intelligible to a reader: his **univocity of being** — the claim that "being" is said in the same sense of God and creatures, which directly contradicts Aquinas's analogical predication. Scotus's univocity is what allows Ockham to apply the same logical tools to God and creatures without special pleading; without it, the nominalism chapter is an unmotivated leap. At era altitude, "univocity" need not be a term-of-art — the point is one sentence: Scotus argued we can reason about God and creatures using exactly the same logical concepts, which Aquinas had denied. **New verification needed: pack does not carry this; the fact-checker will need to confirm the univocity-of-being framing before the sentence ships.**

## SHOULD-CONSIDER LIST

**SC-1 — Hildegard of Bingen: currently absent from the draft (fact pack has her)**
The pack has dates, works, four preaching tours, Doctor of the Church 2012, and two image candidates. Hildegard is the era's most prominent woman philosopher/mystic, and her absence in a draft that foregrounds Héloïse as a woman thinker creates a structural asymmetry: Héloïse the philosopher is recovered from the romance tradition, but the era's foremost independent female intellectual voice is dropped. At era altitude one paragraph would suffice — her visions as a deliberate philosophy of divine creation (viriditas, the whole-cosmos framework), her preaching tours as an institutional defiance, and the fact that she got Bernard of Clairvaux's endorsement at the same Synod of Trier he was condemning Abelard. The framing gate will flag this under "women the canon dropped"; proactively addressing it here removes a framing-gate finding. **No new verification needed; pack has her.**

**SC-2 — The Franciscan / Dominican intellectual divide as context for the Scotus–Ockham skepticism of Thomism**
A single sentence in Ch 6 or the opening of Ch 7 would clarify why both Scotus and Ockham were Franciscans suspicious of Aquinas's Dominican synthesis. The Franciscan intellectual tradition (rooted in Bonaventure, favoring illuminationist epistemology and will-primacy over Thomistic intellect-primacy) is the institutional soil of the "unraveling." Without it, the skeptics look like random contrarians rather than representatives of a rival tradition. This is not an essential gap at era altitude, but it would sharpen the architecture.

**SC-3 — Bonaventure: worth a sentence in Ch 6 as the road not taken**
Bonaventure (1217–1274) was Aquinas's rough contemporary, a Franciscan, who built a competing synthesis: instead of Aristotle baptized, he kept Augustine and Neoplatonism primary, seeing the natural world as a ladder of symbolic ascent toward God. The via moderna searched for Aristotle's limits; Bonaventure had argued you don't need Aristotle's limits, you need to look through him. One sentence in Ch 6's setup would give "the Thomistic synthesis" a named alternative, making Aquinas's choices visible as choices rather than as the only game in town. The fact pack does not currently carry Bonaventure; a fact-checker pin would be needed before the sentence ships.

## ERA BOOKEND VERDICTS

**Why start at Augustine:**
SATISFACTORY. The draft earns the start via the Hypatia handoff (continuity from era 1) and Augustine's Platonist reading (the moment philosophy enters the new container). The "why Augustine and not Clement or Origen or Tertullian" question is answered implicitly by the Plotinus connection — Augustine is the hinge precisely because he is the point where Greek metaphysics becomes the Church's load-bearing frame. A reader is not stranded.

**Why end at Ockham (the 1347–1619 gap):**
UNSATISFACTORY — MA-3. The close is beautiful prose but it silently teleports. The via moderna (Buridan, Peter of Ailly, Biel) is not named; the Reformation connection (Luther trains under nominalists) is absent; the conciliar movement is absent; Renaissance Platonism revival (Ficino, Bessarion) is absent. A reader emerges thinking Descartes in 1619 is the next sentence after Ockham in 1347. Three sentences of honest signposting — "In the 250 years between Ockham and Descartes, his nominalism spread through the universities as the via moderna, trained the generation that launched the Reformation, and ran alongside a Renaissance revival of Platonism that tried to do what Aquinas had done but with Plato instead of Aristotle. None of it settled the question." — would close this gate without becoming era 3.
