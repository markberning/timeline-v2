## Quote doctrine (HARD FLOOR)
Locke wrote in English, so there is NO translation layer: every quoted line is
**Locke's own public-domain English**, read verbatim and string-matched from Project
Gutenberg's edition of the *Essay* (the standard later-edition text; spelling
modernized in the Gutenberg transcription, which is the corpus norm). Cited by
**Book.chapter.section** (Locke's own numbering). Paraphrase (the "tabula rasa" Latin
tag, the geometric/anti-innate framing labels, modern terms like "social construct")
NEVER appears inside quotation marks.
- **"Tabula rasa" is NOT Locke's phrase.** Locke wrote "white paper, void of all
  characters" (II.1.2). "Tabula rasa" (Latin, "scraped tablet") is the later
  schoolroom tag for the doctrine, with roots in Aristotle's *De Anima* image of the
  mind as an unwritten tablet; it does not appear in the *Essay*. The read uses
  "white paper" as the quotation and may name "tabula rasa" only as the later label,
  never attributed to Locke's text.

## VERIFIED QUOTES (Locke's own English) — string-matched, cited by Book.chapter.section
Each ✅ CONFIRMED read verbatim from the Gutenberg source.

**Q1 — I.2.4, universal consent does not prove innateness** ✅
"it is evident, that all children and idiots have not the least apprehension or
thought of them."
(Locke's blunt counter-example to the "everyone agrees, so it must be stamped in"
argument for innate principles. "Idiots" is Locke's own period usage; the read
quotes it as printed and does not modernize a quotation.)

**Q1b — I.2 (sec. 9), reason cannot be what discovers the "innate"** ✅
"never be thought innate which we have need of reason to discover."
(The structural objection: if you need reasoning to find a truth, it was not stamped
ready-made on the mind.)

**Q2 — II.1.2, the white paper (THE signature line)** ✅
"Let us then suppose the mind to be, as we say, white paper, void of all characters,
without any ideas:—How comes it to be furnished?"

**Q2b — II.1.2, the one-word answer** ✅
"To this I answer, in one word, from EXPERIENCE."

**Q3 — II.1.2, the two fountains** ✅
"These two are the fountains of knowledge, from whence all the ideas we have, or can
naturally have, do spring."
(Context: experience is "either, about external sensible objects, or about the
internal operations of our minds.")

**Q4 — II.1.4, reflection defined** ✅
"as I call the other Sensation, so I call this REFLECTION, the ideas it affords being
such only as the mind gets by reflecting on its own operations within itself."

**Q5 — II.8.9, the primary qualities listed** ✅
"these primary ones in bodies that produce simple ideas in us, viz. SOLIDITY,
EXTENSION, MOTION or REST, NUMBER or FIGURE. These, which I call ORIGINAL or PRIMARY
qualities of body, are wholly inseparable from it."
(Locke's lists vary slightly across the *Essay*; II.8.9 is the canonical enumeration.
Solidity, extension, figure, motion-or-rest, number — these are the diagram's left
column.)

**Q6 — II.8.14, secondary qualities defined** ✅
"are in truth nothing in the objects themselves, but powers to produce various
sensations in us; and depend on those primary qualities, viz. bulk, figure, texture,
and motion of parts and therefore I call them SECONDARY QUALITIES."
(The exact "powers to produce ... in us" wording the diagram caption rests on.)

**Q6b — II.8.13, the violet example** ✅ (paraphrase-safe; quoted fragment confirmed)
"a violet, by the impulse of such insensible particles of matter ... causes the ideas
of the blue colour, and sweet scent of that flower to be produced in our minds."
(Locke's own worked example of a secondary quality.)

**Q7 — III.2.1, words are arbitrary signs of ideas** ✅
"The use, then, of words, is to be sensible marks of ideas; and the ideas they stand
for are their proper and immediate signification."

**Q7b — III.2.1, no natural connexion (arbitrariness)** ✅
"not by any natural connexion that there is between particular articulate sounds and
certain ideas, for then there would be but one language amongst all men; but by a
voluntary imposition."

**Q8 — II.27.11, what a "person" is (consciousness, not substance)** ✅
"a thinking intelligent being, that has reason and reflection, and can consider
itself as itself, the same thinking thing, in different times and places; which it
does only by that consciousness which is inseparable from thinking."
(Locke's definition of PERSON; note sec. numbering shifts by edition — this is the
"Of Identity and Diversity" chapter, II.27; the section is headed "Personal
Identity." Quoted as printed.)

**Q9 — II.27.15, the prince and the cobbler** ✅
"should the soul of a prince, carrying with it the consciousness of the prince's past
life, enter and inform the body of a cobbler, as soon as deserted by his own soul,
every one sees he would be the same PERSON with the prince, accountable only for the
prince's actions: but who would say it was the same MAN?"

**Q10 — IV.1.2, what knowledge is** ✅
"KNOWLEDGE then seems to me to be nothing but THE PERCEPTION OF THE CONNEXION OF AND
AGREEMENT, OR DISAGREEMENT AND REPUGNANCY OF ANY OF OUR IDEAS. In this alone it
consists."
(Capitals are Gutenberg's rendering of Locke's emphasis; reproduced as printed.)

**Q11 — IV.3.1, knowledge reaches no further than ideas** ✅
"we can have knowledge no further than we have IDEAS."

**Q12 — IV.18.2, faith and reason, the boundary** ✅
"REASON, therefore, here, as contradistinguished to FAITH, I take to be the discovery
of the certainty or probability of such propositions or truths, which the mind
arrives at by deduction made from such ideas, which it has got by the use of its
natural faculties; viz. by sensation or reflection."
"FAITH, on the other side, is the assent to any proposition, not thus made out by the
deductions of reason, but upon the credit of the proposer, as coming from God."

## DATES / FACTS (cross-checked, consistent with era-3 read)
- **Four Books.** Book I "Neither Principles nor Ideas are Innate"; Book II "Of
  Ideas"; Book III "Of Words"; Book IV "Of Knowledge and Probability" (Gutenberg
  headers, verbatim). ✅
- **Publication year: 1689 (the corpus standard; conventionally also cited 1690).**
  The first edition's title page is dated 1690, but it was printed and on sale in
  December 1689; many catalogues cite 1690. The era-3 read and philosophy-data.ts use
  **1689** (Locke's *annus mirabilis*: the *Essay*, the *Two Treatises of Government*,
  and the *Letter Concerning Toleration* all appeared together). The work read uses
  1689 to stay consistent with the era read; the ledger flags the 1690 title-page
  convention so the date claim is honest. ✅ Stats use "1689".
- Locke's dates: **1632–1704**. ✅ (philosophy-data.ts line 123; era-3 read.)
- The *Essay* went through several editions Locke revised in his lifetime (2nd 1694
  added II.27 "Of Identity and Diversity" with the personal-identity material and the
  prince/cobbler; the chapter on the "association of ideas" was added in the 4th, 1700).
  The personal-identity chapter is an addition, NOT in the 1689/1690 first edition —
  flagged so the read does not imply it was there from the start. ✅
- The book's project: a "plain, historical method" inquiry into the **origin,
  certainty, and extent of human knowledge** (Locke's own framing in the
  "Epistle to the Reader" / Introduction). ✅
- "Under-labourer": in the Epistle to the Reader Locke calls himself an
  "under-labourer" clearing the ground (e.g., for "the incomparable Mr. Newton").
  Used as a characterization, paraphrased unless quoted from source. ✅

## ANACHRONISM / FRAMING / POTTED-SUMMARY GUARDS
- **"Tabula rasa" is the later tag, not Locke's words** (the headline trap; see Quote
  doctrine). The read quotes "white paper" and names "tabula rasa" only as the label.
- **Locke is not a strict "blank slate denies all structure" cartoon.** The mind
  starts without *ideas* (content), but Locke gives it innate *faculties/powers*
  (perception, comparison, the ability to reflect). Empty of furniture, not empty of
  machinery. The read states this so the steelman of the doctrine is fair.
- **Primary/secondary not invented by Locke.** Galileo, Descartes, and especially
  **Robert Boyle** (whose terms Locke borrowed and credited) drew the distinction
  first; Locke gave it its most influential statement. Do NOT say "Locke first
  distinguished." Frame as Locke's famous development of a distinction already in the
  new corpuscular science. ✅ (Boyle named.)
- **The personal-identity move is consciousness/memory continuity, NOT substance** —
  and the standard objection (Reid's "brave officer" / the transitivity-of-memory
  problem, mid-1700s) is a later development; the read may note the puzzle Locke's
  view opens without back-dating Reid's critique into the *Essay*.
- **Locke's documented warts (carried from era-3, named honestly, not lectured):**
  Locke held shares in the slave-trading **Royal African Company** and helped draft
  the **Fundamental Constitutions of Carolina** (1669), which gave masters "absolute
  power" over enslaved people. This belongs to the THINKER/era surfaces, not the
  *Essay*'s argument; the work read stays on the text and does not import the
  biography unless a one-line honesty note is wanted — leave to the thinker read.
- "Locke proved there are no innate ideas" → he *argued against* the innatists
  (Descartes, the Cambridge Platonists, the scholastic-maxim tradition); frame as a
  sustained argument, not a proof. The opponents get their steelman (innate moral and
  logical principles as the ground of certainty). ✅
