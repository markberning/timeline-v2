## B. Verified quotations (Deane 1903) — VERBATIM, string-matched

> **Q1 — the formula (Proslogium II).** "a being than which nothing greater can
> be conceived."
- Matched substring in source: `a being than which … nothing greater can be conceived` (Proslogium II body, the Fool passage). ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **Deane reads "conceived," not "thought."** The popular modern formula "that
  than which nothing greater can be *thought*" (Malcolm/Charlesworth wording) is
  NOT Deane. Deane's own *Introduction* paraphrases it once as "beyond which
  nothing greater can be thought," but the **argument body** consistently reads
  **"conceived."** The read quotes the body wording. Do not "correct" to "thought."
- Location: *Proslogium*, Chapter II ("Truly there is a God, although the fool
  hath said in his heart, there is no God").

> **Q2 — the in-the-mind-alone step (Proslogium II).** "if that, than which
> nothing greater can be conceived, exists in the understanding alone, the very
> being, than which nothing greater can be conceived, is one, than which a greater
> can be conceived. But obviously this is impossible."
- Matched substring: `exists in the understanding alone, the very being, than which nothing greater can be con- ceived, is one, than which a greater can be conceived. But obviously this is impossible.` ✅ CONFIRMED.

> **Q3 — it must exist in reality too (Proslogium II).** "And it assuredly exists
> so truly, that it cannot be conceived not to exist." / the conclusion: "there
> is no doubt that there exists a being, than which nothing greater can be
> conceived, and it exists both in the understanding and in reality."
- Matched substrings: `Hence, there is no doubt that there exists a being, than which nothing greater can be conceived, and it exists both in th[e understanding and in reality]` ✅ CONFIRMED; the "exists in reality; which is greater" clause `then it can be conceived to exist in reality ; which is greater` ✅ CONFIRMED.

> **Q4 — the painter analogy (Proslogium II).** "When a painter first conceives
> of what he will afterwards perform, he has it in his understanding, but he does
> not yet understand it to be, because he has not yet performed it. But after he
> has made the painting, he both has it in his understanding, and he understands
> that it exists, because he has made it."
- Matched substring: `When a painter first conceives of what … he will afterwards perform, he has it in his under- standing, but he does not yet understand it to be, be- cause he has not yet performed it. But after he has made the painting, he both has it in his understand- ing, and he understands that it exists` ✅ CONFIRMED.

> **Q5 — the Fool / the Psalm tag (Proslogium II, quoting Ps. 14:1).** "the fool
> hath said in his heart, there is no God."
- Matched substring (in Anselm's text): `the fool hath said in his heart, there is no God? (Psalms xiv. i)` ✅ CONFIRMED. Deane cites Psalms xiv.1.

> **Q6 — God cannot be conceived not to exist (Proslogium III, chapter title +
> argument).** "God cannot be conceived not to exist."
- Matched substring (chapter heading): `III. God cannot be conceived not to exist` ✅ CONFIRMED. (This is the second, modal step beyond II — existence so necessary it cannot be thought away.)

> **Q7 — Gaunilo's lost island (Appendix, In Behalf of the Fool, §6).** "it is
> said that somewhere in the ocean is an island, which, because of the
> difficulty, or rather the impossibility, of discovering what does not exist, is
> called the lost island. And they say that this island has an inestimable wealth
> of all manner of riches … and that having no owner or inhabitant, it is more
> excellent than all other countries, which are inhabited by mankind, in the
> abundance with which it is stored."
- Matched substring: `is called the lost island. And they say that this island has an inestimable wealth of all manner of riches and delicacies in greater abundance than is told of the Islands of the Blest ; and that having no owner or inhabitant, it is more excellent than all other coun[tries], which are inhabited by mankind, in the abun- dance with which it is stored.` ✅ CONFIRMED.
- **NOTE (prior-era catch carried forward):** Deane reads **"more excellent than
  all other countries,"** NOT "richer." The faith-reason ERA read once misquoted
  this as "richer" (fact-checker MF, faith-reason proof run 2). The thinker read
  uses the verified Deane wording. Do not paraphrase the quote as "richer."

> **Q8 — Anselm's reply to Gaunilo (Appendix, Anselm's Apologetic).** "I promise
> confidently that if any man shall devise anything existing either in reality or
> in concept alone (except that than which a greater cannot be conceived) to which
> he can adapt the sequence of my reasoning, I will discover that thing, and will
> give him his lost island, not to be lost again."
- Matched substring: `I promise confidently that if any man shall devise anything existing either in reality or in concept alone (except that than which a greater cannot be conceived) to which he can adapt the sequence of my reasoning, I will discover that thing, and will give him his lost island, not to be lost again.` ✅ CONFIRMED.
- The core of Anselm's reply, paraphrased in the read: the island objection fails
  because an island is a contingent thing whose non-existence is conceivable,
  whereas "that than which nothing greater can be conceived" is the one case where
  non-existence is inconceivable, so the parody does not transfer.

> **Q9 — Monologium opening (Monologium I).** "If any man, either from ignorance
> or unbelief, has no knowledge of the existence of one Nature which is the
> highest of all existing beings … let him try to teach himself … by reason
> alone."
- Matched substring: `If any man, either from ignorance or unbelief, has no knowledge of the existence of one Nature which is the highest of all existing beings, which is also suffi- … cient to itself in its eternal blessedness` ✅ CONFIRMED. Establishes the *Monologium* as the reason-alone (no-scripture) work; the read paraphrases its purpose and quotes the opening clause.

**Anachronism guards observed:**
- "Ontological argument" is Kant's term — flagged in-prose as a later name.
- Anselm argues to **God's existence from the concept alone, with no appeal to
  the world** — this is the distinctive feature the brief asks be stated plainly,
  and it is the exact thing Aquinas later rejects (Aquinas: existence must be
  proved a posteriori, from effects). The contrast is stated without reading
  Aquinas's framework back into Anselm.
- The staff-forced-into-the-hands scene and other Eadmer color are framed as
  Eadmer's *Vita Anselmi* (Anselm's monk and biographer), the way the Aquinas
  read frames William of Tocco — never asserted as flat documentary fact.
