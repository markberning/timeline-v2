## QUOTES USED (all verbatim, Haldane & Kemp)

### Hook + epigraphs + Book I (Ch. 1–2)
1. **§1 opening** — "The world is my idea:—this is a truth which holds good for
   everything that lives and knows, though man alone can bring it into reflective
   and abstract consciousness." — VERIFIED VERBATIM (Gutenberg 38427; thinker
   ledger #1). Used: hook, Ch. 1 epigraph.
2. **§1 eye/hand** — "what he knows is not a sun and an earth, but only an eye that
   sees a sun, a hand that feels an earth" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (Gutenberg 38427;
   thinker ledger #2). Used: hook, Ch. 2 epigraph + body.
3. **§1 no truth more certain** — "No truth therefore is more certain, more
   independent of all others, and less in need of proof than this, that all that
   exists for knowledge, and therefore this whole world, is only object in relation
   to subject" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (Gutenberg 38427; thinker ledger #3). Used:
   Ch. 2 body.
4. **§2 the subject** — "That which knows all things and is known by none is the
   subject. Thus it is the supporter of the world, that condition of all phenomena,
   of all objects which is always pre-supposed throughout experience" — VERIFIED
   VERBATIM (Gutenberg 38427, §2). Used: Ch. 2 body.
5. **§2 subject only as knower** — "Every one finds himself to be subject, yet only
   in so far as he knows, not in so far as he is an object of knowledge." —
   VERIFIED VERBATIM (Gutenberg 38427, §2). Used: Ch. 2 body.
   - NOTE on the principle of sufficient reason as "the universal form of all
     objects": PARAPHRASED in the read's own prose (no quote marks), because the
     cleanest §5 wording available was a bracketed/partial line; the substance
     (PSR = the form of the world-as-representation, governing object-to-object
     relations only) is standard doctrine. No quoted line asserted there.

### Book II (Ch. 3)
6. **§18 body = will, given in two ways** — "The act of will and the movement of the
   body are not two different things objectively known, which the bond of causality
   unites; they do not stand in the relation of cause and effect; they are one and
   the same, but they are given in entirely different ways." — VERIFIED VERBATIM
   (Wikisource Second Book, §18). Used: Ch. 3 epigraph? (no — epigraph is #7); body.
7. **§22 will alone is thing in itself** — "All idea, of whatever kind it may be,
   all object, is phenomenal existence, but the will alone is a thing in itself." —
   VERIFIED VERBATIM (Wikisource Second Book, §22). Used: Ch. 3 epigraph + body.
8. **§24 the falling stone** — "The impulse given it is for the stone what the motive
   is for me, and what in the case of the stone appears as cohesion, gravitation,
   rigidity, is in its inner nature the same as that which I recognise in myself as
   will." — VERIFIED VERBATIM (Wikisource Second Book, §24). Used: Ch. 3 body
   (gravity/forces as lowest grade of the will's objectification).

### Book III (Ch. 4)
9. **§34 pure will-less subject** — "the pure, will-less, painless, timeless subject
   of knowledge" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (Wikisource Third Book, §34; thinker-ledger
   doctrine §D). Used: Ch. 4 body.
10. **§38 Ixion / Sabbath of willing** — "the wheel of Ixion stands still" and "the
    Sabbath of the penal servitude of willing" — VERIFIED VERBATIM as printed
    clauses (Wikisource Third Book, §38). Used: Ch. 4 body. (Short clauses, quoted
    exactly.)
11. **§52 music = copy of the will itself** — "Music is as direct an objectification
    and copy of the whole will as the world itself" and "music is thus by no means
    like the other arts, the copy of the Ideas, but the copy of the will itself" —
    VERIFIED VERBATIM (Gutenberg 38427, §52; thinker ledger #10, which corrected an
    earlier mis-transcription on 2026-06-15 to exactly this wording). Used: Ch. 4
    epigraph + body. The read quotes the corrected "…by no means like the other
    arts, the copy of the Ideas, but the copy of the will itself…" form.

### Book IV (Ch. 5–6)
12. **§57 all willing arises from want** — "All willing arises from want, therefore
    from deficiency, and therefore from suffering. The satisfaction of a wish ends
    it; yet for one wish that is satisfied there remain at least ten which are
    denied." — VERIFIED VERBATIM (thinker ledger #6, Wikisource Vol. 1 p.444
    region). Used: Ch. 5 epigraph + body.
13. **§57 basis of all willing is need** — "the basis of all willing is need,
    deficiency, and thus pain" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (thinker ledger #7). Used: Ch. 6
    epigraph (paired with #14).
14. **§57 the pendulum** — "like a pendulum backwards and forwards between pain and
    ennui" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (thinker ledger #8; *ennui* = boredom, glossed in
    prose). Used: Ch. 5 body, Ch. 6 epigraph.
15. **§66 the good man regards others' suffering as his own** — "recognises in all
    beings his own inmost and true self, must also regard the infinite suffering of
    all suffering beings as his own" — VERIFIED VERBATIM (thinker ledger #12,
    Wikisource Vol. 1 p.531). Used: Ch. 5 body (compassion / seeing through the
    *principium individuationis*).
16. **§71 the closing "nothing"** — "in whom the will has turned and has denied
    itself, this our world, which is so real, with all its suns and milky-ways—is
    nothing." — VERIFIED VERBATIM, the literal final sentence of Vol. 1 ("END OF
    VOL. I." follows it), transcribed from the Wikisource page-scan
    Volume 1.djvu/574 this session. Used: Ch. 5 closing block. The em-dash before
    "is nothing" is as printed in Haldane & Kemp.

## CLAIMS / DATES / DOCTRINE (non-quote facts, all standard)

- **Four-book structure** (Bk I world as representation; Bk II world as will; Bk
  III the Ideas + aesthetics; Bk IV ethics + affirmation/denial of the will) —
  CONFIRMED (the book's actual table of contents; SEP).
- **"One single thought" / read-it-twice** — Schopenhauer says in his preface the
  work conveys a single thought and must be read twice; standard and well-attested
  (SEP; the preface to the first edition). Framed as his own stated method.
- **Debt to Kant** (appearance vs. thing-in-itself; space/time/causality as the
  mind's forms; the PSR governs appearances only) — CONFIRMED standard; the read is
  careful that the *crossing* of Kant's wall via the body is Schopenhauer's own
  claim, which his critics (incl. Nietzsche) dispute. Cross-link
  [Kant](/philosophy/thinker/kant). — CONFIRMED.
- **Will = blind, aimless, single striving; NOT personal, NOT divine, NOT
  purposeful**; objectified at grades from gravity → human desire — CONFIRMED
  standard (the brief's required framing). The read states this explicitly (Ch. 3).
- **Platonic Ideas as intermediate between the one will and the many particulars;
  will-less contemplation; hierarchy of the arts (architecture → poetry/tragedy)**
  — CONFIRMED standard (Bk III). Plato named but NOT cross-linked (the author-brief
  whitelist lists thinker route `socrates`, not `plato`; to stay inside the
  whitelist the name is left unlinked).
- **Music as the direct copy of the Will itself, not of the Ideas; influence on
  Wagner** — CONFIRMED (§52; Wagner read WWR c.1854 and built his music theory on
  it — thinker ledger §D "Influence forward"). Stated as Wagner building "much of
  his theory of music on it."
- **Pessimism argued (willing = lack = pain; satisfaction merely negative; pendulum
  pain↔ennui)** — CONFIRMED (Bk IV §57; quotes 12–14).
- **Compassion (*Mitleid*) as the basis of ethics, grounded in seeing through the
  *principium individuationis* / the veil of Mâyâ** — CONFIRMED (Bk IV §66; the
  Mâyâ borrowing from the Upanishads is Schopenhauer's own acknowledged source —
  thinker ledger §D, named precisely, not orientalized).
- **Asceticism / denial of the will-to-live as the highest route; suicide REJECTED
  as still an affirmation of the will** — CONFIRMED standard (Bk IV; the suicide
  point is §69, transcribed this session — the suicide affirms the will, wanting
  life on other terms). Stated in Ch. 5.
- **Near-Buddhist ending; the "nothing"; debt to the Upanishads' Mâyâ + the Buddhist
  analysis of craving** — CONFIRMED (§71 + §71's own footnote naming the
  Buddhists' Prajna-Paramita, "beyond all knowledge"; thinker ledger §D). Schopenhauer
  among the first major European philosophers to treat Indian thought as philosophy
  — CONFIRMED standard. Named precisely (Upanishads, Mâyâ, craving), not orientalized.
- **Publication/reception facts**: Vol. 1 printed late 1818, dated 1819, Leipzig,
  F. A. Brockhaus; sold almost nothing, most of the first edition pulped; 1844
  second edition added a second volume of supplementary essays (50 supplements),
  original four books left essentially intact; third edition 1859. Late fame via
  *Parerga und Paralipomena* (1851) + an admiring English review. — CONFIRMED
  (thinker ledger §A/§B; SEP). The "fifty supplements" count is standard.
- **Forward echo — Nietzsche**: discovered WWR by chance in a Leipzig bookshop,
  early disciple (via *The Birth of Tragedy*), then critic; kept the blind striving
  will, flipped pessimism into life-affirmation, recast will-to-live as
  will-to-power, read denial-of-the-will as a failure of nerve / slander against
  life — CONFIRMED standard (thinker ledger §D; the brief's Nietzsche framing).
  Cross-link [Nietzsche](/philosophy/thinker/nietzsche). Also named: Wagner, Freud
  (the unconscious drive ruling the conscious mind — Freud acknowledged the
  affinity; thinker ledger §D). — CONFIRMED.
- **Hegel contrast (the break)**: reality as rational Spirit unfolding toward
  freedom; Schopenhauer sets a blind, goalless will against it (no progress, history
  repeats). — CONFIRMED documented opposition (thinker ledger §E). Cross-link
  [Hegel](/philosophy/thinker/hegel).
