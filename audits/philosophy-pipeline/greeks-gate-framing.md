## MUST-FIX LIST (ship-blocking)

1. **[Ch 2 / Axis: Anachronism] Democritus atoms — cheer without the full brake.** The draft says "the resemblance to modern atoms is genuinely delightful" and tells readers to "resist the cheer" because it was reasoning not experiment — but that sentence immediately precedes the words "the delight is in the *coincidence*." The draft ALMOST lands the brake, but the phrase "ancient science getting it right two thousand years early" (which it introduces only to disavow) primes readers to hear a proto-science claim. The fact pack §2 is explicit: "do NOT present this as modern atomic theory anticipated — it's a metaphysical solution, not an experimental one." The existing disavowal is too weak and too late. MUST-FIX: reorder the paragraph so the brake comes before any form of the "getting it right" framing, not after it.

2. **[Ch 3 / Axis: Strawman] Protagoras' measure doctrine — the relativism read is asserted, not opened.** The draft says the measure doctrine "seems to say that truth is relative to the perceiver." That is one reading — the subjective-relativism reading — but Protagoras scholars also defend an objectivist or proto-pragmatist reading (each perceiver IS the measure, but that does not collapse into "there is no further fact"). The fact pack §2 steelman notes the philosophical range without picking a winner. A framing gate requires giving the measure doctrine its strongest defensible reading before identifying what it is vulnerable to, not just the reading Plato wanted readers to reach. MUST-FIX: frame the measure doctrine as a contested philosophical position whose interpretation is open, not as the relativism story Plato packaged it as.

3. **[Ch 5 / Axis: Anachronism] *Eudaimonia* — the draft hedges correctly but not completely.** The draft says "the standard translation, 'happiness,' is genuinely misleading, so be careful" and offers "flourishing" and "living well across a whole life." This is good and substantially correct. However, the draft then uses "happy" twice in the confirmed Aristotle quotation context ("one day, or a short time, does not make a man blessed and happy") — the word is Aristotle's own (via Ross's translation), so using it is not wrong, but the draft doesn't signal to readers that "happy" there is the translator's word for *eudaimonia*, leaving the exact anachronism-trap it just warned about to re-enter two sentences later. MUST-FIX: add a brief bridging clause or parenthetical — "(the same word, eudaimonia, though Ross translates it 'happy' here)" — so the hedge lands consistently.

4. **[Ch 6 / Axis: Lone-Genius Myth / Women's Erasure] Hipparchia is present — but inadequately sourced and partially misframed.** The draft gives Hipparchia a paragraph (last paragraph of Ch 6), which satisfies the framing gate's baseline requirement that she be named. However: (a) the draft says "by the tradition Diogenes Laertius preserves, [she] threatened to kill herself unless her family let her marry him" — this is the correct legend-frame, and the fact pack confirms DL VI.96 is verified — but the draft fails to note DL VI.96's most important signal: that she gets **her own chapter** in DL's *Lives*, which is exceptional and marks her as a recognized philosopher, not merely Crates' wife. The draft's current framing makes her a biographical anecdote rather than a recognized figure in the tradition. MUST-FIX: state that DL gives her her own chapter — this is the evidence that "the canon almost dropped her" while antiquity itself had not.

5. **[Ch 7 / Axis: Lone-Genius Myth / Arabic transmission] The Arabic handoff is a one-sentence tease with no substance.** The draft's final paragraph does name "Arabic-speaking scholars who, over the following centuries, kept Aristotle alive and thinking when the Latin West had nearly lost him." This technically complies with the Western-overclaim gate (the scope is stated as Western) and names the transmission. But for a gate concerned with the lone-genius myth and with the pipeline doc's explicit requirement that "the Arabic transmission (al-Fārābī, Avicenna, Averroes) … must not be written out," a single clause that names no one and assigns no intellectual work is functionally a dismissal dressed as an acknowledgment. The pipeline doc §"v1 scope" states this is INSIDE the Western story. MUST-FIX: at minimum name al-Fārābī, Avicenna, and Averroes (as the fact pack's influence chain §6 item 16 requires), and say in one clause what they *did* — preserved, extended, and returned Aristotle to the Latin West — not merely that some unnamed scholars "kept" something "alive." This is era-handoff material that earns a sentence of its own, not a clause.

## SHOULD-FIX LIST (non-blocking quality findings)

A. **[Ch 1 / Axis: Western Overclaim] India and China — the qualifier lands correctly but the hook still flirts with the universal.** The throughline metadata reads: "Western philosophy starts the day someone explains the world without a god in the story." This is scoped correctly as "Western philosophy." Ch 1 then adds the qualifier explicitly: "That qualifier matters. India and China had deep traditions of thought that are older than or as old as this one; they are simply a different story, deferred here by scope, not by rank." This is the correct language and it is present. The SHOULD-FIX is the **era hook lead paragraph**, which says "a man named Thales looked at the world and did something quietly radical: he tried to explain it without a single god in the explanation" and ends with "This is the story of people who decided that 'the gods did it' was no longer a good enough answer." The lead contains no Western scope qualifier — a reader who only reads the hook before clicking away gets the universalist framing. The scoping sentence comes well into Ch 1. SHOULD-FIX: add "in the Western tradition" or equivalent to the era hook lead, ideally in the first paragraph.

B. **[Ch 2 / Axis: Strawman] Parmenides — the draft does the right thing but partially undersells his own tradition.** The draft says "Feel the pull of that before you reject it, because the smartest people of the next two centuries couldn't simply wave it away." This instruction is present and correct. However, the draft then describes his conclusion as "insane" — "the conclusion is insane: nothing moves, nothing changes, your whole life is a deception." The word "insane" is the reader's protective recoil, not the steelman. Parmenides himself did not think the conclusion was insane; many scholars argue it was a rigorous and productive move. The draft actually undermines its own "feel the pull" instruction by immediately labeling the conclusion unhinged. SHOULD-FIX: replace "the conclusion is insane" with something that stays inside the thinker's frame — "the conclusion is monstrous by common sense, yet the argument makes every step seem forced."

C. **[Ch 3 / Axis: Lone-Genius Myth] The Academy and Lyceum are named as institutions but their community character is underplayed throughout.** Ch 4 notes that Plato founded the Academy; Ch 5 notes the Lyceum. The draft does not flag that these were self-sustaining intellectual communities — the Academy lasted in some form for centuries, the Lyceum similarly — in a way that frames them as institutional facts rather than Plato-and-Aristotle extensions. This is a lesser form of the lone-genius problem: the buildings are named but the communities are not. SHOULD-FIX: one sentence in each of Ch 4 and Ch 5 noting the school as a community with ongoing students and a life beyond the founder's own.

D. **[Ch 3 / Axis: Posthumous-Misuse / Honest Warts] Athenian democracy's exclusions — the Socrates trial paragraph does name the political context correctly, but democracy's exclusions (women, enslaved people, metics) are absent.** The draft's framing of the trial is admirably honest about the political complexity. However, the pipeline doc's Gate 5 specification flags "Athenian democracy's exclusions if the trial of Socrates leans on 'democracy.'" The draft leans on the democratic context heavily ("Athens had become a democracy," "the man who could stand up in the assembly") and the jury/conviction is framed throughout as an Athenian democratic process. Noting that the democracy in question excluded most Athenians — women, the enslaved, resident non-citizens — would take one sentence and would prevent the word "democracy" from functioning here as an unqualified positive. This is not a lecture-about-it finding; it is a "name it, move on" standard. SHOULD-FIX: one brief clause noting the exclusions the next time "democracy" is used approvingly in Ch 3.

E. **[Ch 2 / Axis: Anachronism / Strawman] "Myth was not stupidity" — Greek religion's own internal logic is not named.** The draft's break block frames the shift from "Poseidon shaking the earth" to natural explanation. The break block correctly says "to explain something was to say which god, in what mood, and the cure was a ritual to fix the mood." This is accurate and not hostile. But the implicit framing throughout Ch 1 — "the gods were not a good enough answer" — recurs enough times to risk reading myth-explanation as simply intellectually primitive, which was the "religion was stupidity" anachronism the pipeline flagged. The draft never says myth was stupid, but it also never says the mythic framework was internally consistent, culturally functional, and not obviously inadequate to the people living inside it. SHOULD-FIX: one clause acknowledging that the mythic framework worked on its own terms — the shift was not a correction of an obvious error but a choice to adopt a new standard of explanation.

F. **[Ch 6 / Axis: Anachronism] The Epicurean Garden "admitted women" framing — correct but the "first" superlative must be avoided.** The draft says "His garden … admitted women and at least one enslaved man as full members." The pipeline doc's §8 "first to" checklist explicitly warns: "the admission itself is sourced (DL, §1) but the 'FIRST' superlative is not verified (Pythagorean women predate). Drop the 'first'; state the documented inclusivity." The draft does not use the word "first" here — which is correct. However, the adjacent sentence in the draft reads "unusually for the age" — this is a relative claim that needs some grounding or qualification. SHOULD-FIX (low priority, may stand as written): verify or hedge "unusually for the age" so it doesn't implicitly revive the "first school to admit women" superlative by another route.

G. **[Ch 7 / Axis: Anachronism] Calling Cicero's Latin work a "survival kit" is figurative but accurate — no fix needed.** The draft's framing of Cicero as transmitter rather than original system-builder is handled well. No anachronism finding here.

## Per-Chapter Verdicts

### Ch 1 — Before philosophy, and the move that started it
**Verdict: FAIR**

The Western-scope qualifier is present and clearly stated. Thales' prediction is appropriately hedged with "the prediction may be the legend's seed rather than a real feat." The fragments-and-hostile-reports caveat is in the break block. The Pythagorean "almost entirely legend" framing is explicit and correct. The Babylonian mathematics acknowledgment is present and specific ("the famous theorem… was known to Babylonian mathematicians long before and was probably never proved by the man himself"). The myth-as-explanation framing in the break block is fair to myth without endorsing it.

Minor concern (captured in SHOULD-FIX A): the era hook lead paragraph lacks the Western scope qualifier that Ch 1's body has. Not ship-blocking at ch1 level given the qualifier is on the very next page.

### Ch 2 — The problem of change
**Verdict: TILTED**

Two findings apply. First (MUST-FIX 1): the Democritus/atoms paragraph introduces "ancient science getting it right two thousand years early" to disavow it, but the rhetorical structure still primes the reader for the cheer before the correction — the brake is real but misplaced. Second (SHOULD-FIX B): Parmenides' conclusion is called "insane," which undermines the immediately preceding "feel the pull" instruction. These together leave the chapter tilted toward modern-framing condescension on the atoms question and a weakened steelman on the Parmenides question.

### Ch 3 — The Sophists and Socrates
**Verdict: FAIR** (with one MUST-FIX)

The Sophists steelman is genuinely strong — naming Plato as the smear's source is exactly right, and Protagoras is given a real position with real stakes. MUST-FIX 2 applies: the measure doctrine is presented primarily in its relativist reading, which is Plato's reading. The strongest Protagoras scholarship admits multiple readings; the draft forecloses this. The Socratic problem (three incompatible Socrateses) is handled correctly and explicitly. The trial's political complexity is handled honestly — the "tangled" framing is exactly right and the fact pack's Critias/Alcibiades context is partially present ("Socrates' former companions had been among the traitors and tyrants"). SHOULD-FIX D (democracy's exclusions) is non-blocking.

### Ch 4 — Plato
**Verdict: FAIR**

The Forms-are-not-Christian-Ideas distinction is handled correctly ("they have nothing to do with ideas in your head"). The "Plato did not think this world is an illusion" correction is present and explicit. The philosopher-kings line is introduced as "one of the most influential and most criticized sentences ever written" — balanced and fair. The Academy is named with the correct hedge ("something like the first enduring institution of higher learning in Europe, though calling it a 'university' … would be reading our world back into his"). The "Plato's Socrates" framing is consistent throughout. No anachronism, strawman, or overclaim findings.

SHOULD-FIX C (Academy as community, not just building) is noted but non-blocking.

### Ch 5 — Aristotle
**Verdict: FAIR** (with one MUST-FIX)

The fake-habit-quote debunking is present and correct. The slavery paragraph is handled exactly as the pipeline requires: "the man who systematized logic and taught the West to value purpose also wrote a justification for human bondage. Both are his." This is the posthumous-misuse honesty the gate requires — named plainly, not sanitized, not turned into a lecture. MUST-FIX 3 applies: the *eudaimonia* hedge is correct but the word "happy" from the Aristotle quotation re-enters the text without flagging that it's the same concept just renamed by the translator. Non-negligible: this is the one anachronism trap the pipeline specifically names for this chapter, and the draft almost closes the loop but doesn't quite.

The "by nature a political animal" gloss (creature built for community, not free spirit) is accurate and present. The teleology description is competent and scoped correctly ("until modern biology learned to explain the same apparent purposes without anyone designing them" — avoids implying teleology was simply wrong, while being honest about its fate in science).

### Ch 6 — Philosophy as a way of life
**Verdict: TILTED** (with two MUST-FIX items)

Three findings apply. MUST-FIX 4 (Hipparchia under-framed) is the most significant: she is present, which the gate requires, but her framing as a biographical anecdote rather than a recognized philosopher (DL gave her her own chapter) misses the gate's point. The Epicurean-glutton correction is present and strong — the "almost the reverse" framing is exactly right, and the cheese-letter quote is excellent. The Stoic-as-gritted-teeth correction is also present and correct. The Cynics' word-drift problem (modern "cynicism" vs. ancient Cynic moral seriousness) is flagged explicitly.

However: the Chrysippus "no Stoa without him" attribution is framed as "the saying went" which is correct (PARAPHRASE-ONLY per the pack), but the Cynics-to-Stoics influence chain (Crates → Zeno of Citium) is carried well. The Diogenes Laertius legend-vector flag is present throughout Ch 6, which is the right practice.

### Ch 7 — Rome and the long afterlife
**Verdict: TILTED** (with one MUST-FIX)

The 529 closure qualification is present and strong — the "too clean" correction is explicit and the modern scholarly doubt is named. The three Stoics spanning slave-to-emperor is the chapter's spine and it works. MUST-FIX 5 (Arabic transmission must name names) is ship-blocking: the final paragraph's single clause on "Arabic-speaking scholars" who "kept Aristotle alive" names no one and describes no intellectual work. Al-Fārābī, Avicenna, and Averroes are required by the pipeline doc and the fact pack's influence chain. As written, the clause is a placeholder that technically avoids erasure while functionally erasing the tradition.

Cicero's role as translator/coin-minter of Latin philosophical vocabulary is present and correctly framed. Lucretius is present and correctly described. The Boethius handoff is present.
