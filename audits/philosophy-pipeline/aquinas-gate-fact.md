### F-12 · "The straw" quote wording — DISCREPANCY

**Draft epigraph (ch6):** `"Reginald, I cannot, because all that I have written seems like straw to me."`  
**Pack §3:** Same wording: "Reginald, I cannot, because all that I have written seems like straw to me."  
**SEP live fetch:** "All that I have written seems to me like straw compared with what has now been revealed to me."

There are **two distinct traditional renderings** in circulation:
1. The shorter form the draft uses ("all that I have written seems like straw to me") — this is the standard condensed form, cited in the pack as the "standard form."
2. The SEP's longer form ("seems to me like straw **compared with what has now been revealed to me**") — this adds the revelatory comparison.
3. The Catholic Encyclopedia's paraphrase ("I can do no more. Such secrets have been revealed to me…") — a third rendering.

All three trace to the same canonization testimony (Bartholomew of Capua, 1319). The pack correctly notes this is third-hand LATE-TRADITION and that "multiple wording variants" exist. The draft quotes the shorter form and carries the full transmission-chain frame. This is acceptable given the pack's explicit multi-version caveat. The frame in ch6 ("The reported answer is the line in the epigraph") appropriately hedges.

**Verdict:** ✅ CONFIRMED — the shorter form is a recognized rendering of the tradition, and the draft's framing adequately signals the uncertainty. The SEP carries a longer version; neither is provably "the" original. The frame in the draft handles this correctly.
