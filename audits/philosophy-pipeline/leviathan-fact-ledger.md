## Verified verbatim quotations (PG #3207, by line)

### Q1 — the state of nature is war (Part I, ch. 13)  [line 4015–4017]
> "Hereby it is manifest, that during the time men live without a common Power to keep
> them all in awe, they are in that condition which is called Warre; and such a warre,
> as is of every man, against every man."
✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. **FLAG (LEGEND/GLOSS):** Hobbes's English is **"every man,
against every man."** The Latin tag **"bellum omnium contra omnes" / "war of all
against all"** is a *later gloss* (it appears in Hobbes's earlier Latin work *De Cive*
in the form "bellum omnium in omnes", and the catchy English "war of all against all"
is a translator's/popularizer's rendering), **NOT his English in *Leviathan***. The
prose uses his actual English and explicitly flags the famous Latin tag as a later
gloss, never as his *Leviathan* wording.

### Q2 — "solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short" (Part I, ch. 13)  [line 4040–4044]
> "…continuall feare, and danger of violent death; And the life of man, solitary, poore,
> nasty, brutish, and short."
✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. Original spelling **"poore"** preserved (the modern misquote
"poor, nasty" drops the doubled-o). The full sentence is the close of his catalogue
of what life lacks without a common power ("no place for Industry… no Arts; no Letters;
no Society").

### Q3 — the covenant / "I Authorise…" (Part II, ch. 17)  [line 5675–5677]
> "I Authorise and give up my Right of Governing my selfe, to this Man, or to this
> Assembly of men, on this condition, that thou give up thy Right to him, and Authorise
> all his Actions in like manner."
✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. This is the wording of the mutual covenant ("as if every man
should say to every man").

### Q4 — the Leviathan as "Mortall God" (Part II, ch. 17)  [line 5680–5682]
> "This is the Generation of that great LEVIATHAN, or rather (to speake more reverently)
> of that Mortall God, to which wee owe under the Immortall God, our peace and defence."
✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. Original spelling **"Mortall God"** preserved. The sovereign is
the "Mortall God" under the "Immortall God."

### Q5 — "Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words" (Part II, ch. 17)  [line 5530–5531]
> "And Covenants, without the Sword, are but Words, and of no strength to secure a man
> at all."
✅ CONFIRMED verbatim. The justification for an enforcing power: the laws of nature and
the covenant do not bind without a coercive sword behind them.

### Q6 — Warre is a disposition, not constant fighting (Part I, ch. 13)  [line 4022–4029]
> "For WARRE, consisteth not in Battell onely, or the act of fighting; but in a tract of
> time, wherein the Will to contend by Battell is sufficiently known… So the nature of
> War, consisteth not in actuall fighting; but in the known disposition thereto, during
> all the time there is no assurance to the contrary. All other time is PEACE."
✅ CONFIRMED verbatim (ellipsis spans the weather simile). Used to show Hobbes does not
mean constant combat but a permanent insecurity; the contrast term is "PEACE."

## Position summaries (paraphrase, tied to the text — no quotation marks in prose)
- **Part I "Of Man"** — sense as motion in the body (objects press the organs, producing
  internal motion); reason as "reckoning" (adding and subtracting of names/consequences);
  the restless succession of desire ("a perpetuall and restlesse desire of Power after
  power, that ceaseth onely in Death"); **no *summum bonum*** (no greatest good or final
  end such as the old philosophers spoke of), only a continual moving of desire from one
  object to the next; equality of men in the state of nature (the weakest can kill the
  strongest); from which competition, diffidence, and glory drive men to the war of every
  man against every man. ✅ All grounded in chs. 1–13. The "restlesse desire of Power
  after power" line is ch. 11; quoted in the ledger as a paraphrase target only (the prose
  may quote it — verify line if quoted: it is in PG #3207 ch. 11). To stay safe the work
  prose paraphrases this rather than quoting unless line-confirmed.
- **Part II "Of Common-wealth"** — the covenant by which each man authorizes one sovereign
  (a man or an assembly) and owns the sovereign's acts as his own; the sovereign (the
  Leviathan, the "Mortall God") holds power that is **absolute and undivided** (Hobbes
  argues a divided sovereignty is no sovereignty, which is why he opposes mixed/limited
  constitutions); the rights of the sovereign are not transferable or forfeitable except
  by failing to protect; obedience is owed in exchange for protection ("the end of
  Obedience is Protection"). The enforcing logic is Q5. ✅ chs. 17–21.
- **Part III "Of a Christian Common-wealth"** — Scripture interpreted to subordinate the
  church to the civil sovereign; no independent spiritual power that can rival the
  sovereign; the sovereign is the head of the church in his own dominion. ✅ chs. 32–43.
- **Part IV "Of the Kingdome of Darknesse"** — his anti-clerical attack: the "Kingdome of
  Darknesse" is the confederacy of those who keep men in error by false doctrine
  (misread Scripture, pagan "Daemonology", scholastic "vain philosophy"), to gain power
  over them. ✅ chs. 44–47.

## Potted-summary / caricature flags (handled in prose)
- 🟡 **"war of all against all" / "bellum omnium contra omnes"** — NOT Hobbes's *Leviathan*
  English (see Q1). Framed as a later Latin gloss.
- 🟡 **"Hobbes the cynic who thought men are evil"** — caricature. Hobbes does not say men
  are wicked by nature; he says that without a common power, rational self-interested men
  in conditions of scarcity and equal vulnerability fall into war. The driver is
  insecurity and the structure of the situation, not innate malice. Prose states the
  documented position, not the caricature.
- 🟡 **Hobbes "an atheist"** — contested and not established; *Leviathan* speaks of the
  "Immortall God" and is full of Scripture (Parts III–IV). The prose does not assert
  atheism; it reports his anti-clericalism and the heresy/atheism *accusations* he drew,
  framed as accusations.
- 🟡 **"Leviathan = totalitarianism"** — anachronistic. Hobbes argues for an absolute and
  undivided sovereign, but from a logic of peace and self-preservation, in a 17th-century
  context (written during/after the English Civil War). Named at era altitude, not
  lectured.
