# GATE READ — Höch, *Cut with the Kitchen Knife* (`kitchen-knife`)
**Gate:** STORYTELLING + LOOKING + CLARITY/VOICE (one of the 5 art gates)
**Verdict:** PASS WITH FIXES. No BLOCKERs. The five-section arc is genuinely compelling — the "only woman holding the knife" runs as a spine from the hook through the afterlife's closing image, the politics land as story not lecture, and the looking section steers the eye well across a famously dense montage with prose pointers only. A handful of FIX-level items (one factpack-accuracy mismatch on the map corner, a couple of light voice/meta tics, one mild reader-command). Nothing blocks ship; the FIX items should be addressed in revision.

## 1. STORYTELLING (primary axis)

Overall: **strong.** Pacing is good across all five sections; each has a clear job and a turn. The Weimar/Berlin-Dada politics are delivered as scene-setting ("Soldiers home from the trenches, generals from the old empire, street-fighting revolutionaries… all crammed into one furious city") rather than a date-list. Höch's precarious position is seeded in §1 (Fair), paid off in §4 (Reading) with her own quote, and resolved in §5 (Afterlife) — a real through-line, not a fact dump. The title-unpacking in §1 ("The picture is the cut. And a woman… is the one holding the knife.") is a high point.

### [NICE] §1 Fair — "This is the Berlin our picture is about, and it is the material it is literally made of."
Location: KnfFair, first paragraph close (line 118).
This is a fine line and does real work (it bridges to §2's "no paint" reveal). Borderline meta — it tells the reader what the chapter is about. It reads as a hinge, not a narration-of-structure, so keeping it is defensible. If trimming for the no-meta rule, "and it is the city this picture is literally cut from" keeps the image and drops the "our picture is about" framing. Optional.

### [NICE] §3 Looking — opening "Stand in front of it."
Location: KnfLooking, first paragraph (line 181).
A reader-command ("Stand…"), but it's the conventional, effective opening move for a looking section and reads as invitation, not instruction. Matched by "Spend a minute" (line 185), "Now watch one device" (line 199). These are mild and serve the looking-section's job of steering the eye. Flagging for completeness per the voice contract; recommend KEEP — they are the looking section's navigation verbs, not condescending commands. If the reviser wants zero imperatives, "In front of it, the first impression is overwhelming…" works, but this is not required.

### [NICE] §2 Making — three-part "the break" structure is signposted ("the first thing," "The second part of the break," "the third part of the break").
Location: KnfMaking (lines 147, 164, 169).
This is light scaffolding that risks reading as a lecture outline. It mostly works because each part is a distinct idea (new medium / political weapon / a woman's critique) and the prose inside each is vivid. No change needed, but if the reviser wants it less listy, the third beat ("And the third part of the break is who is making it") could open on the image instead of the label. Optional.

No dull, disjointed, or genuinely listy passages found. Section transitions are clean.

### [FIX] The diagonal direction is stated only as "a rough diagonal" — factpack gives a specific direction.
Location: KnfLooking, line 185 ("a rough diagonal running across the picture between them").
Factpack §2 states the kitchen-knife cut runs **lower-right to upper-left**. The draft hedges to "a rough diagonal," which is safe but leaves a sourced, vivid detail on the table — and the two camps the draft names (anti-dada upper-right, dada lower-left) are consistent with that diagonal, so naming it reinforces the reading. Suggested: "…with a rough diagonal — the knife's cut — running lower-right to upper-left between them." NICE-to-FIX boundary; treat as FIX because it's a sourced detail the looking section is otherwise built to deliver.

### [FIX] Mild meta-narration: "the work we are here to look at" / "the picture we are here to look at."
Location: KnfFair, line 134 ("the work we are here to look at") and the title-unpack "The picture is the cut" sequence; also §2 line 165 references "this picture is one of the founding monuments." 
"the work we are here to look at" narrates the act of reading rather than telling the story. Low-severity but it's the exact tic the voice contract flags.

### [FIX] Reader-command / second-person instruction cluster in §2 Making.
Location: KnfMaking, line 156 ("You will read, often, that…") and line 157 ("Be careful with that sentence"); §3 line 182 ("You will sometimes see it listed as '144 cm'…").
"Be careful with that sentence" is a direct reader-command and edges toward honesty-labeling (telling the reader how to handle the information rather than just giving the honest version). The content is correct and necessary (the invention feud), but the framing can be flipped to statement: "That sentence is contested. The two of them told an origin story…" — which keeps the honesty without commanding the reader. Same for "Be careful with that sentence, because it is contested" → "It is contested." The "You will sometimes see it listed as 144 cm" (line 182) is the same shape but lower-stakes (it's a useful heads-up about a real transcription error a reader might encounter); acceptable to keep, but could become "It is sometimes listed as '144 cm'… those are transcription slips."

### [NICE] "So the honest version is this." / "the honest line is."
Location: KnfMaking, line 160 ("So the honest version is this.").
This is a soft honesty-label.
