This is strong coverage. Two gaps:

### [FIX] Missing facet — Masson's later break with the group / the automatic line as a *returned* method, not a one-and-done 1924 stunt.
The draft frames the automatic line as if Masson did it in 1924 and then it simply "ran to
Pollock." In fact Masson largely **set automatic drawing aside in the later 1920s, broke with
Breton's circle (c. 1929), and returned to automatism in the late 1930s–40s** — and it is that
*second* automatist phase, carried into his US exile (1941–45), that actually put him in front
of the New York painters who became the Abstract Expressionists. Omitting this makes the
Masson→Pollock jump read as a tidy straight line from one 1924 sheet, when the real mechanism
is Masson-in-New-York in the 1940s. This is the single most consequential comprehensiveness
gap because it is also what makes the afterlife claim *true* rather than hand-wavy.

Suggested addition (one sentence, in `MsnAfterlife` "The line that kept running," after "It
crossed the Atlantic and turned into paint."):
> "Masson himself made that crossing: he spent the war years (1941–1945) in exile in the
> United States, drawing automatically again after years away from the method, and the younger
> New York painters watched him do it."

(Confirmed by web sources: Masson exiled to the US during WWII, "continued to experiment with
automatism and influenced younger painters such as Jackson Pollock.")

### [NICE] Minor facet — the recurrence of the fragments *as Masson's lifelong vocabulary*.
The draft names hands/birds/breast/fish as the things that "recur across Masson's automatic
sheets," which is good. A one-clause note that these creature/body fragments (and the later
fish/labyrinth/Minotaur imagery) became a signature of his whole oeuvre would deepen the
"this is one node in a body of work, not a freak object" framing. Optional; the draft already
gestures at it.

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### Origin-of-automatism overclaim — CLEAR (no fix needed, with one watch-item)
The brief's top risk is any "Masson invented automatism" claim. **The draft does not make it,
and is in fact exemplary here.** It states plainly: "Breton had arrived at the idea first in
**words**" (¶2); "Automatic *writing* already existed" (sub-head ¶1); and frames Masson's
contribution precisely as carrying the method "out of writing and into line." Web-verified:
Surrealist automatism "adapted to art the automatic writing method of André Breton and Philippe
Soupault." The scoping is correct.

- [NICE] One phrase could be tightened. The const `hook` says this is "the first time the
  unconscious itself was let hold the pen." Read in isolation (the hook surfaces on cards, away
  from the careful prose), "the first time" risks reading as "Masson invented unconscious mark-
  making," which the Arp precedent and the writing-came-first point both complicate. Suggest:
  "...one of the first times the unconscious itself was let hold a **drawing** pen." The added
  "drawing" is the load-bearing word — it keeps the (true, defensible) claim about *drawing*
  and drops the (overclaimed) absolute "first time...the pen."

### "Pure" automatism overclaim — CLEAR (handled better than most drafts)
The brief's second risk — over-claiming unplanned/"pure" automatism — is **actively refused**
across two full passages (`MsnMaking` "Not pure all the way down"; the closing caution in
`MsnLooking`). The draft repeatedly separates Breton's "pure state" *ideal* from Masson's
*actual* hand that "could not entirely stop being an artist." This is exactly the correction
the factpack §8.3 demanded. No fix.

### Genius-myth / situating Masson in the group — CLEAR
Masson is placed inside the rue Blomet circle (Miró, Leiris, Artaud), named as joining a
movement Breton defined, and explicitly *not* credited with inventing the loose idea (Arp gets
his due). The `figures` array foregrounds Breton ("Defined automatism") and the studio
neighbors. No lone-genius problem.

### [FIX] Pollock-link verbal-attribution overclaim
This is the one framing/fairness defect that rises above NICE. The draft twice asserts a
**specific verbal statement** by Pollock:
- `MsnBreak` ¶3: "Jackson Pollock **openly admired** Masson's automatism" — fine.
- BUT the section `blurb` and `MsnAfterlife` escalate to: "**Pollock said as much about
  Masson**" and "Jackson Pollock **said plainly that** he admired Masson's automatism."

The *influence* is rock-solid and well-sourced (Masson's US exile, the documented Pollock
debt, Action Painters "under the direct influence of Masson"). What I could **not** verify in
this pass is a clean, quotable, attributed Pollock *statement* — "said as much" / "said
plainly" implies a sourced quotation the factpack does not supply (factpack only says Pollock
"explicitly admired," which is an art-historical judgment, not a quote). Asserting a verbal
"said" without the quote is a small fairness/accuracy overreach and is exactly the kind of
born-from-memory verbal attribution the pipeline guards against.

Suggested fixes (swap the *verbal* framing for the *documented-influence* framing):
- Section `blurb` (afterlife): change "**and Pollock said as much about Masson.**" →
  "**a debt Pollock and the historians of his work openly acknowledge.**"
- `MsnAfterlife` ¶ "From a Paris pen to American paint": change "**and Jackson Pollock said
  plainly that he admired Masson's automatism**" → "**and Jackson Pollock's debt to Masson's
  automatism is one of the best-documented lineages in modern art**."

(If the fact gate can supply a sourced, verbatim Pollock quote on Masson, keep "said" and
attribute it. Absent that quote, drop "said.")
