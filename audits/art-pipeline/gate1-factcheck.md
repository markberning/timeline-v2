## SHOULD-FIX

**SF-1 — Cabanel professorship year (Claim 16) — ⚠️ contested date**  
The draft says Cabanel was made a professor at the École des Beaux-Arts "the same year" as the 1863 Salon. Wikipedia's dedicated *Alexandre Cabanel* article and the Eclectic Light biography both place the appointment in **1864**, one year after the Venus triumph. Only Wikipedia's *Birth of Venus* article says 1863. The 1864 date is better-attested. Recommended fix: change "The same year, Cabanel was made a professor…" to "The following year, Cabanel was made a professor…" (or "in 1864"). This is the one claim in the draft where the more authoritative source contradicts the draft's phrasing.

**SF-2 — Martini engraving credit omits Ramberg (Claim 30) — ⚠️ incomplete credit**  
The engraving is correctly attributed to Pietro Antonio Martini, but Martini executed it *after* a composition by Johann Heinrich Ramberg. All major institutional credits (the Met, Google Arts & Culture, AIC, MFAH, Alamy) list both names. Recommended fix: update the credit line to: *"Pietro Antonio Martini (after Johann Heinrich Ramberg), *Exposition au Salon du Louvre en 1787* (etching), 1787 · The Met, New York"*

**SF-3 — Académie monopoly endpoint "until 1880" (Claim 3) — ⚠️ ambiguous**  
The last Académie-controlled Salon was indeed held in 1880, but the independent Société des artistes français took over for the 1881 Salon (Jules Ferry's decree of 27 December 1880). "Until 1880" is defensible as shorthand for "the last one they ran was 1880," but it risks being read as "the monopoly ended in 1880" when the structural break was really 1881. The surrounding sentence says "in the 1850s the Salon was, flatly, a State-run institution," which is the point that matters for the era draft — so this is a low-priority clarification, not a factual error. Optional tightening: "until 1881" or "through 1880."
