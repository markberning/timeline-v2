## 1. COMPREHENSIVENESS

All five required facets are present and substantively handled:

- **Return to the figure while peers went abstract** — covered hard in `WmnFigure` + `WmnBreak`; names Pollock (drip), Rothko/Newman (color-field), frames it as near-"betrayal." Strong.
- **The two-year struggle + Schapiro** — `WmnMaking` carries the ~fifty-times working, the off-the-stretcher abandonment, the Schapiro visit, the Schjeldahl "luckiest studio visit" line, all flagged as account-not-transcript. Strong.
- **The gestural-figurative fusion** — `WmnBreak` ("figure and ground … the same event") + `WmnLooking`. This is the conceptual core and it lands.
- **The influence** — present but THIN (see [FIX 1.1]).
- **The woman series** — present (six *Women*, Sidney Janis 1953) but the work is treated almost in isolation from the rest of the series (see [NICE 1.2]).

### [FIX 1.1] COMPREHENSIVENESS — the "influence" facet is asserted but not peopled.
The break chapter claims the picture "reopened the figure for a postwar avant-garde" and "prefigured Pop," but the draft never names a single artist who actually carried it forward. Web corroboration (TheArtStory; Whitney) is specific and citable: de Kooning was "an important touchstone for younger artists like **Robert Rauschenberg, Larry Rivers, Grace Hartigan**, and later Pop artists" (and the Rauschenberg *Erased de Kooning Drawing*, 1953, is the famous downstream gesture). A facet the gate is told to check should not be left as a floating abstraction. Add one concrete sentence.

Suggested addition to `WmnBreak`'s "after" paragraph, after "made de Kooning the great counter-argument…":
> "Younger painters took the permission and ran with it — Grace Hartigan, Larry Rivers, and the young Robert Rauschenberg all worked in his wake (Rauschenberg's *Erased de Kooning Drawing* of 1953 is the most famous tribute-by-attack of the era), and the gestural-figurative line he reopened runs straight on toward Pop."

(Classify as [FIX], not [BLOCKER]: the Pop/figuration claim is already in the draft; this only supplies the names the claim is standing on.)

### [NICE 1.2] COMPREHENSIVENESS — *Woman I* is "I" of six; say so once, plainly.
The draft mentions "the six *Women*" at Sidney Janis but never tells the reader that this canvas is the first of a numbered series (*Woman I*–*VI*) and the one that became the flagship. A one-clause aside in `WmnAfterlife` ("the first and most famous of six numbered *Women* he showed together") would orient a zero-knowledge reader to why the title has a numeral.

### [NICE 1.3] COMPREHENSIVENESS — Elaine de Kooning is in the `figures` list but absent from the prose.
She's listed ("Painter; his wife, in the studio years") with no narrative mention. Either give her a half-sentence (she was a painter and critic in those exact studio years) or accept the figures-chip carries her alone. Not load-bearing; flagging for completeness only.

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### The misogyny question — CONFIRMED BALANCED (the headline finding).
`WmnAfterlife`'s "The debate" lays out both sides and explicitly refuses to adjudicate: the feminist/misogyny reading is given as "a serious reading and the picture invites it … the violence is real and it is aimed at a female body," then de Kooning's ambivalence and his stated debt to the ancient idol / the "idol, the Venus, the nude" image-history and humor, then the closing "It is genuinely a debate, not a solved case." Neither side is endorsed nor dismissed. This matches the standing directive and the web record (feminist charge real — e.g. Carol Duncan's "MoMA's Hot Mamas"; scholarly pushback equally real — "self-portrait in drag," "Venus of Willendorf," "All-American girl" charm). The gate's central fairness requirement is met. **No change required here.**

### [FIX 2.1] FAIRNESS / PROPORTION — the "betrayal" framing is asserted three times in the artist's own peers' voice without a dissent.
"betrayal" / "apostasy" / "turning his back" appears in `WmnFigure`, `WmnBreak`, and `WmnAfterlife`. Each time it is the peers'/critics' view, which is correct — but stacked three deep with no counter-voice it starts to read as the draft's own verdict that the return was a transgression, rather than a contemporary reaction the draft is reporting. The fact pack and web both note serious critics saw it as *the most alive painting in the city* (the draft says this once). Recommend trimming one of the three "betrayal" beats OR pairing the third instance with the contemporary pro-view so the proportion is held. Lightest fix: in `WmnAfterlife` para 1, the existing "to others it looked like the most alive painting in the city" already balances it — so the [FIX] is really only to ensure the EARLIER two instances (`WmnFigure`, `WmnBreak`) are clearly attributed ("to his peers," "the orthodoxy said") — they are. Net: this is borderline [NICE]; logging as [FIX] only because the repetition is the kind of drumbeat that tips reporting into editorializing.

### First/only overclaim — CLEAN.
Searched the draft for first/only/greatest superlatives. The risky ones are correctly hedged:
- "the most ambitious picture **he** had ever attempted" — scoped to de Kooning, fine.
- "the great **counter-argument**" — argument-framing, not a primacy claim, fine.
- "**the most prominent** of the Abstract Expressionists" (`WmnAfterlife`) — this is the one genuine overclaim risk. De Kooning was *a* leading figure, but "the most prominent" is contestable (Pollock has at least equal claim, and the draft itself frames Pollock as the orthodoxy-setter). **[FIX 2.2]:** soften to "one of the most prominent of the Abstract Expressionists" or "a leading figure among the Abstract Expressionists." (Note the draft elsewhere says "a central figure" / "at the front of the most aggressive painting" — those are fine; only "the most prominent" trips.)

### Genius-myth / NY School situating — CONFIRMED.
He is introduced (`WmnFigure`) as "a central figure of the **New York School**, the loose group of postwar American painters now lumped together as the Abstract Expressionists," and the prose consistently positions him *against* a collective orthodoxy (Pollock, Rothko, Newman) rather than as a lone genius. The lineage block credits "Gestural abstraction" and "Picasso's distorted figures" as parents, dispersing the originality. No solitary-genius myth. Good.

### Schapiro rescue as account-not-gospel — CONFIRMED.
`WmnMaking` explicitly: "the **standard account** rather than a verbatim-recorded scene," "the *degree* of Schapiro's influence … have been questioned," "hold it as the well-attested story it is, not as a transcript." Exactly the requested handling. The "however much credit the visit truly deserves" close is the right epistemic posture. No change.

### [NICE 2.3] FAIRNESS — name the feminist critic once.
The misogyny side is currently anonymous ("Later critics, feminist critics especially"). The web record gives a concrete, citable anchor: **Carol Duncan, "The MoMA's Hot Mamas"** (1989) is the canonical feminist text on exactly this picture. Naming it would put the debate's two sides on equal evidentiary footing (de Kooning's own quote is named/dated; the charge against him should have a name too). Optional but improves the balance the gate cares about.
