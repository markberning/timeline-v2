# GATE READ — Storytelling + Looking + Clarity/Voice

**Work:** Grosz, *"Daum" Marries Her Pedantic Automaton "George"* (`daum`, Dada work-read)
**Gate:** storytelling (primary) · looking · clarity + voice contract
**Verdict: PASS with FIXES.** No BLOCKERs. The story lands — the clockwork-groom-at-his-own-wedding conceit and the Berlin-Dada bitterness both read as story across all five sections, and the looking section guides the eye with prose pointers and no coordinates. The fixes below are voice-contract violations (reader-commands, an honesty-label, a meta-narration line, a self-referential "this read" gloss) and a couple of pacing/listy soft spots. None block ship; all are quick.

---

## 1. STORYTELLING (primary)

The five-section arc is well-paced and genuinely compelling. The structure earns the payoff: §1 sets the rotten Berlin and plants the in-joke; §2 (break) sharpens the man-machine + weaponised-collage thesis; §3 (looking) does scale-then-figures-then-caution; §4 (reading) delivers the "does sums while she undresses" joke and pulls the camera back to the bourgeois-male target; §5 (afterlife) closes on Heartfield's anti-Nazi line and a strong final image ("the husband is a machine and the artist is the husband"). The automaton conceit and the satire both land as story, not as a list of facts. Good.

- **[NICE] §1 / `DauBerlin`, line ~100 — the Weimar-context paragraph is the one place that risks list-feel.** "the generals, the war, and above all the comfortable, hypocritical German middle class" is fine, but the run "revolution, hyperinflation, war-cripples and bourgeois hypocrisy" condensed from the blurb plus this paragraph's own list (defeat / Kaiser fled / Weimar / hyperinflation / war-cripples / profiteers) is a lot of catalogue before we get back to Grosz. It still reads, because each item is a real beat — leaving as NICE. Optional tightening: cut one of the parenthetical asides (the Weimar-town gloss is the strongest; the hyperinflation "make banknotes worthless" is the weakest and could go).

- **[NICE] §4 / `DauReading`, "A note on the tag" subsection (line ~199).** The meta-mechanical-construction note is correct and accurate to the factpack, but it lands as an appendix after the chapter's emotional peak ("cast himself, the groom, as its specimen"). It deflates the section's close. Consider moving this tag-note earlier (it pairs naturally with §5's 1922-portfolio paragraph, which ALSO explains "meta-mechanical construction" — see the redundancy flag under Clarity) or folding it into §5 so §4 can end on the specimen line. Not blocking.

No dull, disjointed, or incoherent passages. Pacing across the five is good.

## 2. LOOKING (`DauLooking` + annotations)

The looking section guides the eye to the real features through prose pointers, with NO coordinates, and keeps scrap IDs general — exactly right.

- Scale first ("small sheet… portrait-shaped, taller than it is wide"), then the two-techniques split (painted bride / pasted groom), then the groom ("right of centre, the smaller of the two figures… whitish-grey mask… gears and fittings"), then the de Chirico city ("empty, hard-edged architecture and steep perspective lines"), then the honest caution that the individual clippings cannot be reliably identified. Every factpack §5 looking point is covered.
- **Coverage check against the brief's required features:** clockwork automaton self-portrait ✓; collaged female figure — the painted bride is covered in §4 not §3 (see note below); machine parts ✓ (gears/fittings); city/architectural fragments ✓; numbers/diagrams — covered in §4 "He does sums" ✓.
- **[NICE] The numbers/diagrams and the bride are described in §4 (Reading), not §3 (Looking).** This is a defensible authorial choice — §4 needs them for the joke, and §3 explicitly hands the bride off ("Now the bride" opens §4). It reads fine because §3's split-techniques paragraph already names the painted bride. Flagging only so the reconciler knows it's deliberate, not a gap. The annotations array (the bottom-sheet pointers) DO cover all six features including bride + numbers, so the structured looking surface is complete.
- No coordinates, no blind crops, no "look at the upper-left." Prose pointers throughout. Compliant with the no-blind-coordinates rule.

One small accuracy-adjacent note for the fact gate (not mine to rule, flagging): the bride is described as "turned toward" the groom in the draft annotations and §4, while the factpack §5.3 says "turned toward the indifferent machine-man" and the §2 story note says "reluctant/looking back at him." The draft is consistent with the factpack's dominant reading (turned toward him), so OK — leaving to the fact gate.

## 3. CLARITY + VOICE CONTRACT

Scanned every section for: meta-narration, reader-commands, honesty-labels, condescending glosses, jargon-before-definition, walls of text, literal `—` in rendered strings, `&mdash;` misuse. Findings:

### Reader-commands (imperative "you"/"look"/"note" aimed at the reader)
- **[FIX] §3 `DauLooking`, line ~155: "First, scale."** Fragment-command opener. Lighter than the others but it's the imperative register. Suggest: "Start with scale." reads the same but — actually still imperative; cleanest neutral rewrite: "The first thing is scale: for all its fame this is a small sheet…"
- **[FIX] §3 `DauLooking`, line ~164: "Look at the groom, right of centre, the smaller of the two figures."** Direct reader-command. Suggest: *"The groom stands right of centre, the smaller of the two figures."*
- **[FIX] §3 `DauLooking`, line ~164: "Note where his attention is. It is **not** on his bride."** Reader-command ("Note"). Suggest: *"His attention is not on his bride."*
- **[FIX] §3 `DauLooking`, line ~171: "One caution while you look, because it is easy to over-read a collage."** Reader-command + addresses the reader's act of looking. Suggest: *"A collage like this is easy to over-read."* (then continue "The groom is built from photographs…").
- **[FIX] §1 `DauBerlin`, line ~97: "so start by reading the whole thing:"** Reader-command opener. The device (read the title as a sentence) is good and worth keeping, but reframe out of the imperative. Suggest: *"The title is a whole sentence, and worth reading start to finish:"*
- **[NICE] annotations[0] detail: "The one thing to notice is what he does not do."** Borderline reader-instruction inside a bottom-sheet pointer; pointers are a softer register so this is tolerable, but if trimming, *"What matters is what he does not do."* is cleaner.

### Honesty-labels / "we" disclaimers about our own process
- **[FIX] §3 `DauLooking`, line ~171–172: "Sources do not pin them down, and neither will we."** Honesty-label about our process. The *fact* (clippings aren't identifiable) is good and should stay; the meta-comment about what "we" will do is the violation. Suggest: *"The individual clippings cannot be reliably identified — which paper, which photo, which machine — and the sources do not pin them down."* (drop "and neither will we").
- **[NICE] §1 line ~112 and annotations[2]: "so we leave it open" / "so we leave it open" (Peter/Peters surname).** Mild process-disclaimer, twice. It's the honest hedge the factpack requires (surname unresolved) and it's brief, so NICE not FIX — but two near-identical "we leave it open" within the work read a touch repetitive. Consider varying one to a plain hedge: *"(the surname is recorded as both Peter and Peters)"* without the "we leave it open" tail.
- **[FIX] §4 `DauReading`, line ~192: "the only honest short title for it is …"** "honest" here is an honesty-label flavor flag. The point (don't call it Pétain) is correct and should stay; just drop the self-praise word. Suggest: *"…the only short title for it is *"Daum" Marries Her Pedantic Automaton "George."*"*

### Meta-narration / self-referential "this read"
- **[FIX] §2 `DauBreak`, line ~123: "To see what is new here you have to see what came before it, and there are two things."** Meta-narration of the chapter's own structure ("there are two things"). Suggest open straight into the content: *"Two things came before this picture and set it off. The first is the kind of Dada it reacts against. Zurich Dada…"*
- **[FIX] §5 `DauAfterlife`, line ~228: "the creed from the start of this read".** Self-referential pointer to the article itself ("this read"). The callback to the despair/hate/disillusionment line is good; just name the line, not the artifact. Suggest: *"It is Grosz's despair, hate and disillusionment — the creed he drew by — pointed for once not at the generals or the profiteers but at himself…"*
- **[NICE] §2 `DauBreak`, line ~133: "That is the break in one line:"** Borderline meta ("the break" is the section's own framing label). Within the art vertical the "break" block is an established structural convention, so naming it is arguably allowed, but "in one line" narrates the writing. Optional trim: *"Dada stops playing and starts shooting: the man-machine and the scissors become a satirical weapon aimed at the bourgeoisie."*

### Jargon used before inline-defined

### Walls of text
- No walls. Paragraphs are broken by `SectionHeader`s within each section; longest run is §1's Weimar paragraph and it's a single normal-length para. Fine for mobile.

### Literal `—` em-dash in rendered strings / `&mdash;` misuse
- The TS string fields (hook, blurbs, stats, annotations, provenance notes, figures) use literal `—` em-dashes — e.g. `hook`, the section blurbs ("amputees, joins Berlin Dada"), `provenance` notes, `annotations` details ("photomontage when the scraps are press photographs)"). **These are plain TS string fields, and per the voice contract a literal `—` in a rendered string is flagged.** Listing the surfaces so the reconciler can sweep them:
  - **[FIX] PART A string fields with literal em-dash:** `hook` (none — uses commas, OK); `sections[].blurb` — *break* blurb "scissors and the man-machine" (OK, comma); *looking* blurb "Two opposed techniques on one small sheet:" (colon, OK). Re-scanning: the **blurbs actually avoid `—`** (good). The literal em-dashes live in: `provenance[3].note` ("the Berlin state museum of modern art, photography and architecture, in 1995" — commas, OK). On close read, **PART A largely uses commas/colons, not literal em-dashes.** The clear literal-`—` hits are in `annotations`: annotations[3] "rationalised, emotion-dead modern male (he built…" (parens, OK). **Net: I did not find a literal `—` in a plain TS string field in PART A.** The JSX prose (PART B) correctly uses `&ndash;`/`&mdash;`/`&rsquo;` entities throughout. **No FIX needed here** — downgrading to **[NICE]: confirm during integration that no `—` literal slipped into a const string;** my read says clean.
  - `&mdash;` misuse: none found. PART B uses `&ndash;` for the 1893&ndash;1959 date range (correct — en-dash for ranges) and `&hellip;` for the quote ellipsis (correct). No `&mdash;` inside a plain string field.

### Other clarity
