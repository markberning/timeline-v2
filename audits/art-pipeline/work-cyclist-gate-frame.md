## LENS 1 — COMPREHENSIVENESS (what's missing)

The brief flagged four candidate gaps. All four are **already covered**:

1. **Late-1913 push toward near-abstraction** — COVERED. The whole `CycAfterlife`
   opening ("One short step from abstraction") and `CycMilan`'s "The year the subject
   almost vanished" make this the spine of the read. The draft correctly frames the
   work as *one step short of* abstraction that Boccioni declined to take — which is
   both more accurate and more interesting than "near-abstract" left flat.

2. **"Plastic dynamism"** — COVERED, and well. `CycMaking`'s third section ("The theory
   underneath") defines *dinamismo plastico* correctly (the object + the space it tears
   through as one continuous whole), glosses "plastic" in the right art-school sense
   (shaping solid form, not the material), and ties it to Bergson. Annotation #6 ("No
   fixed contour") makes it visible on the canvas. Note: live Wikipedia does **not**
   itself connect "plastic dynamism" to this canvas — the draft's synthesis goes
   slightly beyond the single-page source but stays inside the documented Futurist
   theory, so it is sound, not invented.

3. **Relation to the 1913 sculpture (*Unique Forms of Continuity in Space*)** — COVERED
   in two places (`CycBoccioni` and `CycAfterlife`), correctly dated 1913 and correctly
   framed as the three-dimensional twin of the same idea. Web-confirmed: the sculpture
   is 1913 and expresses the same Futurist motion-into-solid program.

4. **The Mattioli / Museo del Novecento story** — COVERED, and handled with exactly the
   right hedge. Web-check: live EN-Wikipedia now states the present location as **Museo
   del Novecento, Milan (long-term loan from the Gianni Mattioli Collection)**, with the
   old Peggy Guggenheim line flagged as outdated — which is precisely how the draft
   plays it (owner = Mattioli; Venice loan = historical 1997–c.2016; current wall hedged
   to Museo del Novecento "though no single authoritative museum page nails it down").
   No present-tense overclaim. This is correct.

**Nothing material is missing.** One optional enrichment below (#NICE-1).

## LENS 2 — FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### (a) Futurism's glorification of speed/machine/violence + the Fascism tie — PRESENT

Required honest-context line is present in TWO places, attributed and proportionate:

- `CycBoccioni`: *"like Marinetti, who had cheerfully called war 'the world's only
  hygiene'"* — web-confirmed verbatim wording from the 1909 Manifesto ("We will glorify
  war—the world's only hygiene…").
- `CycAfterlife`: *"Whatever else Futurism got wrong (and it got plenty wrong, not
  least its founder's love affair with war and, later, with Fascism)…"* — web-confirmed:
  Marinetti co-wrote the 1919 Fascist Manifesto and backed Mussolini.

This satisfies the framing requirement cleanly: the line is honest, it is one sentence
(not a tribunal), and it does not contaminate the looking-experience chapters. Good.

### (b) Genius-myth inflation — NONE FOUND / actively resisted

The draft repeatedly *deflates* the myth rather than inflating it:
- "It is worth being precise about who did what, because popular books get it wrong:
  **Marinetti founded Futurism. He was a poet, not a painter.**" (the correction is the
  whole point of `CycMilan`).
- `CycBoccioni` repeats the correction: "Boccioni did **not** found Futurism."
- "best painter" / "chief theorist" claims are bounded and sourced (he was the movement's
  leading visual artist — documented), never universalized to "greatest painter of the
  century."
- The death is told plainly (thrown by a horse, training accident, age 33) without
  martyr-mythologizing; the draft even pre-empts over-reading: "the picture does not
  need the biography to work."

No fix needed.

### (c) No "first abstract painting"-style overclaim — CLEAN, this is the standout

The draft is careful exactly where it would be easy to slip. It says the work is "one
short step **away from**" abstraction and that **Boccioni refused to take that step** —
never that it *is* abstract, never "first," never "pioneer of abstraction."

Web-check strongly validates this caution: abstraction had genuinely simultaneous
origins c.1910–1912 (Kandinsky, Kupka, Delaunay, plus Mondrian/Malevich as contenders),
the "first abstract painting" prize is openly contested, and — pointedly — several
artists *backdated* works to claim priority. So any "first" claim here would be both
wrong and a known historiographic trap. The draft sidesteps it entirely. Excellent.

One thing to note (not a defect): the draft never *names* the Kandinsky/Kupka/Delaunay
alternatives. It doesn't need to (this is a single-work read, not an abstraction survey),
and staying silent is safer than a half-claim. See optional #NICE-2 if a one-clause
nod is wanted.

## [NICE] — optional, non-blocking

**[NICE-1] Comprehensiveness — name the "dynamism series" significance once more concretely.**
`CycMilan` lists the sibling 1913 canvases (Human Body, Soccer Player, Plastic Dynamism:
Horse + Houses). That's good. A single added clause noting these were a *concentrated
burst in one year* (the late-1913 push) would sharpen the "near-abstraction" arc the
brief asked about. Already implied — purely additive.
- Current: *"He is in the middle of a run of canvases he calls his 'dynamism' pictures…"*
- Optional add: *"…a concentrated burst across 1913 in which the readable subject thins
  out canvas by canvas."*

**[NICE-2] Framing — optional one-clause nod to abstraction's multiple origins.**
If the coordinator wants to pre-empt a reader inferring "so this nearly invented
abstraction," a single hedged clause in `CycAfterlife` would close the door explicitly.
Strictly optional — the draft's silence is already safe.
- Possible insert (after "This is abstraction … and the *Cyclist* is one short step away
  from it."): *"— a frontier several painters were crossing at almost the same moment,
  among them Kandinsky, Kupka, and Delaunay."*
- If used, it is a NEW factual surface and must be fact-pack-backed; the web-verified
  wording above is safe (all three exhibited abstraction 1911–1912).

**[NICE-3] Framing precision — "best painter" → consider softening to a sourced phrasing.**
The const `sections` and chapter titles use "the movement's best painter." It is
defensible (and the fact pack supports "leading visual artist / chief theorist"), but
"best" is a superlative judgment where "leading" / "foremost" is the sourced, neutral
register. Tiny lean toward house-verdict over documented fact; not a misstatement.
- e.g. "The movement's best painter, dead at 33" → "The movement's foremost painter,
  dead at 33" (optional).
