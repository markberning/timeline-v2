## Findings

### [FIX-1] Provenance omits Paul Guillaume — "made its way through the European art trade" is vaguer than the record
**Where:** PART A `provenance` (the c.1950 Rockefeller entry) + PART B `SngAfterlife` para 1
("the small panel made its way through the European art trade toward America") and para 2
("from the dealer Marcel Raval, in October of that year").

**Issue:** MoMA's documented provenance chain is more specific than the draft implies, and the draft
slightly mis-frames Raval. The verified chain: de Chirico (Paris, 1914 – at least mid-1920s) →
**Paul Guillaume, Paris** (the dealer de Chirico signed with via Apollinaire in 1914) → by 1928
**Marcel Raval, Paris** → **Rockefeller** (purchased from Raval, October 1950) → MoMA (bequest, 1979).
The draft's "from the dealer Marcel Raval, in October 1950" is correct as to the immediate seller, but
the picture passing through **Paul Guillaume** first is a documented, citable link the draft drops in favor
of a vague "European art trade."

**Severity:** FIX (not a blocker — nothing stated is *false*; an established provenance link is merely omitted).

**Suggested correction** (provenance note, c.1950 entry):
> Bought by the American collector and politician Nelson A. Rockefeller from the Paris dealer Marcel Raval
> in October 1950. (The picture had passed from de Chirico through the dealer Paul Guillaume, then to Raval
> by 1928.) No purchase figure is documented in the sources, so it is left blank rather than invented.

Optionally soften the PART B "made its way through the European art trade" line by naming Paul Guillaume.
Leaving the prose vague is acceptable; the const note is the better place to add the link.

### [FIX-2] The enigma motto is itself a verbatim Nietzsche quotation — the draft under-claims it as merely Nietzsche-"influenced"
**Where:** PART B `SngMetaphysical` para 3: the motto is quoted, then a separate sentence says the
mood "owes a great deal to" Nietzsche, "whose vision … saturates de Chirico's early work."

**Issue:** The Latin epigraph "Et quid amabo nisi quod aenigma est?" is **not de Chirico's own coinage** —
it is a quotation de Chirico took from Nietzsche, and the 1911 self-portrait deliberately poses him in the
attitude of a known Nietzsche portrait. The factpack's §4B treats the Nietzsche link as "interpretive,"
which under-states it: the motto-as-Nietzsche-quotation is well-attested, not a soft reading. The draft
isn't *wrong* (it correctly ties the mood to Nietzsche) but presents the motto as de Chirico's inscription
without noting he was quoting Nietzsche — a missed, verifiable attribution.

**Severity:** FIX (accuracy completeness; nothing false, but a verifiable source-attribution is dropped).

**Suggested correction** (optional, light touch — append to the parenthetical):
> … "And what shall I love if not the enigma?" — a line de Chirico borrowed from Nietzsche, the German
> philosopher whose vision of empty, ominous afternoons saturates his early work. That line belongs to the
> self-portrait, not to the painting we are about to look at …

This folds the existing Nietzsche sentence into the motto and fixes the attribution in one move.

### [NICE-2] "two feet five inches tall" rounding (SngLooking)
PART B para 1 rounds 73 cm to "about two feet five inches tall." 73 cm = 28.74 in = 2 ft 4.74 in, i.e.
just under 2 ft 5 in — the "about two feet five inches" is honest as a round-up, and the const carries the
exact "2 ft 4¾ in." Consistent. No change required; flagged only so the two figures aren't read as a conflict.

### [NICE-3] Birthplace, father, Apollinaire-champion all verified
