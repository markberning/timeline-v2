## 2. THE HOOK (one line, for the lead)

A reclining nude that Matisse painted only because the **clay sculpture of her shattered in his
studio** — then sent to the 1907 Salon so ugly-on-purpose that **Chicago art students burned
copies of it and put the painter on trial** six years later. [DOCUMENTED — sculpture accident +
Armory burning, both sourced below.]

(Alt, tighter: "A nude Matisse painted because the sculpture broke — and that a Chicago crowd
later burned in reproduction.")

## 3. NARRATIVE BEATS (~5, in order)

### Beat 1 — Biskra, 1906: the trip behind the "souvenir" [DOCUMENTED]
- In **spring 1906** Matisse spent **about two weeks in Algeria**, including the desert oasis town
  of **Biskra**. [DOCUMENTED — COTE Magazine / Musée Matisse Nice exhibition; Riviera-Buzz.]
- Biskra was a French-colonial picture of "a fertile oasis in a vast desert" sent back to the Paris
  press after French troops took the region (1844). Local weavers used **indigo dyes that stained
  their arms and legs blue** — a detail some accounts tie to the painting's blue body, though the
  title's "souvenir" is the surer link. [DOCUMENTED context — Wikipedia; treat the indigo→blue-body
  causation as **[DISPUTED]**, frame as "some read."]
- The trip is the reason the title says *Souvenir* — it is a **memory/recollection of Biskra**, not
  a scene painted on the spot. Matisse painted it back in France in early 1907. [DOCUMENTED — title
  + chronology.]
- Useful related works from the same trip/year: *Street at Biskra* (1906) and the bathing-idyll
  *Le Luxe I* (1907). [DOCUMENTED — Artchive; SMK/Statens.]

### Beat 2 — the sculpture that broke and became a painting [DOCUMENTED]
- Matisse was modeling a small reclining figure in clay. **The fragile sculpture fell/fractured in
  the studio.** Rather than immediately repairing it, he **turned to oil to work out the same
  composition** — *Blue Nude* is that painting. [DOCUMENTED — Wikipedia ("created this work when a
  sculpture he was developing fractured"); COTE / exhibition accounts.]
- He **later finished the sculpture**, titled **_Reclining Nude I (Aurore)_ / _Nu couché I
  (Aurore)_, 1906–07** — bronze; the painting and the bronze share the same twisted S-curve pose.
  [DOCUMENTED — Wikipedia; artnet (Reclining Nude I, 1906–07).]
- So the canvas is, in effect, **a sculpture problem solved in paint** — the body modeled with
  blue shadow the way you'd model clay with your thumb. Good comparison hook for the "looking" axis.
  [Interpretive framing, grounded in the documented sequence.]
- ⚠️ The "fell and shattered" detail is widely repeated but the precise mechanism (dropped? a stand
  collapsed?) varies by source → **[DOCUMENTED that the sculpture broke/fractured; mechanism
  DISPUTED]**. Frame as "the clay broke," not a cinematic crash.

### Beat 3 — late Fauvism toward the body and "primitivism" [DOCUMENTED, framing-sensitive]
- By 1907 Matisse was pushing past the pure-color landscapes of high Fauvism (1905) toward the
  **monumental, distorted nude** — heavier contour, sculptural mass, "non-naturalistic" color used
  to model a figure rather than just to glow. [DOCUMENTED — standard Matisse periodization;
  Wikipedia interpretation section.]
- The body is deliberately **exaggerated and "primitivized"** — broad, muscular, ungainly by
  academic standards. This is the moment African and non-European sculpture is entering the Paris
  avant-garde (Matisse, Picasso, Derain, Vlaminck all collecting/looking). **Name the sources
  honestly, do not anonymize** (framing gate — colonial-appropriation axis). [DOCUMENTED context;
  art-content-pipeline framing rule.]
- Scholarly readings split and should be presented as **interpretation, not fact**: some argue
  Matisse **conflates colonial conquest with sexual availability** (the odalisque-in-the-colony);
  some argue he **critiques** Western ideals via primitivism; some say he **reinforces** them.
  [DOCUMENTED that these readings exist — Wikipedia interpretation section. Tag the readings
  **[DISPUTED]** / present multiply.]

### Beat 4 — the 1907 Salon des Indépendants scandal [DOCUMENTED]
- Shown at the **Salon des Indépendants** (Société des Artistes Indépendants), **Paris, spring 1907**
  (≈ March). [DOCUMENTED — Wikipedia; multiple.]
- It **shocked the public and critics** — read as gratuitously ugly, "savage," distorted,
  unfinished. [DOCUMENTED — Wikipedia; BMA's own social copy: "the public thought … *Blue Nude*
  was gratuitously ugly."]
- Critic quote — **Louis Vauxcelles** (the same critic who'd coined "Fauves" in 1905 and would
  coin "Cubism"): he described it as **"a nude woman, ugly, stretched out on opaque blue grass
  under palm trees."** [DOCUMENTED quote, widely attributed to Vauxcelles at the 1907 Salon — but
  ⚠️ **verify the exact wording + venue** at the fact-check gate; English wording varies by
  translation ("spread out"/"stretched out"). Treat as **[DOCUMENTED-but-verify-wording]**; if it
  can't be confirmed letter-for-letter, **paraphrase, don't quote** (the Impressionism-run Duranty
  rule).]

### Beat 5 — the 1913 Armory Show: burned in Chicago [DOCUMENTED — the headline anecdote]
- The painting was in the **1913 International Exhibition of Modern Art ("the Armory Show")**:
  opened **New York, 69th Regiment Armory, Feb 17, 1913**, then toured to the **Art Institute of
  Chicago**. [DOCUMENTED — Armory Show history; archive.artic.edu.]
- In NY it was lent from the **Stein collection (Leo & Gertrude Stein, Paris)** — it had not yet
  been sold to John Quinn (that sale is 1920). [DOCUMENTED — artnet: "on loan from the Paris
  collection of Leo Stein and his sister Gertrude." Resolves the apparent Quinn-vs-Stein conflict:
  Stein-owned in 1913; Quinn from 1920.]
- **In Chicago, on / around the show's last day — April 16, 1913 — Art Institute students:**
  - **burned COPIES (reproductions) of three Matisse works** — among them ***Blue Nude***, plus
    (per artnet) *Le Luxe II* and *Goldfish and Sculpture (Goldfish and Sculpture)*; and
  - **staged a mock trial** of the painter, nicknamed **"Henri Hairmattress" / "Henry Hair
    Mattress,"** charging him with **"artistic murder, pictorial arson, artistic rapine, total
    degeneracy of color, criminal misuse of line, general esthetic aberration, and contumacious
    abuse of title."** A student "jury" condemned him to death; a "chaplain" intoned **"We regret
    that you have only one life to give for your principles."** [DOCUMENTED — artnet "Art Bites";
    archive.artic.edu "The Finale"; Chicago Magazine.]
- **CRITICAL DISTINCTION (verify carefully, this is the trap):**
  - **What is documented:** they **did burn REPRODUCTIONS / copies** of the paintings (cheap
    photographic copies, not the originals — the originals were the actual exhibited canvases and
    were not destroyed). [DOCUMENTED.]
  - **What is hedged:** the plan to **burn an EFFIGY of Matisse himself** — *some local news
    reports say authorities/police stopped them before the effigy was lit.* So "burned in effigy"
    is **[DOCUMENTED for the copies of the paintings; DISPUTED/STOPPED for an effigy of the man]**.
    The user's brief said "a copy was burned in effigy in Chicago" — the **accurate version is:
    copies of the paintings were burned; the burning of the artist *in effigy* was attempted and
    reportedly halted.** Author must use the precise version, not "the painting was burned."
    [DOCUMENTED nuance — artnet; the Smart Set; bigapplesecrets.]
  - The **original canvas was never harmed** — it later entered the Cone Collection and survives at
    the BMA. [DOCUMENTED — provenance below.]

### Context beat — the Picasso rivalry, toward the *Demoiselles* [DOCUMENTED, frame carefully]
- *Blue Nude* and *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon* are **the same year (1907)** and the two are bound
  in the Matisse–Picasso rivalry, both pushing the nude through primitivist distortion. **Both
  Braque and Picasso saw / absorbed _Blue Nude_;** it is commonly said to have **partly spurred
  Picasso's _Demoiselles_.** [DOCUMENTED — Wikipedia: the 1907 painting "had a great influence
  on … Braque and Picasso," "partially motivating Picasso's _Les Demoiselles d'Avignon_."]
- ⚠️ Don't overclaim direct causation/chronology (the *Demoiselles* was largely worked out by
  mid-1907 too). Frame as **rivalry + mutual pressure**, "fed into," not "caused." The Steins
  bought BOTH men and hung them in the same rooms — the rivalry was literally on one wall.
  [DOCUMENTED — Stein collecting; SFMOMA "The Steins Collect."]

## 4. "LOOK CLOSER" — 6 ANNOTATIONS (prose pointers, no crops)

Each is `{label, where, detail}`; `where` is a written location phrase. **Verify every one against
the actual painting image at the fact-check gate** (counts, left/right, what's where). The figure
reclines along a horizontal canvas; head to the **upper left**, hip rising at center, palm/foliage
behind.

1. **The twisted, impossible pose** — *where:* "The whole reclining figure, from the head at upper
   left to the legs at right." *detail:* She lies on her side but her **torso wrenches toward us
   while the hips stay turned away** — the same S-curve "contrapposto" twist Matisse was modeling in
   clay, so we read **front and back of the body at once**. One arm bends up behind the head; it is
   a sculptor's pose, not a life-class one. [DOCUMENTED — pose described across sources; ties to
   *Reclining Nude I*.]

2. **Blue shadow doing the modeling** — *where:* "Along the underside of the torso, the far breast,
   the crook of the bent arm, the hollows of the body." *detail:* The "blue" of the title is not the
   skin — it's the **shadow.** Matisse models the body's volume with **passages of blue** the way a
   sculptor pushes shadow into clay, so a flat painting reads as something carved. **The same blue
   bleeds into the grass**, tying figure and ground together. [DOCUMENTED — analysis sources; the
   blue is shadow/modeling, not body color.]

3. **The palm/oasis setting** — *where:* "Upper right and behind the figure — the fronded plant and
   the band of 'grass.'" *detail:* A **palm or palmette** and a strip of vegetation set her in a
   stylized oasis — the **"Biskra" of the title**, remembered, not observed. The ground is the
   "**opaque blue grass**" Vauxcelles sneered at. [DOCUMENTED — Vauxcelles' "blue grass … under
   palm trees"; title.]

4. **The exaggerated hip and heavy contour** — *where:* "Center of the canvas, the great upward
   curve of the hip and buttock; the dark outline around the whole body." *detail:* The **hip swells
   to the highest point of the composition**, and a **thick, deliberate dark contour** rings the
   figure — academic "finish" is gone. The curve of the buttock is **rhymed** with the hollow under
   the palm fronds: body and plant echo each other. [DOCUMENTED — analysis: recurrence of shape,
   buttock-curve rhymes the foliage.]

5. **The African / "primitivist" sculptural source** — *where:* "The whole body's mass — the broad
   shoulders, the blunt, simplified features, the weightiness." *detail:* The body is **bulked and
   simplified** in a way that looks to non-European sculpture Matisse and his circle were collecting
   in 1906–07 — mass over grace, a carved idol more than a Salon nude. **Name the sources honestly;
   don't anonymize** (framing rule), and present the "primitivism" reading as the **contested**
   thing it is. [DOCUMENTED context; framing-sensitive.]

6. **The aggressive, visible brushwork / color recurrence** — *where:* "Everywhere — the skin, the
   plants, the lips and nipples." *detail:* The paint is **laid on visibly and roughly**, and colors
   **recur across body and setting**: **red in both the palm stem and the figure's lips and
   nipples, pink in both the figure and the palm's base, blue in both the body's shadows and the
   grass.** Nothing pretends to be a window onto nature — it's a built surface of repeated color.
   [DOCUMENTED — analysis: shared red/pink/blue across figure and plants.]

## 6. KEY FIGURES (nameplates)

- **Henri Matisse** (1869–1954) — the painter; leader of the Fauves; the rival/counterweight to
  Picasso across the 20th century. [DOCUMENTED.]
- **Leo Stein** (1872–1947) & **Gertrude Stein** (1874–1946) — American expatriate collectors in
  Paris; among the very first to buy Matisse seriously; first owners of *Blue Nude* (1907–20),
  its lenders to the 1913 Armory Show. Gertrude is the writer; Leo the eye who bought the early
  Matisses and Picassos. **Give them agency — not passive props** (framing rule). [DOCUMENTED —
  SFMOMA "The Steins Collect."]
- **John Quinn** (1870–1924) — New York lawyer, the Armory Show's most important collector-backer;
  owned *Blue Nude* 1920–24. [DOCUMENTED.]
- **Dr. Claribel Cone** (1864–1929) & **Miss Etta Cone** (1870–1949) — Baltimore sisters; Claribel
  a Johns Hopkins–trained pathologist. Friends of Gertrude Stein from Baltimore/Hopkins days; began
  buying Matisse after the Steins introduced them post-1905 Salon d'Automne. **Claribel bought
  _Blue Nude_ at the 1926 Quinn sale;** the whole collection went to the BMA on Etta's death. The
  BMA holds **one of the world's largest Matisse collections** because of them. [DOCUMENTED — BMA;
  Baltimore Magazine; Met Cone index.]
- **Pablo Picasso** (1881–1973) — context only: the 1907 rival whose *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*
  was pushed by the same primitivist-nude pressure; the Steins hung both men together. [DOCUMENTED
  context.]
- **Louis Vauxcelles** — critic; the "ugly … blue grass … palm trees" line at the 1907 Salon
  (verify wording). [DOCUMENTED-but-verify.]
