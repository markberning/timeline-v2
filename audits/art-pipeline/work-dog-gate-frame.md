## LENS 1 — COMPREHENSIVENESS (what's missing)

### [BLOCKER] C-1 — The "Contessa Nerazzini" hedge is factually backwards: the museum DOES state it.
The draft (DogMaking, para 2) hedges:

> "(You will often see the owner named as a specific countess, 'the Contessa Nerazzini,' and the dog described as hers. By one often-repeated account that is who she was, but **it is not on the museum's own page** and is not tied to a primary source we can stand behind, so treat the name as a good story rather than a settled fact...)"

The const comment (lines 26–27) and the fact-ledger row (draft line 248: "not on the museum's own page") repeat the same claim, and the fact pack Beat 4 / §7 assert it too ("**not on the museum's own page**").

**This is wrong, and it is web-verifiable against the primary source the hedge cites.** The Buffalo AKG collection page curatorial text reads:

> "Balla painted this amusing study of a skittering dachshund and the staccato steps of his or her owner in May 1912 while visiting one of his students, **the Contessa Nerazzini**."

So the museum DOES name the Contessa — but as **the student/host Balla was visiting**, NOT as the dog's owner. The real, defensible distinction is:
- **Documented (museum):** the student Balla was visiting was the Contessa Nerazzini.
- **NOT documented (the actual legend):** that the dachshund or the lady-in-black-walking-it *is* the Contessa / is hers. Wikipedia and Britannica do not identify whose dog it is.

The draft conflates "the museum doesn't name the host" (false) with "no source ties the *dog* to her" (true). It blocks because (a) it asserts a checkable falsehood about a cited source, and (b) it is exactly the kind of provenance/attribution claim this gate exists to catch.

**Fix — rewrite the parenthetical in DogMaking para 2 to:**

```
(The museum names the student Balla was staying with: the Contessa Nerazzini,
in whose Tuscan house he was a guest. You will often see the leap made from there
to "the dog is the Contessa's" and "the lady in black is the Contessa" — but that
identification of the dog and the walker is not something the museum, or Wikipedia,
actually states. What is documented is the host's name, the month, the black dress,
the dachshund, and the leash; whose dog it specifically was is the open part.)
```

Also fix the const comment (lines 26–27) and the fact-ledger row (line 248) to match: the museum DOES name the host Contessa; the unverified leap is the *dog's ownership*, not the name's existence. (This same error originates in the fact pack — flag back to whoever owns the pack so the next draft doesn't reinherit it.)
*Web source: Buffalo AKG collection page, buffaloakg.org — curatorial text quoted above.*

### [FIX] C-2 — The chronophotography science is present but under-credited as THE real precedent.
The prompt asks whether the Marey/Muybridge chronophotography is treated as "the real precedent." DogScience handles this well and the imagery ("a chronophotograph turned into oil paint") is excellent — but the draft repeatedly soft-pedals it to a "parallel" (correct re: a *copied plate*) in a way that slightly undersells how directly the field-standard sources tie the *device* to chronophotography. Wikipedia states flatly: "Chronophotographic studies of animals in motion, created by scientist Étienne-Jules Marey beginning in the 1880s, **led to the introduction in painting of techniques to show motion**." Britannica/Art-history-project: the painting "was clearly influenced by the chronophotography of Eadweard Muybridge... and Étienne-Jules Marey."

The draft's careful "parallel, not a copied plate" framing is RIGHT and should stay. But it currently risks reading as "we can't really connect these" when the connection at the level of *technique-genealogy* is in fact the consensus. The lineage block already lists "Chronophotography (Marey · Muybridge)" as a **parent**, which is the correct strength — the prose should match the lineage block's confidence.

**Fix — in DogScience para 4, add one sentence after "...rather than as a plate he traced." e.g.:**

```
What is not in doubt is the genealogy: chronophotography is the documented source
of the whole motion-painting toolkit the Futurists used — blur, multiplication,
overlapping phases. Balla didn't need a particular plate; he inherited the idea
the plates had already made visible.
```
This keeps the "no specific copied plate" honesty while crediting chronophotography as the real precedent, matching the lineage block.

### [FIX] C-3 — Balla's specific PLACE in Futurism (vs the others) is thin.
The draft establishes Balla as teacher-turned-signer (good, well-covered) but doesn't situate him *within* the movement's painters: that he became the Futurist most associated with **pure abstraction of motion/light** (the "Iridescent Interpenetrations," *Abstract Speed* series), i.e. he pushed furthest toward non-objective painting of the five — which is exactly why a dog-on-a-leash is a slightly atypical, figurative, early work for *him*. The afterlife/lineage gestures at "Abstraction of motion" as a child, but the reader never learns Balla himself is the one who took it there. One line would let the reader place this charming, legible picture as an early, representational moment from the painter who would go furthest into abstraction.
*Web source: Britannica/Artsy — "Balla's abstract compositions... answer Marinetti's call to depict speed and the direct sensation of dynamism."*

**Fix — add to DogTeacher (end) or DogAfterlife a sentence such as:** "Of the five painter-signers, Balla would push furthest toward pure abstraction — later canvases that drop the object entirely and paint only speed and light — which makes this very legible little dog an early, still-figurative moment from the most abstract of them."

### [NICE] C-4 — The "playful vs militant" split is named but not generalized.
The prompt asks about "the playful vs militant side of the movement." The draft nails the *tension specific to this picture* (the war-glorifying movement made a cute dog), which is the most important version. A NICE addition: Futurism had a genuine **playful/ludic streak** as a movement trait — *parole in libertà*, the provocative *serate* (rowdy performance evenings), Balla's own later toy/design/clothing work — not just war-worship. One clause noting the movement had a deliberately impish, prank-loving side would make the dog feel less like a lone anomaly and more like one legitimate pole of a movement that ran from playful to militant. Optional; the current per-painting framing already carries the load.

## LENS 2 — FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### (a) Overall-movement context (Futurism = speed/machine/violence; Marinetti → Fascism) — PASS, strong.
This is the gate's primary fairness requirement and the draft over-delivers in the right way. DogAfterlife para 3 gives the honest line and more:

> "Futurism was not a gentle movement. Its manifestos didn't just praise the racing car; they glorified violence, the machine gun, contempt for the past, and war, which Marinetti called 'the world's only hygiene.' Several of the Futurists, Marinetti among them, would later attach themselves to Italian Fascism... The picture is not innocent of its movement."

Web-verified accurate: Marinetti's 1909 founding manifesto does glorify "war — the world's only hygiene — militarism, patriotism..."; Marinetti founded the Futurist Political Party (1918), which merged into Mussolini's *Fasci*, and he co-wrote the 1919 Fascist Manifesto. The draft's "several of the Futurists... would later attach themselves to Fascism" is appropriately hedged (the whole movement's relationship to Fascism was uneven; some Futurists were anti-Fascist), and it does not overstate Balla's personal politics, which is the correct restraint. **No fix needed. This is the model for the requirement.**

One **[NICE]** precision-only note: the quote "war — the world's only hygiene" is from the **1909 founding *Manifesto of Futurism* (Marinetti)**, not the 1910 *Technical Manifesto of Futurist Painting* (which the draft quotes for the "twenty legs" line). The draft attributes it loosely to "its manifestos" / "Marinetti called," which is fine and not wrong — but if a curious reader cross-references, anchoring "the world's only hygiene" to the 1909 founding manifesto specifically would be tighter. Optional.

### (b) Genius-myth — PASS.
No lone-genius inflation. The draft actively de-mythologizes (Balla as the *teacher* who joined his students; technique inherited from Divisionism; idea inherited from the manifesto and from chronophotography; "he didn't need a particular plate, he inherited the idea"). The wit is credited to a method ("multiply the moving, hold the still"), not to genius. Good.

### (c) First / only overclaim — PASS, with one watch-item.
No "first painting of motion" or "only Futurist X" claims — the draft correctly routes precedent to chronophotography and Divisionism. The superlatives present are reception claims, properly hedged:
- const `hook` + blurb: "Futurism's most beloved, and most unexpectedly funny, picture" — this is a **popularity/affection** claim, not a primacy claim, and it is well-supported (Washington Post 2021 etc. treat it as the Futurist work the public most loves). Acceptable as written.
- DogAfterlife: "by a wide margin, the Futurist painting that ordinary people actually love" — same; popularity, hedged, fine.

**[NICE] watch-item:** the const-comment phrase "**The flagship Futurism work read**" (line 18) is internal/pipeline scaffolding, not reader-facing, so it's harmless — but if any of that comment ever surfaces, "flagship" edges toward an importance overclaim (art-historically *Unique Forms of Continuity in Space* / Boccioni is at least as canonical). Keep "flagship" out of reader prose. No action needed for ship since it's a code comment.
