## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### [FIX] — Harvey grievance is OVERSTATED; unfair to Harvey's actual stance
This is the one fairness item that matters. The draft (making §2) says:

> "...Harvey noticed. His gallery put out a wry statement on his behalf, the gist of which was that Andy was running away with his box."

That part is fair and accurate. But the surrounding framing tilts toward Harvey-as-aggrieved-loser in a way the record does not support:
- The brief's own concern is "the authorship irony handled **fairly to Harvey**." The verified record (Print Magazine, via Boing Boing; Painter's Keys) is that when Harvey learned of Warhol's appropriation he **largely laughed it off** — he reportedly found it funny. The famous "Andy is running away with my box" line was the wording of a **press release issued by the Graham Gallery on behalf of Stuart and Gunn**, framing Harvey screaming — i.e., it is gallery/PR theater, not a documented personal complaint. The draft's "Harvey noticed... It is the kind of authorship tangle..." reads as if Harvey personally resented it.
- Compounding this, Harvey **died in July 1965** of cancer, barely a year after the show — quietly, not as a man nursing an art-world grudge. Leaning into the grievance frame is the unfair note.

*Suggested replacement (making §2, the sentence beginning "So when a Pop artist got the gallery show..."):*

> "So when a Pop artist got the gallery show, the credit, and the money for reproducing Harvey's box, the art world noticed the irony. Harvey himself reportedly took it with good humor; the famous protest — 'Andy is running away with my box' — came from a tongue-in-cheek press release his gallery put out, not from Harvey nursing a grudge. (He died of cancer the next year, in 1965, at thirty-five.) But the tangle the release pointed at is exactly what the whole work is secretly about: who made this image, and which version of it counts as art — the painter's design for a soap company, or the soap company's carton remade by a painter?"

(Rationale: keeps the irony and the quote, attributes the "scream" to the PR release where it belongs, restores Harvey's actual good-humored stance, and adds the early-death fact so the reader doesn't carry a false picture of a bitter rival. Verified: Wikipedia *James Harvey (artist)*; ARTnews "The Brillo-Box Scandal"; Print Magazine "Shadow Boxer.")

### [FIX] — Harvey carton design year: "1959 to 1961" overstates the spread
The draft (making §2, and the const comment) hedges the design year as "around 1960... sources giving anywhere from **1959 to 1961**." The fact pack flags 1959 vs 1961. Web verification resolves this: **1959 is the year the design firm Stuart and Gunn was founded** (after the team left Egmont Arens), and **1961 is the year Harvey actually completed the Brillo design** for them. So 1959 is not a competing *design* date — the dominant, specific sourcing is **1961**. The "1959–1961" range slightly misrepresents an unsettled-design-date when really the design date is fairly well attested as 1961.

*Suggested fix (making §2 parenthetical):*
> "He drew the Brillo carton in **1961** (he had joined the design firm Stuart and Gunn, formed in 1959; some accounts round the design to 'around 1960')."

*And the const comment* (line ~22): change "Carton design year unsettled → 'around 1960.'" → "Carton design year best attested as 1961 (firm Stuart and Gunn formed 1959); 'around 1960' acceptable as a round figure."
(This is a [FIX] not a [NICE] because the current text presents a false equivalence between two dates that are not equivalent — one is the design, one is the firm's founding.)

### [FIX] — minor first/only-style overclaim in the break section
The break section calls the move "a **founding** move of Conceptual art" and afterlife says "the literal object on which a philosopher built a theory." "Founding move of Conceptual art" is defensible *as influence* but flirts with sole-origin overclaim (Conceptual art has multiple roots — Duchamp, LeWitt, Kosuth, Fluxus). The draft mostly guards this by saying "a founding move" (indefinite article) — good — but the afterlife line "Through that door came Conceptual art, the institutional... theories" can read as monocausal.

*Suggested softening (afterlife §4):* "Through that door came much of **Conceptual art**, the institutional and contextual theories of art, and a whole later practice of **appropriation**..." (insert "much of" to signal contribution, not sole parentage). Low-severity; bordering on [NICE].

Malanga and Billy Name are credited as the actual labor, which actively *counters* the lone-genius myth. No genius-myth problem. No change.
