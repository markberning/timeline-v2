### [FIX] — The harbor is painted as scenery; the real working fishing town is erased.
This is both a comprehensiveness gap AND the Lens-2 romanticizing point (see Lens 2), so it is logged once here. The draft's Collioure is "a working port of red-tiled houses, fishing boats, and a light that is harder and brighter than anything in Paris" — picturesque set-dressing. Web-verified the actual town: in 1905 Collioure was a substantial anchovy/sardine fishing economy with a flotilla of more than a hundred boats and salting stations dotted through the town; it had been a major anchovy/sardine port since the Middle Ages (collioure-locals-guide.com; courtgallery.com citing Hilary Spurling / John Russell). The boats in the painting that the draft calls "the one clearly readable real subject" and "a working harbor" ARE that fishing fleet. The draft gestures at "a working port" but never lands that the boats Matisse painted were a living anchovy fishery, not generic Mediterranean charm.

- **Where:** `WinCollioure`, ch. 1, opening paragraph ("a working port of red-tiled houses, fishing boats, and a light…").
- **Exact text now:** "a working port of red-tiled houses, fishing boats, and a light that is harder and brighter than anything in Paris."
- **Fix:** ground the port in its actual trade, e.g. "a working anchovy-and-sardine port — a fishing town with a fleet of more than a hundred boats, its harbor lined with the salting sheds the catch went into — under a light harder and brighter than anything in Paris." (Or fold a one-clause version into the `looking`/boats annotation so the "real subject" boats are named as the fishing fleet.) This converts the romanticized backdrop into the real economy without adding length.

### [NICE] — Matisse arrived in May, before Derain; the draft compresses this to a vague "summer."
Web (Hilary Spurling, Matisse's authoritative biographer, via courtgallery.com / anglophone-direct.com) has Matisse arriving in **mid-May 1905** (reportedly the first painter to set foot in the town's single hotel), with Derain joining in early July. The draft says only "In the summer of 1905… took his family south" and then "In early July he was joined by… Derain." This isn't wrong, but the May arrival is a documented, vivid, available detail (Matisse alone for ~six weeks before the collaboration) that the draft leaves on the table. Optional enrichment, not a correctness issue.
- **Where:** `WinCollioure`, ch. 1.
- **Fix (optional):** "took his family south… in mid-May" + a clause that Derain joined ~six weeks later in early July. Cite-grade source exists (Spurling).

### [NICE] — No cross-reference to the broader Fauvism movement read / the room-mate work as a built page.
The draft references *Woman with a Hat* in prose but the nesting (lineage) only cross-links abstract concepts. If a Fauvism movement read or a *Woman with a Hat* work read exists in the chain (index 4 of 9), a lineage cross-link would help the reader. ## LENS 2 — FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### Genius-myth inflation — CLEAN, actively well-handled.
The draft repeatedly *de-inflates* rather than inflates:
- "not yet famous" / "thirty-five years old and not yet famous" — no lone-genius haloing.
- Fauvism is framed as "two men working in the same town on the same problem," explicitly NOT "born on a particular Tuesday in a particular doorway." This correctly shares credit with Derain and refuses the single-origin myth.
- The naming is correctly NOT pinned on Matisse or on this canvas ("do not let anyone tell you it 'named Fauvism,' because the room did, and the busts did"; "the painters did not coin the word, a hostile critic did").
No genius-myth [FIX] needed. This is a model of restraint.

### "First / only" overclaim — CLEAN, with one verified soft spot already hedged.

Correctly bounded.

The draft does NOT overclaim primacy for *Open Window* — it calls the two "the room's twin scandals" and "the two Matisse lightning rods." That is fair and accurate. No fix.

### Romanticizing-the-place / erasing the working town — see [FIX] under Lens 1.
This is the one real fairness catch. The draft's Collioure leans pretty ("red-tiled houses… a light harder and brighter than anything in Paris") and treats the harbor as a view rather than a livelihood. The fishing economy (anchovy/sardine fleet of 100+ boats, salting sheds) is documented and is literally what the painting's boats depict. Naming the working town is both more accurate and more interesting; the fix text is given above. Logged as [FIX], not [BLOCKER], because the draft does use the words "working port" and "fishing boats" — it under-develops the town rather than falsifying it.
