### [FIX] 1 — Age inconsistency: "23" (const/stats/afterlife) vs "22" (ObjCafe)

The const stat and ObjAfterlife say Oppenheim was **23** when she made *Object* / when fame hit;
ObjCafe says she was "at twenty-two, one of its youngest ... members." Born **1913**, in **1936**
she turned 23 (b. 6 Oct 1913 → she was **22** for the café exchange and the May 1936 Ratton debut,
and 23 only from October 1936). Sources reflect this: Artnet/NPR call her "22-year-old" at the café;
the "23" framing is the popular shorthand for the year. This is not wrong so much as **internally
inconsistent** — the same draft says both. Reconcile to one of:
- Keep "23" everywhere as the made-it-at-23 shorthand and change ObjCafe's "at twenty-two" → "at
  twenty-two or twenty-three" or simply "barely out of her early twenties"; **or**
- Footnote once that she turned 23 in October 1936 (born 6 Oct 1913), so "22" and "23" both appear in
  the literature for the same year.
Recommended minimal fix: in the hook/stats, "23" is fine as the famous shorthand; just don't also
assert a flat "twenty-two" elsewhere without the reconciling clause. Corrected ObjCafe text:
`"... and was, at twenty-two (she turned twenty-three that autumn), one of its youngest and most
striking members."`

### [FIX] 2 — Café: bracelet admiration attributed to Picasso alone

ObjCafe: "Picasso admired the fur bracelet and joked that you could cover just about anything in fur."
The most-cited sources (Wikipedia *Le Déjeuner en fourrure*; several retellings) phrase it as **Picasso
*and* Dora Maar** remarking on the bracelet, before Oppenheim's riposte to Picasso. NPR/Artnet do center
Picasso for the "anything in fur" line, so attributing the *joke* to Picasso is defensible — but
"Picasso admired the fur bracelet" as a solo act slightly narrows a scene the sources give to both.
Low-stakes (it's already inside an explicitly "the story goes" frame). Optional tightening:
`"Picasso and Maar admired the fur bracelet, and Picasso joked that you could cover just about anything
in fur."` Leave as [FIX]-optional, not blocking.

### [NICE] 1 — Bracelet fur type
Some sources specify the bracelet itself was **ocelot fur** on a brass tube (Artnet). The draft wisely
says only "fur" / "metal tube wrapped in fur" — no error, since it doesn't name a species. If a detail
is wanted, "ocelot-fur" is the sourced descriptor; otherwise leave generic. No change required.

### [NICE] 2 — Department store
Fact pack/sources name **Monoprix**; draft uses "a Paris department store." Correct caution given the
Monoprix/Uniprix retelling variance. No change needed; if naming, Monoprix is the most-cited.

### [NICE] 3 — "the spoon ... metal teaspoon"
ObjMaking calls it "a metal teaspoon," consistent with MoMA's cataloging (cup/saucer porcelain or
white earthenware, spoon metal). Fine; just confirm the read doesn't later imply the spoon is
porcelain (it doesn't). No change.
