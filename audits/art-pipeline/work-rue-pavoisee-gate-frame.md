## LENS 1 — COMPREHENSIVENESS

Each brief-named probe, checked against the draft and the web:

**1. Matisse's *Luxe, calme et volupté* at the 1905 Indépendants as the turn toward Fauvism — PRESENT, correct.**
`RueMaking` builds a full section on it ("The picture that turned him around"), names the
1905 Salon des Indépendants, dates the canvas (1904), notes the Baudelaire title, and states
the effect ("turned his interests hard toward what would shortly be called Fauvism"). It is
stated as EFFECT, never as a flat Dufy quote — exactly per the fact-pack [LEGEND] flag (§8,
§3 Beat 2). Web-confirmed: EN Wikipedia, "Matisse's *Luxe, Calme et Volupté*, which Dufy saw
at the Salon des Independents in 1905, directed his interests towards Fauvism." The looking
section also correctly characterizes the Matisse canvas as "high-keyed, unmixed, almost
shadowless color" (the fact pack notes it was technically Neo-Impressionist/Divisionist; the
draft does not mislabel it as flat-Fauve, so no inflation). Covered.

**2. The national-holiday flag-street subject and why flags suit a Fauve — PRESENT, and it is the draft's spine.**
The 14th-of-July subject is introduced in `RueMaking` (defined plainly, with the US-Fourth-of-July
analog and the tricolor defined), and the "why flags suit a Fauve" argument is the load-bearing
idea of both `RueLooking` and `RueBreak`: the flag is a "found object of pure color," "already
abstract," "the festival doing the Fauve's work for him." This is anchored to the Pompidou
catalogue line (flag flattens the surface) and the histoire-image study (color generates the
space) — the strongest-sourced claims in the fact pack. Covered, thoroughly.

**3. Marquet painting the same Le Havre streets beside Dufy — PRESENT, correct.**
`RueMaking` has a dedicated beat ("Marquet at the next window"): summer 1906, Normandy coast,
"side by side from the windows of hotel bedrooms," same flag-decked streets, with Marquet's own
*14th of July at Le Havre* named as the parallel canvas. Web-confirmed: MuMa Le Havre,
"the two young artists painted side by side from the windows of hotel bedrooms... the city streets
decked with flags"; WikiArt confirms Marquet's *July 14th at Havre, 1906*. Covered.

**4. Dufy's brief Fauve period before his later light decorative style and textile work — PRESENT, correct.**
`RueAfterlife` ("A door he walked back out of") gives the full arc: Fauve "only a few years,
roughly 1905 to 1909," Cézanne pulling him to a subtler/constructed handling, a brief Cubism
flirtation, then "from about 1920" the light decorative style (thin washes over skeletal pen
drawing, regattas/Riviera), and *La Fée Électricité* (1937). Web-confirmed against EN Wikipedia
("until about 1909 when contact with the work of Paul Cézanne led him to adopt a subtler
technique"; the 1920 distinctive style; the 1937 *La Fée Electricité*). The textile-design line
appears in the lineage `children` ("Textile & pattern design") rather than in the prose; see
[NICE-1] — the prose itself does not mention the Bianchini-Férier textile work, which the brief
explicitly flagged as a comprehensiveness item. Covered at minimum; one small prose gap noted.

**Other coverage check (not brief-named, but standard surfaces):**

  web-confirmed via MuMa ("reached full accomplishment in *Street Decked with Flags*").

  This is a real comprehension hazard handled well.

## LENS 2 — FRAMING / FAIRNESS

**Genius-myth inflation — CLEAN.**
The draft credits the discovery substantially to the SUBJECT, not to lone genius: "he figured
it out almost by luck, by being honest about a flag"; "his hometown handed him a street full of
them... for free"; "he had the wit to take the gift and add nothing." This is the opposite of
genius inflation — it locates the radical move in the found object (the tricolor) and Dufy's
restraint, which is exactly what the Pompidou/histoire-image sources support. The repeated
"the festival did the Fauve's job for him" framing is fair and source-anchored.

**"First / only" overclaim — CLEAN, with one phrase to watch.**
The draft makes no "first painting to flatten the surface" or "only Fauve to..." claim. It
explicitly DE-throne-s any first-ness by foregrounding Monet's precedent and Marquet's parallel
canvas (Dufy is "two painters chasing the same subjects," not the sole inventor). The phrase
"the most radical thing in the painting / in it" (used in the hook, `RueLooking`, and `RueBreak`)
is INTERNAL to this one canvas, not a claim about art history — that is legitimate and matches
the Pompidou's own emphasis. Note the Pompidou record's "la plus novatrice" (most innovative)
is correctly scoped by the draft to "the most innovative of HIS flag streets" (`RueAfterlife`),
i.e. relative to Dufy's own versions, not an absolute. No overclaim.

**Eurocentrism — N/A / CLEAN.**
Subject and context are inherently French/European (a Le Havre Bastille-Day street); the draft
makes no universalizing claim that would require a non-Western counterweight. The "first big
shock of twentieth-century painting" line for Fauvism (`RueMaking`) is a standard, defensible
characterization of Fauvism's place in the modern-European story and is not framed as a claim
about world art. No fix needed.

**Nationalism / patriotism over-read — CLEAN; correctly avoided.**
This was the brief's specific risk and the draft handles it well. The 14th of July is treated as
a neutral civic-festival OCCASION that supplies ready-made flat color, NOT as a statement of
nationalist sentiment. The draft never reads patriotic intent into Dufy: the tricolor is "the
festival's own ready-made piece of pure color," "already abstract design," a "found object."
Web-confirmed that the Pompidou record itself "contains no explicit discussion of nationalism or
patriotic intent... focuses on formal pictorial innovation" — so the draft matches the source's
own neutral framing. Good restraint.

No framing/fairness issue rises to [BLOCKER] or [FIX].

## [NICE] — optional, non-blocking

**[NICE-1] — Textile work named only in lineage, not in the afterlife prose.**
The brief lists "textile work" as a comprehensiveness item. The prose in `RueAfterlife` covers
the later decorative style and *La Fée Électricité* but does not mention the textile-design career
(Bianchini-Férier from 1912, patterns used by Poiret — web-confirmed, EN Wikipedia). It survives
only as the lineage chip "Textile & pattern design." Fully optional fix: in `RueAfterlife`, after
"the manner most people picture when they hear the name Dufy," one clause would close the loop —

  Existing text:
  "...regattas and racecourses and the Riviera, the manner most people picture when they hear the
  name Dufy."

  Optional add (insert after that sentence):
  " That decorative gift also carried him into textile design, the printed-fabric patterns he made
  from 1912 for the Lyon silk house Bianchini-Férier."

  (Source: EN Wikipedia, "After 1912, he designed textile patterns for Bianchini-Ferier...";
  the fact pack §5 Beat 5 lists textiles as part of the later career.) Not required to ship.

**[NICE-2] — "the most important colorist in France" (RueMaking) is a mild superlative.**
The line "Henri Matisse... the older painter who was about to become the most important colorist
in France" is a defensible art-historical commonplace, not a hard overclaim, but it is the one
unsourced superlative in the draft. If a reviewer wants zero superlatives, soften to "one of the
most important colorists in France" or "France's leading colorist." Purely stylistic; not a
fairness violation. Optional.
