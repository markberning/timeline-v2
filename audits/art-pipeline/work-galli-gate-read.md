## STORYTELLING

Overall: story-first throughout. The street chapter opens on scene, not on the
painting; the making chapter is structured as a genuine before/after ("two versions");
the dry wit lands ("the painter got the year wrong in print; the painting got the day
right"; "what an evangelist is to a faith"); comparisons are present and earned (garage
door, Uccello, *Starry Night* acquisition mechanism). Carrà's anarchist sympathies and
bodily presence in the riot are the engine, exactly as the brief wants.

**[FIX] Meta-narration — "as we go" / tour-guide framing.** `GalStreet`, final
sentence:
> "That is the difference that makes this painting what it is, and it is worth holding
> onto **as we go**: the picture we are about to look at is not a scene Carrà witnessed."

"as we go" + "the picture we are about to look at" is the chapter narrating its own
structure / the reader's path through it — the exact tic memory `feedback_no_meta_narration`
and the absinthe ref both avoid. (Absinthe does use "the picture we're about to look
at," so that half is in-voice; the "as we go" / "worth holding onto" framing is the
part that reads as tour-guide.) The point itself is great and should stay.
*Fix:* drop the meta scaffolding, keep the punch. e.g. "That is the difference that
makes this painting what it is: the picture is not a scene Carrà witnessed. It is a
scene he was inside."

**[FIX] Meta-narration — "Read that again, because it is the design brief."**
`GalMaking`, para 4:
> "**Read that again, because it is the design brief for this canvas.**"

Direct instruction to the reader about how to read the chapter. Absinthe earns its one
"Reader, file this fact away" as a sly aside; this one is more of a command and it
repeats a beat ("the point of the lines is not to depict the riot. It is to put *you*
in it") that the very next sentences already make. *Fix:* cut the sentence; the
manifesto quote plus "The point of the lines is not to depict the riot. It is to put
you in it" carries it without being told to re-read.

**[NICE] Mild redundancy across making + looking + memory on "lines of force pull you
in."** The manifesto's "encircle and involve the spectator… forced to struggle himself
with the persons" idea is stated in `GalMaking` (para 4), restated in `GalLooking`
(para 5, "it runs off the edge of the canvas, toward you, and pulls your attention into
the brawl"), and the underlying "you can't watch from outside" is in the hook and the
section blurbs. This is mostly *good* reinforcement and the looking-section instance is
the payoff (you actually trace a ray). Just watch that the making-section version
doesn't pre-spend the looking-section's moment. No change required; flag only.

**[NICE] `GalMemory` para 3 leans slightly explainy.** "It is worth being careful
about what this passage is, though" is a soft honesty-label / hedge-frame. It works and
it's short, and the fact-handling (1945 recollection ≠ 1906 transcript) genuinely needs
saying. Could be tightened to lead with the fact ("It is a memoir, not a notebook…")
rather than the meta-frame, but this is voice-consistent with absinthe's careful-hedge
register. Leave unless tightening elsewhere.

No flat stretches found. Pacing is good; every chapter has a turn.

## LOOKING

This is the strongest gate. `GalLooking` does exactly what the brief asks: step-back
gestalt (explosion of red and black) → find the red coffin → the black crowd + mounted
police → the rearing horse flashpoint → the overhead thicket of lances/poles/banners →
the sunburst of force-lines cutting figures → step-in close, bodies dissolve → pull
back, it reassembles. Concrete, directional, located ("upper center," "from the left,"
"top third"). The "walk in close and it comes apart in your hands" close-up/step-back
pass is excellent and matches absinthe's method. The coffin-as-light-source observation
is the single best looking-beat.

**[FIX] A canvas claim the reader can't actually see, stated as seeable: "red
carnations."** `GalLooking` para 2:
> "Galli's coffin, draped and, **in Carrà's memory**, piled with **red carnations**."

This is correctly hedged ("in Carrà's memory") — good, and it's honest that the
carnations come from the 1945 text, not necessarily legible on the canvas. No fact
problem. But as a *looking* instruction it asks the reader to see carnations that may
read only as a red mass. The hedge mostly covers this. *Fix (light):* make explicit
that on the canvas it reads as red, and the carnations are what the red *is* per Carrà —
e.g. "the single reddest passage… a red mass that Carrà remembered as a heap of red
carnations." Keeps the looking honest about what the eye gets vs. what the memoir adds.
Borderline [NICE]; tagging [FIX] because it's a guided-looking surface and the gate
specifically wants the reader to SEE what's there.

**[NICE] "the cranes of the factory district behind them."** Appears in both the
annotation and `GalLooking` para 4. This is a real, specific, see-able detail and a
nice grounding of the riot in the industrial city — keep it. Only flag: confirm at the
fact gate that cranes are actually identifiable in the upper register (looks like a
fact-pack-sourced detail; out of scope for these three gates, noting for the
coordinator).

**[NICE] Could name where the horse/police enter relative to the coffin once more
crisply.** The looking section says the cavalry presses "from the left" and the rearing
horse is "just left of the coffin." Clear. The annotations say cavalry "from the left"
too. Consistent — no fix; just confirming the geography reads the same across surfaces.

Looking gate: **strong pass.** The reader will see the red coffin, the black crowd, the
rearing clash, the overhead lances/poles, the sunburst, and the chaos of fused bodies.

## CLARITY (zero-knowledge glossing)

Very good coverage. All the load-bearing terms are glossed inline on first use:

- **general strike** — glossed `GalStreet` para 1 ("not one trade walking off one job,
  but workers across a whole city downing tools at once…"). ✓
- **anarchists** — glossed `GalStreet` para 1 ("radicals who wanted to abolish the
  state and the bosses outright, not bargain with them"). ✓
- **Divisionism** — glossed `GalMaking` para 1 ("the Italian cousin of Pointillism, in
  which you build an image out of separate small strokes of pure, unmixed color and let
  the eye blend them"). ✓
- **Futurism** — glossed `GalMaking` para 2 ("the loud Milanese movement… worshipped
  speed, machines, crowds, energy, the modern city at full throttle"). ✓
- **Cubism** — glossed `GalMaking` para 3 ("a single object is shattered into facets
  and shown from several angles at once… a built surface of interlocking planes"). ✓
- **lines of force / linee-forza** — glossed `GalMaking` para 3 and the function
  re-explained in `GalLooking` para 5 (light bursting outward, cutting through figures).
  ✓
- **one-point / Renaissance perspective** — glossed `GalMaking` para 1 ("all the
  receding lines aim at a single vanishing point… a deep, believable box of space"). ✓
- **by exchange** — glossed `GalAfterlife` para 3 ("MoMA traded other works for it
  rather than paying cash"). ✓
- **Macchi and Pessoni factory, picket line, mounted police, the memoir** — all
  handled in plain language. ✓

**[FIX] Unglossed term: "Pointillism."** `GalMaking` para 1 glosses Divisionism *by
reference to Pointillism* ("the Italian cousin of Pointillism") — but Pointillism
itself is never defined, so for a true zero-knowledge reader the gloss leans on an
unknown. The very next clause ("in which you build an image out of separate small
strokes of pure, unmixed color and let the eye blend them") actually defines the
technique, so the fix is tiny. *Fix:* attach the definition to Pointillism, not just
Divisionism, so the named anchor is the one explained — e.g. "Divisionism, the Italian
cousin of the French Pointillism — both build an image out of separate small strokes of
pure, unmixed color and let the eye blend them." One word's worth of work; clears the
only genuinely unglossed term in the draft.

**[NICE] "avant-garde."** Used in Part A provenance notes ("Herwarth Walden's
avant-garde gallery") and "the French avant-garde" in `GalAfterlife` para 1. Not
glossed. It's near-common-vocabulary and the absinthe ref also uses "insurgents /
avant-garde-adjacent" language without a full gloss, so this is acceptable house
practice. If you want belt-and-suspenders zero-knowledge, a three-word aside on first
use ("the avant-garde — the experimental new art") would close it. Optional.

**[NICE] "engraver / printmaker / Der Sturm as staging-post."** All clear enough in
context; "Der Sturm… the German staging-post for Futurism" (Part A) is a nice compact
gloss. `GalAfterlife` calls Walden "what an evangelist is to a faith," which carries the
role without jargon. Fine.

**[NICE] "general strike of 1904 … the larger, more famous 1904 general strike."**
`GalMemory` para 4 — the reader meets "the 1904 strike" as a thing assumed known. It's
deployed only to explain Carrà's misdating, and the prose immediately frames it as
"larger, more famous," which is gloss enough for the function it serves. No fix needed;
noting that it's the one historical referent dropped without a one-clause who/what, and
a four-word aside ("the 1904 strike — a bigger, bloodier nationwide walkout") would make
the conflation-motive paragraph fully self-contained. Optional.

Clarity gate: **pass**, contingent on the one [FIX] (Pointillism anchor).
