## [BLOCKER]

### B1 — The "Contessa Nerazzini" ID is NOT legend: it is on the museum's own page. The draft demotes a sourced fact to a rumor.

**Draft text (PART B, `DogMaking`, para 2):**
> "(You will often see the owner named as a specific countess, "the Contessa Nerazzini,"
> and the dog described as hers. By one often-repeated account that is who she was, but it
> is **not on the museum's own page** and is not tied to a primary source we can stand
> behind, so treat the name as a good story rather than a settled fact. What we know is the
> lady, the black dress, the dog, and the month.)"

**Also in:** the PART A header comment (lines 26–27: "the owner is NOT asserted as 'Contessa
Nerazzini' (legend)"), the draft's preamble (line 7), and the fact ledger row "Owner
'Contessa Nerazzini' hedged … LEGEND — framed."

**The error.** The Buffalo AKG collection page — the work's own primary museum source, the
fact pack's #1 citation — explicitly states the painting was made "while visiting **one of
his students, the Contessa Nerazzini, at Montepulciano, near Siena**" in May 1912. The
draft asserts the exact opposite: that the Nerazzini name is "not on the museum's own
page." It is. (The fact pack itself carried this false premise — Beat 4's catch and §7's
ledger both say "not on the museum's own page" — so the author inherited the error from the
pack; but the gate verifies against the web, and the web shows the pack wrong here.)

Two distinct claims must be separated:
- **Nerazzini was Balla's host/student at Montepulciano** — DOCUMENTED by the museum page.
  Assertable.
- **The dachshund was specifically Nerazzini's own dog** — the museum says "his or her
  owner" (gender-neutral) and "the woman," and does NOT explicitly equate the walking
  woman with Nerazzini or call the dog hers. The "it is the Contessa's dachshund" leap is
  the part that is still inferential.

**Correction.** Stop calling the Nerazzini identification a legend / unsupported-by-the-
museum. Re-author to assert what the museum asserts — Balla painted it while staying with
his student **the Contessa Nerazzini** at Montepulciano — and hedge only the narrower,
genuinely-uncertain point (whether the walking lady in the picture *is* Nerazzini and the
dog *hers*, vs. simply a scene observed during that visit). The current text fails the
fact axis (asserts a falsehood: "not on the museum's own page") and the framing axis
(buries a sourced, citable fact under a "good story rather than settled fact" disclaimer).

**Source.** Buffalo AKG collection page,
https://buffaloakg.org/artworks/196416-dinamismo-di-un-cane-al-guinzaglio-dynamism-dog-leash
— "painted this amusing study of a skittering dachshund and the staccato steps of his or
her owner in May 1912 while visiting one of his students, the Contessa Nerazzini, at
Montepulciano, near Siena."

> Note to coordinator: the fact pack must be corrected in lockstep (Beat 4 catch, §7
> ledger row, and the "whose dog?" note all repeat "not on the museum's own page"). The
> draft can't be fixed without fixing the pack premise.

## [FIX]

### F1 — `DogMaking` SectionHeader title drops the dachshund; PART A `making` blurb / annotations say "dachshund," header says "A dog."

**Draft text:** PART B header — `title="A dog, a lady in black, a hill town"` (line 141);
PART A section `making` — `title: 'A dachshund, a lady in black, a Tuscan road'` (line 61).
Minor internal inconsistency (the const titles the section one way, the rendered component
another). Not a fact error; reconcile to one. **NICE-adjacent**, listed as FIX only because
the const and the component are supposed to be the same section. Correction: pick one title.

### F2 — "an elegant woman dressed in black" / "lady in black" — fine, but verify it isn't being treated as museum-sourced when it is secondary.

**Draft text:** repeated as a hard fact ("an elegant woman dressed in black," lines 61,
144, 147; "black hem of her skirt," looking section). **Status: SUPPORTED but not by the
museum page.** The Buffalo AKG page says only "his or her owner" / "the woman" — it does
NOT say black-clad. The "elegant lady dressed in black" detail comes from the secondary
literature (widely repeated; e.g. dailyart-tier write-ups). It is fine to keep, but it
should not be presented as if the museum documents the black dress. Correction: keep the
detail, just don't imply ("That much the museum … support: a lady in black," line 147) that
the *black dress* specifically is museum-sourced — the museum supports the dog, the leash,
the owner's stepping feet, and the Tuscan-dust ground, but says "the woman," not "in black."

### F3 — Wikipedia reception quotes are paraphrased loosely; the draft's quotation marks imply verbatim.

**Draft text (`DogAfterlife`):** "it got called "crude" and "clichéd" in the 1940s …
By 2014 … praised as "elegant and accurate.""
**The record (en.wikipedia):** 1943 — "more crude, less mature, almost childish indeed";
1947 — "a cliché of modern art"; 2014 — "probably the most elegant and accurate works ever
painted in the Futurist tradition." The draft's single-word quotes ("crude," "clichéd,"
"elegant and accurate") are reasonable compressions and the years (1940s / 2014) are right,
but putting them inside quotation marks asserts verbatim text that isn't verbatim ("clichéd"
≠ "a cliché of modern art"). Correction: either quote the actual phrases or drop the
quote-marks and paraphrase ("dismissed as crude and clichéd in the 1940s; praised in 2014
as among the most elegant and accurate Futurist works"). Source: en.wikipedia,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamism_of_a_Dog_on_a_Leash

## [NICE]

### N1 — Marinetti "world's only hygiene" quote — verify exact wording.
Draft (`DogAfterlife`): war which Marinetti called "the world's only hygiene." The
canonical translation of the 1909 Manifesto's clause 9 is "war — the world's only hygiene"
(some renderings: "the only cure for the world" / "the only hygiene of the world"). The
draft's wording is a standard, defensible translation. NICE: footnote which translation, or
soften to "what Marinetti called the world's only hygiene" without hard quotes if a verbatim
source isn't pinned. Not blocking.

### N2 — "bought directly from Balla … the popular shortcut that the painting went straight from the artist to its museum is also wrong."
DOCUMENTED and correct (Wikipedia: "sold … to industrialist Anson Conger Goodyear in
1938"; the Sturm-1913 → Goodyear-1938 → 1964 bequest → 1984 gift chain all check out).
Goodyear's full name is **Anson Conger Goodyear** (Wikipedia uses "Anson Conger Goodyear,"
draft uses "A. Conger Goodyear" — both correct, the museum credit line uses "A. Conger
Goodyear"). No action.
