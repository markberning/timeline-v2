# Gate — COMPREHENSIVENESS + FRAMING/FAIRNESS

**Work:** Francis Picabia, *Ici, c'est ici Stieglitz, foi et amour* (1915) · `stieglitz` · Dada
**Gate scope:** essential-facet coverage + genius-myth / first-only overclaim / satire-vs-homage fairness / proportion-anachronism
**Reviewer note:** WEB-verified the sole-inventor and de Zayas-precedent questions (see SOURCES). Read-only; no edits to draft/src.

---

## VERDICT: REVISE — 1 BLOCKER, 2 FIX, 2 NICE

The draft is comprehensive and the satire/homage balance is handled with real care (best-in-class on fairness). But it commits a **genius-myth overclaim** at two points — calling the object-portrait Picabia's "own invention" and saying he is "the thing he is remembered for" — that the record does not support: **Marius de Zayas's "absolute caricatures" of 1913, including an abstract/mathematical *Portrait of Alfred Stieglitz* (1912–13), preceded and directly influenced Picabia's machine portraits.** De Zayas is named in the draft as a circle member but is never credited as the precedent, so the draft inflates Picabia as sole inventor. That is the one ship-blocker.

## 1. COMPREHENSIVENESS

Essential facets checked: ✅ New York machine-Dada / 291 circle + magazine · ✅ object-portrait / mechanomorph idea · ✅ camera-as-Stieglitz reading · ✅ satire-and-homage duality · ✅ influence on Dada (+ Duchamp parallel, Pop/conceptual). Coverage is strong. One real gap, tied to the framing blocker below.

### [BLOCKER] The de Zayas precedent is a MAJOR missing facet (also the genius-myth fix)
The draft presents the mechanomorph as springing from Picabia alone ("his own invention," "the thing he is remembered for"). It omits that **Marius de Zayas — already named in the draft as a circle member — pioneered abstract, non-representational "absolute caricatures" in 1913** (line/shape + mathematical equations), and made an abstract **_Portrait of Alfred Stieglitz_ (1912–13)** that "directly influenced Francis Picabia in the development of his machine portraits." A reader is told the wrong origin story: that Stieglitz-as-abstraction and the de-faced portrait were Picabia's leap, when de Zayas had already de-faced Stieglitz two years earlier. This is both the missing facet and the genius-myth correction; one edit fixes both (see Framing §A for the exact text).

### [NICE] The suite is named but not shown
The draft says Picabia "made a whole suite of them, one for each of the people who ran the magazine" but names none of the companions. The fact pack's specifics land the idea concretely. Suggested add to `StiMachine` para 1, after "put Stieglitz on the cover.":
> The others ran inside: de Zayas as the wiring diagram of an automobile's electrical system, the writer Paul Haviland as an unplugged portable lamp, Picabia himself as a car horn. Each machine was chosen to fit its man.

### [NICE] "The most important photographer in America"
Appears twice (annotation + `StiMachine`) as flat assertion. True enough as period reception, but consider softening one to "America's foremost champion of photography-as-art" to avoid a bald superlative doing double duty.

Otherwise comprehensive — provenance, rights, medium-trap, the quote, the break, and the looking-points are all present and accurate.

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### A. genius-myth — [BLOCKER] "his own invention" / sole inventor

Two phrasings inflate Picabia as the lone inventor and must be situated against de Zayas (and the 291 circle as a collective):

**A1.** `sections[].machine.blurb`: *"The object-portrait, or mechanomorph, **is his own invention**, and Stieglitz gets the cover."*
Suggested:
> The object-portrait, or mechanomorph, is the circle's new thing — Picabia pushes it furthest — and Stieglitz gets the cover.

**A2.** `StiMachine` para 1: *"Picabia's big idea, **the thing he is remembered for**, arrived almost the moment he reached America: he would draw people as machines."*
Suggested (rework the opening clause and add the de Zayas precedent):
> Picabia's signature move arrived almost the moment he reached America: he would draw people as **machines**. He did not invent the de-faced portrait single-handedly — the Mexican caricaturist **Marius de Zayas**, already in the 291 circle, had been making abstract "absolute caricatures" since 1913 (subjects rendered as arrangements of line and mathematical equation, including an abstract portrait of Stieglitz himself around 1912–13), and that example fed straight into what Picabia did next. What Picabia added was the **machine**: not abstraction, but a specific mechanical thing copied from an advertisement, made to stand in for a person.

This both removes the overclaim and supplies the missing facet. Note the `figures[]` entry "The machine as man / Picabia's whole idea" is fine as a thematic label, but the prose around it must not read as "Picabia alone dreamed it up."

### B. first / only overclaim — [FIX]

`StiCircle` para 3: *"the circle produced **something genuinely new in the history of portraiture**."* And `StiMachine`: the framing that a face-less, machine portrait was unprecedented. With de Zayas's 1913 abstract portraits (and the broader New-York "object portrait" genre that Demuth and Dove also worked), "genuinely new" is too absolute for a 1915 work standing on a 1913 precedent. Suggested for `StiCircle` para 3:
> …the circle produced a portrait of a radically new kind — the human figure thrown out altogether.

Keep the rupture claim (it is real and the Break section earns it), but scope it to "the figure thrown out," which de Zayas's still-recognizable (mustache, glasses) caricatures had *not* fully done — that is the defensible, accurate version of Picabia's step.

### C. fairness — satire vs. homage — [no change; commend]

This is handled exemplarily. `StiAfterlife` holds both readings in genuine balance ("both at once, and the picture is built to be both"), gives the satire case its curatorial weight, then the homage case its provenance clincher ("not what a man does with an insult"), and explicitly refuses to flatten ("To insist it is only one or the other is to miss what makes it land"). The hook and the `afterlife` blurb also keep both. **No fix.** This is the model the other gates should hold the rest of the chain to.

### D. proportion / anachronism — [FIX] "New York Dada" applied to a 1915 proto-Dada object

`StiCircle` para 3 calls the 1915 circle "what historians later called **New York Dada**, a cooler, wittier, more machine-minded cousin of the Dada that was erupting at the same moment in wartime Zurich." Two accuracy problems:

- **Timing.** Dada proper coalesced at the Cabaret Voltaire in **1916**, *after* this work. In summer 1915 there was no "Dada" to be a cousin of — Zurich Dada was not "erupting at the same moment." This work is **proto-Dada / New York machine-Dada in formation**; the *291* July–August 1915 issue is described in the literature as a "proto-Dadaist statement." Calling it New York Dada is fine as the retrospective label (the draft's "what historians later called" hedge is good), but the simultaneity claim is wrong by a year.

Suggested for `StiCircle` para 3, replace the Zurich clause:
> This loose New York cell is what historians later called **New York Dada** — though the word "Dada" itself would not be coined until the Cabaret Voltaire opened in Zurich the next year, in 1916. New York's machine-minded, deadpan version got there first, and on its own, out of the 291 circle.

This keeps the retrospective label, fixes the date, and (bonus) strengthens the New-York-priority point the draft is reaching for. The `StiBreak` "founding documents of New York Dada" line is fine as-is (retrospective, no simultaneity claim).

### E. [NICE] "almost single-handedly dragged American taste"

`StiCircle` para 2 on Stieglitz: "almost single-handedly, dragged American taste toward the new." Mild genius-myth on the *sitter* (the work was a collective effort of a circle that included de Zayas, Edward Steichen, etc.). Low stakes — it is period-accurate as reputation and the section is about Stieglitz's stature — but consider "did more than any one person to drag American taste toward the new." Optional.

- **[BLOCKER]** Add the de Zayas 1913 precedent + drop "his own invention" / "the thing he is remembered for" (Framing A1, A2; also closes the Comprehensiveness gap).
- **[FIX]** Scope "something genuinely new in the history of portraiture" → "the human figure thrown out altogether" (Framing B).
- **[FIX]** Correct the Dada-timing/anachronism: Dada coined 1916, *after* this 1915 proto-Dada work; cut the "erupting at the same moment in Zurich" simultaneity (Framing D).
