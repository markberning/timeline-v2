## 2. THE STORY (the throughline)

Gorky is the **hinge between European Surrealism and American Abstract Expressionism** —
often tagged "the last of the Surrealists and the first of the Abstract Expressionists."
*The Liver Is the Cock's Comb* (1944) is the picture most often used to show the turn:
it takes Surrealism's **automatism** (letting the hand move freely, fed by the
unconscious) and Surrealism's **biomorphism** (soft, organic, body-and-plant-like shapes)
and pushes them toward the large-scale, all-over, abstract field that the next generation
(Pollock, de Kooning, Rothko) would run with.

- **The lush, organic, sexual forms.** The canvas teems with soft biomorphic shapes —
  petal-, organ-, flame-, and limb-like forms — floating in a loose colored field with
  no fixed scene or horizon. Buffalo AKG notes "clear allusions to female and male
  genitalia," but stresses the forms stay **deliberately indefinable**: this is not a
  picture *of* something, it is fertility, growth, and the body **felt rather than
  depicted**.
- **Memory + present landscape.** Gorky painted this in his most fertile years, married
  (1941) to Agnes Magruder ("Mougouch"), spending time on her family's **Virginia farm**.
  Buffalo AKG: the work fuses "the artist's physical surroundings as well as his memories
  of the gardens of his homeland" — i.e. the Virginia countryside **and** remembered
  Armenia. His childhood loss runs underneath it (his mother died of starvation in 1919
  during the Armenian genocide's aftermath — keep proportionate, §8).
- **André Breton's admiration.** Breton, the pope of Surrealism, met Gorky in New York
  during the war (European Surrealists in exile) and championed him: he wrote the
  introduction to Gorky's 1945 Julien Levy Gallery show, **supplied poetic titles** for
  several Gorky works including this one, and praised Gorky's nature-as-cryptogram method.
  He treated Gorky as a genuine Surrealist discovery. (Breton's "one of the most important
  paintings made in America" phrasing is widely attributed but should be quote-flagged —
  §4/§8.)
- **The productive 1940s, then collapse.** 1943–47 were Gorky's breakthrough years. Then
  a cascade: a **1946 studio fire** destroyed many works; a **rectal-cancer diagnosis and
  colostomy**; a **1948 car accident** that broke his neck and temporarily paralyzed his
  painting arm; his marriage broke down. **Gorky hanged himself on 21 July 1948**, leaving
  the chalked words "Goodbye My Loveds." So this 1944 canvas sits inside the short, bright
  window before the fall.

## 3. WHY IT'S A BREAK (required before/after)

**Before:** Two strands sat apart. (a) **Surrealism** had automatism and biomorphic dream
imagery (Miró, Masson, Matta, Tanguy) but stayed largely European, often small,
illustrative, or tied to dream-scenes. (b) **American abstraction** before the war was
mostly geometric/Cubist-derived or regionalist realism. Gorky himself spent the 1930s as a
brilliant **apprentice**, openly painting "through" Cézanne, Picasso, and Miró.

**The break (this canvas):** Gorky **fuses** the two. He keeps Surrealist **automatism and
biomorphic form** but scales them up to **mural-size, all-over abstraction** with no fixed
scene, and he carries his draftsman's **line drawing directly into the painting** — thin,
nervous contours laid over washed, bled, stained color. The result is **abstraction fed by
the unconscious and by personal memory** rather than by Cubist geometry. That hybrid —
organic form + automatism + scale + drawing-into-paint + the unconscious as subject — is
one of the seeds of Abstract Expressionism.

**After:** The younger New York painters take exactly these moves. De Kooning's biomorphic
abstraction, Pollock's all-over automatist field, the move to large unhierarchical canvases
of pure feeling — all owe to Gorky's bridge. Hence the standard line: **Gorky is where
Surrealism becomes Abstract Expressionism.** (Scope the claim — §8 — Gorky is *a* crucial
bridge, not the sole cause.)

## 4. KEY STATEMENT (verbatim, sourced — flag variants)

Use **one** of these. The Gorky quotes are the safest verbatim choices; the Breton "most
important" line should be quote-flagged.

**Option A — Gorky, on memory/nature/growth (his own words, on the related *Garden in
Sochi* series; the same image-world as this canvas).** From the text Gorky supplied to the
**Museum of Modern Art, 26 June 1942**:

> "I like the heat the tenderness the edible the lusciousness the song of a single person
> the bathtub full of water to bathe myself beneath the water."
> — Arshile Gorky, statement to MoMA on the *Garden in Sochi* series, 26 June 1942.

*(Verbatim per Wikiquote's sourced entry; the fuller text continues "…I like Ucello,
Grunewald, Ingres… I measure all things by weight." This is about a sibling work, not
*Liver* specifically — present it as Gorky's image-world, not a caption to this canvas.)*

**Option B — Breton, on Gorky's method (March 1945).** From Breton's introduction to
Gorky's first solo show, **Julien Levy Gallery, 1945**:

> "Here for the first time nature is treated as a cryptogram. The artist has a code by
> reason of his own sensitive anterior impressions, and can decode nature to reveal the
> very rhythm of life."
> — André Breton, introduction to Gorky's exhibition, Julien Levy Gallery, March 1945.

*(Sourced per Wikiquote; the "nature as cryptogram" idea is the cleanest Breton-on-Gorky
line and is directly about the decode-memory-into-form method this painting uses.)*

**VARIANT FLAG — the "most important paintings made in America" line.** Many secondary
sources say Breton, on seeing *Liver*, called it **"one of the most important paintings
made in America."** This is **widely repeated but I could not pin it to a primary Breton
text in this pass.** If used, attribute as *reported/attributed*, not as a sourced Breton
quotation. Prefer A or B as the gated KEY STATEMENT.

## 5. LOOKING POINTS (~6, prose pointers, NO coordinates)

1. **A teeming field, not a scene.** There is no horizon, no ground line, no single
   subject — the eye finds **dozens of soft biomorphic shapes** scattered and clustered
   across a wide colored field. Read it as a swarm of growing things, not a place.
2. **Drawing laid over color.** Look for **thin, dark, nervous contour lines** drawn on
   top of the painted washes. Gorky was a master draftsman; here the **drawing rides on
   the painting** rather than being filled in by it — a key tell of his late method.
3. **Thin washes, stains, and bleeds.** The color is often **soft and watery**, letting
   the canvas show through, with edges that **run and seep** rather than sit in hard
   shapes — automatist accident left visible.
4. **Organic / sexual forms, kept indefinable.** Petal-, organ-, and limb-like forms
   suggest **fertility and the body**; Buffalo AKG flags allusions to **male and female
   genitalia**, but every shape stays **ambiguous** — never quite a flower, never quite an
   organ. The ambiguity is the point.
5. **Bright passages against a quieter field.** A few **hot, saturated accents** (reds,
   yellows) flare out against the more **muted, washed surroundings**, pulling the eye
   across the long canvas.
6. **The scale.** Stand back: this is **roughly 6 by 8 feet**, mural-sized. The intimate,
   doodle-like forms are blown up to a wall — that mismatch of *private, organic mark* ×
   *public, monumental size* is part of why it reads as a step toward Abstract
   Expressionism.

## 8. APOCRYPHA / TRAPS (correct with sources)

- **The title is evocative, not literal.** *The Liver Is the Cock's Comb* is **not a
  description of the picture** and **not Gorky's own phrase** — **André Breton supplied the
  title** (the Breton-Surrealist habit of giving works poetic, deliberately strange names).
  "Liver" carries old associations with the soul/seat of life; "cock's comb" (coxcomb) is
  at once a **flower** (*Celosia*), a **rooster's crest**, and a **jester's cap / a fool**;
  "cock" also reads as crude slang. Buffalo AKG presents these as **layered allusions, not
  a key** — do **not** decode the title as if it labels specific shapes. (Source: Buffalo
  AKG.) **Trap:** the often-repeated "collaboration with Breton on New Year's Eve 1944"
  anecdote is colorful but loosely sourced — keep it as *Breton supplied/invented the
  title*, don't over-specify the scene.
- **Keep the genocide / mother biography proportionate.** Gorky lived the **Armenian
  genocide** and his **mother died of starvation in 1919**; this loss genuinely haunts his
  art. But do **not** turn the read into a trauma caption that "explains" every shape.
  Buffalo AKG frames the homeland as **remembered gardens fused with the Virginia
  present** — memory and place, not literal autobiography. State the loss once, with care,
  and let the forms stay open.
- **Dimensions — get them right.** Support is **73¼ × 98⅜ in / 186.06 × 249.87 cm** (≈ 6 ft
  1¼ in × 8 ft 2⅜ in). Don't confuse with the **framed** size (75³⁄₁₆ × 100⅜ in). Render
  ft/in first per house rule; one of Gorky's largest works but **not** ten feet wide.
- **Scope the Surrealism→AbEx bridge claim.** Gorky is *a* decisive bridge — "last
  Surrealist / first Abstract Expressionist" is a useful shorthand, **not** a claim that he
  single-handedly invented AbEx or that he fully accepted Surrealist doctrine. Sources
  stress he **kept Surrealism's automatism and form but rejected its dream-illustration /
  pure-unconscious dogma**, bending it toward his own memory-and-nature abstraction. Phrase
  the bridge as **influence and synthesis**, not sole cause.
- **"Cubist painting" tag (Wikipedia).** Some pop sources call it a "Cubist painting." It
  carries **Cubist structural DNA** (Gorky learned through Picasso), but the gated read
  should treat it as **biomorphic Surrealist abstraction on the road to AbEx**, not as
  Cubism proper. Don't repeat the bare "Cubist" label uncritically.
- **Name spelling / dates.** Born **Vosdanig Manoug Adoian**; "Arshile Gorky" is an adopted
  name (he sometimes implied kinship with the writer Maxim Gorky — false). Birth year is
  **ca. 1904** (uncertain; he gave conflicting dates). Use "ca. 1904–1948" as Buffalo AKG does.
