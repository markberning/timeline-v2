# A Burial at Ornans — Gate 2: Storytelling + Looking critique

**Overall grade: OK**

The draft is solidly competent and in the right voice. It never badly embarrasses
itself. But "OK" is not a pass without fixes: there are two must-fix storytelling
failures (buried lede in ch1, listy triptych in ch3) and a handful of looking beats
that are either missing or under-lived. Nothing here requires a structural rewrite —
targeted fixes should push this to STRONG.

---

## STORYTELLING

### MUST-FIX

**1. Ch1 open buries the lede (Section "A funeral nobody was supposed to paint")**

> "In September 1848, in a small town in the French countryside, an old man was
> lowered into the ground, and a crowd of his neighbors stood around the hole and
> watched. This happens somewhere on Earth every few seconds. It is the least
> remarkable event a human life contains. Nobody paints it."

The rhetorical build is right but the payoff is too gentle. "Nobody had ever painted
it — not like this, anyway" trails off into qualifications before the reader has been
hit with the size. The punch lands *three sections later* in Ch2 — "ten feet tall and
twenty-two feet wide." That is the hook. It belongs here, woven into the open. Pull
the scale up into Chapter 1's first beat so the reader is already winded before you
explain what the rule was. Something like: "… Nobody paints it. And if they did, they
would not paint it *ten feet tall.*" The rest of Chapter 1 can then name the rule that
forbade exactly that. Right now Chapter 1 ends on the rule (correct) but opens without
the outrage that makes the rule feel like a genuine injustice.

**Fix:** inject the scale — not the exact dimensions, just the enormity — into the
first or second paragraph of Ch1 so the reader enters the chapter already feeling the
transgression.

---

**2. Ch3 "Offense one / two / three" structure is listy and kills momentum**

Chapters 1, 2, and 4 have genuine narrative movement. Chapter 3 breaks into a triptych
of enumerated offenses and the energy drops: you stop reading a story and start reading
a bullet list with paragraph breaks. The three offenses are all real and all worth
stating, but the numbered-offense scaffold is textbook, not house voice.

Specific problems:
- "The first complaint, and the loudest, was simple: it was *ugly*." — correct content,
  but "offense one/two/three" telegraphs the form before the reader feels the feeling.
- The transitions between offenses are mechanical: "The second offense was the one the
  movement read flags as the deep one." The meta-commentary ("the movement read flags")
  kills immersion.
- "Add the three together" (Section "Why it couldn't be ignored") is a summarizing
  committee motion, not a payoff.

The section "Why it couldn't be ignored" is actually the strongest in this chapter —
the Salon-as-bomb-chamber logic is the real insight — and it is currently buried at the
end of a summary beat. Promote it. Lead the chapter with "why a country funeral became
the most talked-about object in France," then let the ugliness/scale/politics emerge
as elements of that argument, not as items one through three.

**Fix:** restructure Ch3 to start with the detonation and work backward to the
ingredients, rather than listing ingredients and summarizing the detonation. The
numbered-offense scaffold must go. The essential content is fine; the scaffold is what
is killing it.

---

### NICE-TO-HAVE

**3. Ch1, Section "The decision": hierarchy-of-genres explanation is slightly over-long**

> "European art ran on a ranking system called the **hierarchy of genres** — an official
> ladder of subjects … You were *allowed* to paint a peasant. You were not allowed to
> paint a peasant at the size of a god."

The last line ("You were *allowed* …") is excellent — keep it. But the paragraph is
longer than it needs to be given that the movement read already explains the hierarchy.
The nesting header (line 6–7 of the draft) explicitly notes this is a terse callback
context. A reader who came through the movement read (expected nesting path) will feel
the drag. Consider trimming the middle of this paragraph to a sentence or two, keeping
only the italicized payoff.

---

**4. Ch4, Section "A manifesto before the manifesto": good argument, weak transition into it**

> "Here is why the work read insists on this picture as the hinge."

"Here is why the work read insists" — the meta-level voice (referring to itself as "the
work read") is a jarring hedge. The argument that follows is one of the draft's strongest
moves (Burial as Realism's public fact before the 1855 manifesto put it into words). It
does not need a nervous apology for why the read is spending time here. Lead with the
claim, not the justification for making it.

**Fix:** cut "Here is why the work read insists on this picture as the hinge." Start
directly with "The Pavilion came five years later" or "The movement read tells the
famous later moment…" — which is the very next sentence and a perfectly good opening.

---

**5. Ch5 afterlife is correct but flat — the "tidy justice" is underplayed**

> "There is a tidy justice in it: the picture the academy treated as an assault on its
> values ended up given, for free, into the keeping of the country whose taste it had
> outraged."

"Tidy justice" is almost there — but "tidy" is the kind of modifying word that
dampens the thing it should amplify. The sister handing the painting to the state is
a genuinely affecting beat, and it deserves one more sentence of weight before moving
to the Orsay section. The final section "Why it still matters" recovers well (the last
paragraph's payoff — "fifty real villagers, life-size, standing around a hole in the
ground, with a bored dog in front and the bones of the last occupant scattered in the
dirt" — is the draft's single best sentence and should be kept verbatim), but the
Juliette beat should be allowed to land before the logistics of museum transfer begin.

---

**6. Ch2 transition into "The jolt of red" is inert**

> "Two notes of color cut the gloom, and they are worth finding because they are
> nearly the only ones."

"They are worth finding because they are nearly the only ones" is functional but dull.
The whole chapter has been building a looking tour of the canvas; the register should
be more like "Scan the whole twenty-two feet and you'll count exactly two notes of
warm color." That small re-pitch keeps the you-are-there energy the chapter earns
everywhere else.

---

## LOOKING

### MUST-FIX

**7. The open grave section (Ch2, "The hole") makes the reader understand but not SEE**

> "Walk to the front-center and you find the thing the whole crowd is gathered around,
> and it is not a coffin or a cross or a grieving widow. It is a hole."

The misdirection (not a coffin, not a cross — a hole) is the draft's cleverest piece
of looking writing and should stay. But after "It is a hole," the description
immediately turns analytical: memento mori, allegory of Death switched off. The reader
never actually looks *into* the hole. How dark is it? Does Courbet paint the depth or
just the edges? The visual experience of a void at the center of a monumental canvas —
that literal blankness — is the radical choice, and the draft explains that it's a
radical choice without making you feel the blankness.

**Fix:** give two sentences of actual visual experience before pivoting to meaning:
what does the grave *look like* — is it a dark rectangle, do you see soil edges, is
it ambiguous in depth? The kneeling gravedigger in shirtsleeves is vivid; stay in
that register for the hole itself before the analysis begins.

---

**8. The limestone cliff (Ch2, "The cliff") is described but not felt**

> "Behind the whole crowd, closing off the top of the canvas, runs a band of low
> **limestone cliffs** under a flat, indifferent gray sky."

"Runs a band of" is spatial but not visual. The cliffs' power in the painting is
their quality: the pale, almost white rock face pressing forward, making the figure
line feel trapped against the viewer, the sky offering nothing. The draft gets to
the meaning (no idealized Italianate distance, just local stone) but skips the
experience of what that cliff band *looks like* as paint — its flatness, its
pallor, the way it functions as a near-featureless wall rather than a receding
landscape.

**Fix:** one more visual beat before the meaning sentence: something on the color
(pale, chalky, almost the same value as the sky above it) and the compression it
creates before moving to the interpretation.

---

**9. The faces are described in aggregate but we never look at a single face**

> "Faces are tired, jowly, distracted, plain; some are frankly unflattering."

This is in the right register (tired, jowly — good) but plural. The draft's own
annotation section (Part B, Annotation 4) says "The women are massed on the right,
some pressing handkerchiefs to their faces." Here in the body text, the women with
handkerchiefs are mentioned but no face is individuated. The painting has FIFTY
named, posed portraits — the beadles with their "frankly ordinary, ruddy" faces
are the one partial exception, and that's the draft's best face-level moment. 

There is room for one more: pick the most visually striking face in the crowd (the
gravedigger, one of the weeping women, the priest reading with his back half-turned)
and give it two sentences. A crowd of fifty feels different once one face in it is
real.

**Fix** (nice-to-have, not blocking, but materially improves the LOOKING axis): in
the "Men, women, and a dog" section, instead of cataloguing the crowd type, single
out one face and describe it before pulling back to the aggregate.

---

### STRONG — KEEP

**10. The dog paragraph (Ch2, "Men, women, and a dog")**

> "And then, down in the right foreground, the detail that tells you everything about
> Courbet's nerve: a **small dog**, back to the grave, sniffing off toward the edge of
> the canvas, completely indifferent to the solemn human business behind it. A dog has
> wandered into the most sacred moment a community has, and it does not care, and
> Courbet not only let it stay, he gave it the front row."

This is the best LOOKING passage in the draft. The dog's indifference is described,
then explained, then made into a claim about Courbet's entire project. The "front row"
line is the kind of phrase that sticks. Do not touch this.

---

**11. The beadles in red (Ch2, "The jolt of red")**

> "The eye snags on them instantly. In a history painting that red would belong to a
> cardinal or a king. Here it belongs to two small-town church functionaries with
> frankly ordinary, ruddy, unidealized faces, and the effect is almost comic — the
> grandest color in the picture spent on its most minor officials."

Vivid, correctly tonal, makes you see the misallocation of color. Keep.

---

**12. The cliff meaning passage**

> "Nature here offers no comfort and no drama. It is just the rock these people live
> under, the rock they will be buried in, going on being rock."

"Going on being rock" — yes. That's the voice. The interpretation lands because the
final phrase gives the cliff a kind of dull eternity. Keep verbatim.

---

**13. The closing paragraph of Ch5**

> "no hierarchy, no idealization, no hero — fifty real villagers, life-size, standing
> around a hole in the ground, with a bored dog in front and the bones of the last
> occupant scattered in the dirt."

The summary restores all the specific visual elements as a coda. This is the right
move and the best sentence in the draft. Keep.

---

## Summary

The draft is capable and mostly in voice. Two things need fixing before it passes:
the Chapter 1 open does not deliver the scale that is the whole scandal (fix: pull
the enormity forward into the first beat), and Chapter 3's enumerated-offense
scaffold drains the life from what should be the climax chapter (fix: restructure
around the detonation, not the ingredient list). On the LOOKING axis, the open grave
and the limestone cliffs are described analytically before they are experienced
visually — both need a beat of actual seeing before the interpretation. The dog, the
beadles, and the closing paragraph are the three strongest passages and must not be
touched. Targeted fixes to the four must-fix items should push this to STRONG without
a structural rewrite.
