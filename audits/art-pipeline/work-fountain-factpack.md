## 1. CORE FACTS

- **Title:** *Fountain* [Tate, Cabinet, Wikipedia]
- **Artist:** Attributed to **Marcel Duchamp** (1887–1968), French-American. Submitted under the
  pseudonym **"R. Mutt."** [Tate; Wikipedia] — see the authorship debate in §2 and §8 before
  asserting sole authorship as settled.
- **Date:** **1917.** [all sources]
- **Medium:** A **readymade** — a mass-produced **porcelain urinal**, rotated 90° onto its back,
  signed "R. Mutt 1917" in black paint. The 1964 replicas are **glazed earthenware painted to
  resemble the original porcelain**, with the signature reproduced in black paint. [Tate; Cabinet]
- **The original is LOST.** It vanished after the 1917 Stieglitz photo. Biographer Calvin Tomkins'
  best guess: it was **thrown out as rubbish**, a common fate of Duchamp's early readymades.
  [Wikipedia, citing Tomkins; Cabinet lists it "Lost / destroyed"]

### The replicas (there is no surviving original — every museum *Fountain* is a replica)
Duchamp authorized a sequence of full-size reproductions [Tate; Cabinet "Seventeen Known Versions"]:
- **1950, New York** — signed by Duchamp at dealer **Sidney Janis's** request (Janis bought a
  urinal at a Paris flea market). → **Philadelphia Museum of Art.** [Cabinet]
- **1953, Paris** — listed in the catalogue raisonné, sold to benefit a friend; current location
  poorly documented. [Cabinet] **[FLAG: location uncertain]**
- **1963, Stockholm** — made by **Ulf Linde** with Duchamp's permission; Duchamp signed it later
  (Milan, 1964). → **Moderna Museet, Stockholm.** [Cabinet]
- **1964, Milan — the Schwarz edition:** an **edition of 8**, fabricated by gallerist **Arturo
  Schwarz**, plus artist's/exhibition proofs and a prototype (so ~17 versions exist in total).
  [Cabinet; Tate]

## 2. THE STORY

- **The 1917 Society of Independent Artists exhibition** (inaugural; Grand Central Palace, NYC,
  opened 10 April 1917). Its founding promise, modeled on the French Indépendants: **no jury, no
  prizes — any artist paying the fee ($6: a $1 initiation + $5 dues) could exhibit anything.**
  [Wikipedia; *Blind Man* text: "They say any artist paying six dollars may exhibit."]
- **The submission:** an ordinary urinal, entered under the name **"R. Mutt."** Because the rules
  guaranteed acceptance for any fee-payer, the work was **not formally "rejected."** Instead the
  board **suppressed it** — it was hidden behind a partition / out of public view and **never
  exhibited.** [Wikipedia; *Blind Man*: "Without discussion this article disappeared and never was
  exhibited."] **[FLAG: it was *suppressed/hidden*, not formally "rejected" by jury — the show had
  no jury. Authors often blur this.]**
- **Duchamp's resignation:** Duchamp sat on the Society's board (he was head of the hanging
  committee) and had submitted *Fountain* anonymously to test the "anything accepted" pledge. When
  the board suppressed it, he **resigned from the board in protest.** [Wikipedia]
- **The Stieglitz photo:** Duchamp had **Alfred Stieglitz** photograph the object at his **291
  gallery**. In the photo, a painting hangs behind it — **Marsden Hartley's *The Warriors*
  (1913).** Stieglitz noted the urinal had **"an oriental look about it — a cross between a Buddha
  and a Veiled Woman."** This single photo is the **only surviving image of the original.**
  [Wikipedia; PhilaMuseum]
- **The defense — *The Blind Man* no. 2 (May 1917):** the Dada magazine edited by **Marcel Duchamp,
  Henri-Pierre Roché, and Beatrice Wood** ran the Stieglitz photo opposite an unsigned editorial,
  **"The Richard Mutt Case,"** which mounted the defense (full text in §4). [PhilaMuseum; Bowdoin;
  Univ. of Iowa Dada Archive]
- **The "Mutt" pun:** Duchamp said **"Mutt comes from Mott Works"** — the **J. L. Mott Iron Works**,
  a well-known NYC plumbing manufacturer — which he altered to "Mutt," also nodding to the popular
  **comic strip *Mutt and Jeff***. **"R." = Richard**, French slang ("richard") for a moneybags/rich
  man. [Wikipedia, quoting Duchamp's interviews] **[FLAG: see §8 — whether the urinal actually came
  from J. L. Mott is itself disputed.]**

### The authorship debate — present as LIVE, not settled
- **The Baroness theory:** Art historians **Glyn Thompson** and **Julian Spalding** argue the work
  originated with **Baroness Elsa von Freytag-Loringhoven** (1874–1927), German Dada poet/artist.
  Their evidence: (a) Duchamp's **11 April 1917 letter to his sister Suzanne** — "**One of my female
  friends** under a masculine pseudonym, Richard Mutt, sent in a porcelain urinal as a sculpture"
  (the feminine *amie*); (b) Thompson's claim the **handwriting** on the urinal is the Baroness's;
  (c) she was in **Philadelphia** in 1917, where (he argues) this urinal model was sold, a city
  Duchamp never visited. [Artnet; Open Culture; Artsy; Art Newspaper; Wikipedia]
- **The rebuttal:** the theory was **strongly contested** in *The Burlington Magazine* (2019):
  **Bradley Bailey**, "The Origins of *Fountain*…/ the Baroness theory debunked" (no. 1399, Oct
  2019), and **Dawn Adès & Alastair Brotchie**, "Marcel Duchamp was not a thief" (no. 1401, Dec
  2019). Bailey surfaced a contemporaneous account by **Louise Norton**: Duchamp "**sent in** a
  porcelain urinal… the committee promptly threw it out and Marcel very angry promptly resigned."
  Adès argues the Baroness camp **mistranslated** the Suzanne letter (the urinal was sent *to the
  exhibition*, not delivered *to Duchamp by* a friend). They also note the **Baroness never claimed
  authorship**, despite being famously outspoken in her destitute final years. [Burlington Magazine;
  Atlas Press; Art Newspaper] **[FLAG: present both sides; do NOT state the Baroness made it as
  fact — the documentary weight currently favors Duchamp's authorship, but the debate is live.]**

## 3. WHY IT'S A BREAK ("the break" block — REQUIRED)

**Before:** for centuries a work of art was something an artist **made** — by hand, with skill, in
a medium (paint, marble, bronze). Value flowed from craft, beauty, originality of execution, and a
recognizable subject. Even radical modern movements before 1917 (Cubism, Futurism) still **painted
and sculpted** — they changed *how* the hand worked, not *that* the hand worked.

**After:** *Fountain* proposes that the artist's essential act is **choosing**, not making. A
manufactured object, untouched by the artist's hand, becomes art because an artist **selected it,
re-titled it, and re-framed it** ("placed it so that its useful significance disappeared… created a
new thought for that object"). [*Blind Man*] This is the **readymade**: art as **idea and decision**
rather than skill and craftsmanship. It severs art from beauty and technique and relocates it in
**concept and context** (and, implicitly, in the institutions — gallery, signature, title — that
confer art-status).

**The lineage it seeds:** *Fountain* is routinely called the **single most influential artwork of
the 20th century** (a 2004 poll of 500 art-world figures ranked it #1, above Picasso). [Wikipedia]
It is the foundation stone of **Conceptual art**, and the direct ancestor of everything from Warhol
and Pop's embrace of the mass-produced, to Minimalism's industrial objects, to the "found object"
in installation art. Concrete contrast for the read: a Renaissance altarpiece asks *how skillfully
was this made?*; *Fountain* asks *who decided this is art, and why?*

## 4. THE KEY STATEMENT (manifesto analog) — "The Richard Mutt Case"

**VERBATIM** (from *The Blind Man*, no. 2, May 1917; transcribed from the published page) — the
editorial was **unsigned**; it is commonly attributed to Duchamp but was produced by the magazine's
editors (Duchamp / Roché / **Beatrice Wood**, who has also been credited as primary author):

> They say any artist paying six dollars may exhibit.
>
> Mr. Richard Mutt sent in a fountain. Without discussion this article disappeared and never was
> exhibited.
>
> What were the grounds for refusing Mr. Mutt's fountain:—
>
> 1. Some contended it was immoral, vulgar.
> 2. Others, it was plagiarism, a plain piece of plumbing.
>
> Now Mr. Mutt's fountain is not immoral, that is absurd, no more than a bathtub is immoral. It is a
> fixture that you see every day in plumbers' show windows.
>
> Whether Mr. Mutt with his own hands made the fountain or not has no importance. He CHOSE it. He
> took an ordinary article of life, placed it so that its useful significance disappeared under the
> new title and point of view — created a new thought for that object.
>
> As for plumbing, that is absurd. The only works of art America has given are her plumbing and her
> bridges.

**Source for born-verified link:** transcription PDF at mariabuszek.com (KCAI PoMo seminar reader),
`https://mariabuszek.com/mariabuszek/kcai/PoMoSeminar/Readings/MDMutt.pdf`; the original spread is
reproduced in the **International Dada Archive, University of Iowa Libraries** and discussed by the
**Philadelphia Museum of Art** and **Bowdoin College Museum of Art** (which holds copies of *The
Blind Man* nos. 1 & 2). [mariabuszek PDF; Univ. of Iowa; PhilaMuseum; Bowdoin]

**Wording-variation flags [FLAG]:**
- **Attribution:** the mariabuszek PDF titles it "MARCEL DUCHAMP, 'The Richard Mutt Case'." In fact
  it ran **unsigned**; scholarship credits the editorial collective and frequently **Beatrice
  Wood** specifically. Do **not** caption it "by Duchamp" without qualification.
- **Punctuation:** some reproductions render "for refusing Mr. Mutt's fountain:" with a colon, others
  ":—". The numbered points 1/2 are sometimes printed inline. "CHOSE" is **capitalized in the
  original** — keep it.

## 5. LOOKING POINTS (~6, for the Stieglitz photo — prose pointers, NO coordinates)

1. **The inverted urinal.** The object is rotated roughly 90° from its normal wall-mounted
   orientation and laid on its back, so the drain holes and flat back face the viewer — the everyday
   function is disoriented at a glance. [object/photo]
2. **"R. Mutt 1917."** The pseudonymous signature is brushed in **black paint on the outer rim/flange**
   (lower-left of the form as photographed) — the only "authorial" mark on a factory object. [Tate;
   Wikipedia]
3. **The "Buddha" silhouette.** Critics and Stieglitz read the smooth, rounded, symmetrical curve as
   a **seated figure — "a cross between a Buddha and a Veiled Woman"** (Stieglitz). The accidental
   resemblance is part of why the photo, not just the object, became iconic. [Wikipedia]
4. **The pedestal/plinth.** Stieglitz set it on a **pedestal/base**, the convention of sculpture
   display — quietly insisting "treat this as sculpture." [PhilaMuseum; photo]
5. **The painting behind it — Marsden Hartley's *The Warriors* (1913).** The angular abstract
   composition hanging in the background grounds the photo at the **291 gallery** and frames the
   urinal within real avant-garde company. [Wikipedia; PhilaMuseum]
6. **The glaze and light.** Stieglitz lit the white porcelain so it **glows against the dark wall**,
   smoothing the industrial object into something almost reverent — the photographer's framing does
   real persuasive work. [PhilaMuseum]

## 6. PROVENANCE

- **Original (1917):** photographed by Stieglitz at 291; reproduced in *The Blind Man* no. 2;
  thereafter **lost — most likely discarded as trash.** No physical original survives. [Cabinet;
  Wikipedia/Tomkins]
- **Replicas:** authorized by Duchamp in **1950, 1953, 1963**, then the **1964 Galleria Schwarz
  edition of 8** (Milan), fabricated to scaled drawings under Duchamp's supervision and signed by
  him. Museums acquired the Schwarz numbers directly or via the market. [Cabinet; Tate]
- **Tate's piece (2/8):** the 1964 Schwarz replica; **purchased 1999 with assistance from the
  Friends of the Tate Gallery**, accession **T07573.** [Tate]
- **Market note:** 5/8 sold at **Sotheby's 1999 for $1,762,500**; a Schwarz proof sold at Phillips
  in 2002 — i.e., authorized **copies of a lost object** command millions. [Cabinet] (Good "money"
  beat for the read.)

## 8. APOCRYPHA / TRAPS (with corrected, sourced versions)

- **"The committee smashed/destroyed it."** **[MYTH]** No evidence it was smashed by the board. It
  was **suppressed/hidden** during the 1917 show; the **original later disappeared** and was
  probably **thrown out as rubbish** (Tomkins). Don't write "smashed by the committee." [Wikipedia]
- **"It was rejected by the jury."** **[TRAP]** The show had **no jury** — that was its whole point.
  It was **suppressed by the board** despite the no-jury rule, which is exactly what made it a
  scandal. [Wikipedia; *Blind Man*]
- **"The Baroness made it" (stated as fact).** **[OVERSTATED]** A **live, contested** theory
  (Thompson/Spalding) — **not** the consensus. The detailed 2019 *Burlington Magazine* rebuttals
  (Bailey; Adès & Brotchie) plus the absence of any claim by the Baroness herself leave Duchamp's
  authorship the better-documented position. Present as **debate**, never as settled fact in either
  direction. [Burlington Magazine; Atlas Press; Art Newspaper]
- **"It came from the J. L. Mott Iron Works."** **[CONTESTED]** This is **Duchamp's own stated
  source** (and the basis of the Mutt/Mott pun), but Thompson argues Mott **didn't stock this urinal
  model**, fueling the Philadelphia/Baroness claim. Safe to write: Duchamp **said** it came from
  J. L. Mott; the sourcing is disputed. [Wikipedia; Artnet]
- **"The Blind Man editorial was written by Duchamp."** **[TRAP]** It ran **unsigned**; authorship is
  shared among the editors and is often credited to **Beatrice Wood**. Caption carefully. [PhilaMuseum;
  Wikipedia]
- **"The original is in [a museum]."** **[FALSE]** Every museum *Fountain* is a **replica**; the 1917
  original is lost. [Cabinet; Tate]
- **Dimensions as a single fixed number.** **[TRAP]** Replicas differ (Tate 36×48×61 cm vs SFMOMA
  38.1×48.9×62.5 cm). Always name which replica. [Tate; SFMOMA]
