## BLOCKERS

### B1 — The verbatim Duchamp quote is attributed to the WRONG interview/source.
Draft (PART B, `LhoPun`, line ~165):
> "He said it in conversation with the critic **James Johnson Sweeney**, in an interview filmed in **1955 and broadcast in 1956**, later reprinted in *The Writings of Marcel Duchamp*."

This sourcing is **false**. Two independent authoritative sources — Toutfait and the Goodyear/USC-Scalar essay (the fact pack's own cited scholarly source) — attribute this exact quote to:
> **Duchamp, radio interview with Herbert Crehan, broadcast by WBAI, New York, in 1961** (published in *Evidence*, no. 3).

The Sweeney conversation is a real, separate 1955 interview (filmed/broadcast 1955–56, in *The Writings of Marcel Duchamp*), but it is **not** the source of the "becomes a man" line. The draft has welded the correct quote to a confidently-stated but incorrect provenance — exactly the kind of fabricated-source error the gate exists to catch. Note the fact pack itself only *guessed* "Sweeney … p.133" and flagged it "Confirm against *The Writings* p.133 before final ship" — that confirmation FAILS; the real source is Crehan/WBAI/1961.

**EXACT corrected text** (replace the sentence at line ~165):
> He said it in a radio interview with **Herbert Crehan**, broadcast by **WBAI, New York, in 1961** (published in the journal *Evidence*). Watch the word **"not."**

(The quote body itself at line ~162 is verbatim-correct and KEEPS the load-bearing "not" — verified present in both authoritative sources. Do not change the quote text. Only the attribution sentence is wrong.)

## NICE (optional polish, not required to ship)

### N1 — "postcard" is the dominant framing but a scholarly source disputes it.
Draft leans throughout on "cheap printed postcard reproduction." Wikipedia, MoMA, WikiArt, Artchive, and the Tumblr cataloguing all say "postcard," and the fact pack's catalogued medium is "pencil on a printed reproduction (postcard)." HOWEVER Toutfait (scholarly) states: *"Contrary to common belief, the original was not a postcard … an inexpensive chromolithographic color reproduction purchased from a shop near the Louvre on rue de Rivoli."* Because the draft already softens to "printed reproduction" in the medium field and the encyclopedic consensus is "postcard," this is defensible as-is. Optional hedge if desired: "a cheap printed reproduction (usually described as a postcard)." Not a blocker.

### N2 — Man Ray held the first replica (1930), a true detail the draft omits.
Toutfait notes the first replica was made early 1930 and possessed by Man Ray. The draft's afterlife/provenance is accurate without it (it says "Duchamp's own later replicas," Centre Pompidou, Philadelphia Boîte-en-valise, 1965 *Shaved*). Adding the Man Ray 1930 first-replica datum would sharpen the afterlife but is not required.
