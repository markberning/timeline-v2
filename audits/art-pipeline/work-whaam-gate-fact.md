## FINDINGS

### [NICE-1] Const comment says "forty-year-old"; chapter prose says "thirty-seven-year-old"
- **Where:** PART A const header comment (`hook`/blurb region, line ~53) describes
  "A forty-year-old abstract painter switches…"; PART B `WhamSource` prose says
  "thirty-seven-year-old painter" in **1961**.
- **Truth:** Lichtenstein b. Oct 1923. In 1961 he was **37** (turning 38 in Oct 1961).
  By the time *Whaam!* was painted (1963) he was 39–40. Neither is wrong in its own
  frame (the const blurb is loosely scene-setting; the prose is dated to 1961), but
  the two reader-facing numbers differ. The **prose "thirty-seven-year-old / 1961"
  is the precise one** and is the one readers see. The const `source` section blurb
  itself says "A forty-year-old abstract painter switches, almost overnight" — this
  is the visible card blurb.
- **Suggestion (optional):** for internal consistency, change the const blurb's
  "forty-year-old" to "thirty-seven-year-old" (matching the 1961 framing), or to a
  non-numeric "a midcareer abstract painter." Not load-bearing; ship either way.

### [NICE-2] "graphite pencil" omitted from the shown medium string
- **Where:** const `medium: 'Magna acrylic and oil on canvas (two panels)'`.
- **Truth:** Tate's own short credit is exactly "acrylic paint and oil paint on
  canvas" (no graphite), so the draft's string is faithful to Tate. The catalogue
  raisonné adds "graphite pencil" (the under-drawing). The fact pack flagged this as
  optional. **Not an error** — Tate-faithful. Mentioning graphite would be more
  complete but is not required and Tate omits it too. No change needed.

### [NICE-3] Source nuance — main aircraft from Grandenetti's #90, layout from Novick's #89
- **Where:** draft consistently credits **Novick / #89 / "Star Jockey"** as THE source.
- **Truth:** This is the standard and correct headline attribution (Wikipedia, CBR,
  Tate all lead with Novick / #89). Scholarship adds that the principal *aircraft*
  image derives from **Jerry Grandenetti's "Wingmate of Doom," #90 (April 1962)**,
  while the composition/layout is Novick's #89. The draft already names Grandenetti
  among the uncredited artists in the afterlife chapter, so the picture is honest.
- **Suggestion (optional, NICE only):** the looking/source chapters could add one
  clause noting the aircraft itself traces to a second Novick-adjacent panel
  (#90, Grandenetti). Not required — the headline "Novick / #89" is correct and the
  multi-artist credit point is already made. Do NOT over-complicate the body.
