## LOOKING

### [FIX] The brushwork is asserted, never made visible — the one looking beat the gate asks for and the draft underdelivers
The brief explicitly lists "the brushwork" as a thing the looking section must
make me SEE. The draft repeatedly tells me there's "no shading, no modeling,"
"big flat areas of saturated color butted up against each other," but it never
guides my eye to HOW the paint sits — flat opaque fields, the thick dark
bounding contour around the figures (the very thing Signac's own quoted letter
names: "a line as thick as your thumb"). The bounding contour line is a
named LOOKING target in the brief and it appears in the PART A annotation
("bounding contour lines") and in the Signac quote, but the LOOKING prose never
tells me to find it on the figures.

`BonLooking`, "The cast" para handles the arabesque (the curve) beautifully but
stops short of the OUTLINE:
> "Their bodies are drawn as long, snaking, sinuous curves, what artists call
> **arabesques**..."

**Fix:** add one or two sentences in `BonLooking` directing the eye to the
heavy dark outline that pens each figure and color field in — e.g. "Look at how
each figure is fenced by a single dark contour line, drawn thick, the way a
stained-glass leading holds a pane of color; Matisse lets the line do the work
that shading used to do." This (a) delivers the brief's brushwork/contour
target, (b) sets up Signac's "line as thick as your thumb" so the quote reads
as describing something the reader has already seen, and (c) glosses the
flat-color-with-outline technique concretely. Right now Signac names the thumb-
thick line in `BonReception` and the reader has never been shown it.

### [NICE] Everything else the brief asks me to SEE is delivered
- Ring of dancers at back center (seed of *The Dance*): yes, "far back, in the
  center distance... a small ring of figures dances, joined hand to hand," with
  the payoff held and then paid. Strong.
- Piping shepherd: yes, "a seated nude plays a double pipe... the music of
  shepherds," and the scale-game (child vs. adult) is genuinely guided looking.
- Reclining / embracing nudes: yes, foreground recliners + lower-right embrace,

- Arbitrary non-local color fields: yes — "The ground is not green; it runs hot

  The sky is not blue." This is the model of concrete guided looking.
- Flattened space + the arching-tree "curtain": yes, "arching across the top
  like a stage curtain... press everything forward, flat against the surface."
  Excellent.

### [NICE] The deliberate-scale-wrongness beat is the standout

## CLARITY

### [FIX] "cadmium colors" / "cadmium-yellow" used before it's glossed
`BonSalon`, "The jury-free show":
> "The **cadmium** colors and the warped, perspective-free space drew a public
> outcry..."

"Cadmium" appears here as a first use with no gloss, and again in `BonReception`
("The cadmium colors and the warped... space" — wait, that's `BonSalon`), and
the actual gloss-by-context only arrives in `BonAfterlife` ("heavy
cadmium-yellow passages are slowly degrading, the bright pigment turning chalky
and brown"). A zero-prior-knowledge reader meets "cadmium colors" with no idea
it means a class of intense modern synthetic pigments. **Fix:** at first use in
`BonSalon`, gloss lightly — e.g. "the **cadmium** colors (the intense, modern,
factory-made pigments, cadmium yellows and reds, that let painters hit a
brightness older paints couldn't)..." Then the `BonAfterlife` decay note reads
as a callback rather than the first definition.

### [FIX] "pointillism" assumed equivalent to Neo-Impressionism without quite landing for a true novice
`BonMaking`, "From dots to flat fields":
> "the technique of **Neo-Impressionism** (the method, also called pointillism,
> of building an image out of small separate touches of pure color meant to mix
> in the viewer's eye)."

This is actually a GOOD gloss — flagging it only as [FIX]-lite. The parenthetical
defines both terms cleanly. The one wrinkle: later, `BonReception` says "the
dot-by-dot Neo-Impressionist method" and "the careful pure-color dots" without
re-gloss, which is correct house style (gloss once per first use). No change
needed to `BonReception`. **Verify only:** confirm `BonMaking` runs BEFORE
`BonReception` in the section order (registry comment lists making → ... →
reception, so yes). No fix required beyond keeping that order; noting it so a
re-sequence doesn't strand the gloss.
