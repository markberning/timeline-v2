## [BLOCKER] — must fix before ship

### BLOCKER 1 — The 1968 Stockholm boxes were CARDBOARD supplied by the Brillo company, not a Warhol-made "second batch / second campaign"
The draft repeatedly frames the 1968 Stockholm boxes as a Warhol fabrication run parallel
to the 1964 plywood originals:

- PART A provenance note (row 2): *"A second campaign was made in Stockholm in connection with Warhol's 1968 retrospective."*
- PART B `BrilAfterlife`: *"A second batch was produced in **Stockholm** in connection with Warhol's 1968 retrospective."*

**Verified fact:** For the February 1968 Moderna Museet retrospective, the museum could not
afford to ship the New York plywood boxes. Intern Kasper König wrote to the Brillo company
(Purex), which sent ~500 **empty cardboard cartons, flat-packed, at ~20¢ each**, assembled
on-site by museum staff. They are **cardboard, not wood**, and are **not considered genuine
Warhols** (collectible "Stockholm type" only). Calling them a Warhol "campaign/batch"
alongside the 1964 originals is materially inaccurate and, worse, it muddies the very
distinction the Malmö paragraph depends on (1964 wood originals vs. later non-originals).
(Sources: Moderna Museet *Warhol 1968*; Art&Object; Wikipedia *Brillo Boxes*.)

**Corrected text (PART B `BrilAfterlife`, replace the Stockholm sentence):**
> For Warhol's 1968 Moderna Museet retrospective in Stockholm, the museum could not afford to ship the heavy New York originals, so it had the Brillo company send roughly five hundred empty cardboard cartons, flat-packed, assembled on the spot — printed cardboard, not Warhol-built wood, and not counted as genuine Warhols.

**Corrected text (PART A provenance row 2 note, replace the Stockholm sentence):**
> For Warhol's 1968 Stockholm retrospective the museum used roughly 500 empty cardboard Brillo cartons sent flat-packed by the Brillo company and assembled on-site — cardboard, not Warhol-built wood.

(If brevity is preferred, the simplest safe fix is to drop the Stockholm mention entirely
from both surfaces; it is optional context, and the present wording is what makes it wrong.)

## [FIX] — should fix; not ship-blocking alone

### FIX 1 — Malmö model: "a 1968-type box" overstates a Warhol provenance the verified record denies
PART A provenance row 3 and PART B both say Hultén used "a 1968-type box as the model."
The factpack and sources support that Hultén *represented* the Malmö boxes as 1968 work
(falsely), and that the model was a Stockholm/1968-type carton. This is defensible but
borderline: the cleaner, fully-verified statement is that he commissioned plywood boxes in
Malmö and **falsely certified/sold them as 1968 originals**. The draft already says the
ruling was "copies," so add the false-certification fact, which is the load-bearing point
(it's why it's a scandal). Suggested tightening of the PART B sentence:
> …commissioned about 105 plywood Brillo boxes from carpenters in Malmö, then certified and sold some as 1968 "originals" made to Warhol's instructions.

This is a precision fix, not an error of fact. The draft's current wording is not wrong,
just thinner than the record.

### FIX 2 — "Billy Linich (Billy Name)" as a box-fabrication assistant is loosely sourced
PART B `BrilFactory` names *"Gerard Malanga and Billy Linich (Billy Name)"* as the
silkscreening assistants on the boxes. Malanga's role is well-documented (he "directed
product acquisition" and was Warhol's principal silkscreen assistant 1963–64). Billy Name
is firmly a Factory figure of the era but his specific role *on the Brillo fabrication* is
not separately confirmed in this pass. Lowest-risk fix: keep Malanga as the named box
assistant and soften Name to the general Factory context (e.g., "his Factory assistants,
among them Gerard Malanga") — or leave as-is only if the factpack's source explicitly tied
Name to the boxes (it does not in the text provided). Recommend softening.

  is a true qualifier if the author wants airtight "first shown" language.
