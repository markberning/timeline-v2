## 1. COMPREHENSIVENESS

### [FIX] FIX-1 — Name the founding of Surrealism (1924) to situate the work in its movement
The draft links frottage to "Surrealist automatic writing" and "André Breton and his Surrealist circle" (break section) but never states that Surrealism was *founded as a movement in 1924*, the year before the floorboards. The work cannot be properly proportioned against its movement without that anchor, and the gate brief specifically asks for the 1924/1925/1926 sequence to be legible. This is the one place the draft leaves the reader to supply the movement's existence.

**Suggested text** — add one sentence to `ErnBreak`, in the "break · after" paragraph that introduces automatic writing (after "…without the conscious mind censoring it."):

> Breton had launched Surrealism as a movement only the year before, in **1924**, around exactly this idea — that the unconscious, let off its leash, was the real source of art — so frottage arrived as the perfect visual proof of a program that was barely a year old.

*(This also tightens the proportion/chronology axis: 1924 founded → 1925 frottages → 1926 portfolio, all three now on the page.)*

### [FIX] FIX-2 — The "automatism" cousin claim is asserted but the mechanism is only half-shown
The draft says frottage "is automatism with a pencil" and that "the eye reading shapes into random marks is the picture-making cousin of automatic writing" — good. But it never closes the loop on *what makes them cousins*: both are techniques to get the **conscious, deliberating mind out of the way** so the unconscious supplies the content. The draft has the analogy without the shared mechanism, which is the actual reason it matters to Surrealism. This is the single facet the factpack flags as essential (§2 "visual cousin of automatism") that lands only partway.

**Suggested text** — extend the existing sentence in `ErnBreak` ("…the unconscious mind write without the conscious mind censoring it. **Frottage is automatism with a pencil.**") to:

> …without the conscious mind censoring it. Frottage is the same maneuver with a pencil: in both, the trick is to **get the deliberate, deciding mind out of the way** — the writer lets the hand run, Ernst lets the floorboard supply the marks — so that whatever surfaces comes from somewhere other than conscious intention. That is precisely the prize the Surrealists were after.

### [NICE] NICE-2 — Grattage/décalcomania forward line is correct but décalcomania's dating is loose
The afterlife and method sections name grattage (1927) and décalcomania as the children of frottage. Grattage's 1927 date is right and well-handled. Décalcomania is given no date and is attributed loosely to "later" / "the broader Surrealist program." For accuracy: décalcomania as a Surrealist method is associated first with **Óscar Domínguez (c. 1936)** and was *adopted* by Ernst around 1939–40 — Ernst did not originate it the way he did frottage and grattage. The draft's phrasing ("Ernst himself carried it straight into grattage in 1927… and later into décalcomania") slightly implies Ernst-as-originator of décalcomania too.

**Suggested text** — in `ErnAfterlife`, change "…and later into décalcomania" to:

> …and, around 1939, took up **décalcomania** (pressing and peeling paint, a chance technique the Surrealist Óscar Domínguez had pioneered) —

*(Low priority: the draft never explicitly claims Ernst invented décalcomania, so this is a precision nicety, not an error to block on. If left as-is it is defensible.)*

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### Genius-myth — PASS (no action)
The draft leans the correct way and sustains it. Ernst is consistently framed as a **reader of accident**, not a lone genius inventing forms: hook ("simply read them out"), ErnMethod ("He was not inventing the forms out of his own head; he was finding them… The wood did half the work and the eye did the other half"), ErnBreak ("from author to… spectator"), ErnAfterlife ("They were already in the wood. He just rubbed a pencil over it and read them out."). He is situated in Dada (Arp "his old Dada comrade," "Even Dada collage, Ernst's own earlier mode") and Surrealism (Breton, automatism). This is exactly what the brief asks for.

### [FIX] FIX-3 — Scope the implicit first/only claim on "chance as a method"
The draft does NOT say Ernst invented rubbing — it correctly opens ErnMethod with "Frottage is so simple a child does it on a coin," which acknowledges rubbing is ordinary and old. **Good.** But the afterlife and break sections make a stronger, unscoped historical claim: that frottage made "chance and found texture a *legitimate* engine for *serious* art" — phrased as if Ernst were *first* to bring chance into fine art. That overclaims. Chance procedures predate him within the same avant-garde: **Dada** already used chance deliberately (Arp's own "chance-arranged" collages, c. 1916–17; Duchamp's *3 Standard Stoppages*, 1913–14). The honest, scoped claim is: frottage was the **first repeatable, drawing-based chance method** Ernst built — and the one that carried chance from Dada gesture into a *systematic Surrealist process*. Rubbing-as-copying is also ancient (2nd-c. AD rubbings, brass rubbings; cf. the Hammer Museum's *Apparitions: Frottages and Rubbings from 1860 to Now*).

**Suggested text** — in `ErnBreak`, the "First, …" item, change "chance and found texture become a legitimate engine for fine art — not a party trick but a generative method" to:

> chance and found texture become a **repeatable, systematic engine** for fine art. Chance was not new to the avant-garde — Ernst's own Dada circle, Arp included, had been letting accident arrange collages a decade earlier — but frottage turned it from a one-off gesture into a method you could run again and again, a generative procedure rather than a party trick,

**And** in `ErnAfterlife` ("Frottage was a proof of concept: it showed that chance and found texture could be a real engine for serious art, not a novelty."), append a clause to scope it:

> …a real, repeatable engine for serious art — Dada had toyed with chance, but Ernst made it a method you could teach.

*(This is a FIX not a NICE because "legitimate engine for fine art," read straight, implies Ernst legitimized chance from scratch, which is precisely the first/only overclaim the gate is charged with catching. The rubbing-history side is already handled by the "child does it on a coin" line, so no separate fix is needed there.)*

### Proportion / anachronism — PASS (one tightening)
Dates are correct and the 1925-vs-1926 distinction is handled carefully and repeatedly (ErnFloorboards explicitly: "Frottages 1925, portfolio 1926, two separate things"). Grattage 1927 correct. No anachronism. The only proportion gap is the missing 1924 Surrealism-founding anchor, covered by FIX-1.

### [NICE] NICE-1 — "discovered/born" language is slightly heroic against the scoped claim
ErnFloorboards ends "Frottage is born." and the stat/section eyebrows use "discovered." Given FIX-3 (rubbing is old; Ernst's scoped achievement is the *method*, not the act of rubbing), "Frottage is born" reads a touch more like a eureka-invention than a method-formation. Minor; the surrounding prose already does the un-myth-ing work.

**Optional text** — change "Frottage is born." to "Frottage — as a deliberate method — is born." Low priority; defensible as-is given the heavy contextualizing prose around it.
