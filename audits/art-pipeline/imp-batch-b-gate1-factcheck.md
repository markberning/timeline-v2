## BATCH-WIDE TOP 5 MUST-FIXES

1. **[L'Absinthe] Andrée's hat described as "small black-trimmed hat"** — the actual hat is a large, elaborate, light-colored (lavender/gray/cream) hat, clearly visible in the canvas and confirmed in multiple descriptions. This is a wrong visual description on a canvas-facing claim.
2. **[L'Absinthe] Desboutin's jacket described as "brown jacket"** — the jacket is dark/black, not brown, confirmed both visually and in secondary sources ("scruffily dressed in black").
3. **[Dance Class] Stage mothers located "left side, on benches against the left wall"** — in the Met canvas the dark-dressed observers are in the upper-right area (near the second room/doorway), not on the left wall. The Look Closer pointer #6 is also wrong on the same point.
4. **[Dance Class] Perrot painted over "an earlier male figure identified by scholarly consensus as Louis Mérante"** — the X-ray of the Met version shows an **anonymous/unidentified** dance master under Perrot, not Mérante. Mérante is associated with the Orsay version. The draft overstates the identification as "scholarly consensus" for the Met canvas.
5. **[Dance Class / Provenance] Faure "sold it [to Payne]... April 4, 1898, for $25,000"** — the April 4 date is the Durand-Ruel → Payne transfer, not a direct Faure → Payne sale. Faure sold to Durand-Ruel on February 19, 1898 (for 10,000 francs); D-R sold to Payne on April 4, 1898 (for $25,000). The draft collapses two steps into one transaction and attributes the April 4 date and $25,000 price to Faure directly.

## WORK 1 — Edgar Degas, *The Dance Class* (1874), Metropolitan Museum

### Hard facts and provenance

**Claim:** "Commissioned in 1873 by Jean-Baptiste Faure (1830–1914)... commission price: 5,000 francs... delivered November 1874."
✅ CONFIRMED — multiple sources corroborate the 1873 commission, 5,000-franc fee, and November 1874 delivery. Met provenance records concur.

**Claim (Chapter 5 / Fact Ledger):** "Faure sold it... on **April 4, 1898**... to **Colonel Oliver H. Payne**... for **$25,000**."
❌ WRONG (MUST-FIX) — The provenance is a two-step sale, not a direct Faure→Payne transaction. Faure sold to Durand-Ruel on **February 19, 1898** (for **10,000 francs**); Durand-Ruel then sold to Payne on **April 4, 1898** (for $25,000). The draft's parenthetical in Chapter 5 partially acknowledges Durand-Ruel as "the dealer in the middle" but then in the Fact Ledger attributes the April 4 date and $25,000 price directly to Faure. The prose reads "Faure sold it... on April 4, 1898... $25,000" which is incorrect — those figures belong to the D-R→Payne leg. Correct to: Faure → Durand-Ruel, Feb 19, 1898 (10,000 francs); Durand-Ruel → Payne, Apr 4, 1898 ($25,000). Source: Met provenance record; confirmed by Grokipedia/Art of Museums cross-reference.

**Claim:** "Bequest of Mrs. Harry Payne Bingham, 1986; accessioned 1987.47.1."
✅ CONFIRMED — Met collection record, accession 1987.47.1, credit line: Bequest of Mrs. Harry Payne Bingham, 1986.

**Claim:** "Shown at the **2nd Impressionist Exhibition, April 1876**, lent by Faure."
✅ CONFIRMED — multiple sources confirm debut at the 2nd Impressionist Exhibition, 1876. The painting was still being worked on when the 1st show opened in April 1874 and was not in it.

**Claim:** "Not in the 1st Impressionist Exhibition of 1874."
✅ CONFIRMED and correctly refused in prose (Chapter 4 opening).

### X-ray / Perrot over Mérante

**Claim (Chapter 2, Fact Ledger):** "The original figure, beneath, was a different man. Comparing the X-ray to Degas's other works of the period, scholars are reasonably sure who the first man was: **Louis Mérante** (1828–1887), the Opéra's actual ballet master..."
❌ WRONG (MUST-FIX) — The X-ray of the **Met version** reveals that Degas replaced "an **anonymous/unidentified** dance master" (seen from the back) with Jules Perrot. The specific identification as Mérante is associated primarily with the **Orsay version** ("Perrot was painted over another male teacher, thought to be Louis Mérante" — Wikipedia on the Orsay painting). For the Met canvas, the under-figure is not definitively identified as Mérante; multiple scholarly sources describe it as an anonymous or unidentified male figure seen from the back. The draft (and Fact Ledger) present this as "identified by scholarly consensus as Louis Mérante" for the Met version, which overstates the identification. The correction: the Met X-ray shows an anonymous dance master replaced by Perrot; the Mérante identification belongs to the Orsay version.
  - Exact draft sentences to fix: "The original figure, beneath, was a different man... scholars are reasonably sure who the first man was: **Louis Mérante** (1828–1887), the Opéra's *actual* ballet master at the time Degas started the picture." And Fact Ledger: "Right-foreground figure is **Jules Perrot** (1810–1892), retired star, painted *over* an earlier male figure identified by scholarly consensus as **Louis Mérante** (1828–1887)."
  - Sources: Multiple searches; search result quote: "X-ray photographs and associated drawings reveal that the painting originally showed the foreground dancer with a fan posed looking out at the viewer and leaning against the piano, while the **unidentified** dance master was seen from the back."

✅ CONFIRMED — Perrot is in the right foreground; verified visually.

✅ CONFIRMED — matches canvas.

✅ CONFIRMED — a figure is cropped at the right edge; verified visually.

**Pointer 5 (watering can):** "On the floorboards, **just to the left of the central group**."
⚠️ SHOULD-FIX — Multiple sources place the watering can in the **lower left corner, under/near the piano** (alongside violin case and other studio items), not "just to the left of the central group." The canvas image confirms the watering can is on the left side near the instruments, not center-left. The prose description is close but the "left of the central group" phrasing is imprecise. Suggest: "lower left, at the base of the piano."

**Pointer 6 (stage mothers):** "**Left side**, on benches against the **left wall**, in shadow."
❌ WRONG (MUST-FIX) — The dark-dressed observers (stage mothers) in the Met canvas are in the **upper right** area, near the secondary room visible through the wide squared opening at the upper right. They are NOT on the left wall. The left side of the painting shows the piano, violin case, and studio props. The main narrative prose in Chapter 1 and Chapter 3 also places "stage mothers" with "sitting on the benches in dark dresses... a row of guardians" at the left — this is wrong for the Met canvas.
  - Sources: "In the background, to the right, can be seen some of the dancers' mothers" (search result from NGA/scholarly description); canvas image verified.

**Pointer 7 (Guillaume Tell poster):** "Back wall, behind the dancers, partly visible."

## WORK 2 — Edgar Degas, *L'Absinthe* (1875–76), Musée d'Orsay

### Exhibition history

**Claim (show chapter, Fact Ledger):** "Shown at the **2nd Impressionist Exhibition, Paris, April 1876**, as *Dans un café*."
✅ CONFIRMED — The degas-catalogue.com catalogue raisonné entry (MS917) shows: "Paris, 11 rue Le Peletier, 1876, n° 52 (?)" — placing it at the 2nd Impressionist Exhibition. Cross-confirmed by impressionistarts.com (second exhibition) and multiple other sources. The Wikipedia entry claiming only the 3rd Exhibition (1877) is incomplete — the painting was shown at both, with the 3rd showing listed as "Hors catalogue" (outside the official catalogue). The draft is correct to lead with the 2nd Exhibition.

**Claim (show chapter, Fact Ledger):** "[shown at the] 2nd Impressionist Exhibition **at rue Le Peletier**."
✅ CONFIRMED — the 2nd Impressionist Exhibition was at 11 rue Le Peletier.

**Claim (show chapter):** "[the 1893 Grafton Gallery scandal was] **seventeen years later**."
✅ CONFIRMED — 1876 to 1893 = 17 years.

### Sitter descriptions and visual claims

**Claim (making chapter):** "a **small black-trimmed hat**, perched rather than worn."
❌ WRONG (MUST-FIX) — Looking at the actual canvas, Andrée is wearing a **large, elaborate, light-colored hat** — clearly visible as a prominent lavender/gray structured hat with decorative elements. This is one of the most distinctive visual features of the painting. Multiple art descriptions refer to it as a "large hat adorned with a bow." It is emphatically not a small black-trimmed hat. This error appears in both the `making` chapter and the `looking` chapter.
  - Exact draft sentence: "She wears a pale, almost cream-white dress with a **pinkish-cream jacket** over it; on her head is a **small black-trimmed hat**, perched rather than worn."
  - The Look Closer pointer does not explicitly describe the hat color but calls it "small" in the pointer text — the hat is large, not small.
  - Source: Canvas image verified; multiple descriptions confirm "large hat adorned with a bow."

**Claim (making / looking chapters):** "[Desboutin wears] a **brown jacket**."
❌ WRONG (MUST-FIX) — Desboutin's jacket is **dark, nearly black**, not brown. This is visible in the canvas (dense dark paint for his jacket), and confirmed by secondary sources: "the man sitting next to her, **scruffily dressed in black**." The fact pack correctly describes him as wearing "a brown jacket" — this appears to be an error in the fact pack itself that carried into the draft.
  - Exact draft sentences: "The bearded man beside her — in the **brown jacket** and the battered tall hat" (making); "He wears a **brown jacket** that takes up a lot of the picture's right side — a thick, lived-in brown, painted in dense strokes" (looking).
  - Source: Canvas image; search result: "the man sitting next to her, scruffily dressed in black."

**Claim:** "The two figures are pushed into the **upper-right corner** of the canvas."
⚠️ SHOULD-FIX — The figures are in the upper portion of the canvas, right of center, but not as far to the corner as the draft implies. They occupy the upper central-to-right area. The zig-zag of tables runs from lower-left. "Upper right" is approximately accurate but "corner" is overstated. The Look Closer pointer is more accurate ("well above center, well right of center, taking up maybe a third of the picture"). This is a minor shading issue. SHOULD-FIX for precision.

**Claim:** "[Desboutin's] clay pipe... eyes are turned **off to the right**, out of the picture."
✅ CONFIRMED — Desboutin holds a pipe and looks to the right/out of frame; canvas verified.

**Claim:** "In front of her, on the table, stands a **tall stemmed glass** of milky-pale, slightly greenish liquid."
✅ CONFIRMED — the glass is visible as a tall stemmed glass with pale/milky liquid; canvas verified.

**Claim:** "[Desboutin's drink] is not her glass, and... his glass is not her glass."
✅ CONFIRMED — visually, Desboutin's drink appears to be a darker/different vessel. The canvas confirms the two drinks are distinct.

**Claim:** "Setting is Café de la Nouvelle-Athènes, Place Pigalle (NOT Café Guerbois)."
✅ CONFIRMED — standard art history fact, properly stated and the Guerbois error properly refused.

**Claim:** "Sitters are Ellen Andrée (1857–1925) and Marcellin Desboutin (1823–1902)."
✅ CONFIRMED.

### Provenance

**Claim:** "Captain Henry Hill (1812–1882), Brighton — collector and tailor."
⚠️ SHOULD-FIX — The dates 1812–1882 come from the fact pack, but the degas-catalogue.com and Wikipedia sources do not confirm Hill's birth/death years explicitly (the catalogue just shows "1876–1892" as his ownership window). The claim he died in 1882 but the painting remained in "his estate" until 1892 (ten years) is internally consistent with "Hill estate → 1892 Christie's sale" — but if he died in 1882, the ten-year gap before auction should be explained. The draft says "Hill died. His collection went to his estate. 1892. The estate sold off the Degas pictures at Christie's" — this is fine but leaves an implicit 10-year gap without comment. No evidence found to contradict 1812–1882; flag as SHOULD VERIFY.

**Claim:** "Christie's London, **February 19, 1892**, lot 209 (sold as *Figures at a Café*)."
✅ CONFIRMED — degas-catalogue.com provenance confirms "Hôtel Drouot" — wait, the fact pack says Christie's London and the draft says Christie's. The degas-catalogue.com entry says "Christie's London" for the 1892 sale. The draft is correct. But note: one source says "Hôtel Drouot" — this appears to be a different source's error; Christie's London is the established record.

**Claim:** "The buyer was **Comte Isaac de Camondo** (1851–1911)... **Camondo bequest to the Musée du Louvre, 1911**."
✅ CONFIRMED — Camondo bequest to Louvre in 1911 is standard record.

**Claim:** "Andrée's 1921 Fénéon interview, Théâtre Édouard VII Paris."
⚠️ UNSUPPORTED (flag, not MUST-FIX) — Independent web verification could not locate a third-party confirmation of the 1921 Fénéon interview details (that Andrée said her glass held absinthe for the picture, Desboutin's was mazagran, she was not an absinthe drinker). The fact pack cites it as DOCUMENTED, referencing the degas-catalogue.com MS917 entry and Mental Floss (which states "Degas himself reportedly said..." rather than citing Andrée directly). The narrative is broadly accepted in Degas scholarship, but the specific 1921 Fénéon-Andrée interview at the Théâtre Édouard VII is not corroborated independently in this verification pass. The claim is in the fact pack as DOCUMENTED; this checker cannot confirm or refute it — it should be treated as requiring source citation. The draft presents it as flat fact in the prose ("Andrée publicly set the record straight... the full record of her response is the **1921 interview** with the critic **Félix Fénéon**"). If the degas-catalogue.com or catalogue raisonné documentation is solid, this is fine; flag for the integration step to confirm the primary source exists.

**Claim:** "[Desboutin's] glass holds **mazagran** (cold, sweetened black coffee)."
✅ CONFIRMED as a well-attested tradition — multiple sources reference Desboutin's drink as mazagran (cold coffee), consistent with Andrée's account. Canvas verified: his drink is visibly different from Andrée's absinthe glass.

### Other

## SHOULD-FIX SUMMARY

Below are findings rated SHOULD-FIX (factually problematic but not a flat falsehood or canvas-visible error):

1. **[Dance Class / Watering Can]** The Look Closer pointer places the watering can "just to the left of the central group." Multiple sources and the canvas place it at the **lower left corner, at the base of the piano / among the studio instruments** — not in the central area. Suggest correcting the "where" field.

2. **[Dance Class / Provenance]** The Faure → Payne transaction: Faure sold to Durand-Ruel on **Feb 19, 1898**; Durand-Ruel to Payne on **Apr 4, 1898**. The draft's parenthetical in Chapter 5 partially acknowledges D-R as broker but the Fact Ledger attributes the April 4 / $25,000 figure to Faure directly. Correct the Fact Ledger and the afterlife chapter framing.

3. **[L'Absinthe / Exhibition]** The Wikipedia article states the painting was shown at the **3rd** Impressionist Exhibition (1877), not the 2nd.
