## Grading key
- **STRONG** — fires on all cylinders; publish as-is
- **GOOD** — solid; minor fixes only
- **NEEDS WORK** — real gap; targeted rewrite of specific passages
- **REWRITE** — the chapter fails its core job; substantial rework required

MUST-FIX = ship-blocking (missing looking, REWRITE/NEEDS WORK grade, buried best-line, wrong image claim)
SHOULD-FIX = quality improvement, not a blocker

### MUST-FIX

**`reception` — ch3, second paragraph of "A slow fame" section: buried best-line.**

This is the sharpest observation in the chapter. It is the last sentence of the final full paragraph and is sitting in ordinary `proseStyle` — that's fine — but the sentence is doing too much work to end a paragraph that trails off into the next chapter tease. It needs to either close the section as a stronger final beat, or the tease that follows it needs to be cut so this lands as the real end. Right now the tease paragraph after it ("That slow climb is the runway…") dilutes its punch.

**Fix:** Move "The Angelus was being lifted…" to close the section (final sentence of the section), and collapse the tease paragraph into the meaning chapter's opener or cut it. The current tease paragraph is fine connective tissue but it's burying the best line in the chapter.

- `looking` ch2: "two-thirds sky" claim — image shows sky occupying roughly the top 55–60% of canvas, not two-thirds (the horizon is about 40% from the bottom). This is a minor over-claim but it's in the looking chapter, which is the most verifiable. **SHOULD-FIX:** adjust to "more than half" or "the top half and more."

This is a strong, specific closer — but it arrives after a paragraph that has already done the argument twice. The paragraph overexplains the "diagram vs. description" point. **MUST-FIX:** Tighten the middle of that paragraph by about 40 words; let the closing sentence land with room.

**`looking` — "A scrum of well-dressed officials and cronies is scrambling on the ground to gather them up and carry them off, toward a government building at the lower left."**

**`looking` — "one of them doubles over a great hamper, loading the king's next mouthful."**

Image check: At the base of the plank, a figure does appear to be loading/lifting a basket or hamper. ✓ Confirmed.

### MUST-FIX

**`looking` ch2 — The boy on the right is misdescribed.**

The text says: "At the right end of the bench, a boy has fallen asleep, slumped sideways, head dropped."

**Image check:** Looking at the painting, the right end of the front bench does NOT clearly show a sleeping boy in that position. What is visible on the right side of the front bench is the OLD WOMAN — who sits center-to-right — and a figure in a dark hat behind/beside her. There is no obviously sleeping boy on the front bench at right. The sleeping-boy figure is less clearly placed than the text implies. The woman in the center is the most prominent figure; to her left is the nursing mother; to her right, in the image, the bench appears occupied by a figure who may be a child but is not clearly "slumped sideways" in the image as reproduced.

More significantly: the text describes "A dark box or chest, the family's luggage, sits in the lower-right corner beside him." In the image, the lower-right area is dark but does not show a clearly distinct box/chest. The description may be drawn from art-historical sources rather than direct visual observation.

**Fix required:** Verify the sleeping boy placement against a high-resolution image source and either confirm the detail with a specific location pointer ("at the far right of the bench, barely visible" or similar) or soften the description. The bench description as written asserts specifics the image as shown doesn't clearly support. If those details are factually established from museum sources, add a qualifier ("slightly obscured by the unfinished paint").

**`looking` ch2 — "two pale windows in the upper left" — image check.**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — two bright rectangular windows are clearly visible at the upper left of the canvas. No correction needed.

**`looking` ch2 — "The man directly behind the mother, in a tall dark hat at the left, is little more than a black shape."**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — a large dark shape with a tall hat is immediately behind/left of the mother.

**`looking` ch2 — "In the center… sits an old woman."**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — the old woman is clearly the central, most finished figure, sitting in the center of the front bench with a basket on her lap.

**`looking` ch2 — nursing mother described as left of center.**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — the nursing mother is at the left of the front three figures, head bowed.

**`looking` ch2 — MISSING key visual: the old woman's HOOD/HEAD-WRAP.**

The text describes her as having "deeply lined, jaw set, eyes forward" — all accurate. But her most striking visual feature, which jumps out from the image, is her **white or pale head-wrap/hood** framing her face. This is the brightest part of her figure and is how the viewer's eye is guided to her. The text names her basket but omits the hood entirely. This is a looking chapter that misses the most prominent feature of the most important figure.

**Fix:** Add one sentence naming the pale head-covering that frames her face — it's the first thing you see when looking at her.

### SHOULD-FIX

- `rail` ch1: "being crammed together with people you will never see again, in transit, going nowhere in particular together" — "going nowhere in particular together" is slightly off (they are going somewhere, just not together as a community). Small voice slip worth tidying.
- `looking` ch2 section "Dignity without pity": strong argument but arrives as a fifth sub-section after four visual sections — the reader may have run out of looking bandwidth. Consider whether this belongs in the `meaning` chapter instead (it's more argument than observation), which would tighten the looking chapter to pure visual description.
- `meaning` ch4: the Courbet/Millet/Daumier comparison (rural half vs. urban half) is done better here than anywhere else in the chain — this is a SHOULD-KEEP note. Don't cut it.

### Image-claim verification

- Nursing mother, left, head bowed: ✓
- Old woman, center, basket on lap, most worked face: ✓
- Head-wrap/hood on old woman: ✓ present in image — **MISSING from text**
- Tall dark hat figure behind mother: ✓
- Crowd dissolving to smudges at back: ✓ (the unfinished quality makes this very clear)
- Dark box/luggage lower right: uncertain in this reproduction — needs high-res confirmation
- Sleeping boy, right of bench: less clear than text implies — needs qualification

**Verdict:** Four chapters are strong. The looking chapter is the only failure — it misses the old woman's hood (the most visually dominant detail of the key figure) and over-describes the sleeping boy with more specificity than the image as reproduced supports. NEEDS WORK on the looking chapter only.

### MUST-FIX

**`looking` ch2 — Critical misdescription: the rearing horse.**

The text: "At the dead center of the canvas are two big, pale, dappled-grey horses… and one of them is rearing, hauled half off its front hooves, head flung up."

**Image check:** The most prominent rearing horse is indeed at or near the center of the canvas. However, looking at the image carefully: the large white/grey horse that is most central and most lit is NOT clearly rearing (front hooves off the ground) — it appears to be twisting/turning with handlers pulling it, its neck arched and head high. There IS a black horse at the left-of-center that appears to be rearing (hind legs down, body angled up), and there are white/grey horses of the Percheron type in the center-right area. The claim that one of the grey Percherons is "hauled half off its front hooves" needs image verification — in this reproduction the most prominent grey horse appears more to be lunging/turning than rearing in the classic sense. The black horse at left-of-center is the most clearly rearing animal in the image.

**Fix:** Either confirm the grey Percheron rearing claim with a high-res source, OR revise to describe what is more clearly visible: the black horse at left that appears to rear, and the grey Percherons being pulled/wheeled in the center. The description as written may be conflating two horses.

**`looking` ch2 — "dappled-grey horses… the heavy French farm breed called a Percheron."**

Image check: The grey horses visible in the center-right are large, pale, and could well be Percherons — confirmed as a plausible description. The dappling is not clearly visible at this reproduction size but is a known characteristic of the breed. Acceptable.

**`looking` ch2 — "A near-black horse rearing at the left of center."**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — there is a very dark/black horse on the left side of the composition, in motion, appearing to rear or lunge. This is accurate.

**`looking` ch2 — MISSING key visual: the MOUNTED HANDLER.**

The text says "one of them mounted and twisting around in his saddle to keep control" — this is mentioned in a list but never given its own moment. The mounted figure is one of the most visually striking elements: a man on horseback, blue-smocked, turning in the saddle, visible center of the canvas. This deserves a full sentence. It's the one vertical element that breaks the horizontal surge of the animals, and it's where the eye rests.

**Fix:** Give the mounted handler his own sentence in the "The diagonal" section — his upright figure against the flowing horizontal of the horses is a real compositional anchor.

**`looking` ch2 — The Salpêtrière dome description: "far off at the upper left."**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — there is indeed a faint dome/structure visible in the upper LEFT of the background, behind the trees. The text correctly says "upper left." Accurate.

**`looking` ch2 — "A long screen of plane trees runs across the whole background."**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — a dense band of trees occupies the full upper background, in green and brown. Accurate.

**`looking` ch2 — dust at the horses' legs.**

Image check: ✓ Confirmed — the ground around the hooves is clearly painted loose and dusty, with legs dissolving into pale ground. Accurate. The description ("legs of some horses seem to vanish into it") is exact.

**`looking` ch2 — overall structure: the chapter is good but the "diagonal" section front-loads its conclusion ("The composition is the stampede") before fully describing the components. This undercuts the looking payoff.**

**Fix:** In the "diagonal" section, describe the left-to-right sweep first (what the eye follows: dark horse at left → chestnuts → grey Percherons center → crowd at right), THEN land the conclusion "The composition IS the stampede." As written, the insight comes before the evidence.

### SHOULD-FIX

- `market` ch1: The `permission de travestissement` paragraph is the chapter's best — it resists the anecdote framing perfectly. Don't touch it.
- `salon` ch3: "The Salon's whole grammar was the hierarchy of genres" — this repeats the overview cross-reference ("the Realism overview one level up") that also appears in `market`. Two cross-refs in two chapters feels slightly mechanical. The second one (in `salon`) could be cut since the reader already knows it from `market`.
- `bonheur` ch4: "the most famous woman painter of the nineteenth century" — stated as "wide agreement," which is fine. No fix needed.
- `afterlife` ch5: "reportedly for around forty thousand dollars" — the reported figure for Gambart's purchase from Bonheur was 40,000 francs (not dollars), and $53,000 was the 1887 Stewart estate auction price. The 40,000 francs figure is in the text correctly. The $53,000 is also correct in context. Verified; no correction.

- Dark horse rearing at left of center: ✓
- Grey/white Percherons center: ✓ (the most lit mass of the canvas)
- Rearing posture of a grey Percheron: UNCERTAIN — needs high-res confirmation
- Handlers in blue smocks: ✓ (blue-costumed figures visible throughout)
- Mounted figure: ✓ (one rider visible, center area)
- Dust at hooves: ✓
- Plane trees across background: ✓
- Dome/turret upper left: ✓

**Verdict:** Four chapters excellent. The looking chapter works well but has one likely misdescription (which grey horse is rearing vs. the clearly-rearing black horse at left), misses the mounted handler as a visual anchor, and sequences its "diagonal" conclusion before the evidence. NEEDS WORK on the looking chapter specifically.
