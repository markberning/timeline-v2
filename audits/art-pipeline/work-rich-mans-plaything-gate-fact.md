## VERIFIED (load-bearing claims, all CONFIRM)

- **Medium = collage, "printed papers on card."** CONFIRM — Tate object record gives medium exactly as
  "printed papers on card" (support 359 × 238 mm). Draft uses "Collage (printed papers on card)." ✔
- **Dimensions 35.9 × 23.8 cm → 14⅛ × 9⅜ in.** CONFIRM — Tate support 359 × 238 mm = 14.13 × 9.37 in.
  Draft's `14 1/8 in × 9 3/8 in` and stat `14⅛″ × 9⅜″` round correctly. Portrait, W/H ≈ 0.66 — draft
  heroAspect 0.67 is right; "smaller than a sheet of letter paper" holds (letter = 8.5×11 in). ✔
- **Tate acc. T01462, "Presented by the artist, 1971" (NOT purchased).** CONFIRM at primary source —
  Tate / Tate Images record reads "presented by the artist 1971," accession T01462. The factpack §6/§8
  flag ("brief said purchased 1971?") is **RESOLVED: presented/given, not purchased.** Draft is correct
  throughout (const `acquired`, provenance row, legacy prose "given… presented, not sold"). ✔
- **The "POP!" cut from TOY POP-GUN packaging; coincidence, NOT the movement's name.** CONFIRM —
  Wikipedia (citing Tate) + Goldmark: the word "pop" was "cut from the packaging of a toy pop gun."
  Draft repeatedly and correctly scopes it as a coincidence and a found gunshot onomatopoeia, and
  explicitly denies the movement was named after it. ✔
- **"First POP" scoped as first-KNOWN-appearance-in-this-context (not "first Pop artwork," not the name's origin).**
  CONFIRM the scoping is defensible — Wikipedia: "the first to include the word 'pop' in its design,
  years before Lawrence Alloway coined the term 'pop art'." Draft hedges harder ("first KNOWN appearance
  of the word 'pop' inside a work of this kind," "prefigures… does not found"), which is the safe framing. ✔
- **Title lifted from the *Intimate Confessions* pulp cover.** CONFIRM — Wikipedia: title taken from words
  on the cover of *Intimate Confessions*, "the largest clipping." Cover lines "Daughter of Sin," "Woman of
  the Streets" corroborated. ✔
- **Made 1947, first shown 1952 (the "Bunk" lecture, inaugural Independent Group, ICA, epidiascope).**
  CONFIRM, and the two dates are kept distinct everywhere in the draft (const note, reception, legacy
  "made 1947, first shown 1952"). Wikipedia: "Bunk" lecture at the ICA in 1952 was "the first event
  presented by the newly formed Independent Group." Epidiascope corroborated. ✔
- **Collage ≠ the 1972 screenprint.** CONFIRM and well-handled — draft's legacy chapter and the const
  banner explicitly separate the unique 1947 collage (T01462) from the 1972 BUNK! screenprint. V&A
  confirms a 1972 screenprint edition (CIRC.542-1972), "screenprint and collage on paper." ✔
- **rights: `in-copyright` (Paolozzi d. 2005).** CONFIRM CORRECT, NOT pd-us. Paolozzi died 2005; UK life+70
  → in copyright to end-2075; US term also in copyright. Draft credit states "in copyright, shown small
  under fair use" — correct, not labeled pd-us. ✔
- **Paolozzi key quote** ("…500 drawings… an image which acts as a metaphor… unless one… arranges the
  images into patterns of irony the point will be lost"). **VERIFIED AT PRIMARY** — it is from "Eduardo
  Paolozzi in conversation with J. G. Ballard and Frank Whitford," **Studio International** (tied to his
  Tate retrospective), not only the 503-ing Tate i-Map page. Draft does NOT quote it verbatim; it
  paraphrases and explicitly flags it as "reported from Tate's material… attributed rather than carved in
  stone." That framing is honest and now over-cautious in a good way. ✔ (See NICE-1 to optionally tighten.)
- **Real Gold logo / cherry + cherry pie / Coca-Cola / WWII bomber "Keep 'em flying!".** CONFIRM —
  Wikipedia/Goldmark: "a cherry with a slice of cherry pie, and a 'Real Gold' logo from a brand of
  California lemon juice." Coca-Cola fragment and the bomber under "Keep 'em flying!" corroborated. The
  pistol "POP" is over/aimed at the pin-up ("aimed at the head of the smiling girl, held by a man, yet
  only his hand and sleeve are visible") — draft's "over the pin-up" placement is correct. ✔
- **Henry Ford "history is more or less bunk" as the BUNK! series name.** CONFIRM — attributed to Ford
  (1916 *Chicago Tribune*), and the draft attributes it to Ford, never to Paolozzi. The `figures` entry
  "Henry Ford — Gave the BUNK! its name" is accurate. ✔
- **Tate catalogues the group as *Ten Collages from BUNK* (1947–52).** CONFIRM — Tate Images titles
  T01462 "*from* Ten Collages from BUNK, I was a Rich Man's Plaything." ✔
- **Paris years 1947–49, material from American servicemen.** CONFIRM as the safe framing (Wikipedia/NGS):
  collected during the Paris period, much from American ex-servicemen; draft avoids the single-anecdote
  trap. ✔

### [FIX] (correct before ship; precision)

- **[FIX-1] BUNK! portfolio print count: draft says "46-print," V&A says a suite of 46 images / Wikipedia
  says 45 collages reproduced.** The numbers float in sources because the portfolio includes a title page +
  text sheets in addition to the reproduced collages (V&A: "One of a suite of 46 images"; Wikipedia: "45…
  were reproduced as screenprints"). The draft's "46-print *BUNK!* portfolio" (legacy chapter) is defensible
  against the V&A "46 images" count, but it's an avoidable point of friction.
  - Location: `RmpLegacy`, "issued as part of Paolozzi's 46-print *BUNK!* portfolio."
  - Suggested text (drop the contested count): replace "a printed reproduction issued as part of
    Paolozzi's 46-print *BUNK!* portfolio, copies of which live in the V&A and elsewhere" with
    **"a printed reproduction issued as part of Paolozzi's 1972 *BUNK!* screenprint portfolio, copies of
    which live in the V&A and elsewhere."** (Removes the number; "1972" already disambiguates.)

- **[FIX-2] Coca-Cola fragment position stated as fact ("Lower right").** Sources confirm a Coca-Cola
  fragment is present and bottom-corner, but the exact corner is described inconsistently across write-ups
  (DailyArt "bottom right"; the pin-up/POP dominate center). The bomber is reliably "lower left." The
  Coca-Cola "lower right" is plausible but not pinned to a museum description. Low risk, but state it less
  absolutely.
  - Location: `RmpLooking` ("Lower right, a fragment of a Coca-Cola advertisement") and the `annotations`
    Coca-Cola entry ("Lower right, a fragment of a soda advertisement").
  - Suggested text: soften "Lower right" → **"In a lower corner"** for the Coca-Cola fragment (keep the
    bomber as "Lower left," which is well-sourced). Purely a precision hedge; not a factual error.
