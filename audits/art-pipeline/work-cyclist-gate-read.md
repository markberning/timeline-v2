# Gate read — *Dynamism of a Cyclist* (Boccioni, 1913)

Gates run: **STORYTELLING + LOOKING + CLARITY** (combined art-pipeline pass).
Target: `audits/art-pipeline/work-cyclist-draft.md` Part B (the five `Cyc` chapters),
with the Part A annotations cross-checked because they ship as reader-facing surfaces too.
House-voice reference: `absinthe-narratives.tsx.txt`.

**Verdict: PASS with fixes.** No factual blockers (a separate fact gate owns those).
The spine — the push toward near-abstraction — is clearly and consistently the throughline.
Two real problems block: a recurring meta-narration tic ("the next chapter is about…"), and
one CLARITY gap (`plastic dynamism` is half-glossed). Everything else is [FIX]/[NICE].

---

## 1. STORYTELLING

The story-first instinct is strong. The opening ("Futurism began as a press release")
is the best line in the piece and exactly the house register. Dry wit lands repeatedly
("the man has become a side effect of it"; "killed by the oldest machine of war there is, an
animal"). Comparisons are doing real work (cartoonist speed lines; a sprinter glimpsed across
a stadium; a spinning wheel going transparent). The near-abstraction spine is named in every
chapter and pays off in the afterlife "edge of a cliff" image. This is publishable voice.

The one systemic STORYTELLING defect is **meta-narration** — the narrator repeatedly stops
telling the story to announce what the next/this chapter will do. This is the exact tic the
house voice avoids (the Absinthe read hands off with *content* — "hold the name, because he's
the entire reason the picture survived" — never with "the next chapter is about X"). Memory
`feedback_no_meta_narration` makes this ship-blocking.

### [BLOCKER] Meta-narration — "the next chapter is about" hand-offs

Three instances announce the chapter structure instead of carrying the story forward:

1. **CycMilan, end (line ~135):**
   > "That distinction is the whole picture, and it is what the next chapter is about — how you take a man on a bike and turn him into a burst of pure motion without losing the fact that he is a man on a bike."

   **Fix:** keep the idea, drop the chapter-pointer. e.g.
   > "That distinction — cycling, not the cyclist — is the whole picture. The hard part was making it: how do you take a man on a bike and turn him into a burst of pure motion without losing the fact that he is a man on a bike?"

2. **CycMaking, end (line ~166):**
   > "The next chapter is where it all lands — on the canvas, where you can watch a human body dissolve into speed in real time."

   **Fix:** end on the recipe, not the signpost. e.g.
   > "Vivid dabbed color, Cubist facets set in motion, force lines doing the real work, all in the service of a philosophy that says nothing ever holds still. That is the recipe. The canvas is where it lands."
   (drop "The next chapter is where…" entirely.)

3. **CycMaking, mid (line ~150):**
   > "The *Cyclist* is Boccioni's attempt, three years later, to actually deliver on it."

   This one is fine — it's story, not signposting. Flagging only to contrast: this is how a
   forward-pointer should read. Leave it.

### [FIX] "It is worth being precise about who did what, because popular books get it wrong"

**CycMilan (line ~124).** The Absinthe read does this same move — the Guerbois correction —
but folds it into the scene ("Reader, file this fact away, because the next time you see…").
The Cyclist version is slightly more lecture-y ("It is worth being precise about who did
what"). It works, but it's the weakest sentence in an otherwise strong paragraph.

**Fix (optional, voice-tightening):**
> "Popular books get this backwards, so be precise: **Marinetti founded Futurism. He was a poet, not a painter.**"

### [FIX] "It is worth repeating the correction from the first chapter"

**CycBoccioni (line ~215).** Explicit cross-chapter bookkeeping ("the correction from the
first chapter"). The repetition of the Marinetti-not-Boccioni fact is *correct* (it's the #1
misconception and earns a second pass), but the framing narrates the structure.

**Fix:**
> "It is the single most common mistake made about him, so it is worth saying flatly: **Boccioni did not found Futurism.** Marinetti did, with a newspaper manifesto, in 1909."

### [FIX] "(We will get to it in the next chapter; it is the three-dimensional twin…)"

**CycBoccioni (line ~218).** Another forward-pointer. Lighter than the BLOCKER cases (it's a
parenthetical aside, and the Absinthe read does use "We'll come back to Hill at length in the
next chapter"), so this is [FIX] not [BLOCKER]. But the sculpture then gets re-introduced
nearly verbatim in CycAfterlife (line ~240), so the promise + payoff reads slightly
redundant.

**Fix:** trim the parenthetical to just the image, drop the pointer:
> "His most famous object is a striding bronze figure whose muscles seem pulled out into wings of wind, a man remade as motion made solid."
(Let the afterlife chapter name *Unique Forms of Continuity in Space* as the payoff; here it
can stay unnamed.)

### [NICE] Flat-ish stretch — the toolkit list-recap

**CycMaking, end of "the theory underneath" (line ~166):** "So that is the recipe. Vivid
dabbed color, Cubist facets set in motion, force lines doing the real work…" — this recap is
useful but is the one place the prose goes briefly inventory-mode. If the BLOCKER fix above
folds the recap into the chapter close, this resolves itself. No separate action needed.

### [NICE] CycAfterlife "Whatever else Futurism got wrong (and it got plenty wrong…)"

**(line ~241).** Good, earned, house-voice aside — keeps the movement's Fascism/war problem
in view without derailing. Keep. Flagged only as a positive model.

## 2. LOOKING (the most important gate for a near-abstract work)

This is the strongest part of the draft. The CycLooking chapter is genuinely guided looking,
not assertion. It does the right thing for an abstract canvas: it gives the reader a *task*
("trace the outline of the cyclist… You will not be able to do it") and a *location* for each
form. The six required things to SEE are all present and concretely directed:

- **bent-forward rider** — ✅ "upper part of the canvas, left of center… a steep forward lean…
  he is a lean." Excellent. Posture-before-face is the right way to find a near-erased figure.
- **wheel / circular forms** — ✅ "lower portion… rounded, repeating arc-forms — curves that
  echo a wheel without ever closing into one." Concrete, located, and the "transparent blur at
  speed" analogy makes the reader SEE why it's an arc not a circle.
- **diagonal force lines** — ✅ "long diagonal lines… lower left up toward the right… they move
  your eye for you." Direction given, and the "you get swept across it" makes the device
  *experiential*, not just labeled. Best looking-passage in the piece.
- **near-dissolved body** — ✅ "center mass… trace where the man ends and the bicycle begins.
  You will not be able to do it." The crux, and it's handled as the crux.
- **color planes of speed** — ✅ "vivid, clashing hue… the blues and ochres jostle and refuse
  to settle… color used as friction." Names actual colors (blues, ochres), explains the
  discord as intent.
- **absent fixed contour** — ✅ "the outer edges… there is no closed outline. Nothing seals the
  cyclist off… He bleeds out into his own motion." Lands the spine on the most abstract
  feature. Strong close.

No blind coordinates, no invented pin positions — compliant with
`feedback_no_blind_image_coordinates` (it's all prose pointers + region-language like "upper
left of center," which is the approved pattern). 

### [FIX] Annotation/chapter duplication on the color point — hedge the located colors

**Caution, cross-surface.** The chapter and the Part-A annotations both assert specific
colors as present on the canvas: "blues and ochres" (chapter line ~202; annotation "color
planes of speed" line ~88 says "blues and ochres jostle"). The opening canvas paragraph also
asserts "blues and ochres" (line ~178). This is asserted three times as fact. **The fact gate
should confirm the canvas actually reads blue + ochre dominant** — if the fact pack supports
it, fine; if the pack only supports "vivid/discordant," soften to "the hues — blues, rusts,
earth-tones — jostle" or hedge. Flagging for the fact gate; not a LOOKING failure in itself,
but LOOKING shouldn't invent a palette. (Note: this is the same class of error the pipeline
doc cites — the *Demoiselles* "two left-hand faces" wrong annotation — so a short caption /
located color is NOT exempt for being confident.)

### [NICE] "behind in the air"

**CycLooking (line ~183) and annotation (line ~84).** This quoted phrase from the preparatory
drawings is used well to anchor the rider's pose. Good concrete hook. Keep — and the fact gate
should confirm it's verbatim-safe from the pack (the fact ledger lists it as DOCUMENTED).

### [NICE] The "find the rider → lose the rider" arc

The chapter structure — find the lean, find the wheel, ride the force lines, then *lose* all
of it in the dissolve — is exactly right for a near-abstract work: it gives the reader footing
before pulling it away. Strong design. Keep.

## 3. CLARITY (zero-knowledge reader; every term glossed)

Glossing is mostly excellent and in-voice (definitions in parentheses / appositive, per
WRITING-RULES). Checked every load-bearing term:

- **Futurism** — ✅ glossed at first use (CycMilan: "a noisy declaration that the past was a
  museum to be burned down…"). Clear.
- **force lines / linee-forza** — ✅ glossed twice, well ("lines they claimed showed how an
  object would resolve itself…" + the cartoonist speed-lines analogy). Best gloss in the piece.
- **Divisionism** — ✅ glossed ("building a picture out of small separate dabs… so the surface
  vibrates") + Pointillism cousin. Clear.
- **Cubism** — ✅ glossed ("the Picasso-and-Braque method of shattering an object into flat
  geometric facets and showing several of its angles at once"). Clear.
- **abstraction** — ✅ glossed inline at the one place it's load-bearing (CycAfterlife:
  "painting that has given up on showing recognizable things and presents pure shape and color
  instead"). Clear. Good that it waits until the afterlife where it's the payoff.
- **Bergson / constant flux** — ✅ glossed ("everything is in constant flux and flow, and that
  our habit of seeing the world as a set of fixed, separate things is a convenient lie").
- **provenance** — ✅ not explicitly glossed in the Cyclist Part B, BUT it never appears as a
  reader-facing word in the prose (the afterlife chapter says "the early chain of ownership"
  in plain English). The Part-A `provenance` array is structural/labelled, not prose. OK — no
  unglossed jargon here. (Contrast: the Absinthe read glosses "provenance" explicitly; not
  required here since the Cyclist prose avoids the word.)

### [BLOCKER] "plastic dynamism" — glossed for the wrong sense, leaves the real meaning fuzzy

**CycMaking (line ~162) and CycLooking (line ~203) and annotation (line ~89).** The draft
glosses the word "**plastic**" ("the old art-school sense — having to do with shaping solid
form, nothing to do with the material") — which is good and necessary — but the *phrase*
"plastic dynamism" itself is only loosely defined as "his idea that a moving body and the
space it tears through are one continuous solid whole." A zero-knowledge reader gets "plastic =
not the material" but is left slightly adrift on what the two-word term as a unit actually
*names*. It's the work's central concept and the annotation leans on it ("This is the idea
Boccioni called 'plastic dynamism'") so it must be airtight.

**Fix:** add one clean appositive that defines the whole phrase as a noun, right after the
"plastic" gloss. e.g.
> "…nothing to do with the material. **Plastic dynamism**, then, is his name for a single idea: that a moving body and the space it tears through are one continuous solid form, not a figure parked in front of a background."

Then CycLooking and the annotation can refer back to it cleanly without re-glossing.

### [FIX] "the dynamic sensation itself" — quoted before it's unpacked

**CycMaking (line ~147–150).** The verbatim manifesto quote ("It shall simply be **the
dynamic sensation itself.**") is gloss-unpacked in the very next sentence ("not the cyclist,
but the *feeling* of speeding"), which is good. But the phrase "universal dynamism" rides
along inside the quote unglossed ("no longer be a fixed moment in universal dynamism"). A
zero-knowledge reader hits "universal dynamism" cold.

**Fix:** add a 4-word parenthetical after the quote, or lean on the existing unpacking to
cover it. Lightest touch:
> "…not the cyclist, but the *feeling* of speeding, the world taken as motion rather than as
> still things — is not a thing you can point a brush at."
This retroactively covers "universal dynamism" without breaking the quote.

### [FIX] "faceting" / "facets" used before "facet" is anchored

**CycMaking (line ~155):** "Boccioni took the faceting and threw away the calm." The word
"facets" is introduced in the Cubism gloss one sentence earlier ("flat geometric facets"), so
a careful reader can back-derive it, but "faceting" (the -ing noun) is a small step beyond.
Minor.

**Fix (optional):** "Boccioni took that faceting — the breaking-into-flat-planes — and threw
away the calm." One inline clause closes it.

### [NICE] "louche"-style ritual glosses

The piece doesn't have an Absinthe-grade ritual gloss to do, but where it could over-explain
(e.g. the manifesto signatories) it correctly stays terse. Good restraint. No action.
