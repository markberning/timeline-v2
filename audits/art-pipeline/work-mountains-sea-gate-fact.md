## FINDINGS

### [FIX-1] The "bridge" quote describes the ARTIST, not "the painting" — tighten the antecedent.
Wilkin's canonical wording is Louis's description **of Frankenthaler** ("the bridge between
Pollock and what is possible"), i.e. the artist is the bridge. The draft twice frames Louis
as having called **the painting** the bridge:
- const `afterlife` blurb: *"Louis later called the painting a bridge between Pollock and what
  was possible."*
- PART B `MtsAfterlife`: *"Louis later gave the painting the line it has carried ever since,
  calling* Mountains and Sea *'a bridge between Pollock and what was possible.'"*

This is a small but real subject-swap: in the source the bridge is **Frankenthaler**, not
*Mountains and Sea*. The variant flag and the Louis attribution are otherwise perfect.

- **Corrected (const blurb):** `...Louis later called Frankenthaler a bridge between Pollock and what was possible.`
- **Corrected (MtsAfterlife):** `...Louis later gave her the line she has carried ever since, calling Frankenthaler "a bridge between Pollock and what was possible."`

(If the authors prefer to keep the picture as the referent for narrative flow, soften to
*"Louis later called the picture a bridge…"* only as a paraphrase — but the verbatim
quotation must attach to Frankenthaler, since that is what the recorded line says. Keeping
the quote marks while swapping the subject to the painting is the thing to avoid.)

### [FIX-2] "First professionally exhibited work" claim is overstated — drop or soften.
The draft (const provenance + MtsAfterlife) says it "first went on public view at her
**second** solo show" — which is correct (her first solo was 1951, before the 1952 painting
existed). No fix needed there. BUT confirm the authors do NOT anywhere call it her "first
exhibited work": some general sources (Wikipedia lede) loosely call *Mountains and Sea* her
"first professionally exhibited work," which is wrong (her 1951 first solo predates it).
The draft correctly says **second** solo show — so this is a **guard, not a defect**: keep
"second solo exhibition," never import the "first professionally exhibited work" phrasing if
a later editor reaches for it. **No change required to current draft text; flagged so the
correct version is not regressed.** (Downgrade to NICE if you prefer; logged as FIX because
the wrong claim is one search away.)

### [NICE-1] Pollock-viewing year — "1951" is right for this work; optionally note "by 1951."
Wikipedia dates her first Pollock encounter to 1950 (Betty Parsons), while the
work-relevant trigger is the 1951 black-and-white show. The draft's "In 1951 Frankenthaler
had seen Pollock's poured paintings" is correct and need not change. If an editor wants to
preempt a "but Wikipedia says 1950" note, "by 1951 Frankenthaler had seen Pollock's poured
paintings" absorbs both. Purely optional.

### [NICE-2] "in a single afternoon's kind of move" (MtsAfterlife close) — flavor, not fact.
The closing line "A twenty-three-year-old poured it onto the floor in a single afternoon's
kind of move" is a deliberate figure of speech (note "kind of"), not a documented claim that
the picture was painted in one afternoon. It reads fine as voice; just confirm it is not
mistaken downstream for a sourced "painted in one sitting" fact. No change needed.

### [NICE-3] "window-wiper/squeegee tools and rollers / sponges" — well-supported, keep scoped.
Draft says she worked the pools "with sponges, squeegee-like wipers, and rollers." This matches
the fact pack and the standard accounts (sponges, window-wiper/squeegee, rollers). Fine; it is
correctly hedged as method description, not a quoted inventory.
