## FINDINGS

### [FIX] 1 — "*Niagara* … in which Monroe had her **first starring role**" is wrong
- **Where:** PART B, `MarMaking`, opening paragraph: *"…made in 1953 for the film noir Niagara, in which Monroe had her first starring role."*
- **Problem:** *Niagara* (1953) was a major star-making vehicle where Monroe got top billing, but it was **not her first starring/leading role**. *Don't Bother to Knock* (1952) was her first leading role. Calling *Niagara* her "first starring role" is a verifiable factual error.
- **Verified:** Wikipedia (Marilyn Monroe; *Niagara*), TCM, classicmoviehub — *Don't Bother to Knock* (1952) precedes *Niagara* as her first starring role; *Niagara* was the breakthrough that "elevated her to movie star status."
- **Corrected text (drop the false specificity; keep it true):** replace
  > "for the film noir *Niagara*, in which Monroe had her first starring role."

  with
  > "for the film noir *Niagara*, the 1953 thriller that gave Monroe top billing and pushed her into full stardom."

  (Or simply cut the clause: "…made in 1953 for the film noir *Niagara*.")
- **Note:** the source-photo facts around this clause are all correct — *Niagara* (not *Some Like It Hot* / *Gentlemen Prefer Blondes*), 1953, Gene Kornman. Only the "first starring role" sub-claim is wrong.

### [NICE] 2 — Tate's own medium line is shorter than the draft's
- The Tate T03093 record prints the medium bluntly as **"Acrylic paint on canvas."** The draft uses **"Acrylic and silkscreen ink on canvas (two panels)"** (matching Wikipedia's "silkscreen ink and acrylic paint on canvas"). The draft's fuller form is **more accurate and is the right call** (the image is screened, not brushed) — flagged only so the team knows the on-page medium intentionally exceeds Tate's bare catalogue string. No change needed.

### [NICE] 3 — Sichel caveat wording is defensible but slightly loose
- **Where:** `MarBreak`, the "I want to be a machine" caveat: *"…what Warhol actually said was looser…"*
- Sichel's 2018 finding (verified) is specifically that the published 1963 *Art News* text was **edited to remove the interview's queer content**, and that Warhol's spoken phrasing in context included **"I think everybody should be a machine."** The draft's "what he actually said was looser, so take it as the famous edited version, not a clean transcript" is a fair, non-overclaiming summary. Optional tightening, not required: "…Sichel showed the printed text was edited (queer content was cut) and Warhol's spoken phrasing was looser…". No correction mandated.
