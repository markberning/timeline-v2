## FINDINGS

### [FIX-1] Quote framing slightly overstates the "outside of his skull" wording as the contested variant
**Where:** PART B, MchBreak, the italic block quote + the sentence after it.
**Issue:** The draft frames the variation as "average" vs "everyday" man and "glued to the outside of his skull" vs "to his skull, on the exterior." That's accurate. But the single most substantive, verifiable variant — that the fuller sourced version names a *national* subject ("the average German" / "the German wants only his order, his king, his Sunday sermon...") rather than a generic "average man" — is the bigger translation/selection choice and is not surfaced. The draft's generic "average man" is a defensible, widely-used rendering, so this is not a blocker, but the hedge would be more honest naming the one variant that actually changes the meaning.
**Suggested text (replace the sentence beginning "though the exact English wording varies"):**
> though the exact English wording varies from source to source — some translations open it with "the everyday man," others tighten it to "the average German," and the skull clause appears both as "glued to the outside of his skull" and "glued to his skull, on the exterior" — so read it as one careful translation rather than a fixed original.

This keeps the chosen "average man" rendering but tells the reader the live variant that matters.

### [NICE-1] Höch dates are firmer than the draft's hedge suggests
**Where:** PART A provenance note (1922–1966) and PART B MchAfterlife.
**Observation:** The draft treats 1922 and 1966 as "reported through that account ... not independently confirmed." In fact both dates come from the Centre Pompidou's own provenance field (1922 = separation, 1966 = Höch returns it after conservation/photography repairs), i.e. the holding institution's record, not a third-party blog. The hedge is harmless and reads as scholarly caution, but if anything it under-credits the source. No change required; optionally soften "not independently confirmed fact" to "as the museum's provenance records them."

### [NICE-2] PART A `year: 1920` vs prose "c.1919–20" — internally consistent, no action
**Where:** PART A `year: 1920`, `stats` "c.1920", `heroCredit` "c.1920".
**Observation:** The numeric `year` field necessarily picks a single integer (1920) for sorting/display; the human-readable surfaces (stats "c.1920", dateLabels "c.1919–20", and all prose) carry the uncertainty. This is the correct pattern — the gate-locked requirement is that the *prose* stay "c.1919–20," which it does. No change. Flagged only so a later reviewer doesn't "fix" the integer field into a range.
