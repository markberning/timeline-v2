## 3. CLARITY + VOICE CONTRACT

This is where the work is. The voice contract bans meta-narration, reader-commands, honesty-labels, and condescending glosses. The draft is clean on jargon (everything is inline-defined: Abstract Expressionism line 117, fleur-de-lis line 173, silkscreen line 148) and clean on em-dash handling (no literal `—` in strings; `&ndash;`/`&mdash;` not misused — the one entity used is `&ndash;` for the date range at line 114, which is correct). But honesty-labels and reader-commands recur.

### Honesty-labels / meta-narration (contract violations)

- **[FIX]** Line 122 `SupLunch`: "he gave one of the great deadpan answers in modern art, **and it is worth quoting exactly as it was published**". The "worth quoting exactly" is a meta-narration honesty-label. Suggested rewrite: drop the clause — "...he gave one of the great deadpan answers in modern art:" and run straight into the quote.

- **[FIX]** Line 128 `SupLunch`: "**(One honest footnote: the published interview was heavily edited** from the recordings, so read it as the standard, widely-cited version of what Warhol said, not a raw transcript.)" Double violation: "One honest footnote" is a banned honesty-label, and "so read it as..." is a reader-command. The factpack caveat is real and worth keeping, but say it plainly. Suggested rewrite: "(The published interview was edited down from the recordings, so this is the standard, widely-cited version rather than a raw transcript.)"

- **[FIX]** Line 147 `SupMaking`: "**Here is the single most common mistake people make about these cans, and it is worth setting straight**, because correcting it tells you what the painting is really doing." Meta-narration + honesty-label. Suggested rewrite: "The cans are almost always mistaken for silkscreens. They are not, and the correction tells you what the painting is really doing." (Then the existing "These 1962 Soup Cans are not silkscreens" follows.)

- **[FIX]** Line 151 `SupMaking`: "**Sit with the strangeness of that.**" Reader-command / meta. Suggested rewrite: cut the sentence, or fold into a plain statement — "The strangeness is the point: the whole feeling of the work is mechanical... and yet Warhol achieved that machine-made look by hand."

### Reader-commands

- **[FIX]** Line 163 `SupLooking`: "**Don't look at any one can yet. Stand back and take in** the whole grid at once..." Reader-commands. The looking voice can describe the effect instead of instructing. Suggested rewrite: "The first thing the set does is refuse a focal point. Take in as a whole, all thirty-two together, and the repetition reads as the subject..." (recasts the imperative as description). At minimum convert "Don't look / Stand back" to descriptive register.

- **[FIX]** Line 167 `SupLooking`: "**Now go can to can and watch for what changes.** The answer is: almost nothing." Reader-command. Suggested rewrite: "Can to can, almost nothing changes."

- **[FIX]** Line 172 `SupLooking`: "**Look closely at the base of any can**, at the thin ring..." Reader-command (also the Looking header at 166 "Read the names, and notice..."). Suggested: "At the base of each can runs a thin ring of small gold ornaments." Recast the section's imperatives to descriptive throughout; this is the dominant tic in the looking section.

- **[NICE]** Line 178 `SupLooking`: "Finally, compare a few cans side by side, slowly." Same imperative register. Lower priority than the above because it reads as natural looking-guidance, but for consistency convert with the others.

### Condescending glosses / hedging

- **[FIX]** Line 204 `SupBreak`: the parenthetical "(You will very often see this rounded off to the smoother 'Pop art is about liking things,' which reads well and **is even repeated by the Warhol Museum**, but it is a paraphrase...)". The paraphrase flag is required by the factpack (§4 Quote B) and is accurate, so keep the substance — but it's a long aside mid-argument and slightly lecture-y. Suggested: tighten to one sentence — "(It's often rounded to the smoother 'Pop art is about liking things,' but Warhol's published words were 'It's liking things.')" Keeps the fact-checkable flag, loses the meta tone.

- **[NICE]** Line 128 closing: "the picture is not *about* Campbell's; in a real way, the picture simply *is* Campbell's." Nice line, accurate to the factpack's "brand itself as subject." Keep.

### Em-dash / string-punctuation check

- **[PASS]** No literal `—` characters inside rendered TS string fields. The const blurbs (lines 55–59) and hook (line 43) use parentheses and commas per the fact-pack handling note. `&ndash;` at line 114 is a correct HTML entity in JSX (date range), not a misused `&mdash;`. No `&mdash;` appears anywhere. Clean.
