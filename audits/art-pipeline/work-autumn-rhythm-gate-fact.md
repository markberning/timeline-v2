## FINDINGS

### [FIX] The palette claim is wrong — there is a small amount of teal/blue
The draft asserts a flatly colorless palette. PART B (`AutLooking`):
> "a tight, nearly colorless palette of **black, white, brown, and tan**. There are no bright hues anywhere, no red, no blue, no green."

And the PART A annotation `The skeins of black, white, brown, and tan`:
> "black, white, brown, and a creamy tan. There are no bright hues"

**Web-verified correction:** multiple sources (Wikipedia; Met search description; TheCollector; artincontext) agree the palette is mostly black/white/brown/tan **plus a small amount of teal / blue-green**. Wikipedia: "mostly browns and white, with a small amount of teal blue." The Met collection-search blurb describes "turquoise drips." So "no blue" is factually false — there is a subtle teal/blue-green accent.

This matters because the draft makes the absence-of-color a *point* ("no red, no blue, no green"), and it's wrong on the blue. Corrected text:

- PART B `AutLooking`, replace:
  > "a tight, nearly colorless palette of **black, white, brown, and tan**. There are no bright hues anywhere, no red, no blue, no green. The autumnal browns and creamy buffs are the only warmth…"

  with:
  > "a tight, nearly colorless palette of **black, white, brown, and tan**, with only a faint trace of teal-blue. There are no strong, saturated hues — no red, no green, and only that one whisper of cool blue against the warm browns. The autumnal browns and creamy buffs carry the warmth…"

- PART A annotation `The skeins of black, white, brown, and tan`, replace:
  > "black, white, brown, and a creamy tan. There are no bright hues; the autumnal browns and buffs…"

  with:
  > "black, white, brown, and a creamy tan, with one faint accent of teal-blue. There are almost no saturated hues; the autumnal browns and buffs…"

  (Optionally rename the annotation label to "The skeins of black, white, brown, tan — and a touch of teal," but the body fix is the load-bearing part.)

### [NICE] Acquisition price/trade detail — draft is correct; note a conflicting secondary figure exists

The draft is right to treat the price as well-sourced-but-secondary and keep it out of the `price` field; no change needed. If anyone wants to harden it, the *Number 17, 1951* trade detail is the specific, citable form. Not ship-blocking — the shippable line (George A. Hearn Fund, 1957, acc. 57.92) is firm and verbatim-correct.

### [NICE] "I am nature" sourcing — flagged correctly; minor provenance note
The draft correctly ships "I am nature" as **anecdotal / second-hand (Krasner), not a sourced quote** — exactly right and required. Web detail: Wikiquote traces it to Lee Krasner's **1964 Smithsonian Archives of American Art oral history**, recounting Pollock's reply when **Hans Hofmann** asked if he worked from nature. The factpack's "~1942 to Hans Hofmann" and the draft's "remembered second-hand by Lee Krasner long afterward" are both consistent and accurate. No change required; the handling is exemplary.

## VERIFIED CORRECT (load-bearing claims that PASS — no change)

**Const / core facts (PART A):**
- **Medium = `Enamel on canvas`** (commercial enamel / house paint, NOT oil) — Met record + Smarthistory + Wikipedia. ✓ The draft's emphatic "It is not oil paint" is correct.
- **Dimensions 266.7 × 525.8 cm → 8 ft 9 in × 17 ft 3 in** (105 × 207 in) — Met primary. The larger 269.9 × 528.3 × 6.7 cm figure is the same object measured with depth. `heroAspect 1.98` (525.8/266.7 ≈ 1.97, landscape) ✓.
- **Credit line "George A. Hearn Fund, 1957"; accession 57.92** — Met, verbatim. ✓
- **Location The Metropolitan Museum of Art**; acquired 1957, year after Pollock's death. ✓
- **`rights: 'in-copyright'` is CORRECT** — 1950 work, Pollock d. 11 Aug 1956; © The Pollock-Krasner Foundation / Artists Rights Society (ARS), New York. NOT pd-us. `heroCredit` "in copyright, shown small under fair use" is the right call. ✓ (Do NOT flip to pd-us.)
- **Year 1950; artist Jackson Pollock (1912–1956)** ✓.

**Title history (the legend axis):**
- Made and first shown as **`Number 30`** — Betty Parsons Gallery (Nov–Dec 1950) and MoMA *15 Americans* (1952); shown as **`Autumn Rhythm`** at the **Sidney Janis Gallery (1955)**. ✓
- The draft hedges the renaming correctly: "later retitled," NOT "Pollock titled it in 1955." T.J. Clark's view that "*Autumn Rhythm* was Pollock's own title" is real and is presented as a scholarly argument, not a documented fact. ✓ Exactly the right handling.

**"Action painting" attribution (the key trap):**
- Coined by **Harold Rosenberg**, "The American Action Painters," **_ARTnews_, December 1952** — two years AFTER the work; **never** in Pollock's mouth. The draft states this in two places and is correct. ✓
- The "arena in which to act" phrase is Rosenberg's (ARTnews 1952), attributed to him with "the phrase is his, and it came after." ✓ Verbatim-confirmed.

**"Controlled, not accidental" (the central misreading):**
- Wright interview is **summer 1950** (same year as the work) — confirmed. ✓
- The draft ships "I can control the flow of paint: there is no accident" and **transparently flags it as a condensation** of the longer transcript. The fuller verbatim ("With experience it seems to be possible to control the flow of paint, to a great extent… I don't use the accident — 'cause I deny the accident.") matches Wikiquote. Honest and correct handling. ✓
- "No accident / controlled / deliberate / methodical" framing — confirmed by Smarthistory and Namuth-sequence scholarship. ✓

**Pollock's "On the floor I am more at ease…" key statement:**
- Verbatim-confirmed against Wikiquote: "My painting does not come from the easel. I hardly ever stretch my canvas before painting. I prefer to tack the unstretched canvas to the hard wall or the floor… On the floor I am more at ease. I feel nearer, more a part of the painting, since this way I can walk around it, work from the four sides and literally **be** in the painting." Source **_Possibilities_ I, no. 1 (Winter 1947–48), p. 79** — exactly as the draft cites. ✓
- The continuation "When I am *in* my painting, I'm not aware of what I'm doing… the painting has a life of its own. I try to let it come through." — verbatim-confirmed, same source. ✓

**Other prose facts:**
- **Hans Namuth shot "more than 500 photographs"** of Pollock at work in 1950 (July–Oct), plus two films — confirmed by the Pollock-Krasner House record and Wikipedia. ✓ The draft's "more than 500 photographs" and "500-plus photographs" are correct. (The factpack's caution that the famous *color* film shows Pollock painting on glass, not *Autumn Rhythm*, is honored — the draft only credits the *photographs* with documenting the build, not the film. ✓)
- **Raw, unprimed canvas / worked from all four sides / poured off sticks, hardened brushes, basting syringe, trowel** — confirmed (Met, Wikipedia, factpack). ✓
- **Allover composition, no focal point, no figure/ground, nothing depicted** — confirmed (Smarthistory). ✓
- **Pollock died 11 August 1956 in a car crash near Springs, age 44** — confirmed (b. 28 Jan 1912). ✓
- **Drip/pour period roughly 1947–1950** — confirmed. ✓
- **Bohm-Duchen sequence (Pollock began at the right third)** — appears only in factpack, not asserted in the draft prose; nothing to verify in shipped text. ✓
