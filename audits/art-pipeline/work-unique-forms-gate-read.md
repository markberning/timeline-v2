# GATE READ — Boccioni, *Unique Forms of Continuity in Space* (1913)

Gates run: **STORYTELLING + LOOKING + CLARITY** (combined critic pass, gated art pipeline).
Subject reviewed: PART B prose (the five `Unq`-prefixed section components) plus the
reader-facing surfaces in PART A that ship as content (hook, section blurbs, the six
`annotations`, provenance notes). Stats / field-set / wiring are out of scope for these
three gates.

House-voice reference: `absinthe-narratives.tsx.txt`.

**Verdict: PASS with fixes.** No BLOCKERs. The draft is strong — story-first, dry, well
glossed, and the LOOKING section does the hardest thing on the list (guide the eye over a
3-D object) genuinely well. A handful of [FIX] items below are real and worth doing before
ship; the rest are [NICE]. Nothing here requires a rewrite.

## 1. STORYTELLING — PASS

The spine the brief flagged as "great material" is fully exploited and lands:
- The **plaster-vs-posthumous-bronze** reveal is set up in `UnqManifesto` ("two facts to
  carry in, because both get garbled constantly"), paid off in `UnqMaking`, made poignant
  in `UnqDeath` ("with the most important sculpture of his career still a white plaster
  cast"), and turned into the central irony in `UnqAfterlife` ("the most famous version is
  the one he never made"). That is a clean, escalating through-line across five sections —
  exactly the absinthe-reference structure (set a fact early, detonate it later).
- The **euro-coin afterlife** is excellent: "small enough to spend," "riding around in the
  change of anyone who has ever bought a coffee in Italy," and the closing image of pure
  forward motion now "travelling constantly, hand to hand, across a whole currency union."
- Dry wit is present and in-voice without being jokey: "killed not by a machine but by a
  horse," "the oldest transport there is," "The artist would have had opinions," "the war
  won" at the figure's feet.
- Comparisons land (Nike, Rodin's *Walking Man*, hull/fuselage, "half man, half engine").
- Scene-setting is solid (Le Figaro front page, the demolition-order manifesto, Verona).

No flat stretches that read as filler. The two notes below are the only storytelling dings.

### [FIX-S1] Meta-narration / "we are going to" tour-guide phrasing — recurring tic
The brief explicitly bars meta-narration, and the global voice note bars narrating what the
chapter is about / how it links. The draft mostly tells the story plainly, but several
lines step out and address the reader about the *structure of the article* rather than the
subject. The absinthe reference does use second-person looking-prompts ("Now to the drink
in the title," "walk into the figures") — those are LOOKING prompts and are fine. The ones
below are different: they narrate the article's own roadmap. Trim or convert to plain
statement.

- `UnqManifesto`, para 1: *"Hold that statue in mind. We are going to walk straight back
  into it later."*
  Fix: cut the second sentence. "Hold that statue in mind." alone does the job; the promise
  of a later callback is the meta part. (The Nike does return in `UnqLooking` regardless.)
- `UnqManifesto`, para 4 (theory): *"The remarkable thing about the figure we are about to
  look at is that it actually does it."*
  Fix: → "The remarkable thing about the figure is that it actually does it." (Drop "we are
  about to look at"; it's roadmap narration.)
- `UnqManifesto`, para 4: *"We will get to how, and why that matters. For now: theory in
  1912, plaster in 1913, and the artist gone by 1916."*
  The recap clause is the weakest line in the section — it narrates the syllabus. Fix:
  cut "We will get to how, and why that matters." Keep the "theory in 1912, plaster in
  1913, artist gone by 1916" beat — that's a legitimate punchy summation of the arc, not
  roadmap.
- `UnqMaking`, "plastic dynamism" para: *"Read those last words again, because they are not
  a description of the sculpture. They ARE the sculpture."*
  This is a strong rhetorical move and reads as voice, not roadmap — **keep it.** Flagging
  only to note the line is doing work, not narration. (No change.)

Note for the reviser: the absinthe house voice *does* permit "We'll get to that" sparingly
(it appears in `AbCafe`). So this is a calibration FIX, not a ban — the draft just leans on
it 3–4 times in the first section, which tips from voice into roadmap. Thinning to one is
the fix.

### [NICE-S2] "It is worth sitting with the strangeness of this for a moment"
`UnqAfterlife`, "The wrinkle" para opener. Borderline meta ("worth sitting with… for a
moment" tells the reader how to read). The paragraph that follows is genuinely good, so
this is low-priority. If touched: → "The strangeness here is real and rarely said plainly:
every bronze of *Unique Forms* is posthumous." Lets the content carry it.

### [NICE-S3] Two near-identical closing images
The hook (PART A) ends on "body torn open into wings of flame… rides in your pocket on the
20-cent coin," and `UnqAfterlife`'s final line ends on "the body torn open into wings of
speed… before he had any idea where it would go." Both are great; both use "torn open into
wings." Since the hook and the section close are far apart in the reading flow this is
acceptable, but if the reviser wants variety, vary one of the two "wings" phrasings.

## 2. LOOKING — PASS (the strongest section)

This is the gate most at risk for a sculpture-in-the-round, and `UnqLooking` handles it
better than most painting LOOKING sections do. It explicitly solves the 3-D problem up
front ("there is no single front… you walk around it. But there is a standard view"),
fixes scale concretely ("a little under four feet tall, roughly chest height… the size of
a striding child"), and then guides the eye **ground-up in a deliberate order** that
matches how the figure reads: legs → flanges → absent arms → wake → torso/machine →
footings. Every item the brief asked the reader to SEE is covered with concrete,
traceable looking:

- **Striding muscular legs** — "the legs are the engine… one driving leg forward with the
  weight thrown onto it, the other trailing behind." ✓ concrete, weight described.
- **Flame/wind-like flanges** — "the metal splits open and streams backward into curved
  flanges… like wings, or like flame bent flat by a wind, or like the wake peeling off the
  hull of a boat," plus the active instruction "Trace one flange with your eye from where
  it leaves the thigh to where it streams off behind." ✓ this is exactly the guided-looking
  the brief wants.
- **Absent arms** — "here is the thing your eye keeps reaching for and not finding: the
  arms. There are none." ✓ framed as a looking experience, not a fact recital.
- **Wake-of-motion forms** — "the curves that seem to belong to the air rather than the
  man… the wake of the stride made solid." ✓
- **Aerodynamic torso** — "less a ribcage than a hull or a fuselage, a thing built to part
  the air." ✓
- **No fixed contour** — "this one has no clean edge at all; man and motion and space are
  one continuous shape." ✓ directly delivers the "figure with NO fixed contour" ask.
- **Two block footings vs. plinth** — covered, tied back to the manifesto. ✓

The six `annotations` in PART A mirror these and are equally concrete (each has a `where`
locating it on the object). Good.

### [FIX-L1] Redundancy between `UnqMaking` and `UnqLooking` reads the same beats twice
`UnqMaking` already describes the no-outline / streaming-fins / stripped-arms / wake at
length ("Its surfaces do not close… peel back and stream off it in curved fins and blades,
as if the air the figure is pushing through is carving it"). `UnqLooking` then describes the
*same* features in *very* similar language ("the metal splits open and streams backward…
as if the air… is carving it"). Reading them back to back, the flanges-as-carved-by-air
image appears twice in nearly the same words.
Fix: `UnqMaking`'s job is "what he built and why (the theory made solid)"; `UnqLooking`'s
job is "stand in front of it and SEE it." Keep the full guided looking in `UnqLooking`;
in `UnqMaking` compress the physical description to one or two sentences that serve the
theory point, and drop the second "the air… carving it" image so it lands fresh in
`UnqLooking`. This is a real read-quality issue (the looking feels pre-spent), but it's an
editing tightening, not a structural problem — hence [FIX], not [BLOCKER].

### [NICE-L2] "you walk around it" then only the one standard view
`UnqLooking` correctly tells the reader it's in the round and they'd walk around it, then
(sensibly, for a single hero photo) anchors on the standard three-quarter striding view.
That's the right call for a phone reader looking at one image. Optional enrichment: one
sentence on what changes as you move around it (e.g., from straight-on the flanges read
more like wings; from the side, like a wake) would honor the "in the round" promise the
section opens with. Purely additive; skip if it bloats.

## 3. CLARITY — PASS

Every term the brief named is glossed inline for a zero-knowledge reader, and the glosses
are in-voice (is / is-not / plain analogy), not dictionary drops:

- **Futurism** — "It announced a movement called Futurism… less an art program than a war
  cry. It worshipped the new century's machines… and it sneered at everything old." ✓
- **Plastic dynamism** — handled carefully and correctly, including the trap term:
  "'plastic' here in its old art-school sense, meaning *to do with shaping and modeling
  form*, not the material plastic, which did not yet exist as we mean it." ✓ This is the
  single most important gloss in the piece and it nails it.
- **Casting / bronze / plaster** — plaster is glossed at first use ("a soft white material
  you build up wet and let set hard, the standard way a sculptor works out a form before it
  is ever cast into permanent metal"). ✓
- **Posthumous cast** — the *concept* is made unmistakable ("Every shining bronze you have
  ever seen of this figure was made after he was dead"), and `UnqAfterlife` defines a
  cast-of-a-cast plainly ("a cast taken from a cast"). ✓
- **Nike of Samothrace / Rodin's *Walking Man*** — both glossed (winged/headless/striding
  Greek Victory in the Louvre; "a striding bronze with no arms and no head") and correctly
  framed as a scholarly *reading*, not asserted intent ("a reading by later scholars… so
  hold it as a likeness, not a confession"). ✓ Matches the fact-ledger requirement.
- **Technical Manifesto of Futurist Sculpture / "abolition of the finished line"** — glossed
  ("a sculpture should no longer be a sealed, self-contained lump that stops cleanly at its
  own outline"). ✓

### [FIX-C1] "engraver/printmaker" vs. this work — N/A here; ignore
(Carryover habit from the absinthe checklist — no such term in this draft. No action.)

### [FIX-C2] The word "casting" is used before its mechanism is ever shown
The reader is told repeatedly that Boccioni "never cast it in metal" and that bronzes were
"cast," but *what casting physically is* (melting metal and pouring it into a mold taken
from the plaster, so the bronze is a copy made from the plaster) is never spelled out.
A zero-knowledge reader can infer "cast = turned into bronze somehow," and the piece leans
on that inference for its whole central irony (a bronze is a *copy* made *from* the plaster,
which is why a posthumous cast is possible at all and why "cast of a cast" makes sense).
Fix: one clause at first use in `UnqMaking` or `UnqAfterlife`, e.g. at "the first two bronze
casts were made from the plaster" → add "(a bronze cast is made by taking a mold off the
original and pouring molten metal into it — so the bronze is a metal copy of the plaster,
which is exactly why it could be done after Boccioni was dead)." This is the one genuine
clarity gap: the mechanism the entire posthumous-bronze story rests on is assumed, not
explained. Worth fixing.

### [NICE-C3] "flanges" — glossed by description, not by name
"Flange" is a slightly technical word. The draft never defines it as a term but always
surrounds it with plain description ("curved fins and blades," "fins and blades that flare
out like wings"), so a reader gets the meaning from context every time. Acceptable as-is;
flagging only because the word recurs. No change required.

### [NICE-C4] "in the round" is used twice, glossed once
`UnqLooking` opens "This is a sculpture in the round" and explains it ("no single front…
you walk around it") — good. The PART A `heroFit` comment also says "a sculpture in the
round" but that's a code comment, not reader-facing, so it doesn't matter. No action.
