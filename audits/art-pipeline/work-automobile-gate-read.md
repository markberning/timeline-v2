## VERDICT

**PASS WITH FIXES.** No BLOCKERs. The draft clears all three axes at a high level:
the painter-who-became-the-noise-man hook is fully delivered and is genuinely the
spine of the piece; the LOOKING chapter does concrete, follow-your-eye guided looking
rather than asserting; clarity is strong, with nearly every term glossed inline on
first use. The fixes below are tightening, not rescue. One soft caution about a
LOOKING claim that is image-dependent (already flagged in the draft's own ledger —
not re-litigated here as a content-gate item, but noted so it isn't lost).

## (1) STORYTELLING

**Strong overall.** Story-first, dry wit present and earned, comparisons land,
scenes are set, and the meta-narration discipline is mostly very good. The five-chapter
arc (born into sound → take Marinetti's sentence at its word → fail to find the car →
put down the brush → custody/verdict) is a real narrative, not a fact dump. The
through-line chapter ("Two attacks on the same target") is the payoff the hook
promised and it pays off cleanly.

Dry-wit highlights that work and should be kept:
- "the curatorial equivalent of a raised eyebrow" (AutMaking, the [sic] date).
- "even the date on a Futurist painting refuses to sit still" (AutMaking).
- "an Italian painter cranked the future out of a wooden box" (AutNoise).
- "It is a quietly perfect custody." (AutAfterlife).
- "they are this painting finishing its sentence." (AutAfterlife verdict).

### [FIX] — meta-narration / "we" address creeping in (AutMaking, opening)
> "This painting is Russolo taking that sentence at its word."

This is fine as a sentence, but combined with the surrounding "Read that the way a
Parisian in 1909 would have" and "the version above is one careful published
rendering," the chapter leans on second-person stage-direction more than the absinthe
ref does. The absinthe ref earns its "you" by putting the reader *in the room* (walk
up to the Place Pigalle); here several "you" lines are instructions about how to read
rather than scene. **Not a blocker** — but trim one of the two reading-instructions in
the *Victory of Samothrace* paragraph so the wit lands once, not twice.
Fix: keep "a calculated outrage… make a respectable reader spit out his coffee"; the
"Read that the way a Parisian in 1909 would have" opener can go, the paragraph stands
without it.

### [FIX] — the honesty-label tic (AutPainter, "The creed")
> "It is worth saying once, plainly, that this was not a gentle program."
> "both are true at the same time, which is the honest way to hold Futurism."

Two honesty-labels stacked in one paragraph ("worth saying once, plainly" + "the
honest way to hold"). The MEMORY voice rule (`feedback_philosophy_voice_plain`,
applied across reads) flags self-narrated honesty/throat-clearing. The Fascism content
is correct and proportionate and should STAY — just drop the framing scaffolding so it
reads as fact, not as the narrator congratulating himself for telling it.
Fix: cut "It is worth saying once, plainly, that" → start the sentence "This was not a
gentle program." And cut "which is the honest way to hold Futurism" → end at "both are
true at the same time."

### [FIX] — flat stretch / redundancy (AutPainter, the "one small precision" paragraph)
> "If you ever see "Russolo signed the 1909 manifesto," you now know it is sloppy. The
> poet went first and alone; the painters signed up afterward."

The factual correction (did NOT sign 1909, signed the 1910 painters' manifestos) is
already made cleanly in the paragraph above it. This paragraph then re-states it three
ways: "the popular books get it wrong," "you now know it is sloppy," "the poet went
first and alone." That is one beat told three times — the only genuinely flat stretch
in AutPainter. The correction is worth keeping (it's a real disambiguation the fact
pack flags), but compress.
Fix: keep the first two sentences ("Russolo did not sign Marinetti's famous 1909
manifesto… That was Marinetti's own solo opening shot.") and the closer ("The poet
went first and alone; the painters signed up afterward."); cut the middle "If you ever
see…you now know it is sloppy" line.

### [NICE] — comparison density is good; one more wouldn't hurt AutNoise
The thrown-stone comparison (AutMaking) and the strip-of-film comparison (AutLooking)
are exactly the house move. AutNoise relies on naming (Varèse/Cage/Schaeffer) rather
than a felt comparison for what the intonarumori actually *sounded* like. Optional: a
one-clause "closer to a factory floor tuned to pitch than to an orchestra" type image
would let a zero-knowledge reader *hear* it. Not required — the "howlers, roarers,
cracklers, hummers, gurglers" list already does a lot.

## (2) LOOKING

**Strong — this is the best part of the draft.** The brief asks: does the canvas
section make the reader SEE the car dissolved into chevron/arrow force-lines, the
left-to-right directional thrust, the color planes, the wedge composition, speed-as-form?
Answer: yes, concretely, with guided looking rather than assertion. Specifics that
work:

- **Dissolved car:** "Go ahead and look for a wheel, a hood, a headlamp, a windscreen…
  You will not really find one." — real instruction, real payoff. The denser-knot-of-planes
  anchor ("a denser knot of planes near the heart of the picture") gives the eye a
  place to stand. Good.
- **Chevron/arrow force-lines:** "Pick one wedge near the dense core of the picture.
  Follow the arrowheads outward from it, the way you would follow a row of road signs."
  This is exactly the no-blind-coordinates, prose-pointer method the standing rule
  requires (`feedback_no_blind_image_coordinates`). Excellent.
- **Directional thrust:** "the whole composition tilts and drives one way… your eye…
  gets caught by the slant of the wedges and pulled along the same line the car takes."
  The runway/straightaway image earlier seeds it. Good.
- **Color planes / speed-as-form:** the strip-of-film comparison + "diagram of motion,
  a blueprint of acceleration with the engineering left showing" makes the stop-frame
  stack legible. Good.
- **The "painting trying to make a noise" close** is the looking-chapter's best move
  and ties LOOKING into the hook.

### [FIX] — the brief says "left-to-right thrust"; the draft says only "one way" (AutLooking)
The brief specifically names a **left-to-right** directional thrust. The draft
deliberately writes the direction-agnostic "one way" / "one direction" everywhere
(e.g. "all of them aimed the same way, in the direction the car is travelling";
"shoved from the denser, busier end toward the open field ahead"). This is the
*correct* caution — the draft's own fact ledger flags wedge-direction as "VERIFY ON
IMAGE at gate-1" and the coordinator note says every left/right `where` must be
re-checked against the born-verified hero before lock. So the prose is right to stay
agnostic UNTIL the image confirms.
Fix (process, not prose): the coordinator must confirm the actual thrust direction
against `ART_IMG.russoloAutomobile`. **If** it reads left-to-right (as the brief
assumes), the prose may be sharpened from "one way" to the concrete direction in at
least one place (the reader sees more from "left to right" than from "one way"). **If**
it does not, leave the agnostic phrasing — do not force the brief's "left-to-right"
onto the canvas. Flagging so the agnosticism is a verified choice, not an oversight.

### [FIX] — "dense core / heart of the picture" is asserted twice without locating it
> "a denser knot of planes near the heart of the picture" (¶2)
> "Pick one wedge near the dense core of the picture." (¶3)

The reader is told to start at the "dense core" but is never given a spatial hook for
*where* the core sits (left, center, the knot the wedges spring from). For a
zero-knowledge reader staring at a near-abstract canvas, "the dense core" may not be
self-evident. The annotations const (PART A) describes it as "the denser knot of
faceted, overlapping planes from which the wedges spring" — that "from which the wedges
spring" is the locating cue and it's missing from the prose chapter.
Fix: in ¶3, add the spring cue: "Pick one wedge near the dense core of the picture —
the busy knot the arrowheads seem to spring *from* —". (Keep image-agnostic re: side,
per the FIX above.)

### [NICE] — size phrasing slightly soft (AutLooking, ¶1)
> "about three and a half feet tall and four and a half feet wide"

The stats/dims const is 3 ft 5¾ in × 4 ft 7⅛ in. "Three and a half tall, four and a
half wide" is a fair round and reads better in prose than fractions — keep it. Only
noting that the kitchen-tray-style physical analogy that the absinthe ref uses ("roughly
the size of a kitchen tray stood on end") is absent here; a one-image size anchor would
match the house move. Optional.

## (3) CLARITY

**Strong.** Nearly every load-bearing term is glossed inline on first use for a
zero-knowledge reader. Confirmed glosses:

- **Futurism** — glossed at first use (AutPainter): "a movement in love with the modern
  industrial world… worshipped the machine, the electric-lit city… and above all speed."
  And the launch mechanism ("the way you launch a product, with an announcement"). Good.
- **Force-line / linea-forza** — fully glossed (AutMaking): "a directional line that
  carries an object's movement and energy out past the edges of the object itself, into
  the space all around it." Re-anchored visually in AutLooking. Good.
- **The Art of Noises / L'arte dei rumori** — glossed (AutNoise) with the argument
  summarized. Good.
- **intonarumori** — glossed + pronounced + etymology ("noise-tuners") + physical
  description. Good.
- ***Victory of Samothrace*** — glossed (2nd-c. BC winged marble goddess, Louvre,
  staircase). Good.
- **musique concrète** — glossed inline ("music built from recorded real-world sounds").
  Good.
- **abstraction** — glossed ("painting that has stopped showing recognizable things at
  all"). Good.
- **provenance** — glossed twice (AutAfterlife). Good — and consistent with absinthe ref.
- **Orphism** — glossed ("an offshoot of Cubism that built whole pictures out of pure
  color and rhythm"). Good.

### [FIX] — "chronophotograph" appears UNGLOSSED in the lineage const (PART A)
PART A `lineage.parents` lists `{ label: 'The chronophotograph', mode: 'civ' }`. The
term **chronophotograph** never appears in the Part B prose at all, and is not glossed
anywhere in the draft. For a zero-knowledge reader, "chronophotograph" is opaque (it's
the Marey/Muybridge stop-motion photo strips — directly relevant, since the LOOKING
chapter literally uses the "strip of film… same figure caught in a row of
slightly-shifted frames" image without ever naming the technology that did it first).
This is the single clearest unglossed-jargon item.
Fix (two options): (a) gloss it where the lineage chip renders if the UI shows a
tooltip/label, OR (b) better — name and gloss it in AutLooking where the strip-of-film
comparison already does the conceptual work: e.g. after "the same figure caught in a
row of slightly-shifted frames," add "(this is the chronophotograph — the
late-1800s motion-study photographs, like Eadweard Muybridge's galloping horse, that
froze a moving body into a row of stills)." That earns the lineage parent in the prose
instead of leaving it as an unexplained chip. **Coordinator: verify Muybridge/Marey
attribution if you add a name — do not assert from memory.**

### [FIX] — "grapeshot" inside the verbatim Marinetti quote (AutMaking) is unglossed
> "a roaring car that seems to ride on grapeshot"

The draft then *names* the dispute ("'grapeshot' or 'machine-gun fire'") which is good
handling, but never tells a zero-knowledge reader what grapeshot *is* (clustered
small iron balls fired from a cannon — i.e., the car looks like it's riding a blast).
The quote is verbatim and must not be altered, so the gloss has to live OUTSIDE the
quote.
Fix: in the unpacking paragraph, one clause — "'grapeshot' (clustered iron shot blasted
from a cannon), so the car seems to ride on an explosion." Light touch; the image
('ride on an explosion') is the point and it's currently left for the reader to supply.

### [NICE] — "faceted planes" leans on Cubism without naming the loan in-chapter
"shattered into faceted planes" / "broken into overlapping shards… the way a sheet of
glass breaks" is well-imaged for the eye (CLARITY-fine on its own terms). The lineage
const credits "Cubism's faceted planes" as a parent, but the prose never tells the
reader the faceting is a *borrowed* Cubist device. Not a clarity failure — the glass
image carries the meaning — but a one-clause nod ("the faceting Russolo borrowed from
Cubism") would let the lineage chip pay off in prose, same problem as chronophotograph
above. Optional.

### [NICE] — pronunciation glosses are consistent and good
Boccioni, Marinetti, intonarumori, Pratella(implicit) all pronounced; matches the
absinthe house pattern. No action.
