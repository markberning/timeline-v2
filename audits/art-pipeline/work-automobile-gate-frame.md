### [FIX-1] Provenance: the attested Robert-Delaunay link is omitted entirely
**Where:** `AutAfterlife` provenance prose (draft lines ~235–238) + PART A `provenance[]` (lines 62–66) + fact pack §5.
**What:** The draft treats the middle of the chain as a total documentary blank ("the record has two ends and an empty middle"). But a secondary source explicitly bridges it: the painting debuted at the **Lacerba-organized Futurist show at Galleria Gonnelli, Florence (30 Nov 1913 – 18 Jan 1914)** and was **acquired there by Robert Delaunay** — which is exactly *how* it reached the Delaunay household and *why* Sonia (his widow) had it to give in 1949. The draft's "we are not going to make the middle up" is admirable doctrine, but it currently reads as if NOTHING is known, when in fact there is a plausible, sourced — if not museum-confirmed — answer.

**Why this is a [FIX] not a [BLOCKER]:** the source is secondary (Grokipedia/caradisiac tier), the Pompidou record does NOT corroborate the Robert-Delaunay purchase, and the doctrine "a missing link beats a wrong one" is correct. So the right move is NOT to assert "Robert Delaunay bought it." But the draft over-claims the silence: writing "empty middle / not recorded anywhere" when a named, repeated attribution exists is its own small inaccuracy.

**Fix (either path is acceptable — do not assert it as fact):**
- *Minimal:* add one hedged sentence to the gap paragraph, e.g.: "Some accounts say the painting was bought out of that Florence show by Robert Delaunay — Sonia's husband and her co-founder in Orphism — which would explain how it reached her hands; but the museum's own record doesn't confirm that link, so we note it and don't lean on it." (This also strengthens the "one speed-painter to another" custody note — it wasn't a stranger's gift, it likely came through her own husband.)
- *Or:* if the coordinator wants to keep the provenance strictly museum-sourced, soften the absolute "not recorded anywhere / empty middle" to "the museum's file doesn't record how it travelled from Russolo to Sonia Delaunay" — i.e., scope the silence to the museum record, not to all scholarship.

**Recommend the minimal hedged-sentence path** — it's both more complete AND more honest about what's actually out there.

## LENS 2 — FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### (a) Futurism-ideology honesty — the load-bearing one for THIS work. **PASS.**
This is the single most manifesto-tethered painting in the chain (its whole premise is Marinetti's "roaring car," point 4), so the honesty line carries the most weight here, and the draft lands it correctly:
- It is **present** (draft lines ~127, the dedicated paragraph in `AutPainter`).
- It is **proportionate** — one clear paragraph, not buried in a caption, not inflated into a lecture: "this was not a gentle program … glorified war as 'the world's only hygiene,' sneered at museums and women … Marinetti would go on, after the First World War, to help found the Italian Fascist movement. The art is genuinely thrilling, the worship of force genuinely ugly, and both are true at the same time."
- It is **non-anachronistic** — the Fascism link is correctly placed *after* WWI (the Fasci were 1919; the manifesto 1909), with "would go on … to help found," so it never back-projects Fascism onto the 1909 text or onto Russolo's 1912–13 painting.
- It correctly **separates Marinetti from Russolo** — the violence/Fascism is attributed to Marinetti and the manifesto, not pinned on Russolo, who signed only the 1910 painters' texts (the draft is precise about this in `AutPainter` and the precision paragraph).
- The "scorn for woman" axis the brief asked about is **named** ("sneered at … women") — VERIFIED against point 9. Good. See [FIX-2] below: I'm promoting the "sneered at women" softness to a [FIX] because the brief explicitly asks to name the misogyny *proportionately*, and "sneered" reads as mere dismissiveness, materially softer than the manifesto's literal Point-9 "scorn for woman" (*disprezzo della donna*) listed in the same breath as glorifying war and militarism (Point 10 adds "fight … feminism"). The verdict is unaffected, but the fix sharpens a load-bearing surface.

### [FIX-2] Sharpen the misogyny phrasing to the manifesto's literal weight
**Where:** `AutPainter` "The creed" paragraph (draft line ~127).
**What:** the draft renders the misogyny as Marinetti having "sneered at museums and women." Point 9's verbatim phrase is **"scorn for woman"** — listed alongside war-glorification and militarism, not as a throwaway sneer.
**Exact draft text:**
> it glorified war as &ldquo;the world&rsquo;s only hygiene,&rdquo; sneered at museums and women, and rang with a violence that was part of the appeal.
**Suggested fix (keeps the one-line proportion):**
> it glorified war as &ldquo;the world&rsquo;s only hygiene&rdquo; and preached &ldquo;scorn for woman,&rdquo; in the same breath as militarism, and rang with a violence that was part of the appeal.
(Museums get their own honest beating in `AutMaking`'s Samothrace passage, so dropping "museums" here loses nothing. If quoting "scorn for woman," it is a verbatim manifesto phrase — quote it as one cited translation, consistent with the draft's own one-translation rule.)

No anachronism, no whitewash, no overcorrection. This is exactly the calibrated treatment the brief asked for.

### (b) Genius-myth — **PASS, clean.**
No lone-genius inflation. Russolo's invention (intonarumori) is correctly **shared with Ugo Piatti** ("with a fellow painter named Ugo Piatti … He built the instruments"), the force-line is credited to **the Futurist painters / Boccioni's manifesto**, not to Russolo personally, and the noise-music lineage is framed as "**a** founding ancestor" / "**counted** a founding ancestor," not "the father of." The "cranked the future out of a wooden box" line is vivid but it's about the instrument's import, not a genius-cult claim. No fix.

### (c) First / only overclaim — **PASS, clean.**
Checked every superlative:
- "the first instruments ever built deliberately to play *noise* as music" — this is **defensible and standard** (the intonarumori are routinely called the first instruments purpose-built for noise-as-music); it's also narrowly scoped ("deliberately … as music"), not a blanket "first noise ever." VERIFIED-adjacent and safe. Keep.
- "the most quoted line Futurism ever produced" / "one of the most beloved sculptures on Earth" — rhetorical, hedged with "most," not falsifiable "the only." Fine.
- "a founding ancestor of noise music" — hedged with the indefinite article. Fine.
- The draft explicitly **avoids** the available overclaim ("Russolo never quite crosses the line into total abstraction here … in the same years that other painters were taking the full step") — it actively cedes the "first to abstraction" crown rather than grabbing it. Good instinct.

No first/only correction needed.
