## SPECIAL-CARE CHECKS (all confirmed)

### 1. rights: 'in-copyright' — CORRECT (not pd-us) ✓
Kline died **May 13, 1962** (Wikipedia, Find a Grave). A 1950 painting by an artist who died 1962 is decisively in copyright in the US; rights run via the Kline estate / ARS. `rights: 'in-copyright'` is right and `pd-us` would be wrong. The `heroCredit` ("in copyright, shown small under fair use; rights with the Franz Kline estate / ARS") and the fair-use framing are correct. **No flag.**

### 2. Medium / dimensions / credit line / accession ✓
- **Medium:** "Oil on canvas" — matches MoMA. ✓
- **Dimensions:** MoMA states **148.3 × 186.7 cm (58 3/8" × 6' 1 1/2")**. Verified math: 148.3 cm = 58.39 in = **4 ft 10 3/8 in** (height); 186.7 cm = 73.50 in = **6 ft 1 1/2 in** (width). Draft's `'4 ft 10 3/8 in × 6 ft 1 1/2 in'` (landscape, height × width) is correct to the rounding. heroAspect 1.27 vs true W/H = 1.259 — fine. ✓
- **Credit line:** MoMA reads **"Gift of Mr. and Mrs. David M. Solinger"** — the full "Mr. and Mrs." form. The draft carries it verbatim in `acquired`, `provenance[2].price`, and the afterlife prose ("*Gift of Mr. and Mrs. David M. Solinger*"). The fact pack's own flag (brief had said "Gift of David M. Solinger") is correctly resolved to the full form. **Confirmed present.** ✓
- **Accession:** 2.1952 — matches MoMA. The "1952 = acquisition year" inference is standard MoMA accession convention and corroborated by Solinger's documented gift. ✓

### 3. The white is PAINTED (black AND white both active) ✓
The draft makes this the load-bearing correction (railroad blurb, the entire "The white" subsection of `ChfLooking`, the `looking` annotation "The white is painted, not blank", the break section). MoMA's own commentary frames the Kline quote exactly this way: he "did not see himself as painting black signs on a white ground." The figure/ground-trade-places reading is well-supported. **Confirmed and correctly emphasized.** ✓

### 4. Kline REJECTED the calligraphy/Zen reading ✓
Draft `ChfAfterlife` "The reading he refused" explicitly presents calligraphy/Zen as a comparison **Kline rejected**, not as fact ("a comparison Kline *refused*, not a fact about the work"). Web-confirmed: Kline denied the equation; the Bokujinkai/Morita Shiryū connection was "later denied" (Wikipedia); Tate's "Contested Comparisons" essay documents the dispute. The draft is correctly framed. ✓

### 5. Bell-Opticon projector story — hedged, not asserted as simple fact ✓
Draft `ChfProjector` is explicitly hedged: tells it as "the standard account," "probably tidied up," "take the eureka-moment framing with some salt," and quotes de Kooning's "took quite a while to work it out." Web-confirmed (Wikipedia): Elaine de Kooning "gave a romanticized account"; Willem de Kooning's actual line is *"Franz had a vision of something and sometimes it takes quite a while to work it out."* **Hedge is correct and well-calibrated.** ✓ (See NICE-1 on the exact quote.)

### 6. "Chief" = a LOCOMOTIVE ✓
Draft says named after a locomotive Kline remembered from his Pennsylvania railroad-town childhood, not a person/Native American chief; declines to over-specify the rail line. Web-confirmed (MoMA Learning, Smarthistory, TheArtStory: "the name of a train that passed through his childhood hometown in Pennsylvania"). Correctly not over-specified. ✓

### 7. The Kline quote — wording + source ✓
**"I paint the white as well as the black, and the white is just as important."** Verified verbatim against MoMA (the museum's own anchor for *Chief*, in the calligraphy-rejection context) and the Sylvester 1960 interview. The draft quotes it exactly in the `ChfLooking` blockquote, the `looking` annotation, and the KEY STATEMENT note. No year/venue asserted (the floated "1958" correctly omitted). ✓
The secondary quote *"calligraphy is simply the art of writing"* is also verified: from the **March 1960 David Sylvester interview** (first publ. *Living Arts*, 1963; later in *Interviews with American Artists*, 2001). The draft's "in a 1960 interview" is accurate to the interview date. Full context confirmed: "calligraphy is writing and I am not writing." ✓

## NICE (optional polish — none ship-blocking)

**NICE-1 — de Kooning quote is a truncated paraphrase, but accurately so.**
The draft quotes de Kooning as: *"took quite a while to work it out."* The full sourced line is *"Franz had a vision of something and sometimes it takes quite a while to work it out."* The draft's clipped form is grammatically embedded ("said the change 'took quite a while to work it out'") and is faithful to the source's meaning. No correction required; if the author wants belt-and-suspenders fidelity, the embedding could read: de Kooning said it *"sometimes … takes quite a while to work it out."* Optional only.

**NICE-2 — Solinger biographical detail is correct and could be tightened.**
Draft calls Solinger a "New York lawyer and modern-art collector (later a notable figure at the Whitney Museum)" / "later a Whitney Museum figure." Web-confirmed he became **President of the Whitney Museum of American Art (1966)** — the first non-Whitney-family president — and was a Kline client/patron. The draft's vaguer "notable figure / later a Whitney Museum figure" is accurate and safely under-claimed; no fix needed. Could optionally sharpen to "later president of the Whitney Museum." Contextual, not load-bearing.

**NICE-3 — Rosenberg "action painting" 1952 date is correct.**
Draft says Rosenberg coined "action painting" in 1952, "two years after *Chief*." Verified: "The American Action Painters," *ARTnews*, **December 1952**. Correct. (Minor nuance for awareness, not a fix: Rosenberg's essay names no individual artists and is often read as keyed to Pollock/de Kooning rather than Kline specifically — the draft does not over-claim Kline as Rosenberg's example, so this is fine as written.)
