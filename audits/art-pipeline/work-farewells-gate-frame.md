### [NICE] C1 — Benedetta Cappa was herself a Futurist artist (one clause)
The draft introduces her only as "Marinetti's widow." She was a painter and writer in
her own right (a leading second-wave Futurist, the *aeropittura* circle), which makes the
chain Boccioni → the movement's founder → **a Futurist painter** → Rockefeller tidier and
truer. Optional one clause in FarAfterlife: "...his widow, **Benedetta Cappa Marinetti**, a
Futurist painter in her own right, who held it until..." Not required; her name + the
hedged 1949 sale already carry the provenance.
*(This also softens, slightly, the manifesto's "scorn for woman" line — a woman Futurist
held the masterpiece for five years — but that's a bonus, not the reason.)*

### [NICE] C2 — the manifesto's first home was an Italian paper
FarFuturism says the manifesto ran "on the front page of a Paris newspaper" (true and
famous — *Le Figaro*, 20 Feb 1909). Strictly, it first appeared days earlier in an
Italian paper (*La Gazzetta dell'Emilia*, 5 Feb 1909). The draft's claim is **not wrong**
(the *Le Figaro* front page is the canonical event), so this is purely optional color, not
a correction.

### [NICE] C3 — "those who go" quote stays in its lane (no action — flagging it's handled right)
The fact pack flagged the "loneliness, anguish, and dazed confusion" line as belonging to
*Those Who Go*, not *The Farewells*. The draft correctly **never** puts it on *The
Farewells*. Web search re-confirms it's Boccioni on *Those Who Go*. The draft could
optionally cite it once in FarStation/FarLooking as documented context for the sibling
panel, but its omission is the safe choice and fully defensible. No change needed.

is a model treatment of the guardrail.

#### [FIX] F1 — the manifesto's "scorn for woman" is dropped, and the brief requires it
The brief's framing item (a) explicitly names two things the 1909 manifesto did: glorified
war/violence **and** "scorn for woman." The draft includes the war half (verbatim "the
world's only hygiene") but **omits the misogyny half entirely**. This is the one
substantive gap on this lens: the manifesto's ugliness is presented as only war-worship,
when the same sentence also enshrined contempt for women — a documented, load-bearing part
of "the honest history of this picture" the chapter promises to give.

Web-verified exact wording (Britannica / Le Figaro text): *"We will glorify war—the
world's only hygiene—militarism, patriotism, the destructive gesture of freedom-bringers,
beautiful ideas worth dying for, **and scorn for woman**."* The two phrases sit in one
sentence; quoting only one misrepresents the document's range.

EXACT text — FarFuturism, "The darker edge," current sentence:

> Marinetti&rsquo;s 1909 manifesto did not only glorify speed and machines; it also
> <strong>glorified war and violence</strong>, calling war (in the famous phrase) the
> world&rsquo;s only hygiene, and sneered at the weak and the old.

SUGGESTED replacement (adds the documented misogyny, keeps it to one proportionate clause,
no new adjectives):

> Marinetti&rsquo;s 1909 manifesto did not only glorify speed and machines; it also
> <strong>glorified war and violence</strong>, calling war (in the famous phrase) the
> world&rsquo;s only hygiene, and in the same breath named a &ldquo;scorn for woman,&rdquo;
> sneering at the weak and the old.

(Use the documented quoted phrase "scorn for woman" — it is the manifesto's own English
rendering — and keep it inside the existing single sentence so the proportion is unchanged.
This is a one-clause add, not a new beat.)

### (b) Genius-myth inflation — CLEAN.
No "lone genius" or "ahead of his time" inflation. Boccioni is "the movement's sharpest
painter and its leading theorist" — sourced and bounded (he was Futurism's principal
painter-theorist; defensible, not hagiographic). The poignant FarAfterlife close ("stopped
almost before he had started... you can paint a feeling after all") is earned elegy, not
genius-myth — it's about the work outliving the man, not crowning him. The lines-of-force
credit is correctly hedged: the **term** is dated to the 1912 Paris catalogue, not claimed
as Boccioni's coinage-on-this-canvas. No change.

### (c) "First / only" overclaim — CLEAN.
No "first to paint motion," "first to paint a feeling," "only," or "invented" overclaim.
Checked every superlative-shaped phrase:
- "the most turbulent" / "the most famous one" — bounded comparatives **within** the
  triptych / between the two versions, both true. ✓
- "the single coldest, steadiest mark on the canvas" / "the one cold thing" — internal to
  *this* picture, descriptive, not a historical first. ✓
- "the oldest human [feeling] there is" (watching someone be carried away) — rhetorical,
  about the emotion, not an art-historical claim. Fine. ✓
- Cubism credited to Picasso & Braque (not to Boccioni); Divisionism placed as a Seurat
