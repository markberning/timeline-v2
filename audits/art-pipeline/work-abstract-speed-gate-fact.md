## [FIX]

### FIX-1 — "alongside Mussolini" overstates Mussolini's co-authorship of the 1919 Fascist manifesto
**Exact draft text** (AbsAbstraction, draft line 241):
> "Marinetti himself went on, a decade later, to **help write the founding manifesto of Italian Fascism** alongside Mussolini."

**Problem:** The founding manifesto of Italian Fascism (the 1919 *Fascist Manifesto* / *Manifesto dei Fasci italiani di combattimento*) was written by **Marinetti and Alceste De Ambris** — De Ambris is the documented co-author of the *text*. Mussolini led the Fasci di Combattimento and the movement, but is not the manifesto's textual co-author. "help write the founding manifesto … alongside Mussolini" reads as crediting Mussolini with co-authorship of the document, which the sources do not support.

**Correction (pick one):**
- Drop the attribution of co-authorship to Mussolini: *"…went on, a decade later, to help write the 1919 founding manifesto of Italian Fascism"* (true: he co-wrote it, with De Ambris); or
- Keep Mussolini but reframe to the relationship, not the authorship: *"…went on to help write the 1919 founding manifesto of Italian Fascism and became an enthusiastic backer of Mussolini."*

The first is the lighter touch and stays inside the fact pack's "proportionate, no over-claim" instruction. The draft's surrounding claims (1909 manifesto "glorified war as the world's only hygiene"; Futurists campaigned for Italy to enter WWI and several died in it) are all **verified and correctly handled** — only the "alongside Mussolini" clause needs the fix.

**Source:** Britannica & Wikipedia, *Filippo Tommaso Marinetti* — "In 1919, Marinetti co-wrote with Alceste De Ambris the Fascist Manifesto." The 1909 line "We will glorify war—the world's only hygiene" is verbatim from the Manifesto of Futurism (Le Figaro, 20 Feb 1909).

### FIX-2 — the Tate's alternative title for the CENTRE panel is mislabeled as "a related panel"
**Exact draft text** (AbsTriptych, draft line 214):
> "This panel is usually called *Abstract Speed + Sound*, but **the Tate's catalogue refers to a related panel as *Line of Force + Noise***, and the various panels get named differently from source to source."

**Problem:** Per the Tate catalogue entry for the right-hand panel (T01222), the Tate names the three panels: left = *Line of Force + Landscape*, **centre = *Lines of Force + Noise***, right = *Abstract Speed – The Car has Passed*. So "Lines of Force + Noise" is the Tate's name for **this very work** (the centre / Guggenheim panel), not for "a related panel." The draft's phrasing makes the title-wobble point correctly *in spirit* (titles differ source to source) but factually misplaces which panel the Tate name attaches to — it understates the point. The fact pack's headline table is explicit: the centre panel "is also referred to in the Tate entry as 'Lines of Force + Noise' / 'Line of Force + Noise'."

**Correction:**
> "This panel is usually called *Abstract Speed + Sound*, but the Tate's catalogue calls **this same central panel** *Lines of Force + Noise*, and the various panels get named differently from source to source."

(Tate's spelling is "**Lines** of Force + Noise" with an *s* — draft has "Line of Force + Noise"; use the Tate spelling or keep it loose, minor.)

**Source:** Tate catalogue entry, *Abstract Speed – The Car has Passed* (T01222): "this painting was originally the right-hand part of a triptych, with the left-hand part called 'Line of Force + Landscape' and the central one 'Lines of Force + Noise'."

### NICE-2 — Russolo aside chronology ("shortly take this idea out of paint")
**Draft text** (AbsMaking, line 156): "The Futurist painter Luigi Russolo would shortly take this idea out of paint entirely and start building noise-instruments…" Russolo's *The Art of Noises* manifesto is dated **11 March 1913** and the intonarumori were performed in 1913–14 — i.e. **contemporaneous** with Balla's 1913–14 work, not strictly "shortly after." "Shortly" reads as sequential; "around the same time" would be more exact. Claim itself (Russolo built noise-instruments to make music from engine/factory noise) is fully verified. Minor; tone-only.

### NICE-3 — Dog provenance detail (not in prose, so no action)
Verified context: the Dog was painted May 1912, sold by Balla to A. Conger Goodyear in 1938, acquired by the gallery (then Albright-Knox, now Buffalo AKG) December 1984. The draft only claims "1912" + "Buffalo AKG Art Museum in New York State" — both correct. Note the museum was renamed Buffalo AKG (from Albright-Knox) in 2023; the draft uses the current name, good.

### NICE-4 — "millboard" medium
Draft const uses `medium: 'Oil on millboard, in the artist's painted frame'` and prose says "millboard (a stiff, heavy paperboard)." The live Guggenheim string is "Oil on paperboard, in artist's painted frame"; the catalogue caption reads "oil on unvarnished millboard." Both are museum-sourced and equivalent (paperboard = millboard) per the fact pack. No change needed; the parenthetical gloss is accurate.
