## [BLOCKER]

### B1 — Bal Tabarin closure year wrong (1952 → 1953); demolition year unsupported
**Where:** §hall annotation prose, BalHall:
> "The hall ran until the end of **1952** and was finally torn down in **1966**, the
> same year Severini died."

**Problem:** Two date errors, one clearly wrong.
- **Closure: 1952 is wrong — it was 1953.** Both the fact pack's own cited source
  (Wikipedia Bal Tabarin) and paris.fr state the hall closed in **1953** ("After the
  war Bal Tabarin was taken over by the proprietors of the Moulin Rouge, who closed
  it in 1953"). The fact pack §3 Beat 3 says "ran until the end of 1952," which is
  itself contradicted by the source it cites — so the error originates in the pack and
  the draft inherited it. Either way it ships wrong.
- **Demolition: 1966 is not supported and conflicts with the sources.** French
  sources (paris.fr, Montmartre-secret, neufhistoire) describe a farewell gala in
  **1963** "after which the Bal Tabarin was destroyed"; the building was later
  replaced by a residential block. The neat "torn down in 1966, the same year Severini
  died" is a too-tidy coincidence with no clean source behind it — at minimum it is
  [UNVERIFIED], and the weight of sourcing points to **1963**, not 1966.

**Correction:** Change to closed **1953**; for demolition either drop the year or
write "demolished after a 1963 farewell gala." Recommended rewrite:
> "The hall ran until **1953**, when the owners of the nearby Moulin Rouge bought it
> and shut it down; the building was pulled down after a final gala in **1963**. By
> then it was the painting, not the building, that kept the name alive."

The "same year Severini died" flourish must go unless 1966 is independently
confirmed (it isn't). **Note for the coordinator: fix the fact pack §3 Beat 3 too
(1952→1953; 1966→1963), since the error is upstream.**
**Source:** Wikipedia *Bal Tabarin (Paris)*; paris.fr "Il était une fois le Bal
Tabarin"; Montmartre-secret.

## [FIX]

### F1 — Scissors-nude placement: "upper-left" vs the sources' "upper portion"
**Where:** annotation #4 `where`: "Tucked into the **upper-left** chaos"; const
comment and BalLooking say "High in the **left-hand** jumble" / "High up in the
left-hand jumble."

**Problem:** Smarthistory/LibreTexts group the three odd figures — black cat head,
camel rider, **nude on scissors** — together in the **upper portion** of the canvas;
they do not pin the nude to the *left*. The draft asserts a specific "upper-left"
position that the sources do not confirm, and §0.4 of the pack itself warns against
captioning specific figure positions not nailed by a source. (Note an internal
inconsistency too: annotation #4's `where` says "upper-left" while annotation #1's
const detail earlier referred to "the upper-left chaos" for the nude as well — both
inherit the same unsupported left-placement.)

**Correction:** Soften to "high in the upper jumble" / "up in the chaos" and drop the
"left." Keep "scissors," "realistic," "small," "riding like a hobbyhorse" — all
sourced. Per the no-blind-coordinates rule, prose pointers should not over-specify a
location the sources don't give.
**Source:** Smarthistory / LibreTexts 5.1.5 ("a black cat head, a North African man
riding a camel, and a nude woman riding a pair of scissors" — grouped, upper area).

### F2 — "born intellectually in Paris" quote is real but lightly clipped
**Where:** BalParis: He later said he was "born intellectually" in Paris.

**Problem:** Accurate in substance but the actual quotation is fuller and worth
getting right since it's in quotation marks: Severini said "The cities to which I feel
most strongly bound are Cortona and Paris: I was born physically in the first,
intellectually and spiritually in the second." The draft's three-word clip ("born
intellectually") is faithful and not wrong, but a quoted fragment should match the
source register — consider "born… intellectually and spiritually" or recasting as a
paraphrase to avoid implying the exact words were just "born intellectually."
**Source:** Severini, quoted via Britannica / Severini biography mirrors.

## [NICE]

### N1 — "VALSE anchors the lower right" is asserted with more confidence than sourced
Annotation #3 and BalLooking place VALSE "toward the lower right." The fact pack §0.1
carries "VALSE anchors the bottom-right," so it's pack-supported, but no primary source
I could re-verify pins VALSE to a specific quadrant (the readable-on-the-image claim).
Low risk because it traces to the pack; flagging only so the coordinator knows it rests
on the pack's reading, not an independently confirmed source. If the image is to hand
at integration, eyeball it.

### N2 — Bergson framing: draft says "duration"; one source emphasizes "élan vital"
BalHall attributes the simultaneity/memory idea to Bergson's "duration." Correct and
standard (Smarthistory ties the work to Bergson). One mirror foregrounds *élan vital*
rather than *durée*, but "duration/memory holds time together" is the mainstream
teaching frame and is accurately the influence-reading the pack asked for (attribute as
influence, not as Severini's stated source — which the draft does). No change needed;
noted for completeness.

### N3 — "martini glasses" is a reasonable read but slightly specific
Sources say "glasses"/the night's drink (Sartle: martini glasses as evidence of
debauchery); "martini" is supported by Sartle but is a more specific cocktail call than
some sources make. It traces to the pack and a source, so keep — flagging only that
"glasses / the night's drink" is the safer floor if a reviewer wants maximum caution.
