Draft hook options (storytelling-first; author picks/sharpens):

- The painting is **under two feet tall** yet was a flashpoint of the 1905 Salon d'Automne scandal that named Fauvism. The hook should carry: (a) the open window onto Collioure's harbor, (b) the unnatural pure color, (c) the "wild beasts" naming.

## 3. NARRATIVE BEATS (~5; each tied to sources)

### Beat 1 — The summer of 1905 at Collioure, with Derain
- Matisse traveled to **Collioure**, a small fishing village/port on France's Mediterranean coast (near the Spanish border, Roussillon), in the **summer of 1905**, and rented an apartment overlooking the harbor. **[DOCUMENTED]** — NGA, henrimatisse.org, Smarthistory.
- He was joined by the younger painter **André Derain** (1880–1954), who arrived in **early July 1905**. **[DOCUMENTED]** — barefootblogger / Glasstire / Apollo (Met "Vertigo of Color" show 2023). The two worked side by side that summer; the collaboration is standard art history (the Met devoted a 2023 exhibition, *Vertigo of Color: Matisse, Derain, and the Origins of Fauvism*, to exactly this summer).
- Collioure is routinely called **"the birthplace of Fauvism."** **[DOCUMENTED as a widely-held characterization]** — Glasstire, barefootblogger. (Frame as "where Fauvism was born" / "the summer that produced Fauvism," not a single-day origin myth.)

### Beat 2 — The window-onto-the-harbor motif
- The painting depicts **the view out the window of his apartment/room in Collioure**, looking over the harbor to the sea. **[DOCUMENTED]** — NGA, Wikipedia.
- Structure (load-bearing for the "looking" gate): a **series of frames within frames** — the wall contains the window; the window frames the middle ground; the balcony crops the harbor/landscape; the harbor reaches to the horizon. **[DOCUMENTED]** — Smarthistory / uen.pressbooks (SmartHistory text), reproduced via search.
- The **casements of a large French window project toward us / open inward**, revealing the harbor. **[DOCUMENTED]** — henrimatisse.org, Smarthistory.

### Beat 3 — Pure, unmixed Fauve color; zone-by-zone brushwork
- Matisse handled **each area of the image with a distinctly different handling of the brush** — interior room, the window itself, the balcony, the harbor view each painted differently. **[DOCUMENTED]** — NGA / ngabiographies.org.
- **Complementary pairs drive the color**: orange-red masts over blue hulls; red blossoms among green leaves; turquoise against pink reflections. **[DOCUMENTED]** — ngabiographies.org (NGA-affiliated).
- Specific color reading (ngabiographies.org, NGA-affiliated highlight essay) **[DOCUMENTED, but attribute to that essay, do not over-assert as canvas-universal fact]**:
  - Interior walls: **peacock blue and fuchsia pink**; doors coral-orange and cranberry red.
  - Window/sill: three flowerpots in crimson red, marmalade orange, royal blue; foliage in cardinal red and turquoise.
  - Balcony: a band of **ultramarine blue** with vines in pine and lime green.
  - Harbor: rust-orange masts; hulls in indigo blue, flamingo pink, forest green, marigold orange.
  - Water: parallel strokes of pale pink and butter yellow. Sky: wavy lines of steel blue, periwinkle, seafoam green.
- The color is **non-naturalistic / expressive** — "bright, unnatural colors that expressed feeling rather than copying what the eye sees." **[DOCUMENTED]** — barefootblogger, Wikipedia/Khan framing.

### Beat 4 — The 1905 Salon d'Automne and the "cage of wild beasts"
- Shown at the **Salon d'Automne, Paris, 1905** (opened autumn 1905, Grand Palais). **[DOCUMENTED]** — NGA, Wikipedia, fr.wikipedia *Salon d'Automne de 1905*.
- Hung in **Salle/Gallery VII**, with works by Matisse, Derain, Vlaminck and others, around **two Renaissance-style sculptures by Albert Marque**. **[DOCUMENTED]** — fr.wikipedia, search-confirmed (SFMOMA / Wikipedia *Woman with a Hat*).
- **Matisse's *Woman with a Hat* (*Femme au chapeau*, 1905, now SFMOMA) hung in the SAME room** and drew the loudest scandal. **[DOCUMENTED]** — SFMOMA, Wikipedia *Woman with a Hat*. (An SFMOMA/NGA 2026 reunion note says the two will hang together "for the first time since 1970," confirming they shared the 1905 room.) Use as a paired beat: *Open Window* + *Woman with a Hat* were the two Matisse lightning rods of that room.
  - **[DOCUMENTED]** Gertrude and Leo Stein bought *Woman with a Hat* at/near the close of the 1905 Salon for **500 francs**. Source: Wikipedia *Woman with a Hat*, SFMOMA. (This is *Woman with a Hat*, NOT *Open Window* — keep them distinct; do not attribute a Stein purchase to *Open Window*.)
- **The "wild beasts" coinage — THE legend trap, handle exactly:**
  - **[DOCUMENTED]** Critic **Louis Vauxcelles**, in *Gil Blas*, **17 October 1905**, wrote of Albert Marque's quattrocento-style busts amid the violent canvases: *"Donatello chez les fauves"* — "Donatello among the wild beasts." This printed phrase is what popularized the name **Fauvism** ("les fauves" = the wild beasts). Source: fr.wikipedia *Salon d'Automne de 1905*; en.wikipedia *Fauvism* / *Louis Vauxcelles*.
  - **[DISPUTED / part-legend]** In his **1939** book *Le Fauvisme*, Vauxcelles said the comparison was sparked by an **unidentified passer-by** who, seeing the room, said to Matisse "Donatello in the cage with the wild beasts." So the famous phrase is Vauxcelles's print version of an anecdote he later credited to someone else. **Frame it**: the printed line is documented; the "someone said to Matisse" backstory is Vauxcelles's own much-later retelling — do not assert the cage-quote as a verbatim live event.
  - ⚠️ Do NOT claim Matisse coined it, or that it was an insult Matisse embraced on the spot, or that *Open Window* specifically provoked the phrase — the phrase was about the **whole room**, prompted by the **Marque sculptures**, not this one canvas.

### Beat 5 — The window as a lifelong Matisse motif
- The **open window** became a **recurring subject across Matisse's whole career** — Paris, then Nice and Étretat, into the late 1940s. **[DOCUMENTED]** — Wikipedia ("Windows became a recurring artistic theme throughout Matisse's career… Nice and Étretat… late 1940s"); NGA framing ("Matisse's career-long penchant for window scenes").
- *Open Window, Collioure* is the **early, foundational** statement of that motif — the threshold where inside and outside meet. Good closing beat: the window is both a literal subject and Matisse's lifelong metaphor for painting as a frame onto color.

## 4. "LOOK CLOSER" ANNOTATIONS (6 — `{label, where, detail}`; visual claims, gated against the image)

> Per pipeline: `where` is a written location phrase; NO crop/pin coordinates. Verify each against the actual painting at gate time. This is a PORTRAIT canvas; "left/right/top/bottom" below are viewer-relative.

1. **The boats in the harbor** — *where:* "Center, the middle band, beyond the balcony rail, out on the water." — Small **sailboats / fishing boats with masts** ride the harbor; the masts are rust/orange-red, the hulls blue, pink, green and orange — and they sit over their own colored reflections. The boats are the one clearly readable "real subject" of the view; everything else dissolves toward pattern. **[DOCUMENTED]** — NGA ("blue-hulled boats float on pink waves"); ngabiographies.org (masts/hulls colors).

2. **The flower pots on the balcony/sill** — *where:* "Lower center, on the floor of the shallow balcony just inside the window opening." — A cluster of **potted plants** (the colored pots themselves picked out in red, orange and blue) sits on the balcony floor, a near foreground that pushes the harbor back. **[DOCUMENTED]** — Smarthistory ("the balcony with flower pots on the floor"); ngabiographies.org.

3. **The mullioned French window frame** — *where:* "The two tall casement leaves swinging in from the left and right edges, with the cross-bars and small panes of glass." — The **open casement window** divides the canvas; its leaves carry dabs of green that read as climbing vines. The window frame is the literal skeleton of the "frames within frames." **[DOCUMENTED]** — henrimatisse.org ("the casements of a large French window project toward us"); Smarthistory (frames within frames; vines down the sides of the window).
   - ⚠️ Confirm against image whether the panes carry the harbor reflection vs. clear glass before asserting; some readings describe the glazed leaves reflecting color. Tag any reflection claim [DISPUTED] until eyeballed.

4. **The pink and green walls** — *where:* "The flat vertical bands at the far left and far right edges — the interior wall to one side, the window-leaf to the other." — The **interior wall** is painted in flat slabs of pink/rose and green (often described peacock-blue + fuchsia-pink). These flat side-bands frame the busier center and are pure invented color, not observed wall paint. **[DOCUMENTED]** — ngabiographies.org (peacock blue / fuchsia pink walls); NGA (zone-by-zone handling).

5. **The patchwork of pure color (zone-by-zone brushwork)** — *where:* "Across the whole canvas — compare the short broken dashes of the sky and water to the flat slabs of the walls and the scribbled dabs of the vines." — Each zone is built from a **different mark**: flat planes for the interior, broken Divisionist-style dashes for sky and water, quick scribbled strokes for the foliage. The colors are laid down **pure and unmixed**, set in **complementary pairs** (orange/blue, red/green). **[DOCUMENTED]** — NGA ("each area… a distinctly different handling of the brush"); ngabiographies.org (complementary pairs; parallel strokes; wavy lines).

6. **The dissolved inside/outside boundary** — *where:* "The seam where the balcony rail and window opening meet the water — the middle of the canvas." — There is **no firm line between interior and harbor**: the same hot pinks and greens run from the room, across the sill and vines, out onto the water and sky, so inside and outside fuse into one continuous field of color. That collapse of the threshold is the picture's core move. **[DOCUMENTED]** — Smarthistory / Khan framing (the patchwork of color unifies in/out; "frames within frames" yet one color-field); NGA color continuity.

## 9. LEGEND / DISPUTED LEDGER (the apocrypha catches — read before authoring)

1. **"Donatello chez les fauves" / the cage quote.** The **printed** phrase (Vauxcelles, *Gil Blas*, 17 Oct 1905) is **[DOCUMENTED]**. The story that an unnamed passer-by said "Donatello in the cage with the wild beasts" to Matisse is Vauxcelles's **1939** retelling — **[DISPUTED/legend]**; frame it ("Vauxcelles later recalled…"), never as a live verbatim scene. And the phrase was about the **whole Salle VII / the Marque sculptures**, NOT *Open Window* specifically — do not pin the naming on this one painting.
2. **"Collioure = the birthplace of Fauvism."** A **widely-held characterization [DOCUMENTED as such]**, but it's a place/summer, not a dated single origin event. Frame as "the summer that produced Fauvism" rather than a hard "Fauvism began on date X."
3. **The Stein 500-franc purchase belongs to *Woman with a Hat*, not *Open Window*.** Do not transfer it. **[DOCUMENTED]** for *Woman with a Hat* (Wikipedia/SFMOMA).
4. **No sale prices documented** for *Open Window* itself (1906 / 1949 / 1952). Do not invent. **[DOCUMENTED absence]** — NGA provenance lists no figures.
5. **Specific named colors** (peacock blue, fuchsia, etc.) come from the **ngabiographies.org highlight essay** — reliable and NGA-affiliated, but attribute the granular palette reading to that source rather than asserting each named hue as canonical fact; the safe, image-verifiable claims are: unnatural/non-naturalistic pure color, complementary pairs (orange/blue, red/green), zone-by-zone brushwork.
