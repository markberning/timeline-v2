## Throughline (the one idea)
Courbet took the single most ordinary event imaginable — a country funeral in his
backwater hometown — and painted it at the size and seriousness Europe reserved for
the death of kings and saints. No hierarchy, no idealization, no hero: just fifty
real villagers, life-size, standing around a hole in the ground. That mismatch of
*humble subject* × *monumental scale* is the whole scandal, and the birth of Realism
as a public fact. Courbet later called it "the burial of Romanticism."

## THE SUBJECT — whose funeral, who is in it
- The picture shows an actual burial in the small town of Ornans. By the usual
  account the funeral is that of Courbet's **great-uncle, Claude-Étienne Teste**,
  who died in **September 1848**. `[VERIFY]` the great-uncle/Teste identification and
  the Sept 1848 date (sometimes contested; frame as the standard account).
- **~50 life-size figures** strung in a long horizontal frieze. They are **real
  residents of Ornans**, who posed for Courbet individually in his studio — the
  mayor, the justice of the peace, the priest, the gravedigger, Courbet's own family
  and friends. `[VERIFY]` the ~50 count and that they were named locals who sat.
- Foreground center: an **open grave**, and a **kneeling gravedigger** waiting beside
  it; a **skull** and bones lie on the ground near the pit. `[VERIFY]` skull detail.
- A **crucifix** is held aloft against the sky at left-center by a bearer.
- **Beadles / pallbearers in red** robes (church officers) stand near the priest, a
  jolt of red in an otherwise black-and-gray crowd. `[VERIFY]` the red beadles.
- The men are massed on the **left**, the women (some weeping into handkerchiefs) on
  the **right**; a small **dog** stands in the right foreground, indifferent.
- Behind: the low limestone **cliffs of the Ornans valley** (the Roche du Mont / the
  real local escarpment) under a flat gray sky. `[VERIFY]` the cliff is the real
  Ornans geology.
- Nobody is idealized or beautiful; faces are ordinary, some unflattering. There is
  **no hierarchy of importance, no allegory of Death, no shaft of heavenly light** —
  the radical absence of the history-painting machinery.

## RECEPTION (Salon of 1850–51)
- Shown at the **Paris Salon of 1850–1851** (the Salon ran across the winter).
  `[VERIFY]` Salon 1850–51.
- Scandal: critics attacked the "ugliness," the crude common faces, and above all the
  **monumental scale spent on a vulgar, provincial, contemporary subject** — the
  scale belonged to history painting (gods, kings, martyrs), not to nobodies at a
  village grave. Some read a socialist threat in dignifying the rural poor so soon
  after 1848. `[VERIFY]` representative hostile-critic language (don't invent quotes).
- Courbet later wrote that the Burial was in fact "**the burial of Romanticism**" —
  i.e., it buried the old idealizing school. `[VERIFY]` the wording + attribution.
