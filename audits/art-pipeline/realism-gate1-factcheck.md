## MUST-FIX (every ❌ and any 🟡 asserted flat without framing)

**1. Claim #10 — Stone Breakers destruction narrative asserted as established fact**

The draft states the painting was "lost in transit toward the fortress of Königstein along with 153 other works from the collection" as if confirmed. This is the *standard account* but it is *officially contested*: the Gemäldegalerie lists the work as **"missing"** not "destroyed," and art historian Richard Raskin (1988) argued it had gone missing in 1944 before the doomed transport. The detail about "153 other works" belongs to the transit-bombing account, which is the disputed version.

Fix: Replace the transit-bombing assertion with language that presents this as the standard account while acknowledging the official "missing" status:

> *The standard account says it was lost in transit toward the fortress of Königstein, along with over 150 other works, when the transport vehicle was hit — but the Gemäldegalerie itself lists the painting simply as "missing," and some scholars argue it had disappeared earlier, in 1944. Either way, it is gone.*

Or retain the simpler reading (since the draft's prose *also* says "the painting no longer exists" and "what burned") but strip the specific transit-route claim that is disputed.

## SHOULD-FIX

**2. Claim #14 — "some fifty mourners" (Burial at Ornans)**

Sources consistently say "more than 40" or "more than 45" figures; "over fifty" appears in a minority of sources. "Some fifty" overstates the consensus. Suggested fix: "more than forty" or "nearly fifty."

**3. Claim #31 — "In 1832 he drew … *Gargantua*"**

*Gargantua* was published **16 December 1831**, not 1832. The prosecution, trial and imprisonment were in 1832. The draft's phrasing "In 1832 he drew the reigning king … as *Gargantua*" gives the wrong year for the caricature. Suggested fix: "In late 1831 he drew … *Gargantua*; the prosecution came early the following year, and by August 1832 he was jailed."

**4. Claim #46 — Russian MEANWHILE over-specifies the students' motivation**

"Wanted to paint Russian life, the Russian poor, the Russian present" goes beyond what the Revolt of the Fourteen itself was about (freedom to choose their own subject). They later became the Peredvizhniki, who *did* paint Russian life — but the walkout was about creative freedom, not specifically Russian subject matter. Suggested fix: change "they wanted to paint Russian life, the Russian poor, the Russian present" to "they wanted the freedom to choose their own subjects."
