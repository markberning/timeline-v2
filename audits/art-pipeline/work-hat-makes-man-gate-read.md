# GATE — STORYTELLING + LOOKING + CLARITY · Max Ernst, *The Hat Makes the Man*

Reviewer hat: storytelling (primary) / looking / clarity + voice contract.
Scope: the work-read draft (`work-hat-makes-man-draft.md`), all five sections plus
the `ArtWorkContent` const surfaces (hook, blurbs, annotations, provenance, stats).
No edits made; findings only.

## VERDICT: PASS WITH FIXES

The draft is strong as story. The "identity = the merchandise piled into a body"
joke lands cleanly and is set up, paid off, and then complicated by the Freud read
in the right order. The Dada→Surrealism hinge is the best-told beat in the piece —
the "anti-art collage that has already started to dream" line is exactly the gear
the chain index 5/9 needs, and the Ernst overpainting quote is deployed as the hinge
in miniature. Looking section guides the eye by prose pointers only, no coordinates,
vivid and concrete. No factual drift from the pack; the careful traps (medium "on
paper," dims 13⅞×17¾, German caption authoritative + English flagged-as-translation,
botany pun named, Freud + phallic read framed as interpretation, Lautréamont line
attributed correctly, acquisition year not invented, PD-US/EU split) are all handled
right. What blocks a clean pass is a cluster of **reader-command / meta-narration**
voice-contract violations, mostly in the Looking and Making sections, plus one
structural oddity (the "afterlife" section is really an inscription/quote/provenance
appendix, not an afterlife). None are factual. Fix the voice tics and the section
naming and this ships.

## STORYTELLING (primary)

**Overall: strong.** Five sections pace well: defeated-city setup → the making and
the joke → looking → the break → inscription/aftermath. The joke is the spine and it
recurs without going stale (made the man / order form / "you are what you buy" /
"recombined order form"). The hinge to Surrealism is genuinely well-built.

- **[NICE] Repetition of the wobble/teetering image across sections.** "Teetering
  towers," "wobbly columns," "lean and wobble," "topple in a breeze" appear in the
  hook, two annotations, Making, and Looking. It's a good image but it's now the
  draft's most-reused phrase. Vary one or two instances (e.g. in Making, let the
  instability be implied rather than re-stated). Not blocking.

- **[NICE] The joke is stated as "the whole work" twice** — Making §"The joke"
  ("Now the joke, which is the whole work") and the Looking caption-beat echoes it.
  Both are fine individually; just confirm they don't read as the author insisting
  on importance rather than showing it. Lean toward showing.

- **[FIX — structure/pacing] The "afterlife" section is not an afterlife.** Section
  id `afterlife`, eyebrow "After," blurb promising "Within a year Ernst makes the
  dream-monster Celebes; within four he is a founding Surrealist…" — but the actual
  section opens with the inscription word-for-word, then Ernst-on-collage, then
  provenance, then rights, and only reaches Celebes/Surrealism in the final
  paragraph. The Celebes/founding-Surrealist beat was *already spent* at the end of
  the Break section ("Within a year Ernst would paint *Celebes*… within four years he
  would be a founding member…"). So the afterlife blurb over-promises a forward
  trajectory the section mostly doesn't deliver, and duplicates the Break's closer.
  Suggested fix: rename the section to match what it is (inscription + sources +
  provenance — e.g. eyebrow "The record," title "The caption, the sources, and the
  road on"), OR move the Celebes/Surrealism trajectory out of the Break closer and
  let it be the genuine spine of the afterlife. Right now the same forward-leap is
  told twice and neither section fully owns it.

- **[NICE] Break section closes on Celebes; afterlife also closes on "the door to
  Surrealism… he walked through it first."** Two endings on nearly the same note. If
  the afterlife is rescoped per the FIX above, this resolves naturally.

## LOOKING

**Overall: passes cleanly on the axis.** The Looking section (HmkLooking) and the
six annotations point the eye to the real visible features by prose only — no
coordinates, no blind crops. All four required features are guided: stacked
catalogue-cut hats as wobbly columns ("read the figures top to bottom… a column of
men's hats… piled one on the next"), the painted connecting tubes/limbs ("smooth
drawn cylinders that act as necks, spines, and legs"), the colored vertical bodies
("tinted in clear blue, red, green, and yellow… bright cylindrical torsos"), and the
handwritten caption ("Along the lower edge, in Ernst's own handwriting"). The
contrast pointer (dull flat hats vs. rounded bright joins) is vivid and concrete and
tells the reader *where* the figure "comes to life." Good.

- **[FIX] Reader-commands cluster in the Looking section.** Looking is the densest
  pocket of imperative "do this with your eye" instructions, which the voice contract
  flags. Instances:
  - "Now read the figures top to bottom."
  - "Look closely at the hats themselves and you can see exactly what they are…"
  - "Watch the contrast: the hats are dull, flat, mechanical…"
  - "Step back from the whole sheet and the argument is plain…"
  - In Making: "Start with what Ernst started with…"
  - In annotations: "Look at the towers closely and they are merchandise…"
  These are pointer-commands, not the worst kind, but the contract wants the eye
  guided by description, not by orders. Suggested rewrites (keep the pointer, drop
  the command): "Read top to bottom, each figure is a column of men's hats" →
  "Top to bottom, each figure is a column of men's hats." / "Watch the contrast: the
  hats are dull…" → "The contrast does the work: the hats are dull, flat,
  mechanical; the joins rounded, bodily, vivid." / "Step back from the whole sheet
  and the argument is plain" → "At full-sheet scale the argument is plain." Not every
  one must go, but the section currently reads as a guided tour with the guide
  talking; thin them to ~one soft pointer per paragraph.

## CLARITY + VOICE CONTRACT (flag every instance)

### Reader-commands (imperative "you do X")
- **[FIX]** Looking + Making + annotations — see the Looking FIX above for the full
  list ("Now read…", "Look closely…", "Watch the contrast…", "Step back…", "Start
  with what Ernst started with…", "Look at the towers closely…", "read it as the
  joke's punchline" in the caption annotation, "Hold the scale in mind" in Looking,
  "for now just notice that…" in Looking). Thin these out.
- **[FIX]** Making §"The joke": "Now the joke, which is the whole work." — directive
  framing ("Now the joke"). Suggest: "The joke is the whole work." or fold into the
  prose.

### Meta-narration (narrating the chapter / what's coming)
- **[FIX]** HmkCologne closing paragraph: "the small collage we are about to look
  at" and "a thing almost too small and too modest to bear the weight: a sheet of
  paper…". "We are about to look at" narrates the reading sequence. Suggest cut to
  the image directly: "And the work that shows the crossing most clearly is almost
  too small to bear the weight…"
- **[FIX]** HmkLooking caption beat: "The full inscription, and what it actually
  says, is the next chapter." — explicit forward-reference to section structure.
  Suggest: drop it, or replace with "The inscription is given in full below" only if
  it survives the reflow; cleanest is to cut.
- **[FIX]** HmkAfterlife open: "so it is worth giving in full" and earlier "is the
  work's own statement of itself, so it is worth giving in full" — "it is worth
  giving" is the author narrating the decision to quote. Suggest: just give it. "The
  line Ernst wrote along the bottom of the sheet is the work's own statement of
  itself:" then the quote.
- **[NICE]** Break: "Here is the hinge." — borderline meta (announces the structural
  pivot) but it's also a strong rhetorical beat and earns its place. Leave unless the
  reviser is sweeping hard; if cutting, "The hinge is this:" reads less like a
  signpost.

### Honesty-labels (telling the reader you're being careful/honest)
- **[FIX]** HmkLooking: "the argument is plain without a word of explanation." — mild
  honesty/meta flavor ("without a word of explanation" comments on the prose).
  Suggest: "the argument is plain."
- **[NICE]** The "treat it as an interpretation… not a meaning he documented" /
  "worth naming as a reading rather than a fact" framing (Making + annotation +
  afterlife) is REQUIRED by the fact pack (Freud read must be framed as
  interpretation), so it stays — but it appears three times (Making §2, the phallic
  annotation, and implicitly the looking-point). That's correct fact-handling, just
  confirm the three phrasings don't pile up; one full framing + two light touches is
  ideal. Not a violation, a watch-item.

### Condescending glosses

  angiosperm/gymnosperm, Lautréamont) are all parenthetical, plain, first-use, and

### Literal "—" in rendered strings / &mdash; misuse
- **[OK]** Spot-checked the const string fields (hook, blurbs, annotation `where`
  and `detail`, provenance `note`, figures `role`). The em-dashes in those plain TS

  HTML entity in a data string. **One watch-item, [NICE]:** the hook and several

  parentheses for definitions over em-dashes — not a hard fail here since these are
  appositive asides, not definitions, but a reviser could convert a few to
  parentheses or periods to match the civ-side rule.

### Curly-quote / entity consistency
- **[OK]** JSX prose uses `&rsquo;`, `&ldquo;`, `&rdquo;`, `&lsquo;`, `&mdash;`,
  `&ndash;` consistently; const string fields use literal curly quotes (’ “ ”) and
