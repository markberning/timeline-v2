# GATE READ — Storytelling + Looking + Clarity/Voice

**Work:** Francis Picabia, *La Sainte Vierge (The Blessed Virgin)* (1920) · Dada · `sainte-vierge`
**Gate:** Storytelling (primary) + Looking + Clarity/Voice-contract
**Reviewed:** PART A const + PART B five sections, against the fact pack.

---

## VERDICT: PASS WITH FIXES

The draft is genuinely strong. It solves the central problem — making a single ink splatter carry five sections — and it does so honestly: the provocation lands ("a flick of ink and a sacred title colliding to insult Church and Art at once"), the "immaculate / stain" pun is the best beat in the piece, and the break section earns its forward sweep without overclaiming (the "None of that is a claim that Picabia sat down in 1920 and planned the next half-century" line is exactly the right honesty). It stays tight to the factpack on every contested fact (391-not-291, dropper-not-flung hedged, mirror-reversal, multiple objects, PD status, no-quote-about-the-drawing).

It does **not** ship as-is. The clarity/voice axis has a cluster of **reader-commands** and a few **meta-narration / "worth saying out loud"** tics that the voice contract forbids, plus one **repetition** problem: the mirror-reversal/drip-direction "tell" is explained **four times** across making + looking, and the deliberate-crudeness paragraph is told nearly verbatim in both looking and break. No BLOCKERs to accuracy or story; the fixes are voice-and-trim. Fix the [FIX] items, sweep the [NICE], and it's a ship.

## 1. STORYTELLING (primary)

**Holds across all five.** Each section has a distinct job (the man + magazine → the (non-)making → the looking → the break → the words + objects), and the work-is-tiny problem is converted into the engine rather than padded around. The hook and break are the high points.

### [FIX] — S1. The drip-direction "tell" is told FOUR times — repetitive
The mirror-reversal fact, and specifically the "drips run the opposite way, which is how you tell which version you're looking at" framing, appears in:
- `making` §2 ("the drips run in opposite directions… the cleanest way to tell, at a glance")
- `making` annotation/prose again ("Keep that in your pocket; it is the cleanest way to tell")
- `looking` §2 ("The drips are the easiest tell for which version is in front of you")
- plus the const `annotations` drips entry ("a quick tell for which version you are looking at")

That is the same sentence four times. It's a good fact once. **Suggested:** state the mirror-reversal fully and memorably **once** in `making` (where the two-versions sub-header lives), and in `looking` reduce to a single clause that does NOT re-explain the tell — e.g. "(these drips run one way here; the magazine plate is flipped)". Drop "the easiest tell / cleanest way to tell / quick tell" from all but the first occurrence.

### [FIX] — S2. "deliberate crudeness" paragraph duplicated between looking and break
`looking` "The ground" closes with: "For centuries a Holy Virgin was the supreme test of a painter's craft and devotion. The deliberate crudeness here is the message. Skill and finish are precisely the things Dada is refusing." `break` "before" then re-runs the identical idea ("For roughly five hundred years, a 'Holy Virgin' was the summit… two things were never in question"). The looking section is the right place for *what crudeness looks like*; the break section is the right place for *why five centuries of Madonnas make it a break*. Right now they overlap on the "supreme test… for centuries" framing.
**Suggested:** in `looking`, keep the *visual* crudeness (lettering plain, blot raw, drips left where they fell, the empty ground) and cut the "for centuries a Holy Virgin was the supreme test" sentence — let `break` own the five-centuries argument. (No content lost; the dedupe just hands each idea to one section.)

### [NICE] — S3. `after` opens on the "no quote" administrative beat
The fifth section leads with "Picabia left no documented words about *this drawing*…". It's honest and correctly factpack-driven, but it opens the section on an absence. Consider opening on the manifesto line itself as the hook ("'Dada wants nothing, absolutely nothing'…") and folding the no-quote caveat into the muted note, so the section opens on a punch rather than a disclaimer. Optional — the current version is defensible.

### [NICE] — S4. const `hook` vs section blurbs lightly repeat "flick of ink / sacred title"
The phrase "a flick of ink and a sacred title colliding" (hook) and "a flick of the wrist titled as a sacred image" (break blurb) are close cousins. Fine as a motif, but watch it doesn't become the only way the piece can phrase the idea.

**Strong and concrete, no coordinates.** The looking section makes the splatter visually real through prose pointers: the small held-in-one-hand sheet, the single irregular blot "with no edge you could call a contour," the drips as "the trace of a thing that was let happen," the hand-lettered blue-violet caption, the signature, the empty gray ground, the deliberate crudeness. Every factpack looking point (§5.1–6) is present and turned into prose. Drip *direction* is handled exactly as the factpack demands (generic / version-flagged, never a false absolute). No blind crops or pins.

### [FIX] — L1. (rolls up with S1) the drips pointer re-litigates the mirror-tell
Per S1, the looking "drips" paragraph spends three sentences re-explaining the 391-flip tell that `making` already covered. Trim to keep the *visual* (what the drips look like, that they record gravity/chance) and drop the version-tell re-explanation.

### [NICE] — L2. caption lettering visual could be a hair more concrete
The caption pointer is excellent on *function* ("three words that do all the work") but lighter on *appearance*. The factpack only gives "hand-lettered, plain, blue-violet" — so don't invent — but you already have "plain and unceremonious"; consider one word on the lettering being handwritten/casual rather than designed, if the source supports it. Optional; do not exceed the factpack.

## 3. CLARITY + VOICE CONTRACT

This is where the real work is. The piece is clear and jargon is inline-defined well (Dada, Cubism, India ink/encre de Chine, immaculate/spotless, tache, readymade all defined on use). But it leans repeatedly on **reader-commands** and a few **meta-narration / "worth saying"** tics that the voice contract bans.

### [FIX] — V1. Reader-commands (imperative "you"-direction) — multiple
The contract forbids commanding the reader. Instances:
- `looking` §1: **"Stand in front of it, and brace for an anticlimax"** — rewrite to state it, e.g. "In front of it, the first thing is an anticlimax — and the anticlimax is the work."
- `looking` §2: **"Look at the drips."** → "The drips are the next thing." / or just open with the drips.
- `looking` "The words": **"Now read the caption across the top"** → "Across the top runs the caption…"
- `looking` "The ground": **"Last, look at everything that isn't there."** → "What is *not* there matters as much: …"
- `making` §2: **"Keep that in your pocket"** → cut (and see S1).
- `making` §1 header **"How do you 'make' a stain?"** is fine (rhetorical, not a command), but `looking`'s "Stand in front of it" is the clearest violation.
- `after` §1: **"since people always ask whether a museum can lock an image like this away"** and **"A warning that catches almost everyone"** — these address the reader's presumed reaction; soften (see V2/V3).

### [FIX] — V2. Meta-narration about the chapter itself
The contract forbids narrating what these pages are doing. Instances:
- `paris-dada` §"issue 12": **"and the rest of these pages are about why so little could be made to do so much."** → cut, or convert to a plain statement about the work ("That collision is the entire work — the holiest possible name on a smear of ink.").
- `looking` §1: "because the anticlimax is the work" is *good* (about the work, not the page) — keep that, only fix the "Stand in front of it / brace" command around it.
- `break` §"after": "What survives instead is his framing… and it tells you exactly what the blot was for" — borderline; "and it tells you" addresses the reader. Soften to "…and it frames exactly what the blot was for."

### [FIX] — V3. "worth saying out loud" / "worth getting straight" — honesty-label / throat-clearing tic
Recurs and reads as the narrator flagging its own candor:
- `looking` "The words" §3: **"There is a sharper joke folded into the choice of subject, and it is worth saying out loud."** → just say it: "There is a sharper joke in the choice of subject."
- `making` §2: **"Here is a thing worth getting straight before you look closely, because almost everyone gets it tangled."** → "The image here is the original drawing, not the magazine plate." (state it).
- `paris-dada` §1 parenthetical: **"A point worth getting right, because the reference books garble it"** → trim to the correction itself ("The title is *391*, with a 3 — you'll see it misprinted as *291*, after an earlier New York magazine, but Picabia's review was always *391*.").
- `after`: **"A warning that catches almost everyone"** → "There is no single object called *La Sainte Vierge*." (state it).

### [NICE] — V4. Condescending gloss watch
Mostly clean. One to watch: `making` §3 "So it is not, strictly, 'just an ink splatter,' even though that is how it is usually described" is fine (correcting a real misconception, factpack-grounded). No true condescension found.

### [NICE] — V5. Em-dash / entity hygiene in rendered TS strings — CLEAN, but verify
- PART B prose correctly uses HTML entities (`&ndash;`, `&rsquo;`, `&ldquo;`, `&hellip;`, `&eacute;`, `&mdash;`-free). **No literal `—` found inside a rendered JSX string**, and `&mdash;` is not misused. Good.
- PART A const string fields use real Unicode curly quotes/primes (`'`, `″`, `½`) directly — consistent with the established const style in this file family; not a violation. Confirm `dimensions: '1 ft 1 in × 9 1/2 in'` matches the const elsewhere (stats uses `9½″`; the prose uses "nine and a half" — all consistent, just note the const mixes `9 1/2` and `9½`; harmless but tidy if you like).
- No literal hyphen-as-em-dash in any string field. No `&mdash;` inside a plain TS (non-JSX) string field.

### [NICE] — V6. Walls of text
Paragraphs are sized well for mobile. The longest is `break` §3 (the lineage sweep: Surrealism → art informel/tachisme → Action Painting → Conceptual). It's one big paragraph doing four moves. It reads well because each clause is a beat, but consider a sentence break before "And let the title do the work" to give the eye a landing on mobile. Optional.
