## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

This is the draft's strongest dimension. It actively defuses the overclaim traps:

- **Genius-myth — handled well.** Ernst is situated *inside* Cologne Dada: "founded
  the local cell with his friend Johannes Theodor Baargeld and, in close dialogue,
  the Alsatian artist Jean (Hans) Arp" (Cologne ch.). Cologne Dada is its own figure
  card and a lineage parent. He is never the lone inventor of anything; collage is
  explicitly credited to the prior Cubist practice (see below). Good.

- **Collage is NOT claimed as Ernst's invention — correct and explicit.** The Break
  chapter states plainly: "Collage already existed: the Cubists Picasso and Braque
  had glued bits of newspaper and printed oilcloth into their canvases around 1912."
  The claim is narrowed precisely to what *is* new here ("does something different
  in kind … built from ready-made commercial print … recombined, not drawn"). This
  is exactly the scoping the brief asked for. No overclaim.

- **"Founder of Surrealism" — accurate, not inflated.** Web-confirmed: Ernst was a
  founding *member* of the Paris Surrealist group (1924); Breton was the founder/
  leader of the movement. The draft consistently says "a founder of Surrealism" /
  "a founding member" / "the first major visual artist to carry Dada over into the
  dream" — never "the founder," never crediting him with the movement itself. The
  "first major visual artist to carry Dada into the dream" claim is hedged in the
  text as "in the standard telling" (Cologne ch.) — appropriate.

- **Anachronism — correctly guarded.** Surrealism (1924) postdates the 1920 work, and
  the draft never says the 1920 collage *was* Surrealist; it says it "points to" /
  "carries over into" / "stands exactly on that line." The Break's hinge logic is
  framed as a later reading ("read … not as a joke about consumer society but as a
  glimpse of a dream — and you have walked … into Surrealism"). No 1920-was-Surrealism
  error.

- **Celebes "first masterpiece" — properly hedged.** Web-confirmed the phrase
  circulates as "the first masterpiece of Surrealist painting in the de Chirico
  tradition." The draft says "*often called* the first masterpiece of Surrealist
  painting" — the "often called" hedge is correct and necessary; do not harden it.

- **Freud / phallic read — kept as interpretation, not fact.** Both the annotation
  ("this is interpretation, likely-inspired rather than documented by Ernst, so it
  sits beside the picture") and the Making chapter ("worth naming as a reading rather
  than a fact … Ernst never spelled this out, so treat it as an interpretation the
  picture invites, not a meaning he documented") frame it exactly right. Matches the
  fact pack's ⚠ requirement.

- **Proportion — balanced.** The consumer/commodity satire (the work's documented
  joke) leads; the Freud reading is the clearly-marked secondary layer; the
  Dada→Surrealism significance is given its due without swallowing the work itself.
  No facet is over- or under-weighted.
