### [FIX-1] — Missing facet: Still's claim to priority among the field-painters
The brief lists "his claim to priority among the AbEx field-painters" as an essential facet, and it is the one item absent from the draft. The draft mentions Rothko and Newman as peers ("alongside Rothko and Newman, pointed the way to Color Field") but never registers that Still himself held — and that a real strand of art history credits — that he *got there first*, at a remove from New York. Web research confirms this is a live, sourceable view (The Art Story: "Most now believe that it was Clyfford Still who first arrived at the style of Color Field abstraction… at some remove from those in New York, such as Barnett Newman and Mark Rothko"), and that Still privately believed Newman copied his line. This belongs in the read because it is half of *why* the withdrawal and disdain make sense — he thought he was the originator and the others were borrowing.

This is a [FIX] not a [BLOCKER] because the read is coherent without it, but adding it materially improves both completeness and the fairness framing (it lets the disdain be motivated rather than just cranky). Suggested text — append to the **Break** "In his own words" paragraph (after the Color Field sentence) OR fold into **StlIndependent**:

> Suggested (add to the close of StlBreak, after "…a moral and existential claim rather than a designed picture."):
> "Still also believed he had gotten there first. He held that he, not Rothko or Newman, had broken through to the torn, edge-to-edge color field, and that the others were latecomers to ground he had already opened — he even thought Newman's vertical line was lifted from him. Art history half agrees: a real strand of it credits Still as the earliest of the field-painters to arrive at this kind of abstraction, working at a deliberate distance from New York. But it is contested, and it was also his grievance as much as a settled fact, so take it as his claim and the field's partial nod to it, not as a scoreboard."

This keeps the claim **scoped, not endorsed** (the framing requirement) while filling the gap.

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### Self-mythologizing — handled well, one tightening
The draft keeps Still's fierce independence and disdain largely as *his stance*, not as gospel: "by temperament and on principle," "He came to see the whole system… as a machine that compromised" (his view, attributed), "dared you to make something of the silence." Good. Two spots edge toward narrator-endorsement of the myth rather than reporting it:

#### [FIX-2] — "the most combative man in American art" (hook) reads as the narrator's verdict
The hook asserts Still was *"the most combative man in American art"* as plain fact; StlIndependent repeats "the least clubbable and the most combative." This is the genius-myth/superlative trap — a real reputation stated as a measured fact. It's well-attested as *reputation*, so the fix is light: anchor it as how he was seen, not a ranking.
> Suggested (hook): change "painted by the most combative man in American art, who refused titles…" → "painted by a man with a reputation as the most combative figure in American art — he refused titles…"
> Suggested (StlIndependent): "the least clubbable and the most combative" → "by reputation the least clubbable and most combative of them" (one "by reputation" carries it).

#### [NICE] — "the suspicious painter" (StlAfterlife)
Minor: "the bond between the suspicious painter and one museum" is the narrator characterizing him. It's mild and arguably earned by the preceding evidence; leave it or soften to "the wary painter." Not required.
