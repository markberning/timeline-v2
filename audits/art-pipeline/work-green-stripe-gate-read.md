Gate: STORYTELLING + LOOKING + CLARITY. Reviewer: art-pipeline critic.

House-voice baseline: `audits/art-pipeline/absinthe-narratives.tsx.txt`.

VERDICT: **PASS with FIXES.** No BLOCKERs. The draft is strong on all three axes —

## AXIS 1 — STORYTELLING

Strong overall. The `GrnCollioure` open ("The summer color slipped its leash"),
the Amélie "not a passive prop" beat, and the "scandal next door" / wrong-painting
disambiguation are all genuinely story-first and carry the dry house wit
("even straight down his wife's nose"; "She sat for this picture; she was not done
to by it."). The reception chapter handles the punishment/marriage reading with the
right framed-not-asserted touch. This matches the absinthe register well.

### [FIX] — STORY-1: A near-verbatim repeat across `Making` and `Looking` on the two halves.
`GrnMaking` ("The two halves" beat):
> "One side of the face is keyed **warm** — pink, with orange and red worked along
> the chin and the ear — and reads as the side in direct light. The other side is a
> cooler **yellow-ochre**, and reads as half-light, the side turned away."

`GrnLooking` ("A warm country and a cool one" beat):
> "On one side of the stripe the face is **warm** — flushed pink, with hotter notes
> of orange and red along the chin and the ear — the side that reads as catching the
> light. Cross the green seam and the face goes **cool**: a flat, dry **yellow-ochre**,
> the side in half-shadow."

These are the same sentence twice, ~30 words apart in adjacent chapters. `Making`
should make the ARGUMENT (color does the work of modelling); `Looking` should make
you SEE. Right now both describe. Fix: in `Making`, compress the warm/cool inventory
to a clause and keep the weight on *why it matters* ("two flat temperatures with the
stripe as the only seam — that is the engine"); let `Looking` own the guided-eye
version. Don't delete from `Looking` (that's where it belongs); thin it in `Making`.

### [FIX] — STORY-2: "color cut loose from description" / "color taking over the job of
shading" is restated three times as a thesis.
- `GrnMaking` para 2: "Color is doing the structure that shading used to do."
- `GrnMaking` final para: "color had quit its old job of telling you what things
  look like and taken up a new one: building the picture."
- `GrnLooking` final para: "a face can be built entirely out of color set against
  color."
The absinthe read states its thesis once per chapter and lets the *evidence* differ.
Three near-identical thesis statements is one above the line. Keep `Looking`'s
(it earns it as the payoff of guided looking) and `Making`'s final-para version
(the textbook-demonstration beat is the chapter's point); trim the mid-`Making`
"Color is doing the structure that shading used to do" to a lighter touch so it
isn't a third full statement of the same claim.

### [NICE] — STORY-3: The Amélie biography (hat shop, four-decade manager, chief
advocate) appears in full in `GrnCollioure` and again in full in `GrnReception`.
This is more defensible than 1/2 — the reception chapter needs it to push back on
the "punishment" flattening, and the absinthe read does re-touch Andrée's
"working actress, not a fallen woman" point across chapters. But it's verbatim
twice. Consider varying the second pass (lead with a fact the first didn't give,
or a sharper single line) so it reads as a callback, not a paste.

## AXIS 2 — LOOKING

This is the strongest axis. `GrnLooking` does exactly what the gate demands and
matches the absinthe `AbLooking` template beat-for-beat:
- **Scale first** ("a face you could carry under one arm," legal-paper size) — good,
  and it's load-bearing, not filler.
- **Green meridian as STRUCTURE/spine** — nailed. "Read it as the *spine* of the
  face… the stripe is the border between them." The thumb-test ("If you covered the
  stripe with your thumb the two halves would simply collide") is the single best
  looking-instruction in the draft — it makes the reader DO something.
- **Warm/cool two-tone halves** — present, guided ("Travel across that border").
- **Three-color background thirds** — present and made to pay off ("the room
  obediently answers in the same two temperatures").
- **Hat and collar** — collar (teal) covered well; the *absence* of the hat handled
  cleverly. See LOOK-2 below on the "thirds" wording.
- **Brushwork** — see LOOK-1, this is the one real gap.

### [FIX] — LOOK-1: The brushwork ("flat blocks, hard edges, mask-like") is described in
`GrnMaking` and in the PART A annotation, but `GrnLooking` never makes the reader
SEE the surface itself. The gate explicitly lists "the brushwork" as a looking
target. `GrnLooking`'s "mask-like" final para is about *effect*, not about the paint:
> "It is intense, flat, and frankly **mask-like** — almost nonhuman."
That's a conclusion, not a guided look at the handling. Fix: add one concrete
looking-instruction to `GrnLooking` pointing the eye at the actual paint — the hard
seams where one flat zone of color butts against the next with no blended transition,
the unbroken blocks. Something like: *"Look at any edge — where the pink cheek meets
the ochre, where the blue hair meets the green — and you'll see no soft transition at
all, just one flat block of color set hard against the next, like leading in a stained-
glass window."* The stained-glass image already exists in `Making`; `Looking` should
let the reader find it on the surface.

### [FIX] — LOOK-2: The blurb (PART A `looking` section) and the gate brief both promise
"a background split into THIRDS / three-color background thirds." The prose only ever
delivers a **two-way** split:
> `GrnLooking`: "It is **split**, exactly the way the face is split: pink and orange
> on one side, teal-green on the other."
Two zones, not three. The PART A annotation agrees (two zones). This is an internal
mismatch: either the painting's background reads as three bands (and the prose
under-describes it) or it reads as two (and the gate brief's "thirds" is the error).
The factpack governs which — but the draft must be internally consistent. As written,
nothing makes the reader see "thirds." Fix: confirm against the factpack/image
whether the background is 2-zone or 3-band, then make blurb + prose + annotation all
say the same thing. Do not leave "thirds" implied anywhere if the prose shows two.

### [NICE] — LOOK-3: `GrnLooking` says the collar ties to "the cool side of the **room**,"
while the background beat comes *after* the collar beat. The reader hasn't been told
about the split room yet at the collar moment. Minor forward-reference; consider
ordering background before collar, or softening to "the cool green elsewhere in the
picture." Not blocking.

## AXIS 3 — CLARITY

Very good. Reader-with-zero-knowledge terms are glossed on first use:
- **Fauvism / *les fauves*** — "from *les fauves*, 'the wild beasts'" (Collioure).
- **modelling** — glossed twice, fully, in both annotation and `GrnMaking`
  ("the gradual shading from light into shadow… that fools the eye into reading a
  flat patch of paint as a solid, three-dimensional head"). Excellent.
- **Salon d'Automne** — "one of the big annual juried art exhibitions in Paris."
- **La Raie verte** — translated.
- **teal** — "(blue-green)" glossed.
- **provenance** — "the documented chain of who owned it" (`GrnAfterlife`).
- **KMSr / Rump fund** — fully unpacked.

### [FIX] — CLARITY-1: "**yellow-ochre**" appears in `GrnMaking` and `GrnLooking` and is
never glossed. The reader-with-zero-knowledge brief is strict, and the absinthe read
glosses comparably homely color/material words (mazagran, louche). "Ochre" is a real
unknown for a lay reader. Add a three-word gloss on first use (in `GrnMaking`):
*"a cooler **yellow-ochre** (a dull earth-yellow)"*. Cheap, and it closes the one
real jargon hole.

### [NICE] — CLARITY-2: "**keyed** warm" (`GrnMaking`, `GrnLooking`) is mild painter's
jargon. Most readers will infer it from context, so this is optional — but if you
want the absinthe read's zero-assumption rigor, "pitched warm" or "all warm" is
plainer. Not blocking.

### [NICE] — CLARITY-3: "Galerie Druet" appears in `GrnAfterlife` glossed as "a Paris
gallery" — good. In the PART A provenance it's "the Paris dealer Galerie Druet."
Consistent enough; no action needed, noting only that the prose handles it correctly.
