## Throughline (the one idea)
Before anyone breaks anything, look at what there was to break: in France around
1850 there was exactly **one** ladder to a painting career and the **State owned
it** — the Académie trained you, the Salon showed you, a medal made you. The
chapter sets that world, shows the taste it rewarded, and shows the modern forces
already loading the gun. It ends by handing off to Chapter 1 (Courbet's tent) —
**it must NOT retell Courbet/Manet's revolt; that is Chapter 1's job.**

## VERIFIED FACTS (coordinator-checked; fact-checker re-verifies independently)

### The institution
- **Académie des Beaux-Arts** — the art division of the **Institut de France**
  (the State's official body of approved excellence). Held a **monopoly on the
  Salon** until Jules Ferry ended it in **1880**. So in the 1850s the Salon was a
  State-run institution. ✅ (en.wikipedia French art salons and academies)
- **École des Beaux-Arts** — the state art school. Long academic training: drawing
  from plaster casts, then the live model, before serious painting. Top prize the
  **Prix de Rome**, which sent the winner to the **Villa Medici in Rome** at State
  expense to study the old masters. ✅ (standard)
- **Hierarchy of genres** — formulated **1667 by André Félibien**. Top = **history
  painting** (*la grande peinture*: myth, religion, history, allegory — the human
  body, grand format). Bottom = **still life** (mere reportage, small format).
  Canonical full order (use this; uncontested at the endpoints): history →
  portrait → genre/everyday scenes → landscape → still life. **NOTE:** some
  sources swap portrait/genre in the middle — keep the prose to the ENDPOINTS
  ("gods at the top, a bowl of fruit at the bottom") to stay safe. ✅ (en.wikipedia
  Hierarchy of genres; KU/study.com)
- **The house style** prized *fini* — a smooth, blended surface with no visible
  brushstroke — and clean **line/drawing over loose colour** (the old Ingres-vs-
  Delacroix axis). ✅ (standard)

### The Salon (the one marketplace)
- The **Salon** was the Académie's official public exhibition — the one place a
  wide Paris public came to see, argue over and BUY new art; a medal there meant
  collectors, commissions, a career. ✅
- **VENUE CORRECTION (do not get this wrong):** the Salon was at the **Louvre
  until 1848**; through the early 1850s it used various locations; it moved into
  the cavernous **iron-and-glass Palais de l'Industrie in 1857**. So "an iron-and-
  glass hall around 1850" is an **anachronism** — phrase it as the Salon's home in
  this period shifting, and the Palais de l'Industrie housing it **from 1857**, OR
  keep it generic ("a vast public hall"). ✅ (en.wikipedia French art salons and
  academies; fr.wikipedia Salon de peinture)
- **The hang:** paintings stacked frame-to-frame from near the floor to the
  ceiling. The prime spot at eye level was **"on the line"**; a picture "**skied**"
  up near the ceiling was effectively unseen. ✅ (standard art-history terms)

### The official taste (the "Looking" anchor)
- **Alexandre Cabanel, *The Birth of Venus*, 1863** — a nude goddess reclining on
  the foam in flawless, soft-focus academic *fini*. Shown to acclaim at the **1863
  Salon** and **bought by Napoleon III** for the imperial collection (one source
  credits Empress Eugénie — say "the Emperor"/"the imperial collection" to be
  safe). **Now in the Musée d'Orsay, Paris.** Cabanel was made a **professor at the
  École des Beaux-Arts the same year** — the establishment embracing its own ideal.
  ✅ (en.wikipedia Birth of Venus (Cabanel); musee-orsay.fr)
- Use Cabanel as the emblem of **what the jury loved** and the contrast the coming
  rebels define themselves against — but **leave the Manet/Olympia confrontation to
  Ch1**; here, at most a one-clause forward nod, framed lightly.

### The forces loading the gun (the disruptors)
- **Photography** — the **daguerreotype**, made public in **1839** (the French
  government acquired Daguerre's process and released it that August). It did, in
  seconds and perfectly, the exact-likeness job painters had been paid for — which
  freed/forced painting toward what a camera could not do. ✅ (standard)
- **Paint in collapsible metal tubes** — patented **1841** by **John Goffe Rand**,
  an American painter in London (England Mar 1841; US Sept 11 1841). Let painters
  carry colour out the door and work in front of the subject. **Often credited with
  making plein-air painting / Impressionism possible — frame as "often credited,"
  not flat causation.** ✅ (Smithsonian; en.wikipedia John G. Rand)
- **Railways** — cheap, fast trips out of Paris to the countryside/coast (1850s–
  60s), feeding outdoor painting. ✅ (standard)
- **Haussmann's renovation of Paris** — under Napoleon III, directed by Prefect
  **Baron Haussmann**, **c.1853–1870**: wide boulevards, gas light, cafés, grand
  apartment blocks, department-store windows — a new, glittering *modern* spectacle
  begging to be painted (if painting modern life were allowed). ✅ (standard)
- **A new market, emerging** — a rising bourgeoisie wanted smaller, cheaper
  pictures for ordinary walls, and a private **art-dealer** trade was beginning to
  sell to them directly (Paul Durand-Ruel's championing of these painters is
  ~1860s–70s, so frame as **emerging/beginning**, the faint outline of a way to
  live without the Salon — NOT yet an alternative in 1850). ✅ (standard)

## LEGEND / CAUTION (the "beautiful anecdote" trap)
- **No invented quotes.** Don't attribute any line to the Académie/critics as a
  real quotation; the "beauty has rules, we know them" line is a *characterisation*,
  keep it clearly authorial, not quote-marked as a real utterance.
- **Martini engraving is 1787.** It depicts the Salon ~60 years before our moment.
  It is used because the floor-to-ceiling hang barely changed — caption/credit must
  state it is the Salon of 1787, never imply it shows 1850.
- **"Paint tube made Impressionism possible"** = popular claim → "often credited."
- **Olympia umbrellas** = Ch1's material (and itself a reported contemporary
  account) — keep out of here.
