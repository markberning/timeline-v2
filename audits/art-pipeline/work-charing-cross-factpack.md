## 2. THE HOOK (1 sentence, house voice — author may rephrase)

A London the eye never saw: the Thames running pink and the sky burning butter-yellow,
painted by a 26-year-old whom a dealer had shipped to England on purpose, to beat
Monet at his own river. [facts all DOCUMENTED below]

## 3. NARRATIVE BEATS (~5; each fact tagged + sourced)

### Beat 1 — Vollard sends him to London (the assignment) [DOCUMENTED, one count [DISPUTED]]
- The dealer **Ambroise Vollard** (1866–1939) sent Derain to London in **1906** *specifically*
  to produce a London/Thames series. This was a deliberate commercial move, not a holiday.
  [DOCUMENTED — NGA: "painted this work in 1906 during trips commissioned by dealer Ambroise
  Vollard"; Met; Tate/Pool-of-London Wikipedia; Courtauld]
- **The Monet trigger:** in **summer 1904**, **Claude Monet**'s London series (Houses of
  Parliament / Thames, painted 1899–1904) was shown at the **Durand-Ruel** gallery in Paris
  to rapturous reception. Vollard sent Derain "in direct response" to that success. [DOCUMENTED
  — Wikipedia *Pool of London* / Met series context; the Monet show date 1904 is well attested]
- **[DISPUTED] How many canvases:** sources split — Vollard commissioned **"50 views"** (Met /
  several secondary), Derain **delivered ~30** (most-cited: "30 paintings in total, of which 29
  are known today"). **Frame it as a commission of dozens, ~30 delivered** — do NOT assert a
  single hard number flat. [DISPUTED — Met (50 commissioned) vs Sotheby's/Courtauld (30 painted,
  29 known)]
- **[DISPUTED] How many trips:** "two trips, 1905 and 1906" (Met) vs "three visits between
  spring 1906 and early 1907" (secondary). Hedge ("over a couple of stays"). [DISPUTED]

### Beat 2 — The Thames, the bridge, Westminster (the subject) [DOCUMENTED]
- The view: the **north bank of the Thames** as it bends toward **Westminster**; on the far
  bank you can pick out **Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament**. The bridge spanning the river
  is the **Charing Cross / Hungerford railway bridge** (hence the alt title "Hungerford Bridge at
  Charing Cross"). [DOCUMENTED — gerrymartinez analysis quoting NGA; arthistoryproject]
- The subject set (bridge + river + barges + bankside city) directly **overlaps Monet's chosen
  London motifs** — same river, near-same vantage in places. Derain "chose similar views to
  Monet, painting some in situ from similar vantage points, but most were painted back in France"
  from sketchbooks. [DOCUMENTED — Wikipedia *Pool of London*; Courtauld]

### Beat 3 — Fauve color on Monet's subject (the break) [DOCUMENTED]
- Same river as Monet, **opposite method**: where Monet dissolved London in atmospheric fog and
  broken light, Derain threw down **non-naturalistic, unblended Fauve color** — the Thames pink,
  the sky yellow. [DOCUMENTED — NGA overview: "color palette and perspective… contrast sharply
  with the Impressionist master's work"]
- **Fauvism** (from *fauves*, "wild beasts") = the label critic Louis Vauxcelles' circle pinned on
  Matisse, Derain & co. after the **1905 Salon d'Automne**, for their violent, arbitrary color.
  [DOCUMENTED — standard; arthistoryproject/Met]
- **Derain's stated aim** (paraphrase, not a verified verbatim quote): he was after **translating**
  a scene's feeling, not **reproducing** its look — color to "stimulate emotion rather than
  describe reality." **[DISPUTED-as-verbatim]** The "translating not reproducing" wording appears
  in secondary web prose (gerrymartinez), NOT pinned to a dated primary letter — **author must
  paraphrase, NEVER set in quotation marks.** [DISPUTED — unverified wording]

### Beat 4 — How Derain's London differs from Monet's (the comparison axis) [DOCUMENTED]
- **Monet:** atmosphere, fog, the *moment* of light, the motif near-dissolved; serial studies of
  the same view under changing weather. **Derain:** flat-ish patches and divisionist spots of pure
  hue, structure asserted, weather irrelevant — the city as a color sensation, not a meteorological
  record. [DOCUMENTED — NGA contrast line; arthistoryproject; Monet London-series facts well attested]
- Use as the chapter's spine comparison (per the art pipeline's "comparisons are the #1 comprehension
  tool" voice lock): **Monet painted the air; Derain painted the feeling.**

### Beat 5 — After the series: Vollard, the market, the afterlife [DOCUMENTED]
- **Vollard bought the London series effectively in its entirety** and the canvases dispersed into
  the market over decades (see provenance). The London paintings are now among Derain's most prized
  works and the heart of the **Courtauld's 2005–06 exhibition "André Derain: The London Paintings."**
  [DOCUMENTED — Courtauld; Sotheby's]
- This specific canvas passed through major dealers and collectors (Quinn, Guillaume, Knoedler,
  Chrysler) before **John Hay Whitney** bought it in **1950** and it reached the NGA as a **1982 gift**.
  [DOCUMENTED — NGA provenance, §5]

## 4. SIX "LOOK CLOSER" ANNOTATIONS ({label, where, detail} — prose pointers, NO crop coords)

All visual claims below are from the NGA verbal-description / overview prose and the secondary
color analyses; **the coordinator MUST re-confirm each against the actual image at gate 6** (the
Demoiselles lesson — short captions are where wrong visual claims hide).

1. **The bridge span across the river** — *where:* the long horizontal structure crossing the
   middle of the canvas, river below, far bank beyond. *detail:* This is the Charing Cross /
   Hungerford railway bridge (the painting's namesake), drawn as a flattened band rather than a
   modeled iron structure — the architecture is asserted, not described. [DOCUMENTED — title +
   subject; CONFIRM the span's placement by eye]
2. **The river in non-natural color** — *where:* the broad lower band of water beneath the bridge.
   *detail:* The Thames is laid in as **short, broken horizontal strokes and dots — mostly bumblebee
   yellow, with bubblegum pink and pale burnt-orange**, much of it over bare off-white canvas. No
   blue, no gray: a river that exists only in paint. [DOCUMENTED — color analysis; CONFIRM hues by eye]
3. **The sky** — *where:* the whole upper band above the far bank. *detail:* Built from **short and
   long vertical strokes of butter yellow, rose pink, pale orange, and patches of watermelon pink** —
   London's famous gray weather replaced outright with a warm glow. The single clearest sign this is
   sensation, not record. [DOCUMENTED — NGA/secondary color analysis]
4. **The boats / barges on the Thames** — *where:* on the water, mid- and foreground. *detail:* Small
   river craft / barges sit on the colored water (the work is tagged "Boats" by NGA). They anchor the
   scale of the bridge and read as dark accents against the pink-and-yellow river. [DOCUMENTED that
   boats are present — NGA subject tags; CONFIRM count/placement by eye, hedge specifics]
5. **The bank with buildings (Westminster / Big Ben)** — *where:* the far (north) bank, behind and
   beyond the bridge. *detail:* The riverfront bends toward Westminster; among the massed buildings
   you can pick out **Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament** — the one anchor that tells you this is
   London at all, glowing orange/turquoise rather than stone-gray. [DOCUMENTED — gerrymartinez quoting
   NGA; arthistoryproject; CONFIRM Parliament is identifiable in the chosen image]
6. **Divisionist spots vs flat patches (the handling)** — *where:* compare the broken, dotted water
   and sky against the broader flat color areas. *detail:* Derain mixes two touches — **Seurat/Signac
   divisionist dabs** (separate spots of pure color meant to mix in the eye) in the water and sky, set
   against **flatter Fauve patches** elsewhere. In his hands the Neo-Impressionist dot loosens into
   "intermittently applied colored spots and lines," not strict points. [DOCUMENTED — arthive/secondary;
   the Seurat–Signac lineage is standard; CONFIRM the two touches coexist by eye]

> **Newcomer glosses the clarity gate will want defined:** *Fauvism* ("wild beasts," arbitrary
> non-natural color, 1905), *divisionism / pointillism* (separate dots of pure color that fuse in the
> eye, Seurat/Signac), *Salon d'Automne*, *the Thames/Westminster geography*, *who Monet/Vollard are*.

## 6. KEY FIGURES (cast nameplates)

- **André Derain** (1880–1954) — French painter; co-founder of **Fauvism** with Matisse; the maker.
  At 26 in 1906. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Ambroise Vollard** (1866–1939) — Paris dealer; **commissioned/sent Derain to London** and bought
  the resulting series. The reason the painting exists. [DOCUMENTED — NGA]
- **Claude Monet** (1840–1926) — the **benchmark**, not a participant: his London/Thames series
  (1899–1904, shown Durand-Ruel 1904) is the thing Derain's series was launched to answer. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Henri Matisse** (1869–1954) — Derain's fellow Fauve; the alongside-whom Fauvism was developed
  (NGA names him explicitly in the work overview). Not present in London, but the movement partner.
  [DOCUMENTED — NGA overview]
- **(secondary, for provenance/series color):** John Quinn, Paul Guillaume, John Hay Whitney —
  collectors in the chain; Durand-Ruel — the gallery that showed Monet's London series.

## 7. LEGEND / DISPUTED CATCHES (the art-pipeline's signature trap — read before authoring)

- **[DISPUTED] Series size:** "**50 commissioned / ~30 delivered / 29 known**" — sources conflict on
  whether 30 or 50 is "the number." **Author: say "dozens commissioned, around thirty delivered,"
  never a single asserted figure.**
- **[DISPUTED] Number of London trips:** two (1905 & 1906) vs three (1906–early 1907). Hedge.
- **[DISPUTED-as-verbatim] The "translate, not reproduce" quote:** the idea is real and on-brand, but
  the *wording* is secondary-web prose with no dated primary cite — **paraphrase only, no quotation marks.**
- **[WATCH] Title collision:** do NOT conflate this NGA canvas with MoMA's *Charing Cross Bridge*
  (different painting) or MoMA's *London Bridge* — the brief's exact warning. This is the **Whitney/NGA**
  canvas, 1982.76.3.
- **[WATCH] "81 × 100 inches":** a units slip in a search result — the real size is **80.3 × 100.3 cm**
  (~2½ × 3¼ ft), a modest easel canvas, NOT a 7-foot mural.
- **[WATCH] Big Ben "named" the bridge:** the painting's bridge is **Charing Cross/Hungerford**;
  Westminster/Big Ben are on the **far bank**, not the spanned bridge. Don't let the prose imply the
  span IS Westminster Bridge (a common loose alt-title).
