## 1. COMPREHENSIVENESS

### [FIX] The "Independent Group" label conflates two distinct things
**Where:** `figures` array, entry 2 — `role: 'British Pop's male founders (Hamilton, Paolozzi, Blake, Boshier)'` under the `name: 'The Independent Group'`.
**Issue:** The Independent Group was a specific circle (ICA London, ~1952–55: Hamilton, Paolozzi, the Smithsons, Reyner Banham, Lawrence Alloway, et al.) that **predates** the 1960s British Pop wave and did **not** include Blake or Boshier. The prose body handles this correctly ("Richard Hamilton and Eduardo Paolozzi of the **older** Independent Group … **then** the younger wave of Peter Blake, Derek Boshier, and Peter Phillips"), but the figure-card label lumps all four under "The Independent Group," which is wrong for Blake and Boshier and slightly muddies who Boty's actual peers were. Boty herself was a co-founder of the **British Pop movement**, not an Independent Group member — keeping the label clean matters for her placement.
**Suggested fix** — relabel the figure card so it names the scene, not the IG:
- `name: 'British Pop's male founders'`
- `role: 'Hamilton & Paolozzi (Independent Group), then Blake, Boshier, Phillips'`
(or simply `role: 'Hamilton, Paolozzi, Blake, Boshier, Phillips — the all-male scene'`). Severity FIX, not blocker, because the prose is already accurate; this is the one surface (the card) that overstates the IG.

### [NICE] "Co-founder" of British Pop is supportable — keep it, it is sourced
The draft calls Boty "one of its co-founders" / "one of its founders" (sole-sister section, factpack §1). Wikipedia and Tate both call her "a co-founder of the 1960s' British Pop art movement" and cite the Nov 1961 "Blake, Boty, Porter, Reeve" show at A.I.A. as one of the earliest British Pop shows. This is well-grounded; no change needed. Flagging only so the reconciler does **not** soften it on a fairness pass — it is her real standing, not an overclaim.

### [NICE] Her place in the scene / the 1961 show
The draft anchors her placement via *Pop Goes the Easel* (1962) and the founders list, which is enough. If room allows, the **Nov 1961 "Blake, Boty, Porter, Reeve" A.I.A. show** (one of the first British Pop shows, with her name in the title) is the single hardest piece of evidence that she was there at the founding, not a latecomer. Optional addition to the sole-sister section. Not required — comprehensiveness already met without it.

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### Boty as agent and intellect — STRONG, no fix
The draft is exemplary here. She is the one who "set out to paint it," who "takes Pop's exact toolkit and turns it back," whose own quote is the "plainest gloss on the work she ever spoke." The death/rediscovery is sequenced **after** the work and its argument (afterlife is the last section), so she is never reduced to "the pretty one" or a tragic footnote. The draft even pre-empts the failure mode explicitly ("It would be easy to flatten Boty into a tidy proto-feminist martyr … and she has been. The truer picture has more friction in it"), which is exactly the Kristal/*Sole Sister* caution from factpack §8.6. Keep all of this.

### [FIX] Two feminist-reading phrasings slightly outrun the sourcing — hedge or attribute
The feminist reading is well-grounded overall, but two spots state the artist's **intent** as flat fact where the sources support it as the dominant scholarly reading, not a documented authorial statement:
- `ManThePair`, final para: *"Boty is not adding women painters to Pop. She is using Pop's own raw material to ask why the line between person and object falls exactly where it does."*
- `ManThePair`, *II* para: *"Boty **deliberately** flattens the supposed gap between the 'high' art nude and the 'low' pin-up, because **both**, she is saying, treat a woman as a thing to be looked at."*

These are the strongest, most confident intent-claims in the read. They are defensible (this is the consensus reading, and her *Talking to Women* quote backs the spirit of it), but "deliberately … she is saying" asserts authorial purpose that no quoted Boty statement directly pins to *these canvases*. Per the standing rule that the feminist reading stay "proportionate and sourced (her own statements)," tie the strongest claims to the reading rather than to a documented declaration.
**Suggested softening (minimal):**
- "…because both, **the pairing argues**, treat a woman as a thing to be looked at." (or "…both, **the picture says**…" — matching the voice you already use for "the picture says without a word")
- The "she is using Pop's own raw material to ask…" line is fine as written **because** the break section grounds it in the cited Nell Dunn quote a few screens later; leave it. Only the "deliberately … she is saying" needs the lighter touch.
Severity FIX — proportion/sourcing, not a factual error.

### [NICE] "Before the vocabulary existed" — keep, but it's the one spot to watch for overclaim
The break section's "a fully feminist Pop argument years before there was a movement to name it" is well-handled: it is immediately grounded (Nell Dunn 1965; Friedan "had only just reached the country"). This is proportionate and sourced. No change. Flagging only because "before the vocabulary existed / first feminist Pop voice" is exactly the kind of first/only claim that drifts into overclaim — here it is anchored to dated facts and stays an art-historical observation, not a coronation. Good as is.
