## Summary verdict

**Significant gaps: 3 MISSING, 3 THIN items.**

The draft is strong on what it covers — the political context, the Courbet arc through the Pavilion, Millet's Gleaners/Angelus, Daumier, Bonheur. The writing is good. The gaps are:

1. **Millet's *The Sower* (1850)** — absent entirely; the most significant omission. His arc starts here, contemporaneously with Courbet's opening salvos.
2. **Photography's arrival (1839/early 1850s)** — a genuine fact-pack blind spot. The draft has no photography at all, which makes the reader's picture of the "why now" incomplete.
3. **Courbet's afterlife** — the Commune, jail, exile, death — is absent. The draft stops at 1855. The political logic of the movement finds its conclusion in Courbet's fate; leaving it out makes the "Realism had real stakes" claim feel abstract.
4. **THIN: Realism as reaction to Romanticism** (not just the academy) — needs one beat naming the double front.
5. **THIN: Corot and the Barbizon school as a named collective** — the colony has no faces; Corot is the dominant figure and is entirely absent.
6. **THIN: Manet as the named bridge** — the handoff names no one; one sentence would close it properly.
