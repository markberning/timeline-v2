## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### a) Einstein/relativity as a denied MYTH — CONFIRMED, handled correctly
The draft does exactly what the prompt requires. `PerCamembert` ("It is not about
Einstein, and he said so") presents the relativity reading as "the thing almost
everyone 'knows' ... and which is wrong," names it as "one that Dalí explicitly
rejected," and grounds the denial in the on-the-record Prigogine exchange with the
verbatim "the surrealist perception of a Camembert melting in the sun." The `break`
section reinforces it ("Not relativity (Dalí denied that): camembert"). Web-verified:
the Prigogine/Camembert-in-the-sun denial is the documented account (Wikipedia, citing
*The Dalí Dimension*, 2008). PASS.

One fairness nuance — see [FIX] below — is that the draft slightly over-flattens the
scholarly side: serious critics (e.g., Dawn Ades) do read relativity *into* the work as
an unconscious symbol even while accepting Dalí's denial of conscious intent. The draft
frames the relativity reading purely as a popular error ("which is wrong"), which is a
touch too tidy.

### [FIX] "and which is wrong" overstates the verdict on the relativity reading
`PerCamembert` para 3: "...the thing almost everyone 'knows' about this painting, and
which is wrong." The relativity *interpretation* is not flatly "wrong" — it is an
interpretation Dalí **denied as his source/intent**. Reputable scholars still read the
soft watches as an unconscious symbol of relativized time (Dawn Ades is the standard
citation). The accurate, fairer claim is about authorship of intent, not about the
reading being false. This is the one place the draft tips from "Dalí denied it" toward
"it is untrue," which is a slightly different and overreaching claim.

Suggested replacement for the sentence:
> "Which brings us to the thing almost everyone 'knows' about this painting — and which
> Dal&iacute; himself rejected."

(Drop "which is wrong." The next paragraph already does the work of showing Dalí said no.
The reader still gets the correction; the prose just stops asserting the interpretation
is false rather than disavowed.) Optionally add, in para 4 after the Prigogine line, a
one-clause concession: "Some critics still read relativity into the picture as an
unconscious symbol; what is settled is that Dal&iacute; denied it as his source." This
makes the fairness airtight. The verbatim quote and the "he gets the last word on his
own painting" framing are otherwise excellent and should stay.

### b) Genius-myth / Dalí-showman — CONFIRMED, handled well
The draft does not launder Dalí's self-mythology as gospel. `PerCamembert` para 2 is
exemplary: "a wonderful story, told by one of the great self-mythologizers of the
twentieth century"; "There is no second witness to the cheese"; "Dalí was an unreliable
narrator on principle." The "Dalí said" tag is foregrounded deliberately ("the phrase
'Dalí said' is going to do a lot of work in this section, so notice it"). This is the
correct posture. PASS, no change.

One small watch-item (not a fix): the `break` section calls Dalí's "hand-painted dream
photographs" his goal/phrase, correctly attributed to him. Good — it stays his term, not
the narrator's claim about the work.

### c) "Icon of Surrealism" / "most famous" overclaim — needs proportional scoping
The draft makes the icon claim in several registers. Most are correctly scoped to the
**general public / mass audience**, which is the defensible frame and matches the fact
pack (§3 "the single most recognizable image of Surrealism *for the general public*").
Where it stays scoped, it is fine:
- `PerAfterlife`: "the image that taught a mass audience what 'surreal' looks like" —
  scoped, good.
- hook / section titles: "The image that taught the world what 'surreal' looks like" —
  acceptable as hook register.

But two spots drop the qualifier and assert a flat superlative:

### [FIX] Unscoped superlative: "the single most recognizable image of Surrealism"
The `afterlife` section blurb (PART A `sections`) reads: "from there made the single
most recognizable image of Surrealism." And `PerLooking` para 1 calls it "The world's
most reproduced surreal image." These are flat first/only superlatives stated as fact.
Web check: MoMA and Wikipedia phrase it as **"one of the most recognizable works of
Surrealism"** — the cautious, sourced form. "The single most recognizable" and "the
world's most reproduced" are plausible but not measured/sourced claims.

Suggested fixes (proportional scoping, keeps the punch):
- `sections` afterlife blurb: "...and from there became, for the general public, the
  most recognizable image of Surrealism." (scope to public) OR "...one of the most
  recognizable images of Surrealism" (match the source).
- `PerLooking` para 1: change "The world's most reproduced surreal image is a
  miniature." to "One of the most reproduced images in Surrealism is a miniature." or
  "This much-reproduced surreal image is a miniature." (Removes the unsourced "world's
  most" while keeping the size-shock payoff.)

The "public face of an entire movement" and "more than any Miró or Ernst or Magritte"
framing in `PerAfterlife` is defensible *as a recognition claim* and reads as evaluative
house-voice rather than a hard ranking — leave it, but it leans on the same scoping, so
fixing the two flat superlatives above is what matters.

### d) Proportion / anachronism — CLEAN
- No anachronism: the relativity-reading is correctly placed as a *later* public
  interpretation, not something Dalí was responding to in 1931. The paranoiac-critical
  method is dated "c. 1930 / 1929–1931" consistent with the fact pack.
- Proportion is good: the camembert and the Einstein-denial each get appropriate weight;
  the size-shock is repeated but earns it (it is the genuine surprise).
- [NICE] The closing line "outlasted nearly every grander, larger, more 'important'
  picture of its century" is a rhetorical flourish bordering on an unscoped superlative,
  but it reads clearly as house-voice peroration, not a factual ranking. Acceptable; if a
  reviewer wants it airtight, soften "nearly every" to "many a." Low priority.
- [NICE] Smarthistory Rorschach attribution in `PerBreak` ("Smarthistory frames it as...")
  is correctly attributed and matches fact pack §3. Good practice — leave it.
