## 2. THE STORY

- **Setting — early Surrealism in Paris.** Miró painted this in Paris in 1924–25,
  right as the Surrealist movement was forming: André Breton's first *Manifesto of
  Surrealism* appeared in 1924. Surrealism prized the unconscious, dreams, and
  **automatism** (letting the hand make marks without conscious planning). This
  canvas is one of the touchstone early Surrealist paintings and is widely held to be
  among Miró's greatest works. [AKG-LEC][WIKI]
- **A teeming room of dream-creatures.** The picture is a small interior crammed with
  **biomorphic** figures — soft, rounded, organic shapes that read as half-human,
  half-animal, half-object. AKG describes the revelers as **hybrid creatures with
  characteristics of both humans and animals**, "playing, singing, dancing, and
  celebrating, with music literally in the air." [AKG]
- **The harlequin.** The traditional commedia-dell'arte Harlequin is reimagined: a
  guitar-like body wearing the figure's costume cues — a **half-red, half-blue mask**
  and a **diamond pattern** on the tunic, plus (per accounts) a **moustache, beard,
  pipe, and admiral's hat**. A **hole in the harlequin's stomach** is read as an
  allusion to Miró's poverty and hunger at the time. [AKG][WIKI][AKG-LEC]
- **The hunger anecdote (verify — it is well-documented, NOT a legend).** Miró was so
  poor in this period that, by the AKG account, he could afford to serve a dinner
  guest only **radishes**, and he described coming home at the end of a foodless day
  and, **"in a kind of trance,"** drawing the forms that became this painting. He
  said his **hunger produced hallucinations** that he tried to capture (see the exact
  quote, §4). [AKG][HOA]
- **Automatism vs. careful composition (verify — it was deliberately composed).**
  Despite the spontaneous, automatist *look*, Miró described a **planned, revised**
  process: "After long meditation on what I proposed to do, I began to paint, and as
  I painted I introduced all the changes I believed to be appropriate" (per the AKG
  lecture). So: dream/hallucination as the *source*, but **deliberate composition** in
  the execution — the automatist surface is partly a constructed effect. [AKG-LEC]
- **Catalonia color/landscape (lighter-weight claim).** One AKG search summary frames
  it as a climactic work in a series infused with the **color scheme and landscape of
  Miró's native Catalonia**; the AKG lecture page itself did **not** stress a
  Catalonia link. `[VERIFY]` if the author wants to lean on Catalonia — keep it soft
  or cut. [AKG-search summary; not in AKG-LEC]

## 3. WHY IT'S A BREAK (REQUIRED — before / after)

- **Before:** academic and even most modern painting still organized a canvas around
  *recognizable subjects in real space* — a still life is a table with nameable
  objects; even Cubism fractured *real* things (guitars, bottles, people). The picture
  obeyed the world.
- **After / the break:** Miró floats an invented **dream/automatist biomorphic
  vocabulary** — squiggles, dots, single eyes and ears, ladders that look back at you,
  notes drifting in the air, half-animal sprites — that answers to no observed scene.
  The forms come from *inside* (hunger-hallucination + the unconscious), not from a
  motif on a table. It swaps the **sober academic still life** for a **playful, weightless
  Surrealist world** where objects are alive and the rules of real space are suspended.
  That shift — from depicting the seen world to staging the unconscious as a populated
  little universe — is the break. [AKG][AKG-LEC][WIKI]

## 4. KEY STATEMENT (verbatim, SOURCED)

**Primary quote — the hunger/hallucination passage** (use this as the key passage):

> "I tried to capture the hallucinations that my hunger produced in me. It's not that
> I painted what I saw in my dreams, as Breton and his lot predicated in those days,
> but that hunger provoked in me a sort of trance."

Source: quoted by the Buffalo AKG account of the work. [AKG-LEC][HOA]
`[NOTE]` This is the sturdiest, best-attributed quote — it ties hunger to the imagery
*and* distances Miró from pure dream-painting. Recommended for the KEY STATEMENT block.

**Secondary — the careful-composition statement** (good for the automatism nuance):

> "After long meditation on what I proposed to do, I began to paint, and as I painted
> I introduced all the changes I believed to be appropriate."

Source: AKG lecture. [AKG-LEC]

**Tertiary — the prose-poem fragment** (vivid but `[VERIFY]` exact wording):

> "The ball of yarn unraveled by cats dressed up as smoky harlequins twisting inside
> me and stabbing my gut during the period of my great hunger that gave birth to the
> hallucinations recorded in this painting."

`[VARIANT/VERIFY]` Wording wobbles across secondary sources ("dressed up" vs
"dresses up," "twisting inside me" vs "twisting around inside me"). This is from
Miró's own writings on the work but I could not pin a single authoritative published
text. **If used, present as paraphrase/with caveat, not as a clean verbatim.**

## 5. LOOKING POINTS (~6 — prose pointers, no coordinates)

1. **The moustached harlequin.** Center-left, a guitar-shaped body wearing the costume:
   a mask split **red/blue**, a **diamond-patterned** tunic, a curling **moustache**,
   a **pipe**, and (per accounts) a small admiral's hat. A dark **hole in its belly** —
   read as the artist's literal hunger. [AKG][WIKI]
2. **The ladder with an ear and an eye.** A **ladder** stands as its own creature — it
   carries a single **eye** and a single **ear**, an anthropomorphized object. The
   ladder is a recurring Miró motif of flight / escape / elevation. [AKG][GAC][AKG-LEC]
3. **The cat(s) with the ball of yarn.** Lower-right, **cat-like creatures** play with
   what looks like a **ball of wool/yarn** — echoing the "ball of yarn unraveled by
   cats" line from Miró's own description. [SARTLE / quote §4]
4. **Musical notes in the air.** Black **musical notes** drift through the room —
   "music literally in the air" (AKG); the whole scene is a noisy carnival of sound. [AKG]
5. **The window with the black triangle (the globe).** An opening at upper right reads
   as a **window**; a **black triangle** in it is commonly read as the **Eiffel Tower**
   (the Paris setting), and a **dark sphere/green globe** stands for the **world**. [WIKI][AKG-LEC]
   `[VERIFY]` the Eiffel-Tower reading is the standard interpretation, not an AKG label —
   attribute as "often read as."
6. **The swarm of small creatures.** The rest of the room teems with **hybrid sprites,
   insects/bugs, fish, winged beings** — playing, singing, dancing. The biomorphic
   crowd is the carnival. [AKG][WIKI]

## 8. APOCRYPHA / TRAPS (correct, with sources)

- **The hunger story — TRUE but don't overstate.** It is **Miró's own account**, well
  documented by the holding museum: hunger → trance → hallucinated forms; the
  radishes-for-dinner detail and the belly-hole reading are AKG's. Do **not** inflate
  it into "he literally starved while painting it" or claim the *whole* picture is an
  unmediated hallucination — Miró explicitly said he **distanced himself from pure
  dream-painting** and **deliberately composed/revised** (§2, §4). Frame as: hunger was
  the *source* of the imagery; the painting itself was *carefully constructed.* [AKG-LEC]
- **Date 1924 vs 1925.** Catalogue date is **1924–25** (spans both years). Avoid
  writing a single clean year; sources that say just "1925" or just "1924" are
  clipping the range. [AKG][GAC]
- **"Automatic" label vs careful composition.** The biggest interpretive trap: the
  picture *looks* purely automatist, and it's a Surrealist icon — but Miró's own
  statement makes clear the **composition was planned and revised** (§4 secondary
  quote). Don't call it "painted automatically/spontaneously" without the
  qualification. [AKG-LEC]
- **Dimensions.** Use **66 × 93 cm (26 × 36⅝ in)** from the holding museum. **Reject
  Wikipedia's 90.5 cm width** as the outlier. Heights agree at 66 cm. [AKG][GAC vs WIKI]
- **Eiffel Tower / globe readings** are **interpretive conventions**, not museum labels
  — attribute as "commonly/often read as," not as fact. [WIKI]
- **Title form.** AKG's own English title is *Carnival of Harlequin*; the common
  English title is *The Harlequin's Carnival*. Both are fine; don't present one as the
  "wrong" form. [AKG][WIKI]
