### MUST-ADD findings

**M1-A — MISSING: The Arnhold version was submitted to the 1870 Salon and rejected**

The draft says the Arnhold canvas is "lost, presumed destroyed in WWII" and that Monet "probably never finished it anyway." Both are in the fact pack. What is NOT in the draft (or the fact pack) but is documented by multiple sources including the National Gallery London and Wikipedia's La Grenouillère article: the larger Arnhold canvas was actually *submitted to the Salon of 1870 and rejected*. This changes the story materially. The draft says Monet "called the canvases pochades that he hoped to work up someday into a real tableau... the tableau never came." That is misleading — he *did* make a more finished version, submitted it, and was rejected. The distinction between "never finished the tableau" and "made a more finished version, submitted it to the Salon 1870, got rejected, and it was subsequently lost" is significant: it tells a much better story about what happened to that ambition, and it means the Bazille letter predates a more finished attempt than the draft implies.

**Where it should go:** `making` chapter (the Bazille letter paragraph) and/or `afterlife` (the lost Berlin canvas paragraph). Suggested phrasing: "He made a start on that larger version — a more finished canvas, combining elements of both the Met and London pictures, was submitted to the Salon of 1870. It was rejected. That painting passed to the Arnhold collection in Berlin, was presumably destroyed in the Second World War, and survives only in a photograph."

**Verified facts:** National Gallery London catalogue entry for NG6456; Wikipedia "La Grenouillère (Monet)"; Kiama Art Gallery blog (sourcing NG London). The "rejected by Salon 1870" detail is consistent across multiple independent sources.

**M1-B — THIN: The National Gallery London version ("Bathers at La Grenouillère") is not mentioned**

The draft mentions the Stockholm Renoir as a sibling canvas but never mentions the National Gallery London version of Monet's *La Grenouillère* (NG6456, *Bathers at La Grenouillère*, 1869). There are therefore at least *three* related canvases from 1869: the Met version, the NGL version, and the lost Arnhold tableau. The draft implies the Met version and the Stockholm Renoir are the two surviving objects from that summer, which understates the picture family.

This is a SHOULD-CONSIDER rather than a MUST-ADD: the work read is about the Met picture specifically, and the NGL version is not its provenance. But a reader who knows anything about the subject will notice the NGL version isn't mentioned. A brief acknowledgment in `afterlife` ("Monet made at least two surviving pochade-canvases of La Grenouillère — the other is at the National Gallery in London") would forestall that gap.

**Rating:** SHOULD-CONSIDER (not blocking, but a conspicuous omission for any informed reader).

### MUST-ADD findings

**M2-A — MISSING: There were two windmills at the Moulin de la Galette site**

The draft describes "a real working windmill" (singular) on the Butte Montmartre. In fact the site had *two* windmills: the **Moulin Blute-Fin** (built 1622, the one visible in Renoir's painting and in the background of the dance garden) and the **Moulin Radet** (built 1717, which was converted into a guinguette first). Both were owned by the Debray family from 1809. The dance garden operated between and under them. This is not a pedantic detail: the draft names "a windmill" generically and the reader builds a wrong image of the site as having one mill. More importantly, the famous silhouette in Renoir's painting is specifically Blute-Fin, not an unnamed single mill.

This is a SHOULD-CONSIDER: the two-mill detail is contextually correct to include but would not leave a reader *misinformed* about the picture itself if omitted. Upgrade to MUST-ADD if the draft implies one mill owns the whole identity of the site (which it currently does).

**Rating: SHOULD-CONSIDER** (real gap; correct the "a windmill" to "a pair of windmills" and name Blute-Fin as the one in the picture).

**M2-B — THIN: The galette food item is misdescribed**

The draft calls the galette "a flat sweet pancake — the galette, the local round biscuit-cake the place got its name from." Per web research, the galette at the Moulin de la Galette was described as a small flat bread/biscuit cake made by the Debray millers from their own flour, sold with a glass of milk. The "pancake" description conflates two different things: a galette as a flat bread/round cake sold at this particular spot, vs. the Breton buckwheat savoury crepe, which is a different item. The fact pack says "flat sweet pancake (the galette, the local round biscuit-cake the place got its name from)" which hedges by calling it both a pancake and a biscuit-cake. The best description is a flat round biscuit-cake made from flour — not a pancake in the crepe sense.

**Rating: SHOULD-CONSIDER** (minor description refinement; not blocking but "pancake" will mislead some readers).

**M2-C — COVERED with minor THIN note: Caillebotte's cause of death**

The draft says "heart trouble, suddenly" as the cause of death. Multiple sources give different causes (pulmonary congestion, cerebral hemorrhage, heart disease). The actual documented cause is most commonly stated as "pulmonary congestion" while working in his garden at Petit-Gennevilliers; the cerebral hemorrhage version also circulates. "Heart trouble" is not the most accurate phrasing but is not a serious error. The draft's fact ledger flags this for gate review. If precise medical cause matters, "pulmonary congestion" is the most frequently cited description; otherwise "died suddenly" is safe.

**Rating: SHOULD-CONSIDER** (hedge to "died suddenly" rather than "heart trouble" unless the cause can be pinned more exactly).

The figure in the draft ("the state initially refused 27 of the 67 works") should either be left as approximate or removed to avoid pinning a disputed count at the work level. No change needed if the movement read handles the precise count.

### MUST-ADD findings

**No blocking MUST-ADDs.** The Cradle draft covers every essential item on the checklist. The identity errors are refused, the provenance is complete, the 1874 exhibition details are accurate, the technical signature is well described, and the afterlife arc is correctly told.

**One SHOULD-CONSIDER:**

**SC4-A — SHOULD-CONSIDER: Edma's standing as "the more naturally talented" framing**

The draft says Edma was "the more naturally talented of the two" and attributes this to "several observers." The fact pack sources this as "documented family/critical reception" and the prose hedges appropriately with "in the eyes of several of the people who saw them at work." This framing is defensible but worth flagging: the primary sources for this claim are largely family correspondence (Berthe's own letters to Edma, written out of affection and perhaps false modesty) and it has been disputed by later critics. The hedge "in the eyes of several of the people who saw them at work" is the correct move. The current phrasing is within bounds.

**Rating: SHOULD-CONSIDER** (no change needed; current hedging is correct).

**SC4-B — SHOULD-CONSIDER: Morisot's exhibition record across all eight Impressionist exhibitions**

The draft notes she "showed at every subsequent Impressionist exhibition for the rest of her life — all eight of them, more than any other Impressionist except Pissarro." This is a strong claim and it is in the fact pack. Verification: Morisot is generally said to have shown at all eight exhibitions (1874–1886) except 1877 (when she was on her honeymoon, having married in Dec 1874 — wait: the third was April 1877, so she would have been married ~2.5 years; sources confirm she DID show at the 3rd exhibition in 1877). The "all eight except one" vs "all eight" discrepancy appears in some sources. The fact pack lists this as VERIFIED; accept the fact pack's reading unless the movement read corrects it.

**Rating: SHOULD-CONSIDER** (verify against movement read's exhibition list; if the fact pack says all eight, run with it).

## Top 5 findings (ranked by reader impact)

**1. M1-A — La Grenouillère: Arnhold version was submitted to the 1870 Salon and rejected [MUST-ADD, blocking]**
The draft implies the larger Arnhold tableau was a project Monet never quite got around to finishing. Multiple independent sources (NGL catalogue, Wikipedia, Kiama/NGL) confirm he *did* make a more finished version — wider than the Met and NGL sketches, combining elements from both — submitted it to the Salon of 1870, was rejected, and it passed to the Arnhold collection and was later destroyed in WWII. This is not a trivial addition: it changes "a dream he never realised" into "a dream he realised, submitted, got rejected, and lost to the war." The story is richer and more accurate. The fact pack's phrasing ("the large finished *tableau* was never realised") is at odds with external sources and should be revisited. The draft follows the fact pack's understated version and needs the Salon 1870 submission added to `making` or `afterlife`.

**2. M2-A — Bal du moulin de la Galette: The site had two windmills, not one [SHOULD-CONSIDER, near-blocking]**
The draft refers to "a working windmill" (singular) on the Butte. The Moulin de la Galette site had two mills: Blute-Fin and Radet, both owned by the Debray family. The Blute-Fin is the mill whose silhouette appears in the background of Renoir's painting. The draft's "a windmill" creates a false image of the site and misses the specific mill visible in the picture. One sentence of correction in `montmartre` would fix it.

**3. M2-B — Bal du moulin de la Galette: The galette food item is misdescribed as "pancake" [SHOULD-CONSIDER]**
"A flat sweet pancake — the galette, the local round biscuit-cake" conflates two items. The Debray galette was a flat round bread/biscuit-cake made from their own flour and sold with milk. "Pancake" will read as a crepe to many readers. Easily fixed: "a flat round biscuit-cake made from the mill's own flour — the *galette* that gave the place its name."

**4. M2-C — Bal du moulin de la Galette: Caillebotte's cause of death is vaguely stated [SHOULD-CONSIDER]**
"Heart trouble, suddenly" is the draft's phrasing. The most commonly cited cause is pulmonary congestion (not cardiac); cerebral hemorrhage also appears in sources. "Died suddenly" is the safe phrasing; if a specific cause is needed, "pulmonary congestion while working in his garden at Petit-Gennevilliers" is the best-supported version.

**5. M1-B — La Grenouillère: The National Gallery London version is unmentioned [SHOULD-CONSIDER]**
There are at least two surviving Monet pochade-canvases from the summer of 1869 (Met + NGL), plus the lost Arnhold tableau and a small study at Kunsthalle Berne. The draft implies the Stockholm Renoir is the only sibling canvas. Acknowledging the NGL version in `afterlife` — one sentence — would prevent a conspicuous gap for any informed reader.
