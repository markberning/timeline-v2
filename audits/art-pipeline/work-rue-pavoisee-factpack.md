## 3. NARRATIVE BEATS (≈5) — all sourced

### Beat 1 — Dufy of Le Havre [DOCUMENTED]
- Born in **Le Havre, 3 June 1877**, the big Norman port at the Seine's mouth. (EN Wikipedia)
- Started evening classes at **Le Havre's École des Beaux-Arts in 1895**; scholarship to the
  **École nationale supérieure des beaux-arts, Paris, c.1900**. (EN Wikipedia)
- Le Havre — its harbor basins, yachts, regattas, sea — is his home subject; the flag motif
  grew directly out of his **Yachts / Regatta Tent** series before it "reached full
  accomplishment in *Street Decked with Flags*." (MuMa Le Havre)
- *La Rue pavoisée* paints HIS port city on its festival day — a hometown picture.

### Beat 2 — The Matisse shock: *Luxe, calme et volupté* at the 1905 Indépendants [DOCUMENTED, with one [LEGEND] flag]
- At the **Salon des Indépendants of 1905**, Dufy saw Henri **Matisse's *Luxe, calme et
  volupté*** (painted 1904, St-Tropez; bought by Signac; shown 1905). It was **"a revelation"**
  / **"le choc fauve"** that "directed his interests towards Fauvism." (EN Wikipedia; Boisgirard-
  Antonini lot essay "le « choc fauve »"; Luxe Calme et Volupté Wikipedia)
- Matisse's canvas itself was **Neo-Impressionist / Divisionist** (Signac's dotted method) —
  so what hit Dufy was high-key, unmixed color, not yet flat Fauve patches; Fauvism proper
  exploded at the **Salon d'Automne 1905** (the "fauves"/wild-beasts naming). The author may
  say Dufy's conversion *began* at the 1905 Indépendants and matured over 1905–06.
- **[LEGEND / frame-it]:** the exact wording "a revelation" / "before the *Luxe, calme et
  volupté* I understood all the new reasons for painting" is the kind of retrospective
  artist-recollection that floats through the literature. Several sources paraphrase Dufy as
  saying the Matisse showed him "the miracle of imagination introduced into drawing and color"
  — **do NOT quote a sentence as Dufy's exact words unless quoting a dated, sourced text.**
  Frame as "by his own later account" / "is widely recorded as a revelation," not a flat quote.
  **[VERIFY before any direct quote.]**

### Beat 3 — Marquet at his side, the festival as motif [DOCUMENTED]
- **Summer 1906:** Dufy worked the Normandy coast (Le Havre, Fécamp, Trouville) with **Albert
  Marquet** (1875–1947), a fellow Fauve. The two **"painted side by side from the windows of
  hotel bedrooms,"** doing the harbor basins, the sea, **"the city streets decked with flags,"**
  and poster-plastered walls "from a plethora of viewpoints." (MuMa Le Havre)
- The **14 July** (Bastille Day, the French national holiday) flag-decked streets were a shared
  motif — **both** men painted them in 1906. Marquet's own *Le 14 juillet au Havre* (1906) is
  the parallel canvas. (MuMa; WikiArt — Marquet "July 14th at Havre, 1906")
- Earlier precedent: Dufy already worked at **Fécamp with Marquet in 1904**. (EN Wikipedia)
- The flag motif "spectacularly reached full accomplishment in *Street Decked with Flags*,
  painted in Le Havre for the 14th of July celebrations of 1906." (MuMa Le Havre)

### Beat 4 — The flags as ready-made pure-color patches; the flatness is the point [DOCUMENTED — strongest sourced claim]
- Pompidou catalogue, verbatim: **"En plaçant frontalement un drapeau français au centre de sa
  composition, Dufy met en évidence de manière radicale la planéité de la surface picturale."**
  → "By placing a French flag frontally at the center of his composition, Dufy radically
  emphasizes the flatness of the picture surface." (Centre Pompidou)
- The **tricolor (bleu-blanc-rouge)** is a found object of pure color: a flat red/white/blue
  bar hung parallel to the canvas. Dufy doesn't model it in light and shade; he lets it sit as a
  flat patch — so the **subject itself is already abstract color**, the festival doing the
  Fauve's work for him.
- histoire-image.org (French Ministry of Culture / RMN study): the painting is built on **"un
  travail sur l'aplat et la couleur vive"** (work on flat color areas and vivid color); a
  high/raised viewpoint **"accentue le caractère animé du tableau"**; and crucially **"la
  couleur, indépendante du motif, génère l'espace"** — color, independent of the subject,
  generates the space. The Norman 1906 canvases are **"un tournant décisif"** from
  Impressionist observation to "personal chromatic expression."

### Beat 5 — A brief Fauve moment, then he moves on [DOCUMENTED]
- Dufy's **Fauve period ran c.1905 to about 1909**, when "contact with the work of **Paul
  Cézanne** led him to adopt a subtler technique." (EN Wikipedia)
- He then **dabbled in Cubism**, and from **c.1920** developed the light, "stenographic" style
  — rapid thin color washes over skeletal drawing — that made him famous (textiles, the
  decorative riviera/regatta scenes, later *La Fée Électricité*, 1937). (EN Wikipedia)
- So *La Rue pavoisée* (1906) catches him at the white heat of his short Fauve experiment —
  one of the high points of a phase he soon left behind. The Pompidou notes this version is
  "the most innovative" of his flag streets, the tricolor foreground set against "the green and
  pink perspective of the houses," **kept by the artist his whole life** (a painter's own
  bookmark of a breakthrough). (FR Wikipedia / Pompidou)
- Monet precedent: Dufy & Marquet's flag-street theme is **"inspired by a famous composition
  painted by Claude Monet"** — Monet's *La Rue Montorgueil* / *La Rue Saint-Denis, fête du 30
  juin 1878* (flag-decked Paris streets on a national festival). Where Monet dissolves the flags
  into Impressionist atmosphere, Dufy organizes them into flat planes of pure color. (FR
  Wikipedia; histoire-image.org) **[DOCUMENTED for "inspired by Monet"; the specific Monet
  canvas (Montorgueil vs Saint-Denis) — both are 1878 fête-flag streets; name it generically
  unless verified.]**

## 4. "LOOK CLOSER" — 6 annotations (PROSE pointers; verify each against the image at gate-1)

Pointers are `{label, where, detail}`. `where` = a written location phrase; NO crop coords.
**All six MUST be re-checked against the actual canvas at the fact-check gate** — left/right,
counts, and "is there lettering?" are exactly where caption errors hide (see pipeline retro pass).

1. **The tricolor that flattens the street** — *where:* the French flag hung **frontally near
   the center** of the composition. *detail:* This is the documented heart of the picture: a
   flat bar of pure blue-white-red, parallel to the canvas, that Dufy uses to "radically
   emphasize the flatness of the picture surface" (Pompidou). It is the festival's own ready-made
   patch of pure color. **[DOCUMENTED — Pompidou catalogue.]**

2. **A street that is all flags** — *where:* the upper register, the flags strung and hung
   along/across the street. *detail:* the *pavoisement* (the act of dressing a street in flags
   for a national holiday) turns the whole upper picture into a confetti of red, white and blue
   patches. **[DOCUMENTED — subject is the flag-decked street; verify the specific arrangement
   (strung overhead vs hung from façades) by eye.]**

3. **The festival crowd** — *where:* the lower street, beneath the flags. *detail:* the high
   viewpoint "accentuates the animated character" of the scene (histoire-image.org); the crowd
   reads as the holiday throng. **[VERIFY by eye — confirm figures are present and roughly how
   they're handled (sketchy patches vs drawn); do not over-specify numbers.]**

4. **Green-and-pink houses in steep perspective** — *where:* the receding **houses lining the
   street, behind/around the central flag**. *detail:* FR Wikipedia/Pompidou: the tricolor
   foreground contrasts with **"la perspective verte et rose des maisons"** — the houses run
   back in green and pink. The perspective is steep/raised. **[DOCUMENTED — colors named by the
   museum source; verify the green/pink reading against the image.]**

5. **Color making the space, not light** — *where:* across the whole surface — the broad flat
   areas (*aplats*) of vivid, unmodelled color. *detail:* there is little Impressionist
   atmosphere; "color, independent of the subject, generates the space" (histoire-image.org).
   Compare to Monet's flag streets, where the flags dissolve into shimmer — Dufy's stay as flat
   shapes. **[DOCUMENTED.]**

6. **Lettering / signs — VERIFY [⚠️ UNCONFIRMED]** — *where:* the shopfronts / façades along the
   street, IF present. *detail:* the brief asks for a lettering/signs pointer. **No source
   consulted confirms readable lettering or shop signs on THIS canvas** (the poster-walls were a
   *separate* Marquet/Dufy motif that summer, per MuMa). **Do NOT author this pointer unless the
   coordinator confirms visible lettering/signage on the actual image at gate-1.** If none is
   visible, drop to 5 pointers or substitute the **high-key palette** as the 6th (see below).
   **[ACTION: confirm against image; likely DROP or SWAP.]**

   *Swap candidate (6b) — The high-key holiday palette* — *where:* overall. *detail:* a bright,
