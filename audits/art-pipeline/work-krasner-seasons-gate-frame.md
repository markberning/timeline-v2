### NICE-1 [NICE] — "the influence" facet is the lightest of the seven
The brief lists "the influence" as an essential facet. The draft establishes Krasner's
*standing* (first-generation AbEx, recovered by feminist art history) and the lineage const
lists `children: Biomorphic abstraction / Feminist art history's recovery / Large-scale
women's abstraction`. But the prose never lands a concrete forward-influence beat — who/what
came after partly because of her. This is a genuinely thin part of the record (Krasner's
influence is more "model and precedent for later women working large in gestural/biomorphic
abstraction" than a documented school), so this is NICE, not FIX. Optional one-clause add to
the `afterlife` reappraisal paragraph, e.g. after "first-generation Abstract Expressionists":

> …and made her a standing precedent for the later generations of women who claimed
> large-scale gestural and biomorphic abstraction as their own ground.

Keep it modest and unnamed — do not invent a specific named follower, which the sources
don't cleanly support.

### NICE-2 [NICE] — collage / *Little Image* reinvention is absent (acceptable)
The draft frames Krasner as already-serious-pre-Pollock but doesn't mention her habit of
cutting up and remaking her own work, or the *Little Image* paintings — the prior reinventions
that make *The Seasons* read as one more deliberate leap rather than a death-triggered
novelty. Omitting it is defensible for a single-work read (scope discipline), so NICE only.
If a one-liner is wanted it strengthens the "agent, not reactive" framing; if cut, no loss.

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS — checked against each brief axis

### 2a. Krasner as AGENT, not a grieving widow / not merely reactive — **PASS, exemplary**
This is the axis most likely to fail on this subject, and the draft handles it deliberately:
- It *names and refuses* the trap in-text (the "grieving widow… too small" passage).
- It frames the studio as an *arrival*, not a catharsis: "not a catharsis so much as an
  arrival: a painter who had finally been handed the room she needed."
- Proportion is held with the 20-years-before / ~30-years-after bracket (`barn` §2), which
  structurally prevents the death from becoming the whole explanation.
- The break and key-statement sections keep her the actor: she "claims that scale and wall on
  her own terms," and the "this was my answer" quote is framed as a painter's declaration of
  continuation, explicitly "Not a memorial to Pollock."

The death is present as real context (it is — and the brief requires it stated), never as the
engine. No change needed.

### 2b. Gender marginalization proportionate, not the entire reading — **PASS**
The marginalization is legitimate content and is allotted roughly its due: it gets the
"Mrs. Pollock" beat in `barn`, the "decorative = coded feminine" beat in `afterlife`, and the
gendered "who gets the big statement" framing in `break`. But the *looking* chapter (the
longest, the actual encounter with the canvas) is almost entirely formal — color, biomorphic
form, allover rhythm, the black drawing — so the gender story does not swallow the read. The
"break" is correctly defined as *two* things at once (who is allowed the statement AND what
it's about), not gender alone. Balance is good.

### 2c. Genius-myth — **PASS**
No lone-genius inflation. Krasner is shown as trained (Hofmann, National Academy), embedded
(federal projects, the AbEx circle), and technically grounded (the draftsman's black line
"is where Krasner's long training as a draftsman shows"). Achievement is attributed to
work and craft, not to mystique.

### 2d. First / only overclaim — **PASS, with one watch-item**
The draft is carefully hedged: "a handful of women in the first generation (Krasner among
the very few)," "almost entirely by men," "almost entirely a man's preserve." It never says
*first woman* or *only woman* to paint at mural scale — correct, since she was not (cf.
Mitchell, Frankenthaler, Hartigan working large in the same milieu). 

### NICE-3 [NICE] — "the largest work of her life to that point" — keep the qualifier everywhere
The const `hook` says "the largest work of her life to that point" and `making` blurb says
"the largest work of her life to that point" — good. But the `looking` prose says "the
largest thing she had ever attempted" and the hero/stat framing says "largest canvas of her
life." Krasner painted larger later (e.g. the *Eleven Ways…* / 1960s–70s big canvases), so
the unqualified "largest of her life" reads as a mild overclaim. Every instance should carry
"to that point" / "up to then." The draft mostly does; just make it uniform. This is the only
factual-proportion watch-item, and it's NICE (the draft already self-corrects with
"to that point" in the binding fields).

### NICE-4 [NICE] — Whitechapel attribution precision
`afterlife` §2 calls the 1965 Whitechapel show a "retrospective" that "put her work in front
of a serious European audience." Sources confirm it was her first retrospective and was
better received than her New York shows — accurate. Optional sharpening: it was notably
*better received in London than at home*, which is the pointed fact (the marginalization was
American-art-world-specific). Could tighten "on its own terms" to land that contrast, but the
current text is not wrong. NICE.
