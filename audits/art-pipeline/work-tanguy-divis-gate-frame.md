## 1. COMPREHENSIVENESS — essential facets

All five required facets are PRESENT and substantive:

- **Invented deep-space dreamworld** — PRESENT. The hook, `world` section, and `looking` carry it ("a single world and spent his life painting it"; high horizon, endless desert distance). Strong.
- **Self-taught entry via a de Chirico in a window (c. 1923)** — PRESENT and accurate. "self-taught," "Around 1923, riding past a Paris gallery, he saw a painting by Giorgio de Chirico ... in the shop window ... resolved on the spot to become a painter." Web-confirmed (Le Cerveau de l'enfant / The Child's Brain, in Paul Guillaume's window on the Rue de la Boétie, spotted from a bus). Draft's looser "riding past" is safe and correct.
- **Meticulous illusionistic rendering of non-existent objects** — PRESENT, and is the spine of `making` + `break` + `looking`. The strongest part of the read.
- **American period after 1939 / Kay Sage** — PRESENT. 1939 emigration, 1940 Reno marriage to Kay Sage, Woodbury CT, naturalization, "American chapter" framing. Complete.
- **The influence** — PRESENT (`break` close + `afterlife`): template for illusionistic Surrealism; the Tanguy/Dalí/Magritte verist line vs. the Miró/Masson automatist line; feeding fantastic/sci-fi/biomorphic imagery.

### [NICE] One genuine facet thinness: the bridge to Abstract Expressionism
Web sources repeatedly frame Tanguy's American period as a conduit that helped seed American Abstract Expressionism (his emigration "helped spread surrealist ideas to the United States, influencing American abstract expressionism"; "a bridge between Surrealism and Abstract Expressionism"). The draft's "afterlife" reaches for sci-fi/fantastic imagery and biomorphic abstraction but skips the most art-historically load-bearing downstream link — the American avant-garde he physically landed among in 1939. Not a MAJOR missing facet (the draft's influence claims stand on their own and are correctly hedged), so [NICE], not [FIX].
- **Suggested optional add** (to `TanAfterlife`, after the sci-fi sentence): "His arrival in New York in 1939 also put a working Surrealist on American soil at the moment a younger generation was forming, and his biomorphic invented forms are one of the currents that fed into the abstraction that came after him here." (Hedged: "one of the currents," not "father of.")

### [NICE] "Title meaning" goes unexplained
The work is called *Indefinite Divisibility* and the read never touches why — what "indefinite divisibility" might evoke (the sense that the forms could be cut down forever and never resolve into a nameable thing). The fact pack doesn't supply a sourced gloss, so do NOT invent an authorial intention. But a single descriptive (non-attributed) sentence connecting the title to the never-resolving forms would close a small comprehension gap. [NICE] only because the fact pack gives no sourced reading — keep any addition descriptive, never "Tanguy meant."

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### 2a. Invented forms stay INVENTED — PASS, well done
Every instance of "totem / machine / marble-run" is correctly framed as a viewer analogy, not an identification. Audited all occurrences:
- `annotations[0]`: "reads, to different viewers, like a totem ... and those are viewers' analogies, not the thing itself ... the honest description is what it does, not what it is." Exemplary.
- `TanMaking`: "the eye keeps reaching for a word ... and the word keeps not fitting"; "those are analogies, not identifications, and Tanguy would have refused all of them." Correct.
- `TanLooking`: "every one of those is a viewer's analogy laid over the form, not the form's identity." Correct.
- The AKG non-referential line ("Without employing direct references to the real world ... strange, indeterminate forms") is quoted and load-bearing. Correct.

No instance hardens into fact. This is the gate's central risk and the draft clears it.

#### [FIX] One soft spot: "biomorphic" in the lineage children
`lineage.children` lists **"Biomorphic abstraction"** as a child, and `afterlife` prose says "biomorphic shapes that look organic and are nothing." The word "biomorphic" (= life-/organ-shaped) is fine as a stylistic family label and is well-sourced as a descriptor of Tanguy's forms. But inside a read whose whole discipline is "these forms refer to NOTHING," asserting the forms are "biomorphic" leans, very slightly, toward the organic identification the read otherwise refuses. The prose handles it ("look organic and are nothing"); the bare lineage chip "Biomorphic abstraction" does not carry that hedge.
- **Recommend:** keep the chip (it's a real downstream art-historical lineage and is sourced), but this is a [FIX] only if a reviewer wants strict consistency. Lowest-friction option: leave as-is — "biomorphic abstraction" is a named movement (Miró/Arp/Tanguy), so the chip names a movement, not a claim about what *Indefinite Divisibility* depicts. **Resolution: downgrade to [NICE] / accept as-is.** Flagging for visibility only.

### 2b. Genius-myth — PASS
Tanguy is firmly situated: "He fell in with André Breton and the Surrealist circle"; "became one of the purest practitioners of the second kind"; placed explicitly against the Dalí/Magritte and Miró/Masson lines. He is not an inexplicable lone visionary — his debt to de Chirico is named four times and treated as formative, and his method is described as a practice, not a miracle. No fix needed.

#### [FIX] "his turn to painting was less a decision than a jolt" + "thunderbolt"-style framing
`TanWorld`: "his turn to painting was less a decision than a jolt ... resolved on the spot ... despite never having trained as one." This is accurate to the sources (it genuinely was a sudden conversion), but the stacking of "jolt," "on the spot," and "stayed in his work for good" edges toward the romantic instant-genius origin myth the gate guards against. It's mild and factually grounded, so [FIX] not BLOCKER.
- **Suggested softening** (optional): change "less a decision than a jolt" to "sudden — by his own account." Keeping "by his own account" (already used later) does the de-mythologizing work: it marks the conversion story as Tanguy's self-narration rather than gate-asserted fact. The draft already does this once ("By his own account he resolved on the spot"); ensure that hedge sits on the FIRST mention too, not only the second.

### 2c. First / only overclaim + proportion

#### [FIX] "the template for illusionistic Surrealism" — definite article overclaims
Both `break` ("set the pattern for illusionistic dream-painting") and `afterlife` ("became **the** template for illusionistic Surrealism") and the hook-adjacent prose use the definite singular. Dalí is at least as central to illusionistic/verist Surrealism in the public mind, and the draft itself names "the Tanguy / Dalí / Magritte line" — i.e., Tanguy is one of three, not the sole template-setter. "**The** template" risks a first/only overclaim.
- **Suggested edit** (`afterlife`): "became **a** template for illusionistic Surrealism" OR "became **one of the** templates for illusionistic Surrealism." Same fix in the `world`-section `sections[]` blurb for `afterlife` ("becomes a template" — already correctly indefinite there; make the prose match the blurb).
- The "cliché because Tanguy made it convincing first" line is defensible and well-hedged (it's about the *specific* invented-object-on-empty-plain image, which is genuinely his signature) — **keep it**. The overclaim is only in "*the* template," where Dalí/Magritte share the credit the draft elsewhere acknowledges.

#### [NICE] "everyone has seen since" / "a century of fantastic and science-fiction imagery"
`afterlife` title "The plain that everyone has seen since" and "a century of ... science-fiction imagery." Rousing and broadly true, but "everyone" and the sweeping sci-fi-lineage claim are unsourced reach. The fact pack supports the *direction* (fed into fantastic/sci-fi/biomorphic imagery) but not the scale ("everyone," "a century"). Proportion is fine for a closing flourish; flagged [NICE] in case a stricter reviewer wants "much of the fantastic and science-fiction imagery that came after" instead of "a century of."
