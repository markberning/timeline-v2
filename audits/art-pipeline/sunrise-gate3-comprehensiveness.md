## How I built the checklist

An exhaustive-but-popular work read on *Impression, Sunrise* must answer every
question a curious reader walking out of the Musée Marmottan would want settled.
I derived the checklist from: (a) the candidate essentials in the brief, (b) the
fact pack's own §7 "context the work read needs" and §10 falsehoods-to-refuse
list, (c) what the fact pack explicitly flags as must-state (documented vs. legend
tags), and (d) my own sense of what a reader who knows nothing would be left
wondering. Movement-level material (the full roster of eight exhibitions, the
whole cast biography, Impressionism's spread to America and Japan, etc.) belongs
one level up — I do not flag terse deferral as missing.

## MUST-ADD items (blocking — reader left misinformed or materially short-changed)

### M1 — Medium: oil on canvas (MISSING from entire draft)
- **Why blocking:** Medium is a basic stat-block fact for any work read. The reader is never told what they're looking at materially. "Oil on canvas" also matters because the wet-into-wet handling described in the-morning only makes sense once you know it's oil paint on fabric.
- **Where it goes:** Opening of `le-havre` (the dimension sentence) or the Look Closer intro. One phrase: "Oil on canvas, about 1 ft 7 in tall and 2 ft 1 in wide."
- **Verified fact:** MARMOTTAN notice 4014 — Medium: Oil on canvas. Standard across all sources.

---

### M2 — The "72" inscription was added AFTER THE FACT (MISSING)
- **Why blocking:** The draft introduces Olson's forensic date (7:35 a.m., Nov 13, 1872) and says it "settled a much older fight" without telling the reader what the fight was. Without knowing that the "72" date was appended post-painting (per the Marmottan's own catalogue record), the reader cannot understand why the date was ever in dispute. This leaves Olson's detective work floating without an anchor. Worse, a reader who later encounters the Wildenstein/1873 argument will feel the draft withheld something material.
- **Where it goes:** The Olson paragraph in `the-morning`, immediately after the 7:35 a.m. introduction. One to two sentences: Monet himself signed the canvas and later added the date "72" — but that inscription was appended after the fact, per the Marmottan's own catalogue record, which is why scholars have debated whether the painting actually dates from 1872, 1873, or even early 1874. Olson's astronomy resolved it in favor of 1872.
- **Verified facts:** MARMOTTAN notice 4014 ("Signed lower left: Claude Monet and later dated: 72"); Wildenstein dissenting view documented in WP-FR; Olson 2014 (SPACE.COM, PHYS.ORG) supports 1872.

---

### M3 — Monet showed SEVERAL works at the 1874 exhibition (MISSING)
- **Why blocking:** As the draft stands, a reader will naturally infer that *Impression, Sunrise* was Monet's one entry — the picture he staked his reputation on. In fact the fact pack lists boulevard des Capucines, a Déjeuner, and other Le Havre/Argenteuil canvases, with *Impression* as catalogue No. 98 among many. The myth that this single sketch "was" the exhibition — and therefore the sole cause of the Impressionism label — is a common, distorting misconception. The fact pack (§7) explicitly flags it as something to deflate. Omitting it leaves the reader with a slightly false picture of what Leroy was actually looking at.
- **Where it goes:** `the-name` chapter, in the exhibition setup paragraph (where the group hung their show). One sentence after "over its run a few thousand people came through": Monet hung several canvases in the show — including a street scene of the boulevard just outside the door, a luncheon, and other coastal works — so *Impression, Sunrise* (No. 98) was one of his entries, not his lone bet.
- **Verified facts:** Fact pack §7 explicitly lists *Boulevard des Capucines*, a *Déjeuner*, and Le Havre/Argenteuil canvases. WP-FR and WIKISOURCE corroborate multiple Monet entries.

## Summary verdict

**MUST-ADD count: 3**

**Top 5 findings (in descending importance):**

1. **M2 — "72" inscription added after the fact (MISSING).** This is the most consequential gap: it is the fact that makes the Olson dating story coherent, and without it the "settled a much older fight" line hangs in mid-air. Every reader who wonders "why was the date disputed?" has no answer.

2. **M1 — Medium: oil on canvas (MISSING).** The most basic stat-block omission. Affects the wet-into-wet description and reader orientation. A single phrase fixes it.

3. **M3 — Monet showed several works at the 1874 exhibition (MISSING).** Lets a common myth stand unchallenged. The fact pack explicitly flags this as something to deflate; skipping it was an oversight.

4. **S3 — Wildenstein 1873/74 minority dating not named (THIN).** The draft says Olson "settled a debate" without saying what the debate was. Stacking S3 with M2 (the inscription) addresses this fully.

5. **S2 — Baudelaire/Manet modern-life context absent (THIN).** The industrial-subject break in `the-break` is the right place to land a short allusion. Not blocking but the framing is richer with it.
