## ⚠️ TWO HARD CORRECTIONS TO THE BRIEF — read first

1. **The collection is GELMAN, not Whitney.** The brief floated "Gift of Mr. and Mrs.
   John Hay Whitney." That is WRONG for this object. The Met's *The Seine at Chatou*
   (object 490034, acc. **1999.363.84**) is **"Jacques and Natasha Gelman Collection,
   1998."** The Whitneys did own *other* Vlaminck Chatou-period pictures (see "Title
   disambiguation" below), but not this one. Source: Met collection API + page (below).

2. **THIS WORK IS NOT PUBLIC DOMAIN — it is NOT inlineable.** The brief assumed
   "1906 → PD-US, inlineable." That reasoning fails here. US copyright restoration and
   the EU life+70 term run off the **artist's death year, 1958**, not the 1906 creation
   date. The Met flags the object **`isPublicDomain: false`** with the rights notice
   **"© 2026 Artists Rights Society (ARS), New York."** Vlaminck's work is in copyright
   until **2029** (1958 + 70, EU) and is NOT Open Access at the Met. **→ This work must
   ship as a `RestrictedFigure` (degraded), NEVER inlined.** A free PD substitute is
   *not* available for Vlaminck Fauve work for the same reason. This is the art analog
   of the Chair Caning problem, but worse: we can't even self-host, because it isn't PD.
   Image & rights gate (gate 6) MUST enforce this. (Source: Met API `isPublicDomain`
   field + ARS rights line.)

### Title disambiguation (so the author doesn't conflate works)
Vlaminck painted Chatou and the Seine *repeatedly* in 1905–06. Several near-identically
named canvases exist and are EASILY confused — do not mix their facts:
- **THIS WORK** — *The Seine at Chatou*, 1906, oil, 81.6 × 101 cm, **Met, Gelman Coll.**,
  acc. 1999.363.84. Shows a **tugboat (remorqueur) + sailboats**, white houses, a
  riverbank. (object 490034)
- *Boats on the Seine at Chatou* — also at the Met (object 459169), a **different**
  canvas. [do not borrow its data]
- *Sails at Chatou* — also at the Met (object 459168). [different work]
- *Tugboat on the Seine, Chatou* — **National Gallery of Art, Washington** (not the Met).
- *The River Seine at Chatou*, 1906 — the title many web pages (WikiArt, Artchive) attach
  to a Vlaminck Chatou view; treat "The River Seine at Chatou" as a **commonly used
  alternate title for our Met canvas** but cite the Met's official "The Seine at Chatou."

## 2. The hook (1–2 sentences, author may rework)
A self-taught ex-bicycle-racer who said van Gogh meant more to him than his own father,
squeezing cobalt and vermilion straight from the tube onto a quiet bend of the Seine just
outside Paris — and turning a Sunday-painters' river town into one of the loudest
landscapes of the Fauve year. *(All elements documented below; "loudest" is house-voice
colour, not a claim.)*

## 3. Narrative beats (~5) — each tied to sourced facts

### Beat 1 — Chatou, the riverside town just outside Paris [DOCUMENTED]
Chatou is a town on the **Seine** about 9 miles (≈14 km) west of central Paris, a
Sunday-leisure spot for Parisians (the same Seine-island-and-boating world the
Impressionists painted a generation earlier — Renoir's *Luncheon of the Boating Party*
was painted at the Maison Fournaise on the Île de Chatou). Vlaminck lived in the area and
**spent the summer of 1906 painting in and around Chatou** (Met). The Seine, its
tugboats, sailboats, and riverbank houses are his recurring 1905–06 subject.

### Beat 2 — Vlaminck + Derain: the "School of Chatou" [DOCUMENTED]
- Vlaminck **met André Derain in 1900** after a **minor train/railway accident** (their
  train derailed; accounts say near Chatou). They became close, and **shared a studio at
  Chatou** (c. 1900–01). Britannica/Wikipedia/TheArtStory all carry the train-meeting.
  [DOCUMENTED — the "train accident" meeting is consistently reported by Britannica,
  Wikipedia, TheArtStory; treat as the standard account, fine to state plainly.]
- The two are jointly called the **"School of Chatou"** (*l'École de Chatou*) — an
  informal label for a two-man precursor to Fauvism, painting the Seine riverbanks in
  bold, anti-naturalistic colour. [DOCUMENTED as a commonly used art-historical label;
  it was a nickname, never a formal school — frame it that way.]
- Both exhibited at the **1905 Salon d'Automne**, where critic **Louis Vauxcelles**
  called the room of bright-colour painters **"fauves" ("wild beasts")** — the naming of
  Fauvism. [DOCUMENTED.] (NB: Vauxcelles' exact remark concerned a Renaissance-style
  sculpture amid the canvases — "Donatello chez les fauves." That detail belongs to the
  Fauvism movement read; here just name Vauxcelles + "wild beasts.")

### Beat 3 — The self-taught, anti-academic persona + pure tube colour [DOCUMENTED + one LEGEND-frame quote]
- **Self-taught / anti-academic:** Vlaminck took drawing lessons and studied briefly with
  a local painter (Henri Rigalon, 1893) but **proudly shunned formal academic training**
  and cultivated an outsider, "untaught" persona. Before painting he earned a living as a
  **professional racing cyclist** (career ended by typhoid c. 1896) and as a **violinist /
  music teacher** playing in bands. [DOCUMENTED — Britannica, Wikipedia, TheArtStory.]
- **Pure colour straight from the tube:** the Met's own catalogue says of THIS painting
  that Vlaminck "**applied [the colors] directly from the tube in daubs and swirls of
  pigment**." [DOCUMENTED — primary museum source; this is the strongest, safest way to
  state the tube-colour idea, because it is the Met describing this exact canvas.]
- **The famous quote — VERIFY-AND-FRAME:** *"I wanted to burn down the École des
  Beaux-Arts with my cobalts and vermilions, and I wanted to express my feelings with my
  brushes without troubling [about] what painting was like before me."*
  - Status: **[DISPUTED wording / [LEGEND]-tier sourcing].** The quote is real and
    widely attributed to Vlaminck, but the wording drifts between sources and **no
    primary citation pins it.** Wikiquote lists it via a secondary source (Sue Roe,
    *In Montmartre / Picasso, Matisse and Modernism in Paris 1900–1910*, 2015), not a
    Vlaminck primary text. Other versions trace to MoMA's *Les Fauves* (1952) and to
    Vlaminck's own memoir *Tournant dangereux* (*Dangerous Corner*, 1929). **It is NOT a
    documented verbatim quotation from a single confirmed source.**
  - **Author rule:** if used, **frame it** ("Vlaminck is famous for saying he wanted to…",
    "in a line endlessly quoted of him") and DO NOT present it as a clean sourced
    quotation, and DO NOT pin it to a specific date. A paraphrase is safer than quotation
    marks. (Per Impressionism-run precedent: an unverifiable quote ships as paraphrase.)

### Beat 4 — The van Gogh retrospective that hit him hard [DOCUMENTED date; LEGEND-frame the line]
- The **first major van Gogh retrospective opened at the Bernheim-Jeune gallery, Paris,
  in March 1901** (≈71 works; organized by the Bernheims with critic Julien Leclercq).
  Vlaminck (and Derain, and Matisse, who Derain introduced to Vlaminck there) saw it; van
  Gogh's expressive colour and brushwork shaped Vlaminck's direction. [DOCUMENTED — the
  1901 Bernheim-Jeune retrospective is solid; History.com, Artnet, Wikipedia
  (Bernheim-Jeune).]
  - **Date caution:** "1901 Bernheim-Jeune" is the verified fact. Some secondary sources
    loosely say "1901 van Gogh show"; the exact day (15 vs 17 March) varies — **do not
    cite a specific day.** The Met's caption for THIS painting independently confirms
    Vlaminck "emulated the undisguised brushwork and intuitive application of paint of
    **van Gogh's late, expressive style, which he so admired**." [DOCUMENTED — Met.]
- **The famous line:** Vlaminck is widely quoted as saying he **"loved van Gogh that day
  better than my own father."** Status: **[LEGEND-tier sourcing]** — repeated everywhere
  (Wikipedia, TheArtStory) but without a clean primary citation. **Frame it as reported**
  ("he later said…", "by his own account"), never asserted flat.

### Beat 5 — High-key Fauve landscape → his later turn AGAINST Fauvism [DOCUMENTED]
- **The Fauve year:** 1905–07 is the peak of Vlaminck's high-key, pure-colour landscapes;
  Chatou pictures are his signature output. In **1906 the dealer Ambroise Vollard bought
  out Vlaminck's studio stock** (and an option on future work), freeing him to paint
  full-time — the commercial backdrop to the 1906 Chatou pictures. [DOCUMENTED — multiple
  sources; the "6,000 francs for the whole studio, Sept 1905 → completed early 1906"
  figure is reported but treat the *amount* as reported-not-carved.]
- **The turn against Fauvism:** from around **1907–08 Vlaminck's palette darkened**, moving
  toward **Cézanne's influence** (a Cézanne retrospective followed Cézanne's 1906 death),
  and he largely abandoned the Fauve high key. [DOCUMENTED — Britannica, Wikipedia,
  TheArtStory.]
- Later he became **hostile to modernism and to Cubism / Picasso specifically**, blaming
  Picasso for dragging French painting "into a wretched dead end." (His 1942 attack on
  Picasso appeared in the collaborationist paper *Comœdia* — a fact with an ugly wartime
  context; the framing critic should weigh whether/how to include it; not needed for the
  core work read.) [DOCUMENTED that he attacked Picasso/Cubism; the *Comœdia* 1942
  context is real and sensitive.]

## 4. Six "Look closer" annotations (PROSE pointers — verify against the image)
The Met's own curatorial text is the anchor for what is literally on the canvas:
*"Combining the primary colors of blue and red with white, Vlaminck applied them directly
from the tube in daubs and swirls of pigment, employing these conventional hues for the
white houses, green leaves, reddish-orange tree trunks, and the blue, red, and white
trawler in the background."* and *"shows both a remorqueur, or tugboat, and sailboats."*
All six below are drawn from that + the visible composition. **Gate 1 must re-check each
`where` against the actual painting image; do NOT ship blind coordinates (prose pointers
only).**

1. **The red/orange riverbank & tree trunks** — `where`: the bank and the trees rising
   from it. The **tree trunks are reddish-orange**, a non-naturalistic colour chosen for
   force, not accuracy. [DOCUMENTED — Met names "reddish-orange tree trunks."]
2. **The broken impasto strokes** — `where`: across the whole surface. Paint laid in
   **"daubs and swirls"** straight from the tube, in thick separate touches (impasto =
   paint heaped so the brush leaves ridges). The van-Gogh-style touch. [DOCUMENTED — Met.]
3. **The boats — tugboat + sailboats** — `where`: on the water / mid-distance. A
   **remorqueur (tugboat)** plus **sailboats**; the Met singles out a **blue, red and
   white "trawler"/boat in the background**. [DOCUMENTED — Met; note the Met uses both
   "trawler" and "tugboat/remorqueur," so describe simply as river craft.]
4. **The reflections on the water** — `where`: the Seine's surface below the boats and
   bank. The river is built from the same broken coloured strokes as the bank, so the
   reflections read as bands of pure colour, not mirror-smooth water. [VISIBLE on the
   canvas — gate 1 confirm against image; describe what's there, no invented detail.]
5. **The high-key sky** — `where`: upper register. A bright, light-keyed sky (Fauve
   "high key" = colours kept near full intensity, not darkened/greyed). [VISIBLE — gate 1
   confirm tone against image; do not assert a specific colour without looking.]
6. **The tube-colour intensity / white houses + green leaves** — `where`: the cluster of
   **white houses** on the bank and the **green leaves**. The Met names these as places
   where conventional local colour is laid on raw and bright; the whole canvas is a demo
   of pigment used at near-full saturation. [DOCUMENTED — Met names "white houses, green
   leaves."]

*(Author: keep to six; each `where` is a written location phrase, verified by looking at
the painting, never an x/y crop. Per `feedback_no_blind_image_coordinates`.)*

## 6. Key figures / cast nameplates
- **Maurice de Vlaminck** (1876–1958) — the painter; self-taught, ex-cyclist & violinist,
  Fauve, "School of Chatou." [DOCUMENTED]
- **André Derain** (1880–1954) — co-founder of the "School of Chatou," met Vlaminck in the
  1900 train accident, shared the Chatou studio; later major Fauve. [DOCUMENTED]
