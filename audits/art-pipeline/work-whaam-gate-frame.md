## COMPREHENSIVENESS

### [FIX] C1 — The single-source ("by Irv Novick") framing overstates a contested point, and that overstatement leans ON the fairness axis.

The draft repeatedly presents the painting as built on *one* named panel: "a single
panel... drawn by Irv Novick," "Lichtenstein built the painting on someone else's
drawing, Irv Novick's panel." That is the **standard primary attribution** and is fine
as the headline — Wikipedia leads with Novick's "Star Jockey" #89 as the foundational
source. But the scholarly record (Paul Gravett, repeated on Wikipedia) holds that
*Whaam!* is likely a **composite**: the overall layout/composition from Novick's
"Star Jockey" (#89), but the **attacking plane plausibly from Jerry Grandenetti's
"Wingmate of Doom" (#90)** and the **target/exploding plane from a Russ Heath drawing**
in "Aces Wild" (#89). The draft already names Heath, Grandenetti, Abruzzo, and Kirby —
but frames them as artists Lichtenstein drew from *across his career generally* ("the
comic artists Lichtenstein drew from also included..."), not as possible co-sources of
*this* painting.

Why it matters for THIS gate: the credit argument the draft makes its moral centerpiece
("keep Novick's name in the room") is slightly *under*-stated if Heath and Grandenetti
may also be uncredited in this very picture. Naming Novick as the sole owed party, when
two more illustrators may be embedded in the same canvas, narrows the fairness frame.
This is a hypothesis, not settled fact, so it should be flagged as such — not asserted.

Suggested text — add one sentence to the WhamSource "source" chapter (after the
sentence introducing Novick / "Star Jockey" #89), or fold into the afterlife "credit
question" paragraph:

> Even the source is not quite a single panel: scholars (notably Paul Gravett) read
> *Whaam!* as a composite, with the firing plane likely drawn from Jerry Grandenetti's
> "Wingmate of Doom" in the next issue and the exploding plane from a Russ Heath drawing,
> all stitched onto Novick's layout. Which only widens the credit question — there may be
> more than one uncredited hand in the picture.

If kept tighter, at minimum change the afterlife line "the comic artists Lichtenstein
drew from **also** included Russ Heath... and Jack Kirby" so it does not read as purely a
career-wide aside — e.g. "and some scholars place Heath's and Grandenetti's hands in
*Whaam!* itself."

### [NICE] C2 — "Influence" facet is present but thin relative to the others.

The afterlife chapter establishes recognition ("one of the most recognized images in all
of modern art") and the credit debate well, but the *influence* facet — what *Whaam!* /
Lichtenstein opened up downstream — is carried almost entirely by the `lineage.children`
chips (Pop Art, Appropriation art, the image-as-readymade). The prose names no successor
practice. This is acceptable (the lineage block covers it structurally), but one clause
in the closing paragraph naming what it licensed (appropriation art, the gallery's
acceptance of the mass-media image as legitimate raw material) would round out the
"influence" requirement in prose, not just in chips. Optional.

## FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### The credit / uncredited-artist question — handled HONESTLY. ✅ (gate's central ask)

The afterlife "credit question" chapter is the strongest part of the draft on this axis
and meets the gate's bar precisely:
- States the fact straight: "he credited the genre, sometimes the comic, but **not the
  artist**. He sought no permission, named no illustrator, and paid no royalties."
- Names the critics without ventriloquizing outrage: Dave Gibbons's "copycat" and the
  *"after Irv Novick"* reformulation are quoted accurately (verified against CBR / BBC
  Four coverage).
- Gives Lichtenstein's **actual defense** its due weight (transformation = new purpose =
  a comment on the image, not the image) AND concedes the real truth in it.
- Lands on genuine proportion: *"both things are true at once... You can admire the break
  and still keep Novick's name in the room."* This is the model answer — neither
  reflexive condemnation nor reflexive excuse.

### "It's just a copy" vs "it's pure genius" — both resisted. ✅

- **Against "just a copy":** the WhamMaking chapter exists precisely to itemize what
  Lichtenstein *changed* (split, recompose, flatten, screen-paint the dots). "Did not
  simply enlarge... like a photocopy. He rebuilt it." Good.
- **Against "pure genius":** the credit chapter refuses the lone-genius myth by keeping
  the working illustrators in frame and admitting the work is "built on the uncredited
  labor of working illustrators who never shared in the fame or the money."
- The "what he actually changed" requirement is satisfied concretely, not by assertion.

### [FIX] F1 — "one of the most recognized images in all of modern art" is a defensible claim but appears THREE times; trim to avoid drumbeat / mild overclaim.

The phrase or a close variant recurs: const blurb ("one of the most recognized images in
modern art"), provenance note ("one of the most recognized works of Pop Art"), and twice
in the afterlife prose ("one of the most recognized images in all of modern art").
Sourcing supports a strong claim — Wikipedia calls it "either Lichtenstein's most famous
work or one of his two most famous works" (alongside *Drowning Girl*) and a culmination
of Pop. But "**all of modern art**" is a notch broader than the sources say (they scope
it to Lichtenstein / Pop), and stating it 3–4 times reads as a marketing drumbeat rather
than a fact. This brushes the "first/only/most overclaim" axis.

Suggested: keep ONE strong statement, scoped as the sources scope it. In the afterlife
prose, change "one of the most recognized images in all of modern art" to:

> one of the most famous works of Pop Art — frequently named, with *Drowning Girl*, as
> Lichtenstein's signature image.

and let the const blurb's softer "one of the most recognized images in modern art" stand
as the single headline. Drop the duplicate in the provenance T00897 note (or shorten to
"now at Tate Modern, on view").

### [NICE] F2 — No genius-myth problem, but one phrase tips toward art-historical
sermon: "that is the work's whole achievement" / "the Pop break in a single image."

These are fine as written and earn their keep, but two of them stacked near the close
("a thirteen-foot deadpan painting... is plainly not a war comic, and that is the work's
whole achievement") slightly inflate. Optional softening; not a fairness violation, just
proportion housekeeping. Leave if you prefer the punch.

### [NICE] F3 — Tate-omitted-Novick detail would strengthen the credit chapter (and is verified).

A documented, on-point fact the draft does not use: when *Whaam!* hangs at Tate, **Novick's
name is omitted from the work's wall description** — the credit gap is not just historical
(1963) but ongoing at the institution that owns it. This is verifiable (multiple secondary
sources; cf. the MoMA/museum-credit coverage) and would sharpen "the artists who were
never named" from a one-time omission into a standing one. Optional add, cite as
secondary. Do not assert specific current wall-label wording as fact without a Tate
source; frame as "has been noted."

### Framing — items confirmed clean:
- **Genius-myth:** resisted (working illustrators kept in frame; defense given, not
  worshipped). ✅
- **First/only overclaim:** none on "first Pop work" or similar — the break is framed as
  *a* crossing of the high/low line, not THE first ever. The only overclaim is the
  "most recognized" repetition (F1). ✅ after F1.
- **Proportion:** the closing both-things-are-true paragraph is exemplary. ✅
- **Plane model:** correctly flagged unsettled (F-86 vs P-51), not asserted — good fairness
  on a contested detail; mirror that same treatment for the composite-source point (C1). ✅
- **Quotes:** the Coplans "programmed / hide the record of my hand" key statement is
  correctly attributed (Pasadena 1967 catalogue, Coplans) and verified. The "worst
  aspects of commercial art" line is correctly used as **paraphrase**, not a verbatim
  blockquote — matching the fact pack's caution that its exact source is loose. ✅
