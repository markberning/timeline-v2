## FINDINGS

### [FIX-1] McHale's contribution is *disputed*, not merely a clean "supplied / composed" split
**Where:** PART A legend ("John McHale supplied the American source images / a rough layout; Hamilton selected, cut, and composed it"); JwiCutting prose; annotations.
**Issue:** Current English Wikipedia frames McHale's role as **actively disputed** — McHale's son claims his father provided "the original measured design and iconic material," while Hamilton countered that McHale supplied only "a rough layout for six pages," of which Hamilton used two. The draft's "two hands, fairly told" framing is *defensible and fair*, but it presents the division of labor as settled fact when the strongest single source flags it as contested. Not a BLOCKER (the draft does not overstate McHale OR erase him), but the certainty is slightly higher than the record.
**Suggested text (JwiCutting, append to the McHale paragraph):** after "the arrangement is entirely Hamilton's." add: *(Just how much McHale shaped it is actually disputed — his family later claimed a larger hand in the design, which Hamilton played down — but no one contests that the cutting and final composition were Hamilton's.)*
**Severity rationale:** precision/honesty, not error. Optional but recommended given the gate covers fair credit.

### [FIX-2] Alloway date "around 1958" — pin to the published first use (Feb 1958), and note IG usage predates print
**Where:** JwiAfterlife ("used it in print around 1958").
**Issue:** Correct, just soft. The first *published* use is Lawrence Alloway, "The Arts and the Mass Media," *Architectural Design*, **February 1958**. "Around 1958" is accurate; if tightening is wanted, "in print in 1958" is exact. Also: the *term* circulated in Independent Group talk earlier (mid-1950s), and its very origin is itself contested (McHale's son dates a "Pop Art" coinage to 1954). The draft's "usually credited to Alloway … and the wider Independent Group circle" already absorbs this correctly. No change required; tighten only if desired.
**Severity rationale:** the claim is true as written; this is a sharpening note.
