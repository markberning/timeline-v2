- **BMA accession 1950.228 / Cone Collection / bequest of Etta Cone** — the BMA object

- **1913 burning — copies vs effigy** — CONFIRMED the draft's precise version.
  artnet + Art Institute archive + Chicago Magazine: students *burned reproductions/copies*
  of three Matisse works (Blue Nude, *Le Luxe II*, *Goldfish and Sculpture*); the plan to

  injunction before it was lit. April 16, 1913, last day, south portico. Mock trial of
  "Henry Hairmattress," charges + "only one life to give" all sourced. The original canvas
  was never harmed. Draft nails all of this.
  > NOTE: Wikipedia's own one-line summary loosely says it "was burned in effigy by
  > students" — that is the imprecise version the draft correctly REFUSES. Do not let a
  > later editor "simplify" the draft back toward Wikipedia's wording.
- **Provenance chain** — Steins (1907–20) → Leo Stein sold to John Quinn 1920 → Quinn

- **Price (120,760 FF ≈ $4,830)** — could not independently re-confirm the exact number

- **1906 Biskra trip (~two weeks, Algeria/oasis)** — confirmed. Draft correct, including

- **Sculpture broke → painting; later finished as bronze *Reclining Nude I (Aurore)*** —

- **Picasso/*Demoiselles* influence** — confirmed Wikipedia: the 1907 painting "had a

## [FIX]

**FIX-1 — "around the show's last day, April 16, 1913."**
Exact draft text (afterlife chapter, "Chicago" section):
> "On **April 16, 1913**, around the show's last day, Art Institute students staged a protest…"
and the sub-head "**Chicago · April 16, 1913**".

Issue: minor over-hedge / slight imprecision. Sources (Art Institute archive "The Finale";
artnet) treat **April 16, 1913 as the actual last day** of the Chicago run, not merely
"around" it — the burlesque happened *at the closing*. "around the show's last day" reads as
if the date is fuzzy when it isn't.
Correction: drop "around" — e.g. "On **April 16, 1913**, the show's last day in Chicago,
Art Institute students staged a protest…" (Optional; not wrong as written, just looser than
the sourcing warrants.)
Source: archive.artic.edu/armoryshow/finale; artnet "Art Bites" (Apr 16, 1913, last day).

paraphrases and hedges the wording. If you ever want to firm it up, the line ran in
*Gil Blas*, March 20, 1907 — that's a citable venue/date you could add to the const note

**NICE-2 — "savage, brutal, unfinished" (salon chapter) is house-voice amplification.**

copy + Wikipedia). "brutal" is a reasonable synonym, not separately sourced. Fine as voice;
flagging only because the gate covers every adjective on a reception claim. No change needed.

**NICE-3 — Three burned works.** The draft says "copies of three Matisse paintings, the
*Blue Nude* among them" without naming the other two. That's the safe, correct move (the
other two are *Le Luxe II* and *Goldfish and Sculpture*, 1912 — both confirmed). Naming them
is optional and adds nothing the reader needs. Leave as is.

**NICE-4 — "single most important American backer" (Quinn).** Sourced as a fair
characterization (artnet/NYPL call Quinn the Armory Show's most important collector-patron).
Superlative is defensible; if a critic objects, soften to "one of the most important." Low risk.
