- **[FIX] — `WmnLooking`, opening: "Stand in front of it."** This is a reader-command / second-person staging device. It is softer than a banned "Notice how…" but it is still instructing the reader's body. Same pattern recurs: "Go to the **face**," "Now the **body**," "Then look at **how it is painted**." The voice contract flags reader-commands. The "Go to the face / Now the body / Then look at the surface" tour-guide cadence is the looking section's main voice risk. Suggested rewrite: convert to declarative pointers — e.g. "The painting is portrait orientation…" (drop "Stand in front of it"); "The face is where everyone goes" → keep (it is observation, not a command); "Now the body. It is massive…" → "The body is massive…"; "Then look at how it is painted, because…" → "How it is painted is where *Woman I* stops being a picture of a woman…". This keeps all the concreteness and loses the imperative drumbeat.

- **[NICE] — `WmnLooking`, "the face is where everyone goes."** Borderline mind-reading-the-viewer ("everyone"). Reads as house-voice observation rather than a command, so acceptable; flag only if the FIX above is applied and you want full consistency — then "The face is the first thing anyone reads" is cleaner.

## 3. CLARITY + VOICE CONTRACT

**No BLOCKERs. Jargon is inline-defined on first use throughout:** New York School ("the loose group of postwar American painters…"), color-field ("floating great soft rectangles… what later got called *color-field*"), non-objective ("meaning it depicted no thing, no person, no scene"), stretcher ("the wooden frame a canvas is tacked onto"), pure abstraction established before use. Dates-of-life given for de Kooning, Schapiro. Good.

**No honesty-labels of the banned kind.** The "treat it as the honest shape of it," "the honest word is simply 'many'," "it deserves to be told carefully" lines are about *how the fact is sourced* (the fact-handling the factpack demanded be surfaced), not "I'll be honest with you" reader-flattery — but see FIX below, the density of "honest" is a tic worth trimming.

- **[FIX] — "honest" tic (3×) + sourcing-throat-clearing.** `WmnMaking`: "Take that not as an audited tally but as the **honest** shape of it"; "the **honest** word is simply 'many'." `WmnAfterlife`: "the record of their decision is one of the great **honest** moments in museum history"; "the **honest** thing is to lay out the sides." Four "honest"s plus "it deserves to be told carefully, because it is the standard account rather than a verbatim-recorded scene" reads as the draft narrating its own epistemic caution. The factpack DID require the ~50 count, the Schapiro story, and the misogyny reading to be presented as account-not-gospel — so the *caution is correct content* — but it can be delivered without the word "honest" and without flagging the act of being careful. Suggested rewrites: "Take that not as an audited tally but as the shape of it"; "there's no reliable source for a number, so the word is simply 'many'"; "the committee left one of the great frank notes in museum history"; "the fair thing is to lay out both sides." Keep the substance (it is gated), drop the self-narration.

- **[FIX] — `WmnMaking`, Schapiro paragraph: "it deserves to be told carefully, because it is the **standard account** rather than a verbatim-recorded scene… so hold it as the well-attested story it is, not as a transcript."** This is the factpack's required "present as account not gospel," but the delivery is meta ("it deserves to be told carefully," "hold it as… not as a transcript" is a reader-instruction). The hedge can be folded into the telling instead of announced. Suggested: tell the visit plainly, then a single in-voice caveat: "How much the visit actually changed his mind has been argued ever since; the bones — he gave it up, Schapiro came by, he went back and finished it — are well documented." Drops "it deserves to be told carefully" and "hold it… not as a transcript."

- **[FIX] — `WmnLooking`, flesh paragraph: the parenthetical "flesh was the reason oil paint was invented" aside.** Factpack-correct in substance (attributed cautiously, not as primary/dated), and the em-dash usage here is inside *JSX text*, not a plain TS string, so `&mdash;` rendering is fine. But the parenthetical is long (a 45-word aside mid-paragraph) and itself does the throat-clearing the voice contract dislikes: "passed down without a fixed source or date, so treat it as a saying often credited to him rather than a documented quotation." "treat it as…" is a faint reader-instruction. Suggested tighten: "(A more famous line — 'flesh was the reason oil paint was invented' — floats around attached to him, though without a fixed source.)" Keeps the cautious attribution, loses the instruction and ~20 words.

**Em-dash / `&mdash;` audit (specifically: literal "—" in a rendered string; `&mdash;` misused inside a plain TS string field):**
- **[NICE] — verify at splice time.** All `&mdash;` and `&ndash;` occurrences I see are inside JSX text children (the chapter components), where the HTML entity is correct and required. The `WOMAN_I` const **string fields** (hook, blurbs, annotation `detail`/`where`, provenance `note`, stats) — these are plain TS strings, NOT JSX — and I see **no** literal "—" and **no** `&mdash;` inside any of them (they use commas/periods). Clean. Flagging only as a coordinator check: do not let any em-dash entity migrate into the const string fields during splice; in those fields a literal em-dash character (—) is the correct form, never `&mdash;`.
- The blurbs use `&rsquo;`/`&ndash;`-free plain text with straight apostrophes inside the const (e.g. `Pollock’s` uses a curly ’ directly, which is fine in a TS string). Consistent.

**Walls of text:** longest paragraph is `WmnFigure` ¶1 (~150 words) and `WmnLooking` grin paragraph (~150 words). Both are within reading-app tolerance and well-punctuated; not flagged as walls.
