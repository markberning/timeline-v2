# GATE READ — STORYTELLING + LOOKING + CLARITY/VOICE — *L.H.O.O.Q.* (Duchamp, 1919)

Gate: storytelling (primary) · looking · clarity + voice contract.
Reviewed: `work-lhooq-draft.md` against `work-lhooq-factpack.md`. No edits made.

## VERDICT: REVISE — passes storytelling and looking on the merits, but BLOCKED by a heavy, repeated voice-contract violation: the draft constantly commands and lectures the reader.

The prose is genuinely strong — well-paced, witty without leering, accurate to the factpack on every flagged trap (postcard not panel, moustache-only 391, the "not" in the Sweeney quote, the softened "fire down below" gloss, the cult-not-Leonardo target, the private-collection location). It cannot ship as-is because of a systemic clarity/voice problem: nearly every section ends by turning to the reader and issuing an instruction or flagging its own honesty ("Keep that distinction," "Hold both," "Watch the word," "Quote the line with the 'not,'" "the rule to carry," "a trap worth disarming now," "worth quoting exactly, because careless quoters mangle it"). These are reader-commands, meta-narration, and honesty-labels — the exact tics the voice contract bans. They read like editor's notes to the author that survived into the finished prose. They are easy to remove and the underlying facts are correct; this is a polish pass, not a rebuild.

## 1. STORYTELLING (primary)

Overall: strong. Five sections build cleanly — object → making → the pun → the rupture → the afterlife. Pacing is good, no listy dead spots, the cross-references ("Start with what we already met") tie the afterlife back without re-explaining. The pun is handled with wit and zero leer (the "schoolboy hears" framing carries the vulgarity without wallowing). Findings are minor.

- **[NICE] — repeated "the cult, not the canvas" beat.** The target-is-the-worship-not-Leonardo point lands four separate times: postcard §2 ("The attack is on the cult, not on the canvas"), pun §end ("neither one insults Leonardo"), break §end ("not an attack on Leonardo"), and is foreshadowed in the const hook/figures. It is the single most-repeated idea in the read. It is *correct* and *load-bearing* (factpack §8 nuance 7), so keep it in the break section where it is strongest — but trim one of the earlier statements of it (the postcard-section line "Keep that distinction; the whole work depends on it. The attack is on the cult, not on the canvas." is the most expendable, and it is also a reader-command, so cutting it solves two findings at once).

- **[NICE] — "the confusion is the point of the next few paragraphs" (afterlife, line 209).** The "afterlife is genuinely confusing" opening is a nice hook, but it leans on telling the reader the section is about to be confusing rather than just being clear. Minor; folds into the meta-narration fix below.

- **[NICE] — pacing of the making section.** Strong, but the "one more date to keep clean" paragraph (lines 138–139) is a four-sentence aside hammering 1919-not-1920 immediately after the same point was just made in the 391 paragraph. Compress to one sentence; the date is already clear from context. (Also a reader-command — see below.)

No BLOCKER or FIX on storytelling grounds. The build is sound.

## 2. LOOKING (looking section / annotations)

The looking content lives in PART A's `annotations[]` array (the const) rather than in a single prose section — six entries: pencil moustache, goatee, untouched Mona Lisa beneath, lettering, cheap postcard print, collision of registers. This maps the factpack §5 looking points exactly.

- **PASS — real visible features, prose pointers, no coordinates.** Every annotation points to something genuinely on the card and locates it in words, not pixels: "Across the upper lip of the printed face," "At the chin… just below the famous half-smile," "In the margin under the image," "The whole card under the two pencil marks." No blind crops, no coordinate pins. Compliant with the no-blind-coordinates rule.

- **PASS — vivid + concrete.** "Two short pencil strokes sit on top of the smooth printed lip… deliberately crude against the soft mechanical image beneath it" is exactly the kind of texture contrast a looker needs. The "flat printed ink at roughly 7¾ by 4⅞ inches… a thing you could hold in one hand and post for a few centimes" makes the cheapness physical.

- **[NICE] — the moustache/goatee annotations each re-explain the 391-omission.** Both the moustache and goatee entries restate "Picabia forgot the goatee / moustache-only is the 391 reproduction." Inside a tap-to-view annotation that may be read in isolation, a little redundancy is defensible — but consider trimming the moustache entry's version-disambiguation and letting the goatee entry own it, since the omission is *about* the goatee.

- **[NICE] — "The collision of registers" is interpretive, not visible.** The sixth annotation ("A sublime Renaissance icon, a schoolyard doodle, and an obscene wordplay all share one tiny card") is a reading, not a thing the eye lands on. It is a good closing thought and the factpack lists it as looking point §5.6, so keep it — just note it is the one annotation that is argument rather than observation.

No BLOCKER or FIX on looking grounds.

## 3. CLARITY + VOICE CONTRACT

This is where the work is blocked. The factual prose is clear and jargon is defined on first use (gramogram, rectified readymade, Jocondisme, épater le bourgeois, Rrose Sélavy, Dada, readymade — all glossed inline). But the draft is saturated with reader-commands, meta-narration, and honesty-labels.

### [BLOCKER] Reader-commands (imperatives aimed at the reader)
The voice contract bans commanding the reader. The draft does it repeatedly, almost always to underline a fact-pack trap:

- **§postcard, line 111** — "Keep that distinction; the whole work depends on it." → cut; the sentences around it already make the distinction.
- **§making, line 136** — "The rule to carry: moustache *and* goatee is the 1919 original; moustache alone is Picabia's 1920 reproduction." → rephrase as plain statement: "So moustache *and* goatee is the 1919 original; moustache alone is Picabia's 1920 reproduction."
- **§making, line 139** — "And one more date to keep clean…" → recast: "The picture is a 1919 picture; Picabia's *391* printing is 1920." (drops the command and compresses the redundant aside).
- **§pun, line 154** — "Hold both: the vulgar literal sense and Duchamp's own genteel paraphrase are two registers of one line, not two competing facts." → recast without "Hold both": "The vulgar literal sense and Duchamp's own genteel paraphrase are two registers of one line, not two competing facts."
- **§pun, line 165** — "Watch the word **'not.'**" and "Quote the line with the 'not.'" → both are commands. Recast to describe the misquote plainly: "A widely circulated version drops the *not* and has Duchamp say it *is* 'a woman disguised as a man,' which reverses his point." Then delete the closing "Quote the line with the 'not.'" — it is an instruction to the reader to do something they will never do.
- **§afterlife, line 217** — "do not confuse the bare 1965 *Shaved* with the mustachioed 1919 card." → recast: "The bare 1965 *Shaved* is not the mustachioed 1919 card."

Suggested global fix: every place the draft turns a trap into an order ("keep / hold / watch / quote / don't confuse / the rule to carry"), restate it as a flat declarative about the work. The information is correct and worth keeping; only the imperative mood is the violation.

### [BLOCKER] Meta-narration (the prose narrating itself / the section)
- **§postcard, line 100** — "The *Mona Lisa* postcard would be something one notch stranger, and we'll get to exactly what." → "and we'll get to" narrates the structure of the read. Cut to: "The *Mona Lisa* postcard would be something one notch stranger."
- **§making, line 135** — "Here is a trap worth disarming now, because it produces two different objects that get muddled." → meta + honesty-label. Recast to just tell it: "Two different objects get muddled here. In March 1920, Duchamp's friend Francis Picabia…"
- **§afterlife, line 209** — "the confusion is the point of the next few paragraphs" → narrates the section. Recast: "The afterlife of *L.H.O.O.Q.* is genuinely confusing — there is no single, stable original sitting safely in one museum…" (keep the hook, drop the self-reference).

### [BLOCKER] Honesty-labels ("worth quoting exactly because…", "the safe statement", "honestly")
- **§pun, line 159** — "that is worth quoting exactly, because careless quoters mangle it." → honesty-label + meta. Recast: "Asked years later about the moustache, he described what it did to the face:" (the quote can simply follow).
- **§afterlife, line 221** — "So where is the 1919 original now? Honestly: not pinned down." → "Honestly" is an honesty-label tic. Recast: "So where is the 1919 original now? It is not pinned down."
- **§afterlife, line 222** — "or at least none that the record reliably confirms" reads as a hedge-label; acceptable as accurate uncertainty (factpack §6 FLAG demands the hedge), but trim to plain: "No museum holds the little 1919 card itself."

### [FIX] Condescending gloss
- **§making, line 126** — "The skill is not in the drawing; a child could draw that moustache." The "a child could draw it" line is fine as voice, but paired with "The skill is in the idea" it edges toward telling the reader the obvious. Keep one of the two sentences, not both. (Borderline — flagging as FIX not BLOCKER.)

### [PASS] Em-dash / entity checks in rendered TS strings
- **No literal "—" found in any rendered string field** (const blurbs, stats, annotations, provenance notes all use commas/semicolons/colons — clean). 
- **`&ndash;` usage is correct** — used only for the year range/date span in JSX prose ("1887&ndash;1968") and the date label "1919&ndash;today" in the const, which is the right entity for a numeric range. No `&mdash;` appears anywhere, so there is no misused em-dash entity inside a plain TS string field. Compliant.
- **`&rsquo; &ldquo; &rdquo; &eacute; &icirc;` etc.** are used consistently and correctly in JSX text and in const strings. No raw smart-quote-in-bare-string issues.

### [PASS] Jargon defined on first use
gramogram (line 151), rectified readymade (line 126), readymade (line 100), Dada (line 103), Jocondisme (line 108), épater le bourgeois (line 111), Rrose Sélavy (line 131), Boîte-en-valise (line 212), *L.H.O.O.Q. Shaved*/rasée (line 217) — all glossed inline at first appearance. Good.
