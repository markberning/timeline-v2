TheArtStory, Centre Pompidou, Thyssen. Reviewed `work-harlequin-draft.md` against

## VERDICT: PASS WITH FIXES

The draft is well-built and unusually disciplined on the two traps this gate exists to
catch: it does **not** call the picture pure automatism (it actively dismantles that
read), and it keeps the hunger anecdote proportionate and attributed. No BLOCKERs.
The one real gap is the **genius-myth / situating** axis: Miró is placed almost
entirely against Breton and the manifesto, and his actual 1924–25 milieu — the rue
Blomet circle (his neighbor André Masson's studio, the proto-Surrealist core) and his
Catalan / Mont-roig roots — is absent. That's a [FIX], not a blocker. A handful of
[NICE] tightenings below.

### [FIX] — Miró's actual Surrealist milieu is thin; he reads as a lone hungry dreamer
This is the comprehensiveness face of the genius-myth finding in §2. The draft's only
named contemporary is Breton, and Miró's tie is described purely as "not a
card-carrying foot soldier of Breton's circle, but … close to it." The documented
reality is more specific and more interesting: Miró's Paris studio was on the **rue
Blomet**, next door to the painter **André Masson**, and that studio was the gathering
spot for the writers and painters who would become Surrealism (Masson, Michel Leiris,
Robert Desnos, and others) — Miró and Masson are routinely named as the two painters
who, through automatic drawing, *started* Surrealism as a visual movement, not as
followers of it. Adding one clause gives the reader the real network. Suggested
insertion in **HrqParis**, "Why this picture matters" paragraph, replacing the first
sentence:

> "Miró was not a card-carrying foot soldier of Breton's circle — he never formally
> joined it — but he was at its center in a more literal way than that sounds: his Paris
> studio was on the rue Blomet, next door to the painter André Masson, and Masson's
> studio was the room where the writers and painters who became Surrealism gathered.
> Miró and Masson, drawing without a plan, are often credited with starting Surrealism
> as a way of *painting*, not just writing. The picture he made across 1924 and 1925
> became one of the touchstone works of that early Surrealism, widely counted among his
> greatest."

(Sources: Wikipedia *Joan Miró*; Met Museum essay; TheArtStory. All agree on rue
Blomet / Masson / "never formally joined but central." Keep it to one or two clauses —
don't let the work-read sprawl into a Miró biography.)

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### a. "Pure automatism" overclaim — [PASS, confirmed]
The brief asked to confirm the draft does **not** call it pure automatism. **Confirmed,
and it's the draft's strongest section.** HrqMaking explicitly: "The picture *looks*
like pure automatism … But Miró was equally clear that the canvas itself was
**deliberately built and revised**," carries the verbatim "after long meditation …"
quote, and closes "The spontaneous, automatist surface is partly a constructed effect,
a careful arrangement made to feel like an accident." The HrqBreak section is careful
too ("the dream/automatist biomorphic vocabulary," not "automatic painting"). This is
exactly right and well above the bar. No action.

### b. Genius-myth — [FIX] (see §1)
Same finding as the comprehensiveness gap. As written, the through-line is "a lone
hungry man hallucinating a private universe" — vivid, but it isolates Miró from the
circle and the Catalan ground that actually fed the work. The §1 insertion fixes the
circle half. The Catalan half:

### [FIX] — Catalan / Mont-roig roots are dropped; the factpack flagged this soft, but "soft" ≠ "absent"
The factpack (§2, last bullet) flagged the Catalonia link `[VERIFY]` and said "keep it
soft or cut"; the draft *cut* it entirely. That's defensible on the strict-sourcing
axis (AKG's own lecture page didn't stress it), but on the framing axis it contributes
to the lone-dreamer read. Miró's annual returns to **Mont-roig** in Catalonia and the
symbolic/landscape vocabulary he developed there are well attested across Met,
TheArtStory, Pompidou, and Thyssen — not just one AKG search blurb. A single soft,
correctly-hedged clause restores the ground without overclaiming a direct Catalonia→
*Harlequin* line. Suggested, in **HrqParis** opening paragraph after "He had come up
from **Barcelona**":

> "— and he split these years between Paris and his family's farm at Mont-roig in
> Catalonia, where the landscape and folk vocabulary he was steadily inventing took
> shape."

If the coordinator prefers to honor the factpack's "or cut," this can stay cut — but
then the §1 rue-Blomet fix becomes load-bearing for the situating and should not also
be skipped. At least one of the two must land so Miró isn't framed as a context-free
genius.

### c. Fairness / proportion of the hunger anecdote — [PASS, confirmed]
**Confirmed proportionate.** The draft (i) attributes it to Miró's own account and the
museum, not omniscient narration; (ii) carries the explicit guard rail in HrqMaking's
last paragraph: "it does not mean he literally starved at the easel, or that the whole
picture is an unmediated vision … The painting on top of it he composed"; (iii) hedges
the belly-hole as a museum *reading*, not a fact ("the belly-hole-as-hunger is a
reading the museum offers, not a label painted on it"). This is exactly the factpack
§8 instruction honored. Not sensationalized. No action.

### [NICE] — one phrase tips slightly toward the sensational register the rest avoids
The hook and HrqMaking both lean on "boiling" / "boiling over," and HrqParis says the
radishes fact will "climb straight into the painting, hole and all." These are fine and
on-voice, but "on the brink of starvation" framing is what the factpack warned against;
the draft never uses that phrase (good). No change required — flagging only so a later
pass doesn't *add* starvation language. Leave as is.

### d. Anachronism — [NICE], one item
- **"Buffalo AKG Art Museum" used as the institution's name in 1940 context.** The
  draft handles this correctly everywhere it matters (HrqAfterlife and provenance both
  say it entered the **Albright-Knox Art Gallery** in 1940 and was "renamed … in
  2023"). The const `location` and `stats` use the present name "Buffalo AKG," which is
  correct for a *current location* field. No anachronism in the prose. **[PASS.]**
- **[NICE] — "Abstract Expressionism" in the lineage `children`.** Defensible (Miró's
  automatist biomorphism genuinely fed the New York School, esp. Gorky/Pollock), and
  it's a lineage tag, not a prose claim, so it can't read as anachronistic to the
  reader. Keep. Flagging only that if any prose ever asserts a *direct* Miró→AbEx line
  it would need a "decades later" hedge; none does now.
- No date anachronisms found. "1924–25" range is held consistently (factpack §8 trap
  avoided — the const `year: 1925` is a single-field necessity, but every prose and
  stat instance says 1924–25). [PASS.]
