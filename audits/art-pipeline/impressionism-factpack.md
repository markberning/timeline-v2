## 0. THE LEGEND LEDGER — documented vs myth (READ FIRST)

| Claim | Verdict | The real fact |
|---|---|---|
| "Impressionism" was coined by critic Louis Leroy | **[DOCUMENTED, with nuance]** | The term entered wide use *because of* Leroy's 25 Apr 1874 satire titled "L'Exposition des impressionnistes." But "impression" was already in the artists' own vocabulary (Monet *titled* the painting *Impression, Sunrise*), and the critic Jules Castagnary used "impressionists" approvingly in *Le Siècle* the same month. Leroy popularized/weaponized it; he didn't invent the word from nothing. Say "the name came from a hostile review," not "Leroy invented the word." [W1][W2] |
| The artists adopted the mocking name | **[DOCUMENTED]** | They called themselves the *Société Anonyme…* in 1874; by the 3rd show (1877) they accepted "Impressionnistes" — Renoir/Caillebotte's faction even used it for the 1877 exhibition. [W1][WHE] |
| *Impression, Sunrise* hung in the 1874 show | **[DOCUMENTED — verify count]** | TRUE. It was catalogue **No. 98**. Monet showed 5 oils there (Nos. 95, 96, 97, 98, 103) plus pastels. So "Sunrise was in the 1874 show" is correct — but don't say it was his only or central work. [W1][W2] |
| The collapsible paint tube "made Impressionism possible" | **[LEGEND / overstated]** | Rand patented the tin tube in 1841 (US Patent 2,252); it genuinely helped plein-air work. But painters had worked outdoors for decades (Barbizon), and the **box easel + portable kit** mattered as much. The famous Renoir line "Without paint in tubes there would have been no Impressionism" is from his **son Jean Renoir's 1962 memoir**, reported decades later, and is itself an overstatement — frame it as a quip, not causation. [W3][PT1][PT2] |
| Chevreul's color science drove broken color | **[LEGEND / overstated for Impressionists]** | Chevreul's *On the Law of Simultaneous Contrast of Colors* (1839) was real and influential — but its *deliberate, theorized* application belongs to the **Neo-Impressionists (Seurat/Signac)**. The Impressionists used colored shadows and juxtaposed strokes largely **by eye/instinct**, not by reading Chevreul. Don't claim they followed a manual. [CH1] |
| The Impressionists were poor, unknown failures their whole lives | **[LEGEND / overstated]** | Some were broke early (Monet, Pissarro, Sisley especially). But Manet and Degas were comfortable; Caillebotte was wealthy; Durand-Ruel bought in bulk from 1872; by the 1880s–90s Monet and Renoir were successful and Monet died rich (1926). "Struggled early, then mostly won" is the honest arc. [DR1][W-Monet] |
| "They all painted outdoors" | **[LEGEND / false]** | **Degas never did** — he scorned plein air, worked in the studio from memory/drawings, on dancers, laundresses, cafés, the racetrack, the bath. Cassatt and Morisot worked largely indoors too. Plein air is a Monet/Renoir/Pissarro/Sisley signature, not the whole movement. [W-Degas] |
| Manet was an Impressionist | **[LEGEND / false — key nuance]** | Édouard Manet **never exhibited in any of the eight Impressionist shows.** He was the older father-figure they orbited (Café Guerbois), influenced them and was influenced back, but he kept submitting to the official Salon his whole life. Call him "the reluctant patriarch who stayed at the Salon," never "an Impressionist." [W1][W-Manet] |
| Impressionism had a manifesto | **[LEGEND / false]** | No signed founding manifesto. The name came from an enemy. The nearest substitute is **Duranty's pamphlet *La Nouvelle Peinture* (1876)** — which the painters did NOT sign and which pointedly *avoided* the word "Impressionism." See §8. |
| Bazille was killed in the war | **[DOCUMENTED]** | True. Killed in action at the Battle of Beaune-la-Rolande, **28 Nov 1870**, age 28, Franco-Prussian War. [W-Bazille] |

## 1. THE NAME'S ORIGIN — the 1874 show and Leroy

- **The First Impressionist Exhibition** ran **15 April – 15 May 1874** at **35 boulevard
  des Capucines**, on the 2nd/3rd floors of the former studio of the photographer
  **Félix Nadar** (he lent the empty rooms). [DOCUMENTED] [W1]
- Organized by the **Société Anonyme des Artistes Peintres, Sculpteurs, Graveurs, etc.**
  ("Anonymous Society of Painters, Sculptors, Engravers, etc."), a joint-stock
  cooperative proposed by **Pissarro**; charter signed **27 December 1873**. Founding
  signers included Monet, Renoir, Sisley, Degas, Berthe Morisot, Pissarro, plus
  Béliard, Guillaumin, Lepic, Levert, Rouart. [DOCUMENTED] [W1]
- **30 artists** in the catalogue, **~165–175 works** (catalogue Nos. 1–165 with gaps;
  ~31 exhibitors counting reviews). Admission **1 franc**; catalogue **50 centimes**.
  **~3,500 visitors**; revenue 10,221.50 fr against 9,272.20 fr expenses, but members
  ended in deficit (~3,700 fr of debt) and the Société was dissolved that December.
  [DOCUMENTED] [W1]
- **Monet's *Impression, Sunrise* (1872)** hung as **catalogue No. 98**. Monet's account
  (to his brother / via Edmond Renoir): asked for a title for the catalogue, and since
  "it couldn't really pass as a view of Le Havre," he said **"Put *Impression*."**
  [DOCUMENTED — Monet's own recollection] [W2]
- **Louis Leroy's satirical review**, *Le Charivari*, **25 April 1874**, titled exactly
  **"L'Exposition des impressionnistes"** ("The Exhibition of the Impressionists").
  Written as a comic dialogue: Leroy walks the show with a fictional conservative
  academic painter, **"Monsieur Joseph Vincent."** In front of No. 98 the punchline runs
  (widely reproduced English translation): *"Impression — I was certain of it. I was
  just telling myself that, since I was impressed, there had to be some impression in
  it… and what freedom, what ease of workmanship! Wallpaper in its embryonic state is
  more finished than that seascape."* [DOCUMENTED] [W2][W1]
- **Nuance to keep honest:** the word wasn't Leroy's coinage from thin air (Monet's
  title; Castagnary's friendly use). Leroy made it *stick* as a mockery; the artists
  then **claimed the insult as a banner** (accepted by the 3rd show, 1877). Tell it as
  "a hostile critic turned their own word into a joke, and they took the joke and wore
  it." [DOCUMENTED + nuance] [W1]

## 2. THE EIGHT EXHIBITIONS (1874–1886)

The group never had stable membership; **Degas was the organizing engine and the chief
fracture line** (his "no Salon if you show with us" rule and his insistence on adding
Realist/illustrator allies drove Monet, Renoir, Sisley out at points). Only **Pissarro
showed in all eight.** Compact table (all [DOCUMENTED] [WHE][W1][BRIT]):

| # | Dates | Venue | Driver / note | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 Apr–15 May 1874 | 35 bd des Capucines (Nadar's studio) | Société Anonyme; Pissarro's idea | ~30 artists; *Impression, Sunrise* (No.98); Leroy's review → the name. Manet absent (feared for his Salon chances). |
| 2 | Apr 1876 | Galerie Durand-Ruel, rue Le Peletier | Durand-Ruel's space | ~20 artists, 250+ works. Caillebotte's *The Floor Planers* a sensation. |
| 3 | Apr 1877 | apartment, rue Le Peletier | Caillebotte financed | ~18 artists. **First show the group openly called "Impressionist."** Degas brings in **Cassatt** (invited; she joins 1879). Renoir's *Moulin de la Galette*, Monet's *Gare Saint-Lazare*, Caillebotte's *Paris Street; Rainy Day*. |
| 4 | Apr–May 1879 | av. de l'Opéra | Caillebotte/Degas | ~15 artists. **Cassatt's debut**; Gauguin's first. Monet/Renoir/Sisley absent (back to the Salon). 16,000 visitors — most successful yet (439 fr dividend each). |
| 5 | Apr 1880 | rue des Pyramides | Degas faction | Monet, Renoir, Sisley absent. Held in a half-built space (noise). Membership disputes sharpen. |
| 6 | Apr 1881 | bd des Capucines (a photo gallery) | Degas-dominated | Monet, Renoir, Sisley, Caillebotte, Cézanne all absent. Degas shows the wax **Little Dancer Aged Fourteen** — a scandal. Morisot + Cassatt praised. |
| 7 | Mar 1882 | Salles du Panorama, rue St-Honoré | Durand-Ruel pushed it | Monet, Renoir, Sisley **return**; **Degas abstains**. The most "purely Impressionist" of the late shows. Critic: a Monet sun like "a slice of tomato"; rumor Monet was colorblind. |
| 8 | 15 May–Jun 1886 | above Maison Dorée, rue Laffitte | **Berthe Morisot & Eugène Manet** organized/financed | ~17 artists. Monet, Renoir, Sisley, Caillebotte all stay away. **Seurat's *A Sunday on La Grande Jatte* and Signac** debut Pointillism — this is the **handoff to Post-Impressionism / Neo-Impressionism**. Degas shows nude bathers. Morisot's circle called it largely a "fiasco." **The last show; the group dissolves.** |

**1886 is the end.** Frame it as: the movement that began with a mocked seascape ends
with a younger painter (Seurat) hanging the picture that makes Impressionism look
suddenly like the past. The story closes here; what follows is the next chapter
(Post-Impressionism) — gesture, don't narrate it.

## 3. THE TECHNIQUE / THE SCIENCE — mechanism vs myth

- **Plein air (open-air painting)** — finishing a canvas outdoors, before the motif, to
  catch real, changing light. [DOCUMENTED] Monet/Renoir/Pissarro/Sisley signature; **not
  Degas** (studio, from drawings/memory). [W-Degas]
- **Broken color / optical mixing** — small, separate strokes of pure-ish color laid
  side by side so the eye blends them at a distance, instead of pre-mixing a flat tone.
  [DOCUMENTED as practice] — but for the Impressionists this was **intuitive**, not the
  systematic dot-theory of Seurat. [CH1]
- **Colored shadows; the death of "black"** — they largely abandoned black and brown
  "earth" shadows for shadows built of **complementary colors** (a shadow on snow gets
  blue/violet). [DOCUMENTED as practice] [CH1]
- **Loose, visible brushwork over invisible *fini*** — the academy prized a licked,
  seamless finish (*fini*); Impressionists left the stroke *showing*, the picture
  looking "unfinished" (Leroy's whole joke). [DOCUMENTED] [W1]
- **The portable tin paint tube** — patented by **John Goffe Rand**, an American painter
  in London, **11 Sept 1841 (US Patent 2,252)**; commercialized by colourmen (Winsor &
  Newton) through the 1840s. It kept pre-mixed oil paint fresh and portable. **Helped,
  did not "cause," plein air** — see §0. [DOCUMENTED invention; LEGEND causation] [W3][PT1]
- **The box easel / pochade box** — portable folding easels and paint boxes were equally
  important to getting out to the riverbank; credit them alongside the tube. [DOCUMENTED]
  [PT2]
- **Chevreul's *Law of Simultaneous Contrast of Colours* (1839)** — real color science
  (juxtaposed complementaries intensify each other). Influence on the *Neo*-Impressionists
  is direct and theorized; on the Impressionists it is **diffuse and largely instinctive**.
  Do not assert they applied Chevreul deliberately. [DOCUMENTED book; LEGEND for direct
  Impressionist use] [CH1]
- **New synthetic pigments** (chrome yellow, cobalt/cerulean blue, viridian, emerald
  green, synthetic ultramarine) gave them brighter, more stable, ready-made colors than
  earlier generations — a genuine material enabler. [DOCUMENTED] [PT1]

## 4. THE CAST (who they are, birth–death, one line, who recruited whom)

- **Claude Monet** (1840–1926) — the purest plein-air Impressionist; the man whose
  *Impression, Sunrise* named the movement; later the serial painter (Haystacks, Rouen,
  water lilies). Outlived them all; died wealthy and famous. [DOCUMENTED] [W-Monet]
- **Pierre-Auguste Renoir** (1841–1919) — warmth, dappled light, crowds and parties;
  *Moulin de la Galette*, *Luncheon of the Boating Party*. Later drifted from
  Impressionism toward a more classical line. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Camille Pissarro** (1830–1903) — the eldest, the steady conscience; **the only artist
  in all eight shows**; mentor to Cézanne and Gauguin; landscapes and Paris boulevards;
  later flirted with Pointillism. [DOCUMENTED] [WHE]
- **Alfred Sisley** (1839–1899) — British-born, Paris-based; the most consistently
  devoted pure landscapist; stayed poor; never had Monet's late success. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Edgar Degas** (1834–1917) — the indoor, modern eye: dancers, laundresses, the café,
  the racetrack, the bath. **Hated being called "Impressionist"** (preferred "Realist" /
  "Independent"), **never painted plein air**, yet organized the shows and pushed hardest.
  The group's great internal antagonist. **Recruited Mary Cassatt.** [DOCUMENTED] [W-Degas]
- **Berthe Morisot** (1841–1895) — **founding member**; exhibited in **all but one** of
  the eight shows (missed 1879, the year she had her daughter); married **Eugène Manet**
  (Édouard's brother) in 1874; with Eugène, **organized/financed the final 1886 show**.
  Domestic and female-world subjects — by access, not lack of ambition. [DOCUMENTED] [WHE]
- **Mary Cassatt** (1844–1926) — **American** (Pittsburgh-born, Paris-based); **recruited
  by Degas** (invited 1877, debuts 1879); mother-and-child and women's daily life;
  Japonisme adopter; **advised the Havemeyers**, steering major Impressionist works into
  American collections (now the Met). [DOCUMENTED] [W-Cassatt]
- **Édouard Manet** (1832–1883) — the older father-figure of the Café Guerbois circle;
  *Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe* (1863) and *Olympia* (1863) scandalized the Salon and lit the
  fuse; influenced and was influenced by the younger painters — **but NEVER exhibited in
  any of the eight Impressionist shows**; sought Salon recognition to the end. The crucial
  "not technically one of them" figure. [DOCUMENTED] [W1][W-Manet]
- **Gustave Caillebotte** (1848–1894) — wealthy engineer-painter; **patron and financier**
  of several shows; sharp-edged urban realism (*Paris Street; Rainy Day*, *The Floor
  Planers*). His will left ~67 Impressionist works to the French state — the **bequest
  fight** (see §9). [DOCUMENTED] [BRIT-C][WB]
- **Frédéric Bazille** (1841–1870) — the gifted early member of the Gleyre-studio circle
  (with Monet, Renoir, Sisley); **killed in action at Beaune-la-Rolande, 28 Nov 1870**,
  age 28, before the movement had a name. The great might-have-been. [DOCUMENTED]
  [W-Bazille]
- **Paul Cézanne** (1839–1906) — showed in the 1st (1874) and 3rd (1877) exhibitions, was
  savaged by critics, then **withdrew** to work alone in Provence; his later structural
  painting becomes a foundation of Post-Impressionism / modernism. Frame as "passed
  through, then left." [DOCUMENTED] [WHE]
- **Eva Gonzalès** (1849–1883) — **Manet's only formal pupil**; painted in the
  Impressionist orbit; **died at 33** (six days after Manet, of an embolism following
  childbirth). Often dropped from the story — name her with the other two women. [DOCUMENTED]
- *(Adjacent: **Émile Zola** — novelist/critic, Café Guerbois regular, early champion of
  Manet; **Edmond Duranty** — critic, author of the nearest-thing-to-a-manifesto, §8.)*

## 5. THE WOMEN, HONESTLY (framing-critical — real agency, not footnotes)

- **Berthe Morisot** — not a wife-of, not a muse: a **founding member of the Société** and
  one of its most loyal exhibitors (**all but one** of the eight shows). Her subjects —
  women, children, gardens, interiors — were **constrained by what a respectable
  bourgeois woman could go and observe** (no café, no brothel, no backstage), not by lack
  of nerve or skill. She married into the group (Eugène Manet) and **co-ran the final
  1886 exhibition**. Make her decisive. [DOCUMENTED] [WHE]
- **Mary Cassatt** — **American**, came to Paris, was **brought into the group by Degas**,
  and became its great painter of mothers and children and women's interior lives —
  again, the subjects open to her. A serious printmaker shaped by Japanese woodblocks.
  Off the canvas she **shaped American taste**: advising her friends the **Havemeyers**,
  she funneled first-rate Impressionist and Old Master pictures into the U.S., which is
  why so many hang in the Met today. She had agency over the *market*, not just the
  easel. [DOCUMENTED] [W-Cassatt]
- **Eva Gonzalès** — Manet's pupil, a real talent inside the circle, **dead at 33**.
  Include her so the women aren't a duo-token. [DOCUMENTED]
- **Framing rule:** present the three as artists who hit a *wall of access* (the city's
  male spaces were closed to them) and worked brilliantly within it — NOT as women who
  "chose gentle domestic subjects." The constraint is the story; name it.

## 7. THE BREAK (REQUIRED block — what made it genuinely new)

**Proposed single best before/after pair:**
- **BEFORE:** a Salon-perfect academic nude — **Alexandre Cabanel, *The Birth of Venus*,
  1863** (Musée d'Orsay), bought by Napoleon III, the smash hit of the *same 1863 Salon*
  that rejected Manet. Porcelain skin, invisible brushwork, a mythological alibi, light
  that comes from nowhere real. [DOCUMENTED] [BRIT]
- **AFTER:** **Monet, *Impression, Sunrise*, 1872** — a real harbor at a real dawn, in
  loose visible strokes, finished where it stood, no story, no myth, "unfinished" by
  Salon rules. [W2]
- **Why this pair:** same culture, a decade apart, opposite *everything* — Cabanel is what
  the establishment crowned; *Sunrise* is what it mocked. It stages the whole quarrel in
  two pictures. (If a Realist "before" is wanted instead — to show the *gradual* path —
  use a **Barbizon landscape (Daubigny/Corot)** or Courbet, which the era/Realism read
  already covers; the Cabanel pairing is sharper for THE BREAK.)

**The concrete changes (name these, not "revolutionary"):**
1. **Colored light over local color** — a haystack isn't "yellow," it's whatever the
   6 a.m. or 5 p.m. light makes it; shadows go blue/violet, not black.
2. **The fleeting moment over the posed tableau** — a glance, steam dispersing, light at
   one instant — instead of a frozen, eternal, staged scene.
3. **Visible brushwork over invisible finish** — you can *see* the paint and the hand;
   *fini* is abandoned (the "wallpaper" insult).
4. **Modern leisure / modern city as fit subjects** — dance gardens, boating parties,
   railway stations, cafés, racetracks, rainy boulevards — not gods, kings, or scripture.
5. **The studio abandoned for the riverbank** — the picture *finished outdoors before the
   motif* (Monet/Renoir/Pissarro/Sisley). **Caveat:** Degas keeps the studio and the
   modern subject without plein air — so the "break" is light+subject+finish, and
   open-air is one strong-but-not-universal limb of it.

## 8. THE MANIFESTO QUESTION (REQUIRED block — RESOLVED)

**Verdict: there was NO Impressionist manifesto. Build this as an `absent: true`
block** (tell the story of the silence + the name coined by an enemy + the nearest
substitute), matching the Cubism pattern (`project_manifesto_block`).

The verified facts to support that decision:
- **No signed founding manifesto, no agreed program.** They were a loose cooperative
  (the Société Anonyme) bound by exclusion from the Salon, not a doctrine. Their *name*
  itself was handed to them by a **hostile critic** (Leroy). That absence-of-a-creed,
  plus a mocking-name origin, IS the story to tell. [DOCUMENTED] [W1]
- **The nearest substitute — Edmond Duranty, *La Nouvelle Peinture* (1876).** Full title:
  *La nouvelle peinture: à propos du groupe d'artistes qui expose dans les galeries
  Durand-Ruel* ("The New Painting: concerning the group of artists exhibiting at the
  Durand-Ruel galleries"). A **38-page pamphlet**, published to coincide with the
  **2nd exhibition (1876)**. [DOCUMENTED] [DAH][MET]
  - **What it argued:** the first serious attempt to set these painters in an
    intelligible context — that a *new painting* of **modern contemporary life**, in a new
    style (new color/light, figures shown in their real environment, modern dress and
    settings), was a genuine revolution. Its unnamed exemplar is clearly **Degas**
    (and Caillebotte). [DOCUMENTED] [DAH]
  - **Did the painters endorse it?** **No.** It was Duranty's own essay; the painters did
    not sign or commission it as a manifesto. Crucially, **Duranty deliberately AVOIDED
    the word "Impressionism"** (he called it "the new painting") and did not name the
    artists in the text. So it is a sympathetic critic's framing, **not** the group's
    own creed — which is exactly why the honest move is `absent: true` with Duranty as
    "the closest anyone came." [DOCUMENTED] [DAH][MET]
  - Duranty: born 6 June 1833, died 9 April 1880, Paris; Realist novelist/critic, Café
    Guerbois regular, close to Degas. [DOCUMENTED] [DAH]
- **The other "substitute" voice — Émile Zola** — championed Manet and the new painting
  in his Salon criticism from 1866 (collected as *Mon Salon* / *Édouard Manet*). Also a
  critic's voice, not a group manifesto. [DOCUMENTED] [W-Manet]

## 9. CONTEXT / MECHANISMS

- **Haussmann's rebuilt Paris** — Baron Georges-Eugène Haussmann's 1853–70 demolition and
  reconstruction (wide boulevards, uniform façades, parks, the new Opéra) literally
  *built the subject*: the grand boulevard, the café, the railway station, the rainy
  street became Impressionist motifs (Caillebotte, Monet's *Boulevard des Capucines*,
  Pissarro's boulevard series). [DOCUMENTED] [BRIT]
- **The Franco-Prussian War (1870–71)** — scattered the group. **Bazille killed**
  (28 Nov 1870, §4). **Monet and Pissarro fled to London**, where they studied Turner and
  Constable AND — crucially — **met the dealer Paul Durand-Ruel**, who had also moved his
  stock to London for safety. The war forged the dealer relationship that would carry the
  movement. [DOCUMENTED] [W-Bazille][DR1]
- **Paul Durand-Ruel (1831–1922)** — the dealer who made the market. From **1872** he
  **bought in bulk** (a famous lot of ~23 canvases from Manet's studio for 35,000 fr;
  buying everything he could from Monet/Pissarro/Renoir/Sisley) — a then-radical model of
  cornering an artist's output. It **nearly bankrupted him by the mid-1880s**. He was
  **rescued by America**: invited by the American Art Association, his **1886 New York
  exhibitions** were a hit, opened the U.S. market, and he opened a New York branch in
  1887. His own line: *"Without America, I would have been lost, ruined, after having
  bought so many Monets and Renoirs. The two exhibitions there in 1886 saved me."*
  [DOCUMENTED] [DR1]
- **Railway / leisure / the suburbs** — new rail lines put the Seine-side resorts
  (Argenteuil, Chatou, Bougival, La Grenouillère) and the seaside within a day-trip of
  Paris; a new middle-class leisure culture (boating, dancing, day-tripping) became the
  movement's defining subject matter. [DOCUMENTED] [W-Gren]
- **Japonisme** — Japan reopened to trade (1850s–60s); the term *Japonisme* was coined by
  critic **Philippe Burty (1872)**. Japanese woodblock prints (*ukiyo-e* — Hokusai,
  Hiroshige, Utamaro) reached Paris and reshaped composition: **flat planes of color,
  asymmetry, high/odd viewpoints, radical cropping** (a figure sliced by the frame).
  Strongest in **Degas and Cassatt** (Cassatt's color prints openly echo Utamaro);
  visible in Monet too. [DOCUMENTED] [JP1]
- *(Cafés as the social engine — the **Café Guerbois** (Batignolles, ~1866–c.1874,
  Thursdays/Sundays, centered on Manet; Renoir, Sisley, Bazille, sometimes Degas,
  Pissarro, Monet, Zola, Duranty, Nadar) then the **Café de la Nouvelle-Athènes**
  (Pigalle, the later 1870s, the setting evoked in Degas's *L'Absinthe*). [DOCUMENTED]
  [CG1] — these are where the talk happened, not where decisions were signed.)*

## 10. PROPOSED CHAPTER / MOVEMENT MAP (7 movements — suggestion for the coordinator)

Distinct dramatic movements, each one throughline. Chapter count = movements (no cap).

1. **The world that said no** *(rise / the wall)* — Haussmann's new Paris, the Salon
   machine and its *fini*, Manet's *Déjeuner*/*Olympia* scandals; a generation that can
   see the modern city but can't get it past the jury. *(Nest: lean on the Modern-era +
   Realism reads for the academy; go deeper on the modern-city subject and Manet's role.)*
2. **Paint what you actually see** *(technique / the new eye)* — plein air, broken color,
   colored shadows, the death of black, the visible stroke; the tube and the box easel
   (with the myth disarmed); *La Grenouillère*, Monet & Renoir side by side.
3. **The seascape they laughed at** *(crisis → name)* — April 1874, Nadar's studio, the
   Société Anonyme, *Impression, Sunrise* (No. 98), Leroy's review, the insult adopted.
4. **A cooperative at war with itself** *(the group / the dealer / the eight shows)* —
   the eight exhibitions, Degas's faction-fights, the Salon defectors, and **Durand-Ruel**
   buying in bulk through near-ruin to the America that saved them.
5. **The women, in the rooms they were allowed** *(parallel / honesty)* — Morisot
   (founder, all-but-one show, ran 1886), Cassatt (Degas's recruit, shaper of American
   collections), Gonzalès; agency inside a wall of access.
6. **Degas and the indoor modern eye** *(transformation)* — the man who never went
   outdoors: dancers, laundresses, cafés, the bath; Japonisme, cropping, the off-balance
   moment; *L'Absinthe*, *The Dance Class*, the *Little Dancer* scandal.
7. **The last show** *(peak → dissolution / handoff)* — late triumphs (Monet's series:
   Haystacks, Rouen) and prosperity for some; 1886, Morisot/Eugène Manet's show, Seurat's
   *Grande Jatte* — and the movement, having won, becomes the thing the young paint
   *against*. Hand off to Post-Impressionism (gesture, don't narrate).
