### [FIX] — "Young woman among the AbEx men" is present only as age, not as the gendered fact the sources foreground.

The draft repeatedly hits her **age** (23) hard, which is good, but her being a **young woman** breaking into a scene the literature explicitly frames as male-coded is essentially missing. The fact pack itself underweights this (it leads with "at age 23" and never names the gender dynamic), so this is a genuine comprehensiveness gap, not just a draft slip. The standard scholarship treats it as material: she "claimed an original voice prioritizing fluidity, emotion, and color over muscular drawing… combatting the supremacy of [AbEx's] larger, male-coded gesture" (Women in AbEx / CUNY; corroborated by the general literature). The draft's own "break" argument — that she drained away the "heroic gesture" and "the loaded stroke" — is *the same observation* the gender reading rests on, but the draft never connects them.

This is a one-or-two-sentence addition, not a restructure. It must be done WITHOUT tokenizing (see Framing §2c below): name it as fact and tie it to the work, don't make her gender the headline or a sidebar.

**Suggested text** — add to `MtsNovaScotia`, end of ¶2 (after the Greenberg/companion sentence), so it sits in the "where she's coming from" setup:

> She was also, at twenty-three, a young woman entering a New York scene whose reigning style prized exactly the things she was about to drop — the big, muscular, visibly male gesture of de Kooning's brush and Pollock's flung arm. The move she made here ran the other way, toward soaked, quiet, drawn color, and it is part of why the picture read as a break.

(Keeps it as context for the *work*, not a biographical aside; pays off in MtsBreak where the "heroic gesture drains away" line already lives.)

## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### (a) Soak-stain "invention" scope — **CONFIRMED, not overclaimed. PASS.**

The draft scopes this correctly and repeatedly, exactly as the brief requires:
- MtsNovaScotia ¶3: "(Pouring paint was Pollock's precedent, not Frankenthaler's invention…)"
- MtsMaking ¶4: "Frankenthaler did **not** invent pouring; Pollock had been pouring enamel for years… What she pioneered is the **soak-stain**."
- MtsBreak hinge ¶: "not the first abstract painting, not the first poured painting… The first **soak-stain**."

This matches the verified record: Pollock poured enamel c.1947+; she pioneered the *pour-as-stain-into-raw-canvas*. The const-header hook ("the soak-stain she pioneered here") and annotation 1 ("what Frankenthaler pioneered with this canvas") both use the safe verb "pioneered," never "invented." **No change.** This is a model of correctly scoped attribution.

### (b) The "bridge" line — **[FIX] WRONG REFERENT, recurs twice, plus a dropped article.**

The brief asks to confirm the "bridge" line is Louis on **Frankenthaler (the artist)**, not on the painting. The draft gets the **speaker** right (Louis, not Frankenthaler/Greenberg — good, and explicitly flagged) but the **referent wrong**: it twice says Louis called *the painting* a bridge.

Web-verified against two independent sources:
- **Morris Louis, Wikipedia:** "Speaking later about the importance of seeing Frankenthaler's technique, Louis called **her** 'the bridge between Pollock and what was possible.'" → referent = **her** (the artist).
- **Panero / Karen Wilkin:** "…Louis's frequently quoted description of **Frankenthaler** as 'the bridge between Pollock and what is possible.'" → referent = **Frankenthaler** (the person).

Note also: the canonical phrasing is "**the** bridge," not "**a** bridge." The draft uses "a bridge" both times.

The fact pack §4 actually labels it correctly — "(NOT Frankenthaler; **Morris Louis ON the painting**)" — but that parenthetical is itself the error the gate must catch: the sourced wording is Louis *on Frankenthaler*. The draft inherited the fact pack's slip.

**Two draft locations to correct:**

1. **PART A — `sections` afterlife blurb (line ~80):**
   - Current: *"Louis later called the painting a bridge between Pollock and what was possible."*
   - **Suggested:** *"Louis later called Frankenthaler the bridge between Pollock and what was possible."*

2. **PART B — `MtsAfterlife` ¶2:**
   - Current: *"Louis later gave the painting the line it has carried ever since, calling* Mountains and Sea *"a bridge between Pollock and what was possible." (… It is **Morris Louis**'s characterization … not a saying of Frankenthaler's…)"*
   - **Suggested:** *"Louis later gave Frankenthaler the line her work has carried ever since, calling her "the bridge between Pollock and what was possible" after seeing this picture. (… It is **Morris Louis**'s characterization of Frankenthaler herself, reported later and popularized through the scholar Karen Wilkin — not a saying of Frankenthaler's and not Greenberg's; and it circulates in two forms, "what *was* possible" and "what *is* possible." Treat it as Louis's reported paraphrase, not a transcript.)"*

   This keeps the (correct) speaker-flag and the (correct) was/is variant-flag, and fixes only the referent + the article.

3. **Const-header comment (lines 37–39)** carries the same error ("the 'bridge between Pollock and what was possible' line is MORRIS LOUIS's reported paraphrase") — speaker right, but if you want the header self-consistent, note it is Louis *on Frankenthaler*. [NICE], it's a comment, not reader-facing.

This is a [FIX], not a [BLOCKER]: it is a real factual-attribution error on a gated quoted surface, but it is contained, the source is unambiguous, and the speaker/variant handling is otherwise correct.

### (c) Gender / age as context without tokenizing or sidelining the work — **PASS (and the §1 fix must respect this).**

The draft currently handles age as integral context (the "23-year-old pours…" hook, "She was twenty-three" closing MtsMaking) — it's woven into the making, never a trophy. This is the right register. The §1 gender addition above is written to match it: it lands inside the work's argument ("part of why the picture read as a break"), not as a standalone "woman artist" beat. **As long as the §1 fix follows that pattern, fairness holds.** Do NOT add a separate "Frankenthaler as a woman in AbEx" section or sidebar — that would tokenize.

### (d) Genius-myth / proportion — **PASS.**

The draft resists the lone-genius myth well:
- It credits Pollock's precedent explicitly and repeatedly (not a virgin-birth invention story).
- It shows the influence flowing through real mechanism (Greenberg's studio visit → Louis/Noland → Washington Color School), not destiny.
- It includes the **dissent** ("looks to many people like a large paint rag, casually accidental and incomplete") and frames it as the normal resistance a break meets — this is good proportion, not hagiography.
- The "Impression, Sunrise" analogy is the standard textbook claim and is correctly framed as "the standard line," not asserted as the draft's own grand verdict.

One micro-watch [NICE]: MtsAfterlife closing — "A twenty-three-year-old poured it onto the floor in a single afternoon's kind of move" — the "single afternoon" phrasing risks a mild prodigy-myth flourish (the making was not necessarily one afternoon; the sources don't time it). It's hedged ("kind of move") so it's not a factual claim, but consider trimming to "poured it onto the floor" to stay clean of an unsourced speed-of-genius beat. Optional.

### (e) Proportion of the influence claim — **PASS.**

"Launched Color Field and the Washington Color School" is correctly scoped: the draft says Louis and Noland "began staining… themselves, and out of that work grew" the school — i.e., she seeded/opened-the-door, the Washington painters built it. Not overclaimed as "she created Color Field." Good.
