4. The Prix de Rome as the pinnacle prize

### II. THE SALON AS MARKETPLACE
8. The Salon as the only marketplace (not just a show — where art was actually *bought*)
9. The physical experience: the hang (floor-to-ceiling, "on the line"/"skied")
10. The scale of the event — how many people, what kind of crowds
11. A visual anchor showing the official taste in action

### III. THE MODERN FORCES
12. Photography (daguerreotype 1839) — the threat to the portrait/likeness trade; the forced reorientation of painting
13. Paint in collapsible metal tubes (1841, Rand) — portable colour; plein-air possibility
14. Railways — painters can now leave Paris cheaply
15. Haussmannisation / urban modernity — Paris rebuilt as a modern spectacle that *cannot* be painted under academic rules
16. The emerging private market / new bourgeois buyer — the faint alternative to the jury

### IV. STAKES
17. The central question: who decides what a painting is for?
18. The hand-off gesture toward Chapter 1 (the first rebel)

## One item needing attention — SHOULD-CONSIDER

**The private atelier (master's studio) as distinct from the École.**

The École des Beaux-Arts taught drawing and theory; the private *atelier* — run
by an established academician — is where painting was actually taught, and where
the master's taste got transmitted to the next generation. (Cabanel himself ran
one of the most attended ateliers of the 1860s–70s.) The draft correctly describes
the École's drawing-first sequence but doesn't note that students then trained in
an academician's *atelier* for the practical painting instruction. This is a
*structural* feature of how the institution reproduced its taste in living people.

**Verdict: SHOULD-CONSIDER, not MUST-ADD.** The draft's current framing (the
Académie controlled the professors who ran the school, and used the same pool
to staff the jury) captures the closed-loop essence without this detail. A one-
clause mention ("practical painting taught in an academician's private studio")
would sharpen the closed-loop reproduction mechanism — the same people who judged
at the Salon also trained the painters — but the reader already grasps that the
institution was a closed system. It is not a comprehension-blocker.
