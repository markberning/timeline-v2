# GATE READ — Balla, *Abstract Speed + Sound* (1913–14), Part B prose

**Gates run:** (1) STORYTELLING, (2) LOOKING, (3) CLARITY.
**Draft:** `work-abstract-speed-draft.md` (Part B, the five `Abs`-prefix chapter components).
**Voice ref:** `absinthe-narratives.tsx.txt`.
**Verdict:** PASS with minor fixes. No BLOCKERs. The LOOKING chapter is strong and does the core job an abstract work demands (it makes the reader SEE the absent car). A handful of clarity gaps, two soft meta-narration tics, and a couple of flat/repetitive seams to tighten.

Legend: **[BLOCKER]** ship-blocking · **[FIX]** should fix before integration · **[NICE]** optional polish.

## 1. STORYTELLING

The story-first spine is intact. The "keep the motion, throw away the thing" hook lands in AbsDog and is paid off in AbsLooking; the dachshund→nothing arc is a genuine narrative, not a wall of facts. Dry wit is present and in the absinthe register ("a little long-bodied sausage of a dog"; "He runs out of road and keeps going anyway"; "built into the carpentry"). Comparisons are the strongest asset (the runner-photo vs. footsteps-recording; the stone-in-the-pond wake; the train-window posts). Scene-setting is good (the 1913 automobile as "loud, expensive, slightly dangerous novelty").

### [FIX] Meta-narration — "the part this whole chapter exists for"
**Where:** AbsLooking, para 2. Text: *"Now the hard part, the part this whole chapter exists for: there is no car."*
**Problem:** Narrating what the chapter is for is exactly the meta-narration the voice rules ban (`feedback_no_meta_narration`: never narrate what the section is about). The absinthe ref never says "this chapter exists for X."
**Fix:** Cut the self-reference, keep the turn: *"Now the hard part: there is no car."* Or *"Now the hard part. There is no car."*

### [FIX] Meta-narration — "this whole chapter exists for" echo + "the part this whole chapter"
Same tic also surfaces softly in AbsLooking para 1 ("Hold the smallness in mind") and AbsTriptych para 2 ("because honesty about what we know is part of the picture"). The first is fine (it's a looking instruction). The second is borderline — it announces the move rather than making it.
**Where:** AbsTriptych, para 2. Text: *"Now the warning, because honesty about what we know is part of the picture."*
**Fix:** Trim the justification clause: *"Now the warning. The triptych reading is proposed, not proven."* The honesty is shown by the hedging that follows; it doesn't need to be announced.

### [FIX] Flat/repetitive seam — "runs off its own frame" used as two section titles
**Where:** The `triptych` section in Part A is titled "The middle of a picture that runs off its own frame," and AbsTriptych's SectionHeader repeats it verbatim — fine, that's the same section. BUT the *concept* "the picture that runs off its edge/frame" appears as: AbsLooking frame subtitle ("The picture that runs off its own edge"), AbsTriptych title ("...runs off its own frame"), plus the phrase recurs in prose ("refuses to stop at its own edge," "A car does not stop at a frame. Neither does this picture," "motion never stops at the edge").
**Problem:** The "won't stop at the edge" beat is the work's best idea, but it's stated ~5 times across two chapters in nearly identical words. By the AbsAbstraction close ("refuses to stop at its own edge") it has lost punch through repetition.
**Fix:** Keep the full statement once in AbsLooking (the frame paragraph) and once as the structural payoff in AbsTriptych (frames connect not divide). Vary or cut the rest. In particular the AbsTriptych line *"A car does not stop at a frame. Neither does this picture."* directly re-runs AbsLooking's *"motion never stops at the edge"* — make them say different things or drop one.

### [NICE] AbsLooking para 1 "Hold the smallness in mind" then "Hold the smallness in mind" payoff
The smallness point is made twice in one paragraph ("about a foot and a half tall..." → "Hold the smallness in mind. This is not a wall-sized declaration. It is a hand-sized one"). Slightly redundant but voice-consistent. Optional trim.

### [NICE] AbsDog "as we will see" / AbsLooking "as we'll see" / AbsTriptych "the thing we ended the last chapter on"
These forward/back cross-refs are used 4–5 times. The absinthe ref uses them too ("We'll get to that," "in two chapters Victorian critics will..."), so this is in-voice and acceptable. But AbsTriptych opens *"a fact that changes how you see everything we just looked at"* and AbsAbstraction's pivot is also a "here's the harder half" move — two consecutive chapters opening on a "now here's the twist" framing. Consider varying one opening.

## 2. LOOKING (most important for an abstract work)

This is the heart of the gate and the draft does well. Every required element is present, named, and given a concrete "find it on the canvas" instruction:

- **Green landscape band** — AbsLooking "Earth and sky" para + annotation. SEEN: "Low across the picture runs a band of green... not a field, not a meadow, no grass or furrow you could name, just the single fact of land, reduced to its color." Strong — it tells the reader what to look for AND why it proves abstraction (no furrow/fence/farm). ✓
- **White road / speed lines** — "The road" para. SEEN: "find the white. A pale channel cuts diagonally... those strokes are the car." ✓ The "follow them with your eye and you are tracing the route" instruction is exactly the concrete guided-looking an abstract work needs.
- **Sky arcs** — "Earth and sky" para. SEEN: "It bends. It curves into arcs, as if the sky itself has been grabbed and dragged... the air is bent." ✓ Properly hedged ("read as") in the fact ledger.
- **Painted frame continuing the image** — its own "The frame" para. SEEN: "look at the frame... Balla painted his own frame, continuing the picture's forms and colors right out onto it." ✓ Best paragraph in the chapter.
- **Directional thrust** — "The thrust" para. SEEN: "almost everything in the picture leans the same direction... the repetition shoves the gaze along." ✓ The moving-train-posts comparison is excellent guided looking.
- **The ABSENT car** — opened in AbsLooking para 2 ("there is no car"), closed in the final para ("No car, and yet a car — assembled out of the bent sky, the white road..."). ✓ This is the payoff the abstract work lives or dies on, and it lands.
- **The noise / "+ Sound" crisscross** — "The noise" para. SEEN: "look for where the lines stop agreeing... a buzzing crisscross... it cuts against the grain." ✓ The "speed is in the strokes that flow; the sound is in the strokes that snag" line is a genuinely useful seeing-tool.

### [FIX] LOOKING — "where" pointers in annotations vs. prose vagueness on the noise/crisscross LOCATION
**Where:** AbsLooking "The noise" para; annotation "The car that is not there" / "+Sound."
**Problem:** Every other element gets a spatial anchor (green = "low across," blue = "above," white = "diagonal channel," frame = "border all around"). The crisscross noise gets only "Over the swooping speed-forms" / "laid over the rush" — no location relative to the panel. For a reader staring at a genuinely confusing abstract image, "where on the board is the buzz?" is the one missing spatial cue.
**Fix:** Add a placement beat, e.g.: *"...look for the marks that crisscross the speed-lines rather than running with them — they cluster densest where the forms pile up toward the center, a lattice laid over the rush."* (Confirm "center" against the image before asserting; if uncertain, say "across the busiest stretch of the board.")

### [NICE] LOOKING — the reader is told to find white "diagonal" road, but AbsMaking calls it the road the car "tears down"
Minor: AbsMaking and AbsLooking both describe the road but the orientation language differs slightly (AbsMaking implies the car moves along it; AbsLooking says the channel cuts "diagonally"). Not a contradiction, but a reader cross-checking might wonder if the road is horizontal (a road "runs sideways," AbsLooking para 1) or diagonal (AbsLooking "The road" para). Reconcile: the road runs sideways across the landscape format but the painted channel reads as a diagonal slash. One clause acknowledging both would remove the friction.

### [NICE] LOOKING — color of the "+Sound" crisscross
The crisscross is described by behavior (cuts the grain, buzzes) but never by color/value. If the lattice has a describable color on the actual board, naming it (even "darker," "paler") would sharpen the find. Optional and image-dependent — do not invent.

- **abstraction / pure abstraction** — built up across AbsDog ("keeps the motion and throws away the thing") and nailed in AbsLooking close ("no recognizable object... built entirely from line, color and force") and AbsAbstraction ("works with no recognizable object in them at all"). ✓
- **lines of force** — AbsLooking "The road" + annotation: "his term for the trajectory of speed itself, the path the motion takes drawn as a thing you can see." ✓ Glossed cleanly.
- **chronophotography** — AbsDog: "the ancestor of the flip-book and the film strip." ✓
- **triptych** — AbsTriptych: "a picture made of three panels meant to be read together, a format that goes back to medieval altarpieces." ✓

- **louche** — N/A (that's absinthe ref).
- **rumore / velocità astratta** — AbsMaking: "rumore, Italian for 'noise'"; "velocità astratta + rumore, abstract speed plus noise." ✓

### [NICE] CLARITY — "the multiplication of the number of lines and planes" (museum quote)
**Where:** AbsLooking "The thrust" + annotation; AbsTriptych para 1.
**Problem:** The quoted museum phrase is slightly opaque on its own for a zero-knowledge reader ("number of lines and planes" — what's a plane here?). **Fix (optional):** On first use of "planes" (AbsLooking "Earth and sky" doesn't use it; first is "The thrust": "The lines lean; the planes lean"), add a two-word gloss: "the planes — the flat shape-facets — lean." Low priority.

## 4. Cross-cutting notes

### [NICE] Voice consistency vs. absinthe ref
The draft matches the absinthe register well: drop caps, em-dash asides, the "here is the thing to hold onto" direct address, the dry parentheticals. One difference: the absinthe ref leans harder on pronunciation glosses ("mon-MART-ruh," "loosh"). This work has fewer foreign terms but "Velocità astratta + rumore," "Giacomo Balla," "Boccioni," "Severini," "Kupka," "Delaunay," "Marinetti" all go unpronounced. Not required (the absinthe piece is French-dense; this is lighter), but a pronunciation for "Velocità astratta + rumore" or "Giacomo" would be in-house-voice. Optional.

### [NICE] The Russolo aside (AbsMaking para 4)
Fact ledger flags it [VERIFY] tone-only. As prose it's a good "the idea jumped media" beat and proportionate. No storytelling/clarity objection — leave to the FACT gate.
