## 2. FRAMING / FAIRNESS

### The "first POP" claim — **correctly scoped (PASS).**
The legacy chapter and the `annotations` "POP!" entry handle this exactly as the brief demands:
- States the *defensible* version ("first known appearance of the word 'pop' inside a work of this kind," "roughly a decade before the movement got the name").
- Explicitly disclaims the over-claims: did NOT invent or name Pop Art; Alloway named it later; the movement was "emphatically *not* named after this burst."
- Identifies the "POP!" as a **toy pop-gun packaging** sound effect, a coincidence, "not a baptism."
- Uses **"prefigures, does not found"** — the precise word.

This is the model for how this trap should read. No change needed; do NOT let any revision pass soften it back toward "the first Pop artwork."

### Findings:

**[FIX] F-1 — "the inaugural meeting of the Independent Group" is stated as flat fact in three places; it is the *mainstream* framing but is contested, and the draft's own certainty over-reaches.**
The draft asserts (reception chapter, const note, and provenance) that the Bunk lecture *was* "the inaugural meeting of the Independent Group." Mainstream sources (Wikipedia, ICA's own 60th-anniversary material, most overviews) do say the IG's first meeting in April 1952 was Paolozzi's epidiascope session, so this is **defensible and may stand**. But IG scholarship (e.g. Anne Massey) distinguishes the *founding/first convening* of the group from the *first event in its programme*, and some sources phrase it as "the first event presented in the Independent Group's programme" rather than literally the group's first-ever meeting. The framing gate's job is to flag the unhedged certainty, not to force a rewrite.
- **Suggested text (reception ¶1):** change "was the **inaugural meeting of the Independent Group**" → "opened the **Independent Group** — a small, restless circle…" and let the existing "in effect, the opening shot" carry the precedence. OR keep "inaugural meeting" but it is acceptable as-is given source weight. **Minimum required fix:** none if "inaugural meeting" stays, but do NOT additionally claim it was the *very first thing the group ever did* beyond what sources support. (Defensible-to-keep; logging as [FIX] so a reviser doesn't over-strengthen it.)

**[FIX] F-2 — "a talk he called the 'Bunk' lecture" / "in a talk" slightly over-states the verbal content.** Multiple accounts stress the Bunk presentation was largely a **wordless run of projected images** — Paolozzi fed magazine collages through the epidiascope, Dada-style, with little or no explanatory commentary; it is often noted that he gave essentially no spoken lecture. The draft calls it "a talk he called the 'Bunk' lecture" and (in const) "the 'Bunk' lecture." "Bunk lecture" is the standard catalogue name and is fine as a *title*, but "a talk he called… / in a talk" implies spoken exposition the event mostly lacked.
- **Suggested text (reception ¶1):** "…in a talk he called the **'Bunk' lecture**." → "…in his now-famous **'Bunk' presentation** — mostly a wordless flood of projected images, with little spoken commentary." Keep the name "Bunk lecture" if you like (it is the received title), but add the "mostly wordless / images, not words" qualifier somewhere in the chapter so the reader isn't picturing a podium talk. This also *strengthens* the draft's own thesis (the pictures did the arguing).

**[FIX] F-3 — "almost nobody thought of as art at all" / sculptor weighting (genius-myth-adjacent over-statement).** Setting ¶3 ends: "he did something that, at the time, almost nobody thought of as art at all. He started gluing it down." Collage was *not* a novel or non-art act in 1947 — Cubist papier collé (1912), Schwitters, Dada/Surrealist photomontage, Höch, Hausmann were three to four decades established, and the draft itself elsewhere credits "Dada photomontage" as a parent. The radical thing was not *that it was collage* but *the material* (American consumer/ad print as celebratory subject). As written, the line slightly inflates Paolozzi into a lone inventor of gluing-as-art, which the lineage block correctly contradicts. This is the framing gate's proportion concern.
- **Suggested text (setting ¶3):** "he did something that, at the time, almost nobody thought of as art at all." → "he did something almost no one had yet done with *this* material — the throwaway commercial print of the American magazine." (Keeps the punch, removes the false "collage = not art" implication, and is consistent with the draft's own Dada-parent credit and the break ¶.)
- Pair this with the [NICE] sculptor weighting (C-2): one clause noting he was a serious sculptor, for whom these collages were a private side-stream, both earns the "almost no one had yet done this" and avoids the lone-genius read.

**[NICE] F-4 — date discipline: clean, with one inherited discrepancy to confirm.** Made-1947 vs shown-1952 is held rigorously throughout (const TRAPS, reception ¶1, legacy ¶2 "made 1947, first shown 1952"). The 1947 collage vs 1972 screenprint distinction is also handled explicitly and well (legacy ¶2). **One flag, inherited from the factpack, not introduced by the draft:** Wikipedia dates the *BUNK!* series span as **1941–52**, while the draft/factpack use **1947–52** (and the const note says "Ten Collages from BUNK (dated 1947–52)"). The "1947–52" matches Tate's *Ten Collages from BUNK* sub-group dating, so it is defensible for *this* object's grouping; just be aware the broader series is sometimes dated from 1941. No prose change required — the draft only ever says "1947–52" about the Tate sub-group, which is correct. Confirm against the live Tate page per the factpack's open-items list.

**[NICE] F-5 — "one of the most consequential gestures in twentieth-century art" (legacy ¶3) is a strong superlative.** It is broadly within the consensus framing for this work and is softened by the surrounding plain voice, so it passes. Flagging only so it isn't escalated further in revision; it should not gain a "*the* most" or "single most." As-is: acceptable.

**[NICE] F-6 — "Real Gold" citrus logo is in the factpack and the `making` chapter ("a citrus-juice logo") but dropped from the `annotations` and the `looking` chapter.** Not a fairness issue, a completeness micro-gap; the looking chapter enumerates the fragments and omits this one. Optional: add "and a citrus-juice logo ('Real Gold')" to the looking ¶3 list, or leave it — it is the least iconic fragment and the chapter is already dense.
