## 1. REALISM — Courbet's "Realist Manifesto" (1855)

### ✅ The 1855 Exhibition Context — CONFIRMED

Gustave Courbet submitted 14 paintings to the 1855 Exposition Universelle jury. Three were rejected — including *A Burial at Ornans* and *The Painter's Studio* — reportedly for lack of space rather than explicit aesthetic objection. Courbet withdrew all his accepted works in protest and erected his own independent tent structure — the **Pavilion of Realism (Pavillon du Réalisme)** — next to (within sight of) the official exhibition. He displayed approximately 40 paintings there.

**Sources:**
- Wikipedia, "Gustave Courbet": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Courbet
- EBSCO Research Starters, "Courbet Establishes Realist Art Movement": https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/courbet-establishes-realist-art-movement

### ✅ Authorship of the Manifesto — CONFIRMED: Courbet alone

Courbet wrote the statement of purpose himself as the **introduction/preface to the catalogue** of his Pavilion of Realism exhibition. This catalogue preface is what is called his "Realist Manifesto."

**⚠️ Caveat — two separate documents, often confused:**
A second text exists: Champfleury (Jules François Félix Fleury-Husson) published "Du réalisme. Lettre à Madame Sand" in *L'Artiste* on September 2, 1855 — a separate letter-essay defending Courbet and Realism, addressed to George Sand. Some sources (including one EBSCO entry) conflate this with Courbet's catalogue preface, or claim Champfleury "co-wrote" the manifesto. These are **two distinct documents**. The catalogue preface was Courbet's own. Champfleury's "lettre" is sometimes called a parallel manifesto of Realism but it is not the same text.

**Sources:**
- Wikipedia, "Gustave Courbet" (manifesto written by Courbet for catalogue): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Courbet
- Persée, "Champfleury-Sand, Du réalisme" (Champfleury's letter as a separate text): https://www.persee.fr/doc/roman_0048-8593_1994_num_24_84_5963

### ✅ VERIFIED QUOTABLE LINES

Two sources independently reproduce the same English translation, and it is also cited in Gardner's *Art through the Ages* (14th ed., 2013), a standard university textbook — making this the most widely circulated and authoritative English wording.

**— OPENING (safe to quote):**

> "The title of Realist was thrust upon me just as the title of Romantic was imposed upon the men of 1830."

Confirmed verbatim in:
- ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/artcultures/chapter/realism/ (quotes this opening line exactly)
- arthistoryunstuffed.com/avant-garde-realism-france/ (same wording)

**— CREDO (safe to quote):**

> "To know in order to do, that was my idea. To be in a position to translate the customs, the ideas, the appearance of my time, according to my own estimation; to be not only a painter but a man as well; in short, to create living art — this is my goal."

Confirmed verbatim in:
- ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/artcultures/chapter/realism/ (exact wording)
- arthistoryunstuffed.com/avant-garde-realism-france/ (same wording, minor punctuation variation: "painter, but")

**⚠️ Translation variation — note for editors:**

A condensed/paraphrased version circulates in some secondary sources (e.g., arteidolia.com, citing Gardner's Art through the Ages):

> "To be able to translate the customs, ideas, and appearances of my time as I see them — in a word, to create a living art — this has been my aim."

This is a legitimate published paraphrase (Gardner/Kleiner textbook), not the fuller translation. It is recognizably the same passage but compresses "according to my own estimation" into "as I see them" and drops "To know in order to do" entirely. The **longer version** (credo above) is the better-sourced direct translation. Use it; do not mix the two versions.

**Additional confirmed line (Smithsonian Magazine source):**

> "Painting is an essentially concrete art and can consist only of the representation of things both real and existing."

Source: smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/larger-than-life-31654689/

This is a third standalone line from the manifesto, consistently confirmed across sources, safe to quote.

- arthistoryunstuffed.com: https://arthistoryunstuffed.com/avant-garde-realism-france/

## 2. CUBISM — The "No Manifesto" Case

### ✅ Picasso and Braque issued no manifesto — CONFIRMED

Multiple independent sources confirm that Picasso and Braque deliberately avoided publishing theoretical statements about their Cubist work.

- Wikipedia on *Du "Cubisme"*: "Picasso, Braque and Gris made almost no published statements on the subject before 1914."
- The Art Story (cubism.theartstory.org): "Picasso and Braque rarely exchanged long letters or wrote manifestos."
- peterbrooke.org (independent Cubism scholar): same finding.
- Note: their exclusivity contract with dealer Daniel-Henry Kahnweiler kept them out of public salons entirely, reinforcing their silence.

**Sources:**
- Wikipedia, "Du Cubisme": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_%22Cubisme%22
- The Art Story, "Cubism": https://www.theartstory.org/movement/cubism/
- Peter Brooke, "Picasso and Braque in context": http://www.peterbrooke.org/form-and-history/cubisme/context/picasso.html

### ✅ Albert Gleizes & Jean Metzinger, *Du Cubisme* (1912) — CONFIRMED

- **Authors:** Albert Gleizes and Jean Metzinger — two painters associated with the Salon Cubists / Puteaux Group.
- **Year:** 1912. Published by Eugène Figuière Éditeurs, Paris.
- **First major theoretical book on Cubism:** CONFIRMED. Wikipedia describes it as "the first major text on Cubism" and "the first manifesto of the Cubist painting movement to be published in book form." Prior commentary existed in magazine articles (André Salmon, etc.) but *Du Cubisme* was the first self-contained book-length theoretical statement.
- Metzinger maintained close contact with Picasso and Braque's circle (Le Bateau-Lavoir), giving him a bridge position between the gallery Cubists and the Salon Cubists who theorized publicly.

**⚠️ Minor caveat:** Apollinaire's *Les Peintres Cubistes* (1913) and André Salmon's *Histoire anecdotique du cubisme* (also 1912) are close contemporaries. *Du Cubisme* (1912) has clear precedence over Apollinaire's book; Salmon's 1912 text was a journalistic history, not a theoretical treatise, so Gleizes/Metzinger retain the "first major theoretical book" designation without meaningful challenge.

**Sources:**
- Wikipedia, "Du Cubisme": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_%22Cubisme%22
- Fundación Juan March exhibition page: https://www.march.es/arte/palma/exposiciones/ducubisme/?l=2

### ✅ Guillaume Apollinaire, *Les Peintres cubistes: Méditations esthétiques* (1913) — CONFIRMED

- **Full French title:** *Les Peintres Cubistes, Méditations Esthétiques*
- **English title:** *The Cubist Painters, Aesthetic Meditations*
- **Author:** Guillaume Apollinaire (1880–1918)
- **Year:** 1913 — CONFIRMED (Internet Archive original 1913 edition exists; first edition sold by antiquarian booksellers confirms this date)
- **Who was Apollinaire?** Polish-French poet, playwright, and pioneering art critic. He is credited with coining the term "Cubism" publicly (first using it at the 1911 Salon des Indépendants) and was described by Wikipedia as "one of the most impassioned defenders of Cubism." He served as "a decisive interface between artists and poets of the early 20th century."
- **His role:** He championed Cubism through journalism, introductory essays for exhibitions, and this book — the definitive critical account of the movement at the time.

**Sources:**
- Wikipedia, "The Cubist Painters, Aesthetic Meditations": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cubist_Painters,_Aesthetic_Meditations
- Wikipedia, "Guillaume Apollinaire": https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Apollinaire
- Internet Archive (original 1913 edition): https://archive.org/details/lespeintrescubis00apol

### ✅ Section d'Or / Salon Cubists — CONFIRMED

- **What it was:** A second-wave group of Cubist painters — formally known as the **Groupe de Puteaux** — who gathered regularly at studios in the Paris suburb of Puteaux (at the home of the Duchamp brothers). Named after the mathematical golden ratio (*section d'or*).
- **Key members:** Albert Gleizes, Jean Metzinger, Robert Delaunay, Henri le Fauconnier, Fernand Léger, Marcel Duchamp, Jacques Villon, Raymond Duchamp-Villon, Francis Picabia.
- **Timeline:** Began meeting formally around 1911 after exhibiting together at the Salon des Indépendants. Mounted the **Salon de la Section d'Or in October 1912** — described as "the most important pre-World War I Cubist exhibition."
- **Key distinction from Picasso/Braque:** While Picasso and Braque initiated Cubism but "rarely commented publicly," the Section d'Or painters exhibited at public salons, published theoretical texts (*Du Cubisme*, 1912), and had Apollinaire deliver the opening address at their 1912 salon — actively promoting Cubism as a movement.
- Apollinaire joined the Puteaux Group in 1911 and is closely associated with the Section d'Or.

**Sources:**
- ideelart.com, "What the Section d'Or Meant for Cubism": https://ideelart.com/blogs/magazine/what-the-section-d-or-meant-for-cubism-and-abstract-art-at-large
- Wikipedia, "Guillaume Apollinaire": https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Apollinaire
