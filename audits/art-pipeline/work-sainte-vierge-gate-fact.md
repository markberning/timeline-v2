## [BLOCKER]

### B1 — *Manifeste Cannibale Dada* venue is wrong: it was NOT the Salle Gaveau
The draft (PART B, `SvgAfter`, twice) and the const header context say the *Manifeste Cannibale Dada* was "performed at the **Salle Gaveau** festival on **27 March 1920**." The **date is right** (27 March 1920) but the **venue is wrong**.

- The *Manifeste Cannibale Dada* was read (by André Breton) at the **third Dada soirée at the Théâtre de la Maison de l'Œuvre on 27 March 1920**. It was published in *Dada* no. 7 / *Dadaphone* (March 1920).
- The **Salle Gaveau "Festival Dada" was a *separate, later* event — 26 May 1920.** The draft has fused two different Paris Dada events.

This is exactly the kind of confidently-wrong specific the gate exists to catch, and it appears in body prose AND a muted source-note (two reader-facing surfaces).

**Corrected text (PART B, `SvgAfter`, the sentence introducing the Cannibale quote):**
> There is a second, separate text from the same month worth setting beside it, kept separate on purpose. Picabia's <em>Manifeste Cannibale Dada</em> ("Cannibal Dada Manifesto") was read aloud by André Breton at the third Dada soirée at the Théâtre de la Maison de l'Œuvre on <strong>27 March 1920</strong>, with its flat little litany:

**Corrected text (the muted source-note below the litany):**
> (From the <em>Manifeste Cannibale Dada</em>, read at the third Dada soirée, Théâtre de la Maison de l'Œuvre, 27 March 1920, and printed in <em>Dadaphone</em> / <em>Dada</em> no. 7. This one was <em>not</em> printed inside <em>391</em> no. 12, so keep it separate from the lines above; it belongs to the festival season, not the magazine that carried the drawing.)

(Optional simplification: if naming Breton-as-reader adds clutter, "was performed at the third Dada soirée at the Théâtre de la Maison de l'Œuvre on 27 March 1920" is enough — but DROP "Salle Gaveau" entirely.)

*Sources: Dada-companion / UbuWeb (Cannibale read 27 March 1920, Maison de l'Œuvre, pub. Dadaphone/Dada no. 7); Festival Dada at Salle Gaveau dated 26 May 1920.*

## [FIX]

### F1 — Quote variant: const header says "for imbeciles," displayed quote uses "for imbeciles," but the cited source (UbuWeb / 391.org) prints "for idiots"
The KEY-STATEMENT block (PART B, `SvgAfter`) displays the quote as:
> "Art is a pharmaceutical product for **imbeciles**."

The verified source text (UbuWeb "Dada Manifesto," March 1920, *391* no. 12; corroborated by 391.org) reads **"Art is a pharmaceutical product for idiots."** Both render the same French original (*un produit pharmaceutique pour imbéciles*), and the draft's muted note already *acknowledges* the two translations — but the displayed quotation should match a real published English source verbatim, with the variant noted, not the reverse. As written, the headline quote uses the wording that is NOT in the cited source while the note treats "imbeciles" as primary.

**Two acceptable fixes (pick one):**

(a) Display the source wording and keep the note:
> "Art is a pharmaceutical product for **idiots**."

…note: "(That last line turns up in English as both 'for idiots' and 'for imbeciles'; both render the same French original, <em>un produit pharmaceutique pour imbéciles</em>.)"

(b) Keep "for imbeciles" in the display but change the citation to a published source that uses "imbeciles" (the draft does not currently cite one — so (a) is the safer fix).

Recommend (a): it matches the two sources actually verified (UbuWeb + 391.org both say "idiots").

*Sources: UbuWeb "Dada Manifesto" (391 no. 12, March 1920) = "for idiots"; 391.org Dada Manifesto = "for idiots."*

### F2 — Drip direction is verifiable and could be stated, not left fully generic (and one risk to avoid)
The draft (annotations + `SvgMaking`/`SvgLooking`) correctly says the drawing and the *391* plate are mirror images and the drips run opposite ways — that is right and well-handled. But the Pompidou record is specific and the draft leaves it generic: **on the original drawing the drips run leftward; in the reversed *391* plate they run rightward.** This is a NICE-to-add precision, BUT note the risk: the wired hero is the *drawing*, so if any caption ever asserts a direction it must say **leftward** (drawing), never rightward. As currently written the draft asserts no specific direction, so there is no error — just an opportunity. Keep generic if preferred; if you state a direction, it is **leftward in the drawing**.

*Source: Centre Pompidou catalogue — "drips run leftward on the original, rightward in the reversed printed version."*

## [NICE]

### N1 — "more than a hundred cars" / "burned through several fortunes"
PART B `SvgParisDada` says Picabia "at one point owned more than a hundred cars and burned through several fortunes." Picabia's wealth, car obsession, and his squandering of inherited money are very well documented in the biographical literature; "more than a hundred cars" is the commonly cited figure (often given as ~127 over his life). Not load-bearing to the work; widely repeated and broadly accurate. No change required.

### N2 — "blot let fall... from a great height" detail not in draft (consistent, not contradicted)
The Pompidou record describes the ink as "projected onto gray paper from a great height," producing the splatters and four drips. The draft's hedged "applied (probably dropped/spattered) and let run" and its explicit refusal to assert "flung across the room" is fully consistent with this and correctly interpretive — the factpack's making-method hedge is honored. No change required; if anything the "from a height / projected" reading mildly supports a spatter over a gentle pipette-drip, but the draft's even-handed hedge is the safe call.

## VERIFIED CORRECT (load-bearing, confirmed on the web)

- **Title / English:** *La Sainte Vierge* / *The Blessed Virgin* (lit. "The Holy Virgin") — Pompidou + Commons. ✓
- **Artist / dates:** Francis Picabia, 1879–1953. ✓
- **Date:** 1920; reproduced *391* no. 12, **March 1920**. ✓
- **Medium:** "Encre de Chine, mine graphite et encre sur papier" = India ink + graphite + ink on paper. Draft const ("India ink and graphite on paper") and prose ("India ink, graphite pencil, and ink") both correct. ✓
- **Dimensions:** 33 × 24 cm. Conversion: 33 cm = 12.99 in ≈ 13 in (1 ft 1 in); 24 cm = 9.45 in ≈ 9½ in. Draft "1 ft 1 in × 9 1/2 in" and "thirteen inches tall by nine and a half wide" are correct imperial conversions (imperial-only per house rule). ✓
- **Location / accession:** MNAM / Centre Pompidou, Cabinet d'art graphique, **AM 2008-91**. ✓
- **Acquisition:** "Achat avec la participation de Monsieur Frieder Burda, 2008" → "Acquired 2008 (with the participation of Frieder Burda)." ✓ (Frieder Burda is German — draft's "German collector" is correct.)
- **Wired image = the DRAWING, not the plate:** confirmed. The *391* no. 12 plate was "légèrement recadrée et reproduite à l'envers" (slightly cropped and reproduced reversed), inverting drip direction. Draft keeps the two separate throughout and never conflates them. ✓ (This was the central trap — handled correctly.)
- **"291" is a typo for *391*:** confirmed (the Commons/Wikipedia file text says "291"; *291* was Stieglitz's earlier NY magazine; Picabia's review is *391*). Draft uses *391* throughout and explicitly flags the "291" slip in `SvgParisDada`. ✓
- **No Picabia quote ABOUT the drawing:** correct; draft substitutes manifesto lines and labels them as such, not as captions to the drawing. ✓
- **Dada Manifesto quotes** ("wants nothing… understand nothing, nothing, nothing"; "Art is a pharmaceutical product for idiots") — verified verbatim, published in *391* no. 12, Paris, March 1920 (the same issue that carried the drawing). ✓ (wording fix = F1)
- **Cannibale litany** ("like your idols: nothing / … / like your religions: nothing") — verified verbatim. ✓ (venue = B1)
- **Two manifestos kept separate** (391-issue manifesto vs Cannibale) — draft does this correctly and says so explicitly. ✓
- **Yale lithograph** — separate object, *La Sainte-Vierge*, lithograph, sheet 55.5 × 37.4 cm, acc. 1953.6.251, "Gift of the Estate of Katherine S. Dreier." Draft's "a separate lithograph… held by the Yale University Art Gallery" is correct and does not mix its dimensions with the drawing's. ✓
- **Multiple-objects / two-drawings argument** — supported by the *Dada/Surrealism* Joan-of-Arc essay; draft hedges appropriately ("Scholars even argue… probably made two closely related drawings"). ✓
- **Rights = `pd-us`:** correct. Published 1920 (PD in US); Picabia d. 1953 → PD in France/EU since 1 Jan 2024 (life+70). Draft's rights prose and const comment are accurate. ✓
- **"Exuberantly nihilistic" / "good taste and sound morals" / "anti-religious, anti-pictorial, anti-aesthetic":** verified against Public Domain Review; draft attributes the quoted phrase to "one historian" — accurate. ✓
- **Dada origin** (Zurich 1916; name from a dictionary, French baby-talk for hobby-horse) — draft hedges with "reportedly," which is correct given the contested origin story. ✓
