# Gate Read — *I was a Rich Man's Plaything* (Paolozzi, 1947)

**Gate:** Storytelling + Looking + Clarity/Voice
**Source:** `audits/art-pipeline/work-rich-mans-plaything-draft.md` (factpack skimmed)
**Verdict:** PASS WITH FIXES. No blockers. Storytelling is strong across all five
sections — the austerity-Britain longing, the "POP!" coincidence, and the
decade-early framing all land, and the looking section is concrete and
coordinate-free. The fixable issues are voice-contract tics (reader-commands,
honesty-labels, an entity-in-prose punctuation hazard) and a couple of
near-duplicate passages between Making and Looking. Fix the [FIX] items before
ship; [NICE] are optional polish.

---

## 1. STORYTELLING (primary) — STRONG

The narrative works. Five-section arc: setting (the country he'd never seen) →
making (cutting up the dream) → looking (what's on the card) → reception (the
"Bunk" lecture / Independent Group) → legacy (the decade-early POP + the break).
The central images all land: GIs' magazines leaking into a ration-book country,
the title lifted off a pulp cover, the toy-gun "POP!" read decades later as
prophecy, and "the supermarket walked into the gallery, and it walked in here."
The break block (legacy ¶4) is one of the better ones in the corpus — it names
the before (pulp filed under "throwaway"), the Dada contrast, and the
austerity-Britain timing, without over-claiming it "invented Pop."

### [FIX] Making ¶3 and Looking ¶1–3 are substantially redundant
**Location:** `RmpMaking` ¶3 (lines 128–130) vs `RmpLooking` ¶1–3 (lines 139–148).
**Issue:** Making ¶3 already walks the whole inventory — *Intimate Confessions*
pin-up + title-lifted-off-cover, the "POP!" pistol, Coca-Cola, the bomber +
"Keep 'em flying!", cherry pie. Then Looking re-walks the identical inventory
(pin-up + cover lines, the "POP!" toy-gun cut, Coca-Cola, bomber + Lockheed
identification, cherry pie). A reader hits the pin-up/title-source point twice and
the toy-gun-POP point twice within two screens. Making should *make* (the method,
the smallness, the found title) and hand the full visual tour to Looking.
**Suggested rewrite:** Trim Making ¶3 to the title-as-found-object beat only, and
stop after "the headline of the trashy magazine it is partly made of." Cut the
"Over that pin-up he glued a man's hand..." inventory sentence onward (lines
129's second half) — let Looking deliver the gun/Coke/bomber/pie tour for the
first time. This sharpens both sections and removes the only "listy" feel in the
draft.

### [NICE] Reception ¶3 quote hedge interrupts momentum
**Location:** `RmpReception` ¶3 (line 169).
The parenthetical "(The quote is reported from Tate's material on the artist;
treat the exact wording as attributed rather than carved in stone.)" is honest
sourcing but reads as a footnote spoken aloud mid-story (see CLARITY below — it's
also an honesty-label). The *content* of the beat (cutting is easy, arranging is
the meaning) is excellent and should stay; just relocate the provenance hedge out
of the reader's face.

## 2. LOOKING — STRONG, concrete, no coordinates

`RmpLooking` makes the small collage concrete through pure prose pointers, with
no coordinates and no blind crops. All six factpack looking-points are present and
spatially anchored by relation, not pixels:
- pin-up in red ("the biggest thing... fills most of the sheet") + the cover
  lines still printed around her — concrete.
- the "POP!" pistol burst, correctly scoped as cut from toy-gun packaging, "a
  genuine coincidence" — concrete and trap-aware.
- Coca-Cola ("lower right... dropped in whole, no comment attached") — concrete.
- bomber ("lower left... Lockheed Hudson or Ventura type... 'Keep 'em flying!'")
  — concrete, correctly hedged with "usually identified as."
- cherry pie ("tucked among them... sitting unnervingly close to the gun") —
  concrete.
- collage texture / visible seams ("each with its own paper edge... typeface...
  color registration... seams left showing") — concrete; the "you are looking at
  consumer culture, cut up and glued down" close is the right payoff.

No looking blockers or fixes. This section is the strongest in the draft. (The
"lower right / lower left" relative placements are descriptive, not coordinate
pins, and match the factpack — acceptable.)

## 3. CLARITY + VOICE CONTRACT

### [FIX] Reader-commands (imperative "you"/"look"/"keep straight") — multiple
The voice contract bans reader-commands. The draft leans on them, mostly in
Looking and Legacy:
- **Looking ¶1, line 140:** "**Start with** the biggest thing" — imperative open.
- **Looking ¶4, line 149:** "Last, **do the thing that is easy to skip: look at**
  how it is built." — double imperative.
- **Looking ¶2, line 144:** "a good one to **keep straight**." — reader-command.
- **Legacy ¶2, line 195:** "**Keep two other things straight** while you are at
  it." — reader-command opener.
- **Legacy ¶3, line 198:** "**Stand close to it**, and the real surprise..." —
  imperative.
- **Reception ¶1, line 162:** "Here is a fact that surprises people:" — softer,
  but a "let me tell you a fact" framing tic.

**Suggested rewrites (declarative, keep the content):**
- "Start with the biggest thing, because it fills most of the sheet: the pin-up
  in red." → "The biggest thing on the sheet is the pin-up in red, and it sets the
  terms for everything else."
- "Last, do the thing that is easy to skip: look at how it is built." → "The
  easiest thing to miss is how it is built."
- "a good one to keep straight." → "and the coincidence is worth getting right."
- "Keep two other things straight while you are at it." → "Two other things often
  get tangled with this one."
- "Stand close to it, and the real surprise is still the smallness" → "Up close,
  the real surprise is still the smallness".

### [FIX] Honesty-labels — three instances
The contract bans honesty-labels (the narrator certifying its own candor):
- **Legacy ¶1, line 192:** "**The honest word is that** this collage *prefigures*
  Pop." → "It prefigures Pop; it does not found it." (the fact stands on its own).
- **Legacy ¶1, line 192:** "the **defensible statement is** that this 1947
  collage holds the **first known appearance**..." — "defensible statement is" is
  a hedge-label; the "first known appearance" wording already carries the
  caution. → "What it actually holds is the first known appearance of the word
  'pop' inside a work of this kind..."
- **Reception ¶3, line 169:** "treat the exact wording as attributed rather than
  carved in stone" — honesty-label + meta. Move sourcing to a comment, not prose
  (see Storytelling [NICE]).

### [NICE] Mild meta / "people assume" framing
- **Making ¶2, line 126:** "People assume a thing this famous must be a wall-sized
  canvas" and **Reception ¶1, line 162** "Here is a fact that surprises people"
  are both "readers wrongly think X" framings. Not banned outright, but two in one
  read is a tic. Consider letting the smallness land by statement
  ("It is small — about fourteen inches by nine...") rather than by correcting an
  assumed misconception.

### [FIX] `&mdash;` / `&ndash;` literal-entity hazard in data string fields
This is the contract's "&mdash; misused inside a plain TS string field" axis.
**In the JSX prose (`proseStyle` paragraphs) the entities are fine** — JSX renders
`&mdash;`, `&ndash;`, `&ldquo;`, `&rsquo;` correctly. **The risk is the PART A
const and the MeanwhileSheet props**, which are plain TS string values, not JSX —
there an HTML entity renders *literally* as the text `&mdash;`.

Audit of the data strings:
- **PART A const** (`hook`, `blurb`, `detail`, `where`, `note`, stats, credit):
  I see **no `&mdash;`/`&ndash;`/`&ldquo;` entities** in these — they use literal
  ASCII commas, straight quotes `"..."`, and curly `'` apostrophes typed directly
  (e.g. line 76 `Paolozzi`, line 77 `man's`). **GOOD — no entity hazard in const.**
  Confirm at splice time that no entity sneaks in.
- **However:** the const strings contain **literal straight double-quotes inside
  double-quoted JS values is NOT present** (they're inside single-quoted JS
  strings or use `"..."` as content of `'...'`), so no string-termination bug.
  OK.
- **`heroAspect: 0.67` with `// support 359 × 238 mm → W/H ≈ 0.67 (portrait)`** —
  359/238 = 1.51 is H/W; W/H = 238/359 = **0.663 ≈ 0.66**, so 0.67 is right for a
  portrait (W < H). Math checks. Not a voice issue; noting since the comment is
  load-bearing for the hero frame.
- **MeanwhileSheet** (`RmpReception`, lines 173–181): `region`, `when`, `title`,
  `body`, `ctaLabel` are plain string props. They use **straight ASCII** ("That
  same London circle...", single quotes around 'POP', 'This Is Tomorrow') — **no
  `&mdash;` entities. GOOD.** Verify the renderer doesn't HTML-escape, but as
  authored there's no literal-entity hazard.

**Net:** no actual `&mdash;`-in-data bug found, but flagging as a **[FIX]
verify-at-splice** because the draft mixes entity-in-JSX (correct) with
ASCII-in-data (correct) and a careless splice could copy a `&mdash;` from a prose
paragraph into the const `blurb`/`detail` fields, where it would render literally.
Reviewer must confirm post-splice that PART A and the MeanwhileSheet contain zero
`&`-entities.

### [FIX] Literal em-dash check in data strings
The contract also flags a **literal `—` character inside a rendered data string**
when the field is later processed expecting `&mdash;` or ASCII. The PART A blurbs
use literal commas and ASCII throughout (e.g. setting blurb line 56, looking blurb
line 58) — **no literal `—` in const fields. GOOD.** The em-dashes all live in
JSX prose as `&mdash;`, which is correct for that context. No fix needed; recorded
as checked.

### [NICE] Glossing density
Inline definitions are good and contract-compliant (collage/*coller*, ration
books, epidiascope, ICA, Independent Group all defined on first use, in
parentheses, not em-dashes — matches WRITING-RULES). One borderline
**condescending-gloss** risk:
- **Making ¶1, line 123:** "a **collage** (from the French *coller*, 'to glue': a
  picture assembled from glued-on scraps rather than painted)" — this is a genuine
  inline-define of a term, appropriate. Keep.
No condescending glosses found (no "simply put," "in other words for the
layperson," etc.). Good.

### [NICE] Jargon-before-define check — CLEAN
"epidiascope," "Independent Group," "ICA," "collage," "ration books" are each
defined at first use. "BUNK!" is contextualized via Henry Ford's "bunk" line.
No jargon used before its inline definition.
