## Lens 1 — COMPREHENSIVENESS (what's missing?)

The four web-flagged targets — checked, all PRESENT:

- **Self-taught / anti-academic story** — PRESENT and well-handled (ChaTube, beat "The
  ex-cyclist who would not be taught"). Names the drawing lessons + brief study with a
  local painter, the cyclist and violinist lives, the refusal of academic training, and
  the burn-down-the-École boast (framed as legend). Good.
- **1901 van Gogh retrospective debt** — PRESENT and central (ChaTube "The van Gogh that
  hit him"; ChaAfterlife closing symmetry). Correctly: Bernheim-Jeune, 1901, ~70 works,
  no exact day pinned, "loved van Gogh better than his own father" framed as reported.
  Met's own brushwork-emulation line is used. Strong.
- **School of Chatou with Derain** — PRESENT (its own chapter, ChaSchool). Train meeting
  1900, shared studio, two-man informal label, precursor to Fauvism, 1905 naming. Good.
- **Later rejection of Fauvism** — PRESENT (ChaAfterlife "The man who turned on his own
  movement"). Palette darkening toward Cézanne, abandonment of the high key, later
  hostility to modern painting / Cubism / Picasso. Good.

### [FIX] Matisse met Vlaminck *at the 1901 van Gogh show* — a one-clause add
- **Where:** ChaTube, the "Paris · 1901 — The van Gogh that hit him" section, and/or
  ChaSchool.
- **Issue:** The draft tells the van Gogh-retrospective story and the Derain story but
  omits the single most-cited *consequence* of that 1901 room: it is where **Derain
  introduced Vlaminck to Henri Matisse** — i.e., the 1901 show is not only the influence
  event, it is the social origin of the core Fauve trio. The fact pack notes this
  explicitly (beat 4: "Vlaminck (and Derain, and Matisse, who Derain introduced to
  Vlaminck there)"). Web confirms ("Vlaminck met Matisse for the first time in 1901
  during the van Gogh retrospective"). Its omission is the one real comprehensiveness gap:
  the reader currently meets Matisse only as a name in the 1905 room, with no link to the
  Chatou pair.
- **Fix (suggested clause, author's voice):** in the 1901 paragraph, after the van Gogh
  impact, add e.g. — "It was at this same show that Derain introduced Vlaminck to
  **Henri Matisse**, so the room that lit Vlaminck up also, in passing, assembled the
  three painters who would be the core of Fauvism four years later." (Keep it to one
  sentence; this is a NICE-to-FIX, not load-bearing for the *work* read, but it closes
  the loop between the two chapters that currently sit unconnected.)

### [NICE] "First major" van Gogh retrospective — guard the superlative
- **Where:** ChaTube, "In **1901**, the first major retrospective of **Vincent van
  Gogh**…".
- **Issue:** "the first major retrospective" is defensible (the 1901 Bernheim-Jeune show
  is routinely called the first large van Gogh exhibition in Paris / the show that made
  his reputation) and the fact pack says "first major van Gogh retrospective." It is a
  borderline superlative; sources more often say "first large" / "major" without ranking
  it definitively first-ever. No action required, but if the framing gate elsewhere is
  strict on "first" claims, soften to "a large early retrospective" or "the landmark 1901
  retrospective." Low priority — keep if you prefer.

### [NICE] School-of-Chatou studio dates — minor span mismatch
- **Where:** ChaSchool: "for a stretch around 1900 and 1901 they shared a studio at
  Chatou."
- **Issue:** Most sources give the shared-studio / intensive-collaboration period as
  roughly **1900–1902** (some say c.1900–01). The draft's "1900 and 1901" is inside the
  range and the fact pack itself says "c. 1900–01," so this is consistent with the fact
  pack — not an error. Optional: "around 1900 to 1902" tracks the more common phrasing.
  Do not over-precise it; the fact pack is the floor and it says 1900–01. Leave as-is is
  acceptable.

## Lens 2 — FRAMING / FAIRNESS

The two big risks the brief named (over-romanticized wild-genius myth; van-Gogh debt
buried under a pure-originality story) are **both already defused** in the draft. The draft
explicitly says Vlaminck "built a whole identity out of being the untaught outsider,"
frames the boast as "the boast it was," and repeatedly names van Gogh as "the painter
standing behind the Chatou canvas." This is good, honest framing. Two tightenings and one
sensitive-context note follow.

### [FIX] The "rebel/wild self-taught genius" is now a DOCUMENTED self-fashioning reading — make it explicit, don't just imply it
- **Where:** ChaTube, "The ex-cyclist who would not be taught" — currently: "he made a
  point of being unlike every other serious painter… proud of it to the point of
  belligerence… built a whole identity out of being the untaught outsider."
- **Issue (this is the heart of the framing lens):** The draft *gestures* at self-fashioning
  but stops at temperament ("proud," "boast"). Web research (the 2024–25 Museum Barberini
  retrospective, literally titled *Maurice de Vlaminck: Modern Art Rebel*, plus Studio
  International's review) makes a stronger, citable point the draft should land: scholars
  now read the wild-rebel persona as a **deliberately propagated image** — "more than any
  other member of the Fauvists, he identified with the attribute of wildness and
  **propagated the image** of a modern artist rebel," and his self-taught-ness was
  *amplified by* his identification with van Gogh's own outsider-genius myth ("Van Gogh's
  self-taught training and his maturing myth as an unrecognised artistic genius
  strengthened the identification that would also shape Vlaminck"). I.e., Vlaminck partly
  *modeled* his own legend on van Gogh's legend. That is a sharper, fairer framing than
  "he was a brash self-made man," and it is exactly the anti-myth the brief asked for.
- **Fix (suggested, one sentence, author's voice):** after the existing "built a whole
  identity out of being the untaught outsider," add e.g. — "It is worth being honest that
  this was partly a performance: Vlaminck cultivated the wild-man, untaught-rebel image as
  much as he lived it, and he modeled it, knowingly, on van Gogh, the unschooled outsider
  whose own legend he had fallen for in 1901." This converts an implicit hint into the
  documented reading and ties the self-fashioning directly to the van Gogh debt the draft
  already credits — closing both Lens-2 risks in one move.

### [FIX] "Turned on his own movement" / "abandoned" overstates an evolution — soften the verb, keep the substance
- **Where:** ChaAfterlife: section title "The man who turned on his own movement"; body
  "abandoned the bright Fauve key," "became openly hostile to modern painting."
- **Issue:** Two distinct things are being run together, and one is overstated:
  1. **The stylistic shift away from Fauve color (c.1907–08 toward Cézanne)** — web
     sources consistently describe this as a *gradual stylistic evolution*, not an
     "explicit rejection or regret of his Fauvist period." "Abandoned the bright key" is
     fine for the *result*; but the chapter title "turned on his own movement" and the
     framing imply a repudiation that the color-shift alone does not support. The color
     turn is a maturation toward Cézanne (whose 1907 retrospective hit him), not a
     renunciation.
  2. **The later hostility to modernism / Cubism / Picasso** — THIS is real, documented,
     and is the actual "turned hostile" content (the body paragraph gets it right). It is
     hostility to *Cubism/Picasso and the modern-art direction*, not specifically a
     recantation of his own Fauvism.
- **Why it matters for fairness:** conflating "he moved past his Fauve palette" with "he
  turned on his own movement" risks a tidy-but-false arc (rebel-renounces-rebellion). The
  honest version: his *palette* evolved toward Cézanne; *separately and later*, he became
  a vocal reactionary against modernism/Cubism. The draft body actually says this
  correctly — it is the **chapter title and the "abandoned the bright Fauve key"** framing
  that lean too hard.
- **Fix:** (a) retitle the section to target the real object of his hostility, e.g.
  "The man who turned on modern painting" (he turned on Cubism/Picasso/modernism, not
  cleanly on "his own movement"); and (b) optionally soften "abandoned" → "left behind"
  or "moved past" the bright key, to mark it as evolution. The substance (darkening,
  Cézanne, anti-Picasso) is all correct and well-sourced — this is a verb/title precision
  fix, not a content cut.

### [FIX] No Eurocentric / "first" overclaim — one phrase to watch
- **Where:** ChaSchool closing: "a Fauve painting made on the home ground where Fauvism's
  color had first been tried out, years before anyone thought to call it anything."
- **Issue:** Mild. "where Fauvism's color had first been tried out" reads as a "Chatou was
  the birthplace / first site of Fauve color" claim. Fauvism's color has multiple roots
  (Matisse at Collioure with Derain in summer 1905; van Gogh and the Neo-Impressionists
  before that; Gauguin). The School of Chatou is *a* precursor / *one of the* sources of
  Fauvism (fact pack and web both say "one of the sources"), not the singular first
  trial-ground of Fauve color. The draft elsewhere correctly says "a precursor" and "one
  of the two men" — this one closing phrase overreaches into an origin-claim.
- **Fix:** soften to "where this kind of bold, anti-naturalistic colour had been tried out
  early, years before anyone thought to call it anything," or "one of the places Fauve
  colour was worked out early." Drop the implied "first/birthplace." This is the only
  Eurocentric/first-style overclaim in the draft and it is small.

### [NICE] The 1942 *Comœdia* Picasso attack — sensitive context, currently omitted (correctly optional)
- **Where:** ChaAfterlife, the anti-Picasso paragraph.
- **Issue/judgment:** The fact pack flags that Vlaminck's most notorious attack on Picasso
  ("dragging French art into a wretched dead end") was published in **1942 in the
  collaborationist paper *Comœdia*** under the German occupation — an ugly wartime context.
  The draft's paraphrase "blaming Picasso for dragging French art into a dead end" uses
  this material **without** the collaborationist context. For a *work* read this is a
  defensible scope call (the fact pack itself says "not needed for the core work read"),
  and including the occupation politics could unbalance a short afterlife coda. **No fix
  required.** But flagging for the integrator's awareness: if the quote-phrase stays, it is
  drawn from a collaboration-era polemic; either keep the paraphrase generic (as now) or,
  if ever expanded, do not launder the 1942/*Comœdia* context. Current handling is
  acceptable.

### Framing items checked and CLEAN (no action):
- **van Gogh credited honestly, not buried** — CLEAN. Van Gogh is named as the decisive
  influence in the hook, the figures list, two chapters, and the closing symmetry. No
  pure-originality genius-myth. This is the model for how to do it.
- **Legend quotes** — CLEAN. Both the "burn down the École" and "better than my own
  father" lines are framed as reported/boast, undated, with the no-clean-primary caveat
  stated in prose. Exactly per fact pack.
- **Provenance honesty** — CLEAN. The draft refuses to invent the middle of the chain,
  states only Gelman→Met, and explicitly does NOT assert Vollard owned this canvas. Good
  fairness on documentation limits.
- **Vauxcelles "fauves" as insult, painters didn't choose it** — CLEAN and fair.
