The line works rhetorically but isn't factually anchored. **Optional hedge** — say "Renoir's late bathers" rather than imputing a specific quality. ## Chapter 3 — `cezanne` (Aix · 1870s–1906)

### MUST-FIX
*(none)*

### SHOULD-FIX

6. **"Banque Cézanne-Cabassol" — bank name.**
   The factpack says: *"co-founded Banque Cézanne-Cabassol."* The actual historical name is normally given as **Banque Cabassol** (Cabassol & Cézanne, or Cézanne et Cabassol). Wikipedia French and the Cézanne biographies refer to it as **Banque Cabassol** with Cézanne as a partner. The factpack's "Banque Cézanne-Cabassol" form is unusual. **Verify against the Cézanne Wikipedia page** before locking in; if the canonical English form is "Banque Cabassol" or "Cabassol & Cézanne," update both factpack and draft. Flag as **[VERIFY]**.

7. **"The Jas de Bouffan ('the gust-house')"**
   Line: *"at his own farmhouse, the **Jas de Bouffan** ('the gust-house,' the rambling Cézanne family estate just outside Aix)."*
   "Jas de Bouffan" is **Occitan for "sheepfold of the wind"** — *jas* = sheepfold, *de bouffan* = of the wind/gust. "Gust-house" is a passable colloquialization but slightly off — *jas* is not "house," it's a shepherd's pen / fold. **Recommend** softening to: *"the 'sheepfold of the wind' in Provençal"* or "the windy fold." This is also where the Card Players' models worked (factpack §10 / W-Cards confirms).

8. **"The fifth version sold privately in 2011, reportedly to the Qatari royal family, for a figure variously reported between $250 and $320 million."**
   Verified against W-Cards. Sale was 2011, to Qatar, $250–320M reported range. Fine.

9. **"Bathers… the biggest, at the Philadelphia Museum of Art, is about 7 ft × 8 ft 2 in"**
   Factpack §10 says "~210 × 250 cm" = 6 ft 10.7 in × 8 ft 2.4 in. The draft rounds to "about 7 ft × 8 ft 2 in" — acceptable.

10. **"Picasso would say, later, that 'Cézanne was my one and only master.'"**
    This is real, attributed to Picasso; factpack §9 has it. Fine.

11. **"Braque would later say that the late Cézannes were like discovering a country no one had been in."**
    This paraphrases Braque's actual remark *"It is more than an influence, it is an initiation"* and similar. The specific "country no one had been in" wording is **not in the factpack** and is not a verbatim Braque quote. **Recommend:** rephrase to a paraphrase without quotation marks: *"Braque later said the late Cézannes were like a revelation."*

### NICE-TO-HAVE

12. **"He had been working outdoors, was caught in a thunderstorm, kept painting, walked home soaked, collapsed in the street…"**
    Standard story; sourced in factpack §5 ("caught in a rainstorm while painting, pneumonia"). The "collapsed in the street" detail is the traditional version. Fine.

## Chapter 4 — `van-gogh` (Arles · 1888–1890)

### MUST-FIX

13. **"He painted nearly 200 canvases in those Paris months."**
    Line: *"He painted nearly 200 canvases in those Paris months."*
    **Wikipedia (Vincent van Gogh):** Van Gogh produced ~200 paintings during his entire two-year Paris period (March 1886 – Feb 1888). The factpack does not specify the Paris-period count; this is a reasonable estimate. **However**, the wording "in those Paris months" understates the time (it was 24 months, not "months"). **Fix:** change to *"He painted nearly 200 canvases in his two Paris years."*

14. **"produced something like 900 paintings in his life, sold very few of them for actual money"**
    Line (Chapter 2): *"produced something like 900 paintings in his life, sold very few of them for actual money"*
    Wikipedia: ~860 oil paintings, ~2,100 artworks total. "Around 900" is fine for oil paintings; the precise canonical figure is ~860. **No fix required** if "paintings" is read as "oil paintings."

15. **"It is the most reproduced single picture in Western art after the *Mona Lisa*, and Van Gogh painted it locked up."**
    The "most reproduced after Mona Lisa" claim is conventional wisdom but unsourced. Factpack §8.4 hedges it as "the most-reproduced painting in Western art outside the *Mona Lisa*." Echoes the pack — fine. (The factpack already inherits this claim; if the coordinator wants to strip it, do so in both places.)

16. **"Theo died six months later, on 25 January 1891, age 33, in Utrecht — of complications related to syphilis"**
    Factpack §5 confirms: Theo dies 25 January 1891. The location is given in the factpack only as "six months later (syphilis-related complications, age 33)." Wikipedia says Theo died in Utrecht. **Verify** the Utrecht detail — it's standard biographical fact (he was committed to an asylum in Utrecht where he died). Confirmed via Wikipedia (Theo van Gogh art dealer). Fine.

17. **"He stepped off in Arles, in Provence, looking — he wrote — for 'the Japan of the south,' meaning the clean, bright, hard light"**
    Van Gogh's "Japan of the south" / "le Japon du Midi" phrase is real (Letters to Theo). Factpack doesn't explicitly cite it but it's well documented. Fine.

18. **"The town was a sleepy provincial Roman survival of about 20,000 people on the Rhône."**
    Arles in 1888 was around 23,000 (Wikipedia, demographic history). "About 20,000" is fine.

19. **"He stayed for a year."** (re Saint-Rémy)
    Factpack §5: "8 May 1889 – May 1890" — that's slightly over a year. The draft's "a year" is fine.

20. **"the Auberge Ravoux, the inn where he was lodging"**
    Confirmed. Fine.

21. **"Wheatfield with Crows … treat as one of his last, not the last."**
    Standing refusal held — draft does this correctly.

### SHOULD-FIX

22. **"a woman known in the records as **Rachel**"**
    The Wikipedia article on Self-Portrait with Bandaged Ear confirms a prostitute named **Rachel** received the ear. The 2016 research by Bernadette Murphy (*Van Gogh's Ear: The True Story*) argues her real name was actually **Gabrielle Berlatier**, a young farm girl who worked at the brothel as a cleaning maid, not a sex worker, and that "Rachel" was a nickname. **Not blocking** — the draft's "a woman known in the records as Rachel" is appropriately hedged. **Nice-to-have:** add a parenthetical: *"(more recent research argues her real name was Gabrielle Berlatier and she was a cleaning maid, not a prostitute, but the brothel detail still holds)."*

23. **"shot himself in the chest with a small revolver"**
    Line: *"and shot himself in the chest with a small revolver."*
    Wikipedia: the **traditional account is a chest wound** (Adeline Ravoux: "a wound near his heart"), but Naifeh-Smith argue for an abdominal entry. The standing refusal allows either phrasing as long as Naifeh-Smith is flagged as hypothesis. **Recommend:** soften to *"shot himself in the upper torso"* or *"shot himself"* (no body part) to dodge the chest-vs-abdomen contestation, since the draft does mention Naifeh-Smith further down. **Not blocking** — current wording is the traditional account.

24. **"Bedroom in Arles, October 1888, now Van Gogh Museum"**
    Factpack §8.3 confirms first version is October 1888, Van Gogh Museum. Fine.

25. **"He furnished it, slowly, on Theo's money. He bought a bed. He painted a picture of his bedroom"**
    Factpack confirms the Yellow House rental (May 1888) and bedroom painting (Oct 1888). Fine.

26. **"twelve giant single-stalked sunflowers in a yellow vase"**
    The famous Sunflowers (F.454, National Gallery London version) shows ~15 sunflowers, NOT 12. Other versions vary (12 in some, 15 in others). Wikipedia (Sunflowers series): the NG London version has 15. The 1888 series produced four versions in Arles (three with 14-15, one with 12). **MUST-FIX:** drop the specific count or say "a dozen-plus" or "a vase full of." Saying "twelve giant single-stalked sunflowers" is wrong for the painting most readers will be picturing.
    Source: Wikipedia, *Sunflowers (Van Gogh series)*.

27. **"the spare bedroom, the one he was preparing for the friend he was hoping above all to come south."**
    Confirmed — Van Gogh painted the Sunflowers explicitly to decorate Gauguin's room. Fine.

### NICE-TO-HAVE

28. **"He had been working at the rate of about a canvas a day for two and a half years"**
    Rough but defensible — Van Gogh's Arles+Saint-Rémy+Auvers output (~Feb 1888–Jul 1890, ~30 months) is ~400–450 canvases, so ~a canvas every 2 days. "A canvas a day" is slightly inflated; **soften** to "at near-frenzied pace" or "almost a canvas a day during the most prolific stretches."

29. **"the bullet was lodged where they couldn't get at it"**
    Confirmed — neither Mazery nor Gachet attempted extraction. Fine.

30. **"Theo arrived from Paris the next morning."**
    Correct.

## Chapter 5 — `gauguin` (Pont-Aven · 1886; Tahiti · 1891–1903)

### MUST-FIX
*(none)*

### SHOULD-FIX

31. **"working at the firm of Galichon Bourdon"**
    Factpack §5: "stockbroker, Paris (Galichon Bourdon)." Wikipedia says **Paul Bertin's brokerage** (Bertin) was where he started, then **Galichon**. The exact firm name varies by source. Factpack accepts "Galichon Bourdon"; draft mirrors. **Verify** — the form **"Bertin"** or **"Bourdon, agent de change"** is more often given. Flag **[VERIFY]** but not blocking.

32. **"He moved the family to Copenhagen in 1884 to stay with Mette's parents."**
    Wikipedia confirms 1884 move to Copenhagen. Fine.

33. **"By 1885 he had walked out of the marriage."**
    Confirmed — Gauguin left Mette in June 1885, returning to Paris alone. Fine.

34. **"He met a younger painter named Émile Bernard — 19 years old"**
    Bernard born 28 April 1868. In summer 1888 he was **20**, not 19. Met Gauguin at Pont-Aven, August 1888. **Fix:** change to "20 years old" or "around 20." (Factpack §6 gives Bernard's dates as 1868–1941.)

35. **"On 1 April 1891, Gauguin sailed from Marseille for Tahiti. He arrived in Papeete on 9 June 1891."**
    Factpack confirms departure 1 April; arrival 9 June. Wikipedia confirms 1 April 1891 sailing. The 9 June 1891 arrival is in the factpack but Wikipedia gives the arrival as "after a 63-day voyage" — i.e. early June. **Verify:** common date is 8 or 9 June 1891. Not blocking.

36. **"attempting suicide with arsenic in late 1897 (he survived — the dose was too strong and he vomited it up)."**
    Factpack §5: "**1897–98** attempts suicide by arsenic." The traditional date is **December 1897 / January 1898**. The "vomited it up" detail is real but typically described as "the dose was too large and his body rejected it," not always specifying vomiting. **Acceptable.**

37. **"Teha'amana" / "Pau'ura a Tai" / "Vaeoho Marie-Rose"** — ages and chronology.
    The factpack does **not** detail these names or ages — this is content the draft has imported from outside the pack. The information is documented (Belinda Thomson, Gauguin: Maker of Myth; Tate 2010 exhibition catalogue; etc.). The ages are:
    - **Teha'amana**: traditionally said to be 13–14 when Gauguin took her in (1892). Some scholars (Sue Prideaux et al.) hedge that her actual age may have been older.
    - **Pau'ura**: ~14 when their relationship began in 1896.
    - **Vaeoho Marie-Rose**: 14 when she moved in with Gauguin in 1901; she gave birth to a daughter in 1902, age 15.
    The draft's "all of whom were adolescents" framing is correct. Because this is outside the factpack, **MUST-FIX:** flag to the coordinator to either (a) confirm against Gauguin Wikipedia and biographers and add to factpack, or (b) soften to a less name-specific framing. Two of the three names are anglicizations/orthographies that vary across sources (*Tehura* vs *Teha'amana*, *Pahura* vs *Pau'ura*). **Recommend:** keep the framing, but trim to "two of his successive Polynesian partners were 13–14 when those relationships began" and drop the third name unless re-verified. — Marking as **[VERIFY-NAMES]** rather than MUST-FIX since the substance is correct.

38. **"He referred to all three as his 'wives' in his journals."**
    Defensible; Gauguin uses *vahiné* (Tahitian for wife/woman) in *Noa Noa* and letters. Fine.

39. **"He died on 8 May 1903 on Hiva Oa, age 54, of a heart attack while taking morphine for the syphilis pain."**
    Factpack: death date confirmed. Cause of death is variously given as heart attack, morphine overdose, or syphilis complications. Wikipedia: "He died of syphilis-related causes" / "morphine overdose." The "heart attack while taking morphine" is one version. **Soften:** "died … of complications of syphilis and morphine use" is safer.

### NICE-TO-HAVE

40. **"The French Catholic bishop, with whom Gauguin had been feuding bitterly, buried him in the local cemetery."**
    The bishop was Joseph Martin; Gauguin had attacked him in pamphlets. Confirmed.

41. **"His pictures and effects were auctioned cheap on Tahiti the following autumn."**
    Factpack §5: "The estate sale on Tahiti, 1903." The auction was September 1903 in Papeete. Fine.

## Chapter 6 — `seurat-lautrec` (Paris · 1884–1901)

### MUST-FIX

42. **"the first one ran from 15 May to 1 July 1884, in an army barracks on the Champs-Élysées"**
    Line: *"They would mount an annual **Salon des Indépendants** every spring (the first one ran from 15 May to 1 July 1884, in an army barracks on the Champs-Élysées; it would move to a beer hall later that year and to the Pavillon de la Ville de Paris in subsequent years)."*
    **Wrong on venue.** Wikipedia (*Salon des Indépendants*): the first Salon ran 15 May – 15 July 1884 (not 1 July), at the **Pavillon de la Ville de Paris (Champs-Élysées)**. Wikipedia (*Bathers at Asnières*) says the painting was hung in "an unglamorous location of the exhibition beer hall" in a "temporary building in the place du Carrousel, adjacent to the Louvre" — sources disagree on the venue, but **"army barracks" is not in any of them** and appears to be a draft fabrication. **FIX:** change to "the **Pavillon de la Ville de Paris** on the Champs-Élysées" (the most-cited venue) OR drop the venue and just give the dates. The "beer hall" the Bathers were stuck in was an annex within the exhibition complex, not a separate venue.
    Source: Wikipedia, *Salon des Indépendants*; Wikipedia, *Bathers at Asnières*.

43. **"the American physicist Ogden Rood's *Modern Chromatics* (New York, 1879; translated into French in 1881 as *Théorie scientifique des couleurs*)"**
    Verified — Wikipedia confirms American physicist, 1879 New York, French translation 1881. Fine. *(Listed as MUST-FIX only because the factpack itself only specifies American + 1879 + 1881; draft adds "physicist" which is correct per Wikipedia.)* — actually, **no fix needed.** Move to CLEAR.

44. **"The whole modern poster industry — Mucha, Cassandre, the entire 20th-century commercial graphic look — descends from this single sheet of paper, glued to a wall in Paris in November 1891."**
    The 1891 *Moulin Rouge: La Goulue* poster is December 1891 in some sources, October–November 1891 in others. Factpack §5 just says "1891, his first poster." **Soften** to "in autumn 1891" or "in 1891" rather than nailing it to November. **Not blocking** but factually loose. → SHOULD-FIX.

### SHOULD-FIX

45. **"It is now in the Musée d'Orsay, Paris, RF 1985-13"**
    The Talisman: Factpack §6 says **RF 1985-13**. Confirmed via Musée d'Orsay collection. Fine. (Listed in Chapter 7, not 6.)

46. **"In the right foreground a face looms up close, sliced off by the canvas edge, lit a lurid pale green from below — this is the English dancer May Milton."**
    Verified against Wikipedia (*At the Moulin Rouge*): "English dancer May Milton, with painted red lips, her face aglow in a distinctive greenish light and shadow." The draft says "pale **green**" — confirmed. Fine.

47. **"Lautrec himself painted into the picture, the short top-hatted man in the back beside his tall cousin Gabriel"**
    Wikipedia confirms Lautrec is in the center-left background "standing in front of and next to, **Dr. Gabriel Tapié de Céleyran**." Wikipedia does **not** describe Gabriel as "tall" — but it's standard biographical fact that Tapié de Céleyran was tall (over 6 ft), and his pairing with Lautrec is the visual joke of the picture (short cousin / tall cousin). The "tall" detail is defensible from photographic record. **Optional soften:** "his cousin Gabriel Tapié de Céleyran" (drop "tall," or add "in profile"). **Not blocking.**

48. **"the right-hand strip with May Milton's face was removed for decades, until AIC reattached it in 1959."**
    Factpack §8.9 confirms: "AIC reattached it in 1959." Fine.

49. **"In the upper half of the picture — separated from the women by a hard diagonal trunk of an apple tree slicing the canvas — Jacob and the angel are actually visible"**
    [VERIFY-VISUAL] — The Wikipedia article on *Vision after the Sermon* describes the diagonal divider only as "a tree trunk," not specifically as an apple tree. The factpack §8.5 also calls it "a diagonal **apple-tree trunk**." This is the **conventional** scholarly reading (echoed in the Scottish National Gallery's own materials and Belinda Thomson, Belinda Thomson's Gauguin, Christopher Lloyd's *Gauguin*) — Gauguin's biblical reference is to Jacob wrestling beneath a tree, and the apple tree reading derives from Bretagne's orchard landscapes. **Defensible**, but the writer should be aware Wikipedia doesn't lock it. Mark **[VERIFY-VISUAL]** but acceptable as drafted.

50. **"a 24-year-old painter who had just had his first major canvas — a six-and-a-half-by-ten-foot picture"**
    Seurat was born 2 Dec 1859; in spring 1884 he was 24. **Bathers at Asnières** was rejected by the 1884 Salon. Confirmed. Dimensions 201 × 301 cm = ~6 ft 7 in × 9 ft 10½ in — "six-and-a-half-by-ten-foot" is a fair approximation. Fine.

51. **"the bored prostitutes between customers"**
    Lautrec's brothel scenes are well documented; the "between customers" reading is correct. Fine.

52. **"He drank himself to death. Heavily. **Absinthe** mostly"**
    Lautrec did drink heavily and absinthe was a part of it, but he was famously fond of cocktails too (he invented the "Earthquake" cocktail of cognac and absinthe). "Absinthe mostly" is a slight simplification. Not blocking.

### NICE-TO-HAVE

53. **"the first time a serious painter had put his full design intelligence into a commercial street poster"**
    Defensible but rhetorical — Chéret had been making large color lithograph posters since the 1860s, and was the actual inventor of the modern lithographic poster. Lautrec's innovation was treating it as fine art with the full vocabulary of post-Impressionist composition. **Soften:** "the first time a major painter had put his full painterly intelligence into a commercial street poster." (Distinguishes "serious painter" from "professional poster artist like Chéret.")

54. **"about 4 ft 0½ in × 4 ft 7¼ in"** (At the Moulin Rouge dimensions)
    Factpack §8.9: 123 × 140.5 cm. 123 cm = 4 ft 0.4 in; 140.5 cm = 4 ft 7.3 in. Match. Fine.

55. **"3 February 1888 he had had enough of Paris and got on a train south"**
    Actually the draft says "By February 1888" without a specific date. The actual departure was around **19–20 February 1888**. Acceptable.

## Chapter 8 — `fry` (London · 8 November 1910)

### MUST-FIX

67. **"Roger Fry was 44 years old in 1910"**
    Line: *"Roger Fry was 44 years old in 1910, an English critic…"*
    **Wikipedia:** Fry was born **14 December 1866**. In November 1910, when the show opened, he was **43 years old** (he turned 44 on 14 December 1910, after the show had opened).
    **FIX:** change to *"Roger Fry was 43 years old in 1910"* — or, safer, *"Roger Fry was in his mid-forties in 1910"* / *"about to turn 44 when the show opened."*
    Source: Wikipedia, *Roger Fry*.
