## GLOBAL RULES FOR ALL MEDIEVAL JAPAN PROMPTS — read before drawing any map in this file

**CRITICAL RULES — read carefully before drawing:**

1. **Label cleanly.** Spell every label correctly. A long river or coast MAY be labeled more than once along its course (normal cartography) as long as each instance is correct and the repeats are well separated — not touching, stacked, or adjacent. Do NOT duplicate a city/site/region label, do NOT place two copies of a label next to each other, and do NOT repeat an annotation/caption.
2. **Spell every label exactly as written in this prompt.** Do not invent words. Do not abbreviate. Do not generate any word that is not in the prompt text.
3. **Include the exact site name on every label.** The site name is the word or phrase before the first parenthesis or em-dash. Never drop a site name. The parenthetical after a site name is placement guidance for you — it is NOT label text and must NOT be drawn.
4. **North is at the top of the map. East is to the right.** Place every location according to its real-world geography in this orientation. Do not rotate or tilt the map.
5. **All water is light blue.** The Pacific Ocean, the Sea of Japan, the Inland Sea, bays, straits, lakes, and rivers are rendered in light blue. Do not render water as tan, cream, or white. Land is beige/tan; mountainous high ground is gray-brown; plains and lowlands are soft green.
6. **Do not invent any labels, annotations, sites, or features that are not in this prompt.** If it is not in the prompt, do not draw it.
7. **Geography correct & readable.** Place features in their correct real-world locations. Faint modern country/region outlines or labels as orientation aids are *welcome*. Real geography defects = invented/misspelled place names, wrong locations, or labels contradicting the prompt — NOT the presence of reference outlines.
8. **No compass words as labels.** Never print the words North, South, East, West (or the letters N/S/E/W) anywhere on the image.
9. **No ornamental glyphs.** No decorative star, four-point sparkle, twinkle, compass-rose flourish, or corner ornament.

The chapter title "Chapter N: <Title>" should appear at the top of the map image, centered, in a clean serif font, inside a header bar that spans the full width of the image. The header bar is the ONLY bordered element of the image. Draw NO border, outline, frame, rectangle, or hairline anywhere else — the map and its water must bleed off all four outer edges. Never enclose the map in a rectangle. Render the chapter title **exactly once**, only inside that single full-width header bar.

**MEDIEVAL JAPAN ORIENTATION RULES:**

1. North is at the top. East is to the right. This is the Japanese archipelago: a long arc of mountainous islands off the eastern edge of Asia, the islands filling the center and right of the map, the Asian mainland at the far left (west) across the sea.
2. The main island is **Honshu**, a long curved island running roughly southwest-to-northeast down the middle of the map; almost everything in this story happens on it or just off it. Southwest of Honshu is the island of **Kyushu** (the part of Japan closest to the Asian mainland). East of Kyushu, across a narrow sea, is the smaller island of **Shikoku**, tucked under the southern side of Honshu.
3. The **Sea of Japan** is the body of water to the west and northwest of Honshu, between the islands and the Asian mainland. The **Pacific Ocean** is the open water to the east and south of the islands. The **Inland Sea** is the sheltered strip of water enclosed between southwestern Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu.
4. **Kyoto** is the old capital, on the broad inland plain of central-western Honshu (the Kansai region); it holds the emperor and the court through the whole story. **Nara** is just south of Kyoto, the older imperial capital and great-temple center. **Osaka** is on the coast just southwest of Kyoto, at the head of the Inland Sea. **Lake Biwa** is the large lake just northeast of Kyoto.
5. The **Kantō** is the wide flat plain in the east of Honshu (where Tokyo stands today). **Kamakura** sits on the Kantō coast and is the seat of the first warrior government; **Edo** (the future Tokyo) sits on the same Kantō plain and becomes the seat of the last. **Odawara** is on the Kantō's southwestern edge.
6. On Kyushu, **Hakata Bay** is the broad harbor on the north coast (the natural gateway from the mainland); **Nagasaki** and **Kagoshima** are ports on the southwest; **Tanegashima** is a small island off the southern tip of Kyushu.
7. **Korea** and the **Asian mainland** lie across the sea to the WEST. Show the southern Korean coast at the far left edge when a chapter names it (for the Mongol invasions, which sailed from Korea to Hakata Bay, and for Hideyoshi's Korea campaigns).
8. Always keep Honshu the long main island down the center, Kyushu to its southwest, Shikoku tucked beneath it, the Sea of Japan to the west/northwest, the Pacific to the east/south, and Korea and the mainland at the far left across the sea.
