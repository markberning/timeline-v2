## GLOBAL RULES FOR ALL LATIN AMERICAN INDEPENDENCE PROMPTS — read before drawing any map in this file

**CRITICAL RULES — read carefully before drawing:**

1. **Label cleanly.** Spell every label correctly. A long river or coast MAY be labeled more than once along its course (normal cartography) as long as each instance is correct and the repeats are well separated — not touching, stacked, or adjacent. Do NOT duplicate a city/site/region label, do NOT place two copies of a label next to each other, and do NOT repeat an annotation/caption.
2. **Spell every label exactly as written in this prompt.** Do not invent words. Do not abbreviate. Do not generate any word that is not in the prompt text.
3. **Include the exact site name on every label.** The site name is the word or phrase before the first parenthesis or em-dash. Never drop a site name. The parenthetical after a site name is placement guidance for you — it is NOT label text and must NOT be drawn.
4. **North is at the top of the map. East is to the right.** Place every location according to its real-world geography in this orientation. Do not rotate or tilt the map.
5. **All water is light blue.** The oceans, seas, gulfs, lakes, and rivers are rendered in light blue. Do not render water as tan, cream, or white. Land is beige/tan; high mountains (the Andes) are gray-brown; deserts are pale yellow; tropical lowland/rainforest is soft green.
6. **Do not invent any labels, annotations, sites, or features that are not in this prompt.** If it is not in the prompt, do not draw it.
7. **Geography correct & readable.** Place features in their correct real-world locations. Faint modern country/region outlines or labels as orientation aids are *welcome*. Real geography defects = invented/misspelled place names, wrong locations, or labels contradicting the prompt — NOT the presence of reference outlines.
8. **No compass words as labels.** Never print the words North, South, East, West (or the letters N/S/E/W) anywhere on the image.
9. **No ornamental glyphs.** No decorative star, four-point sparkle, twinkle, compass-rose flourish, or corner ornament.

The chapter title "Chapter N: <Title>" should appear at the top of the map image, centered, in a clean serif font, inside a header bar that spans the full width of the image. The header bar is the ONLY bordered element of the image. Draw NO border, outline, frame, rectangle, or hairline anywhere else — the map and its water must bleed off all four outer edges. Never enclose the map in a rectangle. Render the chapter title **exactly once**, only inside that single full-width header bar.

**LATIN AMERICAN INDEPENDENCE ORIENTATION RULES:**

1. North is at the top. East is to the right. This is the Americas: **North America** with Mexico narrowing into the **Central America** isthmus at the top-left, the **Caribbean** islands across the upper-center, and the great triangle of **South America** filling the lower two-thirds of the frame, tapering south toward Patagonia at the bottom.
2. The **Andes** are the long mountain spine running down the entire western (left) edge of South America, parallel to the Pacific coast — render them gray-brown. The **Pacific Ocean** is the water to the WEST (left) of the Andes; the **Atlantic Ocean** is the water to the EAST (right) of South America; the **Caribbean Sea** is between Central America and the north coast of South America.
3. Place the major cities in their real positions: **Mexico City** (central Mexico, top-left), **Caracas** (the north coast of South America, on the Caribbean), **Bogotá** (high in the Andes of New Granada / Colombia, inland from the Caribbean coast), **Lima** (the central Pacific coast of Peru), **Buenos Aires** (the Atlantic coast far to the south, at the mouth of the Río de la Plata), **Santiago** (the Pacific side of the southern Andes, in Chile), **Rio de Janeiro** (the Atlantic coast of Brazil, southeast).
4. The great rivers, all light blue: the **Orinoco River** (Venezuela, draining north and east to the Atlantic), the **Amazon River** (the huge river crossing Brazil west-to-east to the Atlantic), and the **Río de la Plata** (the wide estuary on the southeast Atlantic coast at Buenos Aires/Montevideo).
5. Keep the four old Spanish viceroyalty zones roughly where they belong when relevant: **New Spain** (Mexico/Central America, top-left), **New Granada** (the northwest of South America — Colombia/Ecuador/Venezuela), **Peru** (the central Andean Pacific zone), and the **Río de la Plata** (the southeast — Argentina/Uruguay/Paraguay/Bolivia). **Brazil** is the vast Portuguese bulge filling the Atlantic (eastern) side of South America.
6. Faint modern country outlines as orientation aids are welcome. Always keep the Pacific WEST (left), the Atlantic EAST (right), the Caribbean across the top of South America, and the Andes hugging the western coast.
