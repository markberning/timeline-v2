# War map prompts — Forts Jackson & St. Philip (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map forts-jackson-strategy

The-great-port — strategic lower Mississippi approach, April 1862. The South's greatest city left open at the bottom of a river. North up.

Draw the Gulf of Mexico as a wide light-blue fill across the BOTTOM third of the frame. Draw the Mississippi River as a winding light-blue band running from the Gulf northward (upward) to the top of the frame, where a gray city block sits labeled "New Orleans". Show the river's mouth opening broadly into the Gulf at the base, labeled "Head of Passes" at the point where the river splits into distributary channels before reaching the Gulf. Partway up — roughly one third of the way between the Gulf and New Orleans — place two small RUST-RED pentagonal fort blocks facing each other across the river: one on the west (left) bank labeled "Fort Jackson", one slightly upstream on the east (right) bank labeled "Fort St. Philip". Draw a thin dark horizontal line between the two forts across the river to represent the chain-and-hulk barrier. Show Farragut's Union fleet as a bold BLUE arrow entering from the Gulf at the bottom, running up the river, aimed at the fort pair and beyond toward New Orleans.

Six labels: New Orleans · Fort Jackson · Fort St. Philip · Head of Passes · Mississippi River · Gulf of Mexico.

## Map forts-jackson

Running-the-forts — the April 24, 1862 night passage. Farragut's fleet threads the chain barrier between the two Confederate forts under fire. North up; the river flows south-to-north toward New Orleans.

Draw the Mississippi River as a broad light-blue band running up the center of the frame with a gentle bend, the south (Gulf side) at the bottom and New Orleans implied off the top. On the WEST (left) bank draw a RUST-RED pentagonal fort block labeled "Fort Jackson". On the EAST (right) bank, set slightly UPSTREAM (higher on the frame), draw a second RUST-RED fort block labeled "Fort St. Philip". Between the two forts draw a tight row of small dark blocks strung across the river labeled "Chain Barrier". Show the breach in the chain as a gap near the east bank (close to Fort St. Philip). Draw RUST-RED fire-arc wedges projecting from both forts into the river channel, aimed downstream (southward), to show the cross-fire the fleet had to run through. Show the Union fleet as a column of BLUE ship blocks with a bold BLUE arrow threading up through the gap in the chain, squeezed between the two forts, and emerging clear of the guns to the north.

Five labels: Fort Jackson · Fort St. Philip · Chain Barrier · Mississippi River · New Orleans.

## Map forts-jackson-city

The-fall-of-new-orleans — from the forts to the city, April 25–28, 1862. The fleet past the guns, the Chalmette batteries brushed aside, the city undefended. North up.

Draw the Mississippi River as a sinuous light-blue band running from the bottom of the frame northward. At the bottom of the frame, show the two RUST-RED fort blocks (smaller, behind the fleet now) labeled "Forts" — these are Fort Jackson and Fort St. Philip, already bypassed. A few miles above the forts, on the east bank, draw a small RUST-RED gun-line labeled "Chalmette" to mark the batteries Farragut overwhelmed on the way up. Continuing upriver to the top, draw a gray city block on the river labeled "New Orleans" with a small dock/levee mark on the riverfront. Show a bold BLUE arrow running up the river from the forts past Chalmette all the way to New Orleans to trace the fleet's final approach. Off to the northeast of the city, place a small dot labeled "Camp Moore" to mark where Confederate General Lovell withdrew his troops — roughly 78 miles to the north but shown schematically.

Six labels: Fort Jackson · Chalmette · New Orleans · Camp Moore · Mississippi River · Gulf of Mexico.
