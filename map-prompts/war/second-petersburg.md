# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map second-petersburg-overview

The stolen march to the back door, mid-June 1864 — Grant slips the whole army across the James and reaches for Petersburg from the east while Lee looks the wrong way. A strategic setting overview, north up.

Across the TOP (north) place a small gray city block labeled "Richmond". A short way below it, in the center, place a second gray town block labeled "Petersburg", sitting on the south bank of a flat light-blue river band running left-to-right through the center labeled "Appomattox River". Off the upper-RIGHT (northeast), draw a wider flat light-blue river band angling down toward the field labeled "James River"; at the point where it bends nearest the field set a small gray block straddling the water labeled "pontoon bridge" — the crossing where the Union army slipped over. Wrap the EAST face of Petersburg in a long RUST-RED entrenched arc — a thick rust-red line bristling with short tick marks (earthworks) curving north-to-south to ring the town's eastern side — labeled "Dimmock Line". From the "pontoon bridge" crossing send one bold tapered BLUE arrow sweeping down and to the LEFT, curling toward Petersburg from the EAST, its point aimed at the rust-red Dimmock Line. Keep Richmond calm and unthreatened; the whole motion is the single blue arrow reaching for the town's back door.

The only text anywhere on the map is the six labels below, each bold near-black on a solid white pill, spelled EXACTLY as written (note: "Appomattox River" — two p's, two t's). Render NO other text — no color words, no terrain or instruction words.

Six labels only: Richmond, Petersburg, Appomattox River, James River, pontoon bridge, Dimmock Line.

## Map second-petersburg-june-15

June 15, 1864 — Hinks's U.S. Colored Troops storm the thinly held eastern face of the Dimmock Line and carry a string of its batteries at the point of the bayonet. A tactical assault view, north up.

Place a gray town block labeled "Petersburg" in the lower-LEFT (west), on the south bank of a flat light-blue river band along the TOP edge labeled "Appomattox River". Curving down the RIGHT-center of the field, between the town and the east edge, draw the eastern stretch of the enemy works as a RUST-RED entrenched line bristling with short tick marks, labeled "Dimmock Line". Along that rust-red line set two small gray arrowhead-shaped earthwork blocks (captured battery strongpoints) — now overrun, each flying a tiny BLUE flag/marker — labeled "Batteries 6 through 11". From the RIGHT (east) drive the assault as several bold tapered BLUE arrows sweeping WEST, overrunning a long stretch of the rust-red Dimmock Line and punching through it toward Petersburg behind it — show the blue arrows crossing the rust-red works where the captured batteries sit. Off to the east, behind the blue arrows, place a crisp BLUE unit block labeled "Hinks" leading the advance. The town of Petersburg sits open behind the broken line to the west.

The only text anywhere on the map is the five labels below, each bold near-black on a solid white pill. Render NO other text — in particular print NO color words anywhere (no "rust-red", "blue", "red"); the line itself carries no color-name label.

Six labels only: Petersburg, Appomattox River, Dimmock Line, Batteries 6 through 11, Hinks.
