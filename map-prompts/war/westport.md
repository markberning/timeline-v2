# War map prompts — Westport (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style chosen 2026-05-22 (replaced the old
flat-tan schematic). Label style = the locked no-box rule (canonical preamble
copied from `wilsons-creek.md` rule 4, 2026-06-01).

Battle: Westport (slug `westport`, Trans-Mississippi theatre), October 1864.
Tactical maps only — the section opener already carries a {locator}, so NO
locator map here. The battle is fought against the rivers: every map shows the
Big Blue River and/or Brush Creek (and the Missouri River where relevant) and any
crossing, with arrows attributed to the commander who actually led each move.

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

---

**LABEL PLACEMENT — applies to EVERY label on EVERY map (critical):** Each label, together with its box/halo, must sit FULLY INSIDE the frame with a clear margin on all four sides. NO label may touch, be clipped by, or run off any edge — pull every edge-hugging name inward toward open ground. No two labels may overlap or touch each other. When a label cannot sit in clear open space right beside the place or arrow it names without colliding with another label, an arrow, troop blocks, or the edge, move it to the nearest open area and draw a THIN GRAY LEADER LINE from the label to its dot/marker so the link stays obvious. River and water labels must be SOLID and STRONG (a deep, readable blue), never pale, faint, or washed-out; if blue-on-water would be low-contrast, set the river name on open ground beside the water with a halo instead.

Shelby has just forced the Big Blue River crossing at Byram's Ford. NOT a battlefield close-up.

Terrain:

- A light-blue river runs vertically (north–south) down the CENTER, joining the Missouri River at the top, labeled "Big Blue River". A small gray ford marker (a gap in the river bank) on the Big Blue toward the center is labeled "Byram's Ford".
- A small gray town marker labeled "Westport" sits just south of the Missouri River, WEST of the Big Blue (between the Big Blue and the left edge).
- A small gray town marker labeled "Independence" sits to the RIGHT (east), in open ground east of the Big Blue.

Movements (three arrows, each attributed to the commander who led it):
- "Shelby": one bold rust-red arrow that CROSSES the Big Blue River at Byram's Ford from the east bank to the west bank — the crossing of the blue river band must be plainly visible — its head pointing west toward Westport.
- "Pleasonton": one bold BLUE arrow driving WEST from the right edge, through Independence, toward the Big Blue behind the Confederates (the rear of the column).
- "Curtis": a row of solid blue unit blocks planted across the WEST side near Westport, FACING EAST toward the oncoming Confederates (the force blocking the road into Kansas), with a short label "Curtis".

EIGHT labels (more than the usual six is allowed here): Missouri River, Big Blue River, Byram's Ford, Kansas City, Westport, Independence, Shelby, Pleasonton, Curtis — that is NINE; keep all nine BIG, bold, and spread over open pale ground. Nothing else.

## Map westport-brush-creek

Morning, October 23, 1864 — the main fight south of Westport along Brush Creek. The Union line on the high ground, Blunt's attack across the creek, Shelby's counterattack, and Curtis's flanking move up the hidden ravine. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole field.

Terrain:
- A light-blue creek runs west-to-east (left to right) across the CENTER of the map, labeled "Brush Creek".
- A pale-tan band of high ground runs along the NORTH (top) edge, with a small gray town marker on it labeled "Westport".
- A thin gray ravine (a narrow gully) curves from the Union high ground in the north around toward the WEST (left) flank of the Confederate position; leave the ravine unlabeled.
- Open pale ground (the fields, a city park today) fills the area south of the creek.

Forces and movements:
- The Confederates hold the SOUTH, FACING NORTH: a row of solid rust-red unit blocks south of Brush Creek. One bold rust-red arrow drives NORTH across the creek toward Westport, labeled "Shelby" (his counterattack that nearly pushed the Federals into the town).
- The Union holds the high ground in the NORTH, FACING SOUTH: a row of solid blue unit blocks along the top. One bold blue arrow drives SOUTH across Brush Creek into the fields, labeled "Blunt". A second bold blue arrow swings out of the north, follows the ravine around the WEST flank, and strikes the exposed side of the rust-red line, labeled "Curtis".

FIVE labels only: Brush Creek, Westport, Shelby, Blunt, Curtis. Nothing else.

## Map westport-byrams-ford

Morning, October 23, 1864 — three miles east, the other jaw of the trap. Pleasonton's Union cavalry forces the Big Blue River at Byram's Ford against Marmaduke's defending division. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole field.

Terrain:
- A light-blue river runs vertically (north–south) down the CENTER of the map, labeled "Big Blue River". A small gray ford marker (a break in the bank) at the center is labeled "Byram's Ford".
- The west bank (left of the river) is a pale-tan rise of higher ground the defenders hold; the east bank (right) is open pale ground.

Forces and movements:
- The Confederates defend the WEST bank, FACING EAST: a row of solid rust-red unit blocks along the high west bank above the ford, labeled "Marmaduke".
- The Union attacks from the EAST: solid blue unit blocks on the east bank, with one bold blue arrow forcing ACROSS the Big Blue River at Byram's Ford — the crossing of the blue river band must be plainly visible — and continuing up the west bank, labeled "Pleasonton".

FOUR labels only: Big Blue River, Byram's Ford, Marmaduke, Pleasonton. Nothing else.

## Map westport-mine-creek

October 25, 1864 — two days after Westport. The pursuit turns into a rout just over the line in Kansas, where a Union cavalry charge smashes Price's rear guard at Mine Creek and the Marais des Cygnes. A running-fight tactical view, NOT a close static line.

Terrain:
- A light-blue creek runs west-to-east (left to right) across the LOWER HALF, labeled "Mine Creek".
- A second light-blue stream runs across the UPPER portion, labeled "Marais des Cygnes".
- Open pale ground (Kansas prairie) between and around them.

Forces and movements:
- The Confederate rear guard, FACING NORTH, retreating SOUTH: solid rust-red unit blocks caught between the two streams near Mine Creek, with one bold rust-red arrow pointing SOUTH (the retreat), labeled "Marmaduke".
- The Union pursuit from the NORTH: solid blue unit blocks above the Marais des Cygnes, with one bold blue charging arrow driving SOUTH across the open ground and into the rust-red blocks at Mine Creek, labeled "Pleasonton". A second bold blue arrow pressing south from the upper-left, labeled "Blunt".

FIVE labels only: Mine Creek, Marais des Cygnes, Marmaduke, Pleasonton, Blunt. Nothing else.
