# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map nashville-overview

The stage, mid-December 1864 — Hood's wrecked army parked too weak to attack and too proud to run outside a city the Union had spent three years fortifying. The setting, north up.

Across the TOP (north), draw the **Cumberland River** as a flat light-blue band running along the upper edge, labeled "Cumberland River". Just below it, set the city as a small cluster of gray blocks labeled "Nashville". Wrap the south and west of the city in a long sweeping arc of BLUE field fortifications — crisp blue earthwork blocks forming a semicircular ring facing outward (south), labeled "Union fortifications". Several miles to the SOUTH, facing the city, draw Hood's line as a thin rust-red arc of unit blocks stretched east-to-west across the pale-tan hills, labeled "Hood". From the city draw two thin gray roads running straight DOWN and out the bottom (south) — the western one labeled "Granny White Pike", the eastern one labeled "Franklin Pike". These two pikes are the only roads out. Keep the field calm — no arrows; this map is the stage, not the fight.

Six labels only: Nashville, Cumberland River, Union fortifications, Hood, Granny White Pike, Franklin Pike.

## Map nashville-december-15

December 15, 1864 — the door swings shut: a loud Union feint pins Hood's right in the east while the main blue mass wheels in a grand right turn that crushes his left in the west. A day-one tactical overview, north up.

Place the gray city block labeled "Nashville" along the TOP, with the BLUE fortification ring just below it. To the SOUTH, draw Hood's thin rust-red line as an arc of unit blocks across the pale-tan hills. On the EAST (right) end of that line, label the rust-red blocks "Cheatham" — and against them, from the north, send a SMALL blue tapered arrow labeled "Steedman" striking Hood's right (this is the feint, meant only to fix him). On the WEST (left) end, label the rust-red blocks "Stewart". Against Stewart's left, swing the main blow: a single BIG bold blue tapered arrow originating in the west/southwest and wheeling around in a great clockwise arc to crash into the rust-red left flank — label this sweeping arrow "Wilson" (the cavalry forming the outer edge of the wheel). The blue arrow must visibly curve, hinged toward the center, its tip rolling up Stewart's western end.

Six labels only: Nashville, Cheatham, Stewart, Steedman, Wilson, Franklin Pike.

## Map nashville-december-16

December 16, 1864 — the kill: Union assaults take Shy's Hill in the west and storm Overton Hill in the east, and the last Confederate field army in the West breaks into a rout fleeing south down the Franklin Pike. A day-two tactical overview, north up.

Hood has fallen back to a short, desperate line anchored on two heights. In the WEST (left), draw a pale-tan rise topped with rust-red unit blocks labeled "Shy's Hill". In the EAST (right), draw a second pale-tan rise topped with rust-red unit blocks labeled "Overton Hill". Against Shy's Hill in the west, drive a bold BLUE tapered arrow up and over the crest from the northwest (the hill is being carried). Against Overton Hill in the east, send a bold BLUE tapered arrow storming straight up the slope from the north. As the western flank gives way, show the rust-red line collapsing: a single bold RUST-RED retreat arrow streaming SOUTH off the bottom edge down a thin gray road labeled "Franklin Pike" — the rout pouring out of the trap. North up.

Six labels only: Shy's Hill, Overton Hill, Franklin Pike.
