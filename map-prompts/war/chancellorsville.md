# War map prompts — Chancellorsville (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

---

**LABEL PLACEMENT — applies to EVERY label on EVERY map (critical):** Each label, together with its box/halo, must sit FULLY INSIDE the frame with a clear margin on all four sides. NO label may touch, be clipped by, or run off any edge — pull every edge-hugging name inward toward open ground. No two labels may overlap or touch each other. When a label cannot sit in clear open space right beside the place or arrow it names without colliding with another label, an arrow, troop blocks, or the edge, move it to the nearest open area and draw a THIN GRAY LEADER LINE from the label to its dot/marker so the link stays obvious. River and water labels must be SOLID and STRONG (a deep, readable blue), never pale, faint, or washed-out; if blue-on-water would be low-contrast, set the river name on open ground beside the water with a halo instead.

## Map chancellorsville-overview

April 30, 1863 — Hooker's turning march swings three corps far upstream and brings them down behind Lee at the Chancellorsville crossroads, while Sedgwick demonstrates below Fredericksburg. A campaign overview, north up.

Draw the Rappahannock River as a flat light-blue band running roughly west-to-east across the upper-middle of the map, with the Rapidan River joining it as a second light-blue band coming in from the WEST (upper-left) — the two rivers meet near the center-top. On the lower (south) bank, in the center, place a small gray house block labeled "Chancellorsville" set in a broad soft sage-green woods fill (the Wilderness). About ten miles to the EAST (right), on the south bank, place a small gray town block labeled "Fredericksburg". Along the upper rivers, west of the junction, mark the upstream crossing point with a small gray ford block labeled "Germanna Ford". Show the Union turning column as bold tapered BLUE arrows: arrows crossing the Rappahannock and Rapidan at the upstream fords (upper-left, near Germanna Ford) and curling DOWN and EAST to converge on Chancellorsville from behind — a cluster of blue unit blocks planted at the crossroads. Downstream at Fredericksburg, show a separate group of blue unit blocks below the town with a short blue demonstration arrow labeled "Sedgwick" pushing across the river toward the town. Between the two Union forces, just south of the river near Fredericksburg, place a row of RUST-RED unit blocks labeled "Lee" facing the threat on both sides — set the "Lee" label on a SOLID WHITE pill with BOLD near-black (dark gray) text, exactly like the other labels — the "Lee" text must be the SAME dark ink as every other label, NEVER rust-red or any colored text. Label the Rappahannock ONCE, on a single solid white pill; do NOT add any second, faint, or italic river label floating directly on the water.

Six labels only: Chancellorsville, Fredericksburg, Germanna Ford, Sedgwick, Lee, Rappahannock River.

## Map chancellorsville-flank

May 2, 1863 — Jackson's twelve-mile flank march loops southwest past Catharine Furnace, screened by the Wilderness, then crashes north into the exposed Union right at dusk. A flank-attack view, north up.

Fill most of the map with a soft sage-green woods (the Wilderness). Run a thin gray road west-to-east across the upper-middle labeled "Orange Turnpike". Near the center-right of this road, place a small gray house block labeled "Chancellorsville". On the far WEST (left) end of the Orange Turnpike, draw a row of BLUE unit blocks FACING SOUTH (downward) with their left/west end hanging open — label this exposed force "Howard" (the Union XI Corps on the far right). Southwest of Chancellorsville, lower-center, mark a small gray furnace block labeled "Catharine Furnace". Show Jackson's flank march as a single long bold tapered RUST-RED arrow: starting near Chancellorsville's south side, looping DOWN and WEST past Catharine Furnace (screened through the woods), then curving sharply UP and NORTH to the western end of the Orange Turnpike — its tip smashing into the open west flank of Howard's blue line from the side. Label the arrow's striking head "Jackson". Show Howard's blue blocks beginning to break and scatter eastward toward Chancellorsville under the blow.

Six labels only: Chancellorsville, Orange Turnpike, Howard, Catharine Furnace, Jackson, Hazel Grove.

## Map chancellorsville-collapse

May 3, 1863 — the converging Confederate wings squeeze the Union back onto the Chancellorsville crossroads while Sedgwick breaks through at Marye's Heights and is stopped at Salem Church to the east. A two-front collapse view, north up.

Draw the Rappahannock River as a flat light-blue band across the top. In the center, in a soft sage-green woods fill, place a small gray house block labeled "Chancellorsville" with a tight horseshoe of BLUE unit blocks pulled in around it, backs toward the river. Just southwest of the crossroads, mark a pale-tan open hilltop labeled "Hazel Grove" crowned with a row of RUST-RED artillery symbols (massed cannons). From the WEST and the SOUTH drive bold tapered RUST-RED arrows converging on the blue horseshoe, squeezing it inward toward Chancellorsville. To the EAST (right), place a small gray town block labeled "Fredericksburg" on the river, with a BLUE unit cluster and a bold blue arrow striking a pale-tan ridge just west of it labeled "Marye's Heights", then pushing further WEST. A few miles west of Fredericksburg, between it and Chancellorsville, place a small gray church block labeled "Salem Church" with a RUST-RED unit block in front of it stopping the blue advance cold.

Six labels only: Chancellorsville, Hazel Grove, Fredericksburg, Marye's Heights, Salem Church, Rappahannock River.
