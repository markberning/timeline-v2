# War map prompts — Bentonville (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style chosen 2026-05-22 (replaced the old
flat-tan schematic). Label style = the locked no-box rule (canonical preamble
copied from `wilsons-creek.md` rule 4, 2026-06-01).

Bentonville, North Carolina, March 19–21, 1865 — the last full Confederate
offensive of the war. The opener already carries a {locator}, so these are
tactical maps only. Arrows are attributed to the commander who actually led
the column (Hardee / Hoke / the Army of Tennessee remnant and Johnston for the
Confederates; Carlin / Morgan / Slocum / Howard / Mower for the Union), never
to the army namesake.

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map bentonville-cole-farm

Quarter to three, the afternoon of March 19, 1865 — Johnston springs the trap on the head of Slocum's Left Wing. A zoomed-in tactical view of day one, NOT the whole battle.

Terrain:
- A thin gray road runs from the LEFT edge (west) across the map to the RIGHT, labeled "Goldsboro Road" — the road Slocum's wing is marching up.
- Soft sage-green woods fill the CENTER and UPPER portions, the timber the Confederate line comes out of; leave clear pale ground along the road for the open fields where Carlin's division is caught.
- A small gray farm marker just SOUTH of the road at center-left, labeled "Cole's farm" — the ground where the assault falls.
- A small gray farm marker on slightly higher pale ground at the RIGHT (east), labeled "Morris farm" — where the broken wing rallies behind massed batteries.
- A small gray town marker near the TOP edge, north of the woods, labeled "Bentonville".

Forces and movements:
- The Confederate line bends in a great hook ACROSS and north of the Goldsboro Road: draw a curved row of solid rust-red unit blocks (the hook) facing SOUTH and WEST toward the oncoming Union column.
- The Union head of column is caught in the open along the road at center: a short row of solid blue unit blocks labeled "Carlin", facing the hook.
- Confederate attack arrows (attribute to the men who led them, three rust-red tapered arrows):
  - "Hardee" — one bold rust-red arrow sweeping SOUTH out of the woods onto Carlin's exposed line at Cole's farm.
  - "Hoke" — one bold rust-red arrow driving in alongside Hardee from the north.
  - "Army of Tennessee" — one bold rust-red arrow rolling the Union left back westward through the woods and fields.
- The Union stand: a tight cluster of solid blue unit blocks SOUTH of the road holding a patch of woods and fighting in two directions, labeled "Morgan". Show one short bold blue arrow pulling survivors EAST onto the higher ground at the Morris farm.

This day-one map carries SEVEN labels (one more than usual is allowed here) — keep each BIG, bold, and over open pale ground: Goldsboro Road, Cole's farm, Morris farm, Bentonville, Carlin, Morgan, plus the attack labels Hardee, Hoke, and Army of Tennessee. Use the attack labels directly on their arrows; do not add any unlisted name.

## Map bentonville-howard-arrives

March 20, 1865 — Howard's Right Wing closes the gap and the odds slam shut; Johnston bends his line and clings to the field with the Mill Creek bridge at his back. A day-two situation map.

Terrain:
- A thin gray road runs LEFT-to-RIGHT (west–east) across the lower-center, labeled "Goldsboro Road".
- Soft sage-green woods scattered through the center.
- A light-blue creek band crossing the UPPER-RIGHT (north) of the map, labeled "Mill Creek", with a small gray bridge marker on it labeled "Mill Creek bridge" — the single crossing at Johnston's back.

Forces and movements:
- The Confederate line: a long curved row of solid rust-red unit blocks bent back into a tight horseshoe, its open end toward the Mill Creek bridge, facing the Union forces to the south and west. Show one short bold rust-red arrow pulling the right of the line back to guard the flank, labeled "Hoke".
- The Union Left Wing already on the field, south of and facing the rust line: a row of solid blue unit blocks labeled "Slocum".
- The Union reinforcement: one bold blue tapered arrow swinging UP from the lower-right (the south road) and filing onto Slocum's right, labeled "Howard".

Six labels only: Goldsboro Road, Mill Creek, Mill Creek bridge, Hoke, Slocum, Howard. Nothing else.

## Map bentonville-mowers-charge

March 21, 1865 — Mower drives at the thin Confederate left toward the Mill Creek bridge and Johnston's headquarters; Hardee's scratch counterattack stops him. A zoomed-in tactical view of day three, NOT the whole battle.

Terrain:
- A light-blue creek band running across the TOP (north), labeled "Mill Creek", with a small gray bridge marker on it labeled "Mill Creek bridge" — Johnston's one road out.
- Soft sage-green woods through the center, wet pine country.
- A small gray marker just south of the bridge, labeled "Johnston's headquarters".

Forces and movements:
- The thin Confederate left: a sparse, short row of solid rust-red unit blocks (cavalry and skirmishers) covering the ground between the woods and the bridge, facing south/southwest.
- The Union attack: one bold blue tapered arrow driving NORTH through the thin line, past Johnston's headquarters, reaching to within sight of the Mill Creek bridge, labeled "Mower".
- The Confederate counterattack: one bold rust-red tapered arrow striking the flank of Mower's arrow and shoving it back south, labeled "Hardee".

Five labels only: Mill Creek, Mill Creek bridge, Johnston's headquarters, Mower, Hardee. Nothing else.

## Map bentonville-withdrawal

Night, March 21 into March 22, 1865 — Johnston gives up the field and slips his whole army across the Mill Creek bridge in the dark, burning it behind him. A withdrawal map; the creek crossing is the point of the map.

Terrain:
- A light-blue creek band running LEFT-to-RIGHT across the CENTER of the map, labeled "Mill Creek", with a small gray bridge marker crossing it at center, labeled "Mill Creek bridge".
- Soft sage-green woods on the SOUTH (lower) bank; open pale ground on the NORTH (upper) bank, the road away.
- A thin gray road running from the bridge NORTH off the top edge toward Smithfield, labeled "Goldsboro Road" along the south approach.

Forces and movements:
- The withdrawing army: solid rust-red unit blocks on the SOUTH bank funneling toward the bridge, with one bold rust-red tapered arrow CROSSING the bridge from south to north and continuing off the top edge, labeled "Johnston". The crossing must be unmistakable — the arrow passes over the blue creek at the bridge.
- A small rust-red rear-guard block left on the south bank screening the crossing, labeled "Hampton".

Five labels only: Mill Creek, Mill Creek bridge, Goldsboro Road, Johnston, Hampton. Nothing else.
