# War map prompts — Antietam (battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps**, not territorial-civ maps. The style is deliberately
minimal so it reads on a phone, beside prose: a flat schematic, a few bold
arrows, very few labels. Generated by `scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses
`## Map <slug>` sections, writes `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these (read before drawing)

**CRITICAL BATTLE-MAP RULES:**

1. **This is a clean, flat, schematic battle map — NOT a topographic or terrain map.** Draw NO contour lines, NO hill shading, NO relief, NO textured ground, NO forest stippling, NO elevation tinting. The base is a single flat pale tan (#e7ddc8) land area and, where water is shown, a single flat light blue. Nothing else fills the background.
2. **Use side colors for forces.** Union (United States / North) positions, lines, and arrows are BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) positions, lines, and arrows are RUST RED (#b44d3b). Use no other strong colors. A force line is a simple thick colored band or arc; a movement is a bold tapered arrow in that side's color. The words BLUE, RUST, and RED name the ink to use — they are instructions to you, NEVER text to print. Never render a color name as a label anywhere on the map.
3. **Very few labels — at most SIX, large and legible.** Each label is a bare proper name only (e.g. "Sunken Road"), in a clean sans-serif, in dark ink, with a thin white halo so it stays readable over the map. Never write a sentence on the map. Never label more than six things.
4. **No road network, no creeks, no railroads, no county lines** unless a single one is explicitly named below. A battlefield is shown by the two armies' shapes and the arrows between them, not by terrain detail.
5. **Spell every label EXACTLY as written below. Invent no words, no extra labels, no annotations.** The only text on the image is the labels listed for that map — nothing more.
6. **North is at the top. East is to the right.** Place everything in correct real-world orientation. Do not rotate or tilt.
7. **NO title bar, NO chapter number, NO caption text on the image.** The map carries no title — the page caption describes it. Render no header bar.
8. **NO frame.** Draw no border, box, rectangle, or hairline anywhere. The map bleeds off all four edges.
9. **No ornaments, no compass rose, no scale bar, no legend, no north arrow, no inset.** Do not print the words North/South/East/West or the letters N/S/E/W. Do not draw a decorative star or sparkle.

## Map antietam-campaign

A clean schematic march map of the Maryland Campaign, September 1862 — Lee's first invasion of the North.

Draw a flat tan land area crossed by a single thin light-blue river running west-to-east and then bending southeast, labeled "Potomac" — the border the armies cross. Place a small town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" just north of the river near the center. Place a small marker labeled "Frederick" to the east. Place a small marker labeled "Harpers Ferry" on the river to the southwest.

One bold RUST arrow crosses the river from the south and curves up into the land past Frederick, labeled "Lee"; a short RUST branch splits off it down toward Harpers Ferry. One BLUE arrow follows from the southeast, trailing behind toward Frederick, labeled "McClellan".

Six labels only: Potomac, Sharpsburg, Frederick, Harpers Ferry, Lee, McClellan. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-cornfield

A clean schematic close-up of the morning fight at Antietam, dawn September 17, 1862 — the northern end of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle. Fill the frame with the action; do not leave large empty areas.

In the center, draw a defined rectangular field with a thin dark outline and a faint pale-gold wash, labeled "The Cornfield" — the contested killing ground. Flank it with two stands of trees: "West Woods" on the left and "East Woods" on the right, so the Cornfield sits between them.

The Confederates defend the SOUTH, facing NORTH: draw the Confederate line in rust-red ink, running east–west across the lower edge of the Cornfield — its western end anchored in the West Woods, its eastern end stopping short of the right edge (a held position, not a wall spanning everything). Place a small building marker labeled "Dunker Church" just behind (below) the line.

Show the back-and-forth that made this the bloodiest ground of the day with TWO arrows that meet in the Cornfield: one bold blue Union arrow driving south from the top into the Cornfield, labeled "Hooker", and one bold rust-red Confederate counterattack arrow pushing back north from the line into the Cornfield.

The ONLY text anywhere on the image is these five labels: The Cornfield, Dunker Church, West Woods, East Woods, Hooker. Print no colour words. Print nothing else.

## Map antietam-sunken-road

A clean schematic close-up of the fight for the Sunken Road at Antietam, midday September 17, 1862 — the center of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Draw a worn farm lane running roughly west-to-east across the center as a thick RUST line, where the Confederates sheltered, labeled "Sunken Road". Place a small town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" to the west.

Show the Union attack as two bold BLUE arrows driving down from the north against the lane, and one BLUE arrow curling around to its eastern end to fire along its length.

Two labels only: Sunken Road, Sharpsburg. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-bridge

A clean schematic close-up of the fight at Burnside's Bridge, afternoon September 17, 1862 — the southern end of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Draw Antietam Creek as a thin light-blue line running top-to-bottom (north–south), with a small stone bridge crossing it near the center, labeled "Burnside's Bridge". On the west (far) bank above the bridge, a short RUST line holds the high ground. Place a town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" to the northwest.

Show the Union attack as one bold BLUE arrow forcing across the bridge from the east and continuing up toward Sharpsburg, labeled "Burnside". Show a bold RUST arrow arriving from the bottom (south) and striking the flank of that blue advance, labeled "A. P. Hill".

Five labels only: Burnside's Bridge, Antietam Creek, Sharpsburg, Burnside, A. P. Hill. Nothing else.
