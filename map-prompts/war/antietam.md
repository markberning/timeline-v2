# War map prompts — Antietam (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style chosen 2026-05-22 (replaced the old
flat-tan schematic). Label style = the locked no-box rule (canonical preamble
copied from `wilsons-creek.md` rule 4, 2026-06-01).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map antietam-campaign

September 1862 — Lee's first invasion of the North, the Maryland Campaign. A wide operational march map (not a battlefield close-up).

Terrain, left (west) to right (east):
- A light-blue river runs along the BOTTOM of the map from the lower-left, bending southeast and off the lower-right edge, labeled "Potomac" — the border the armies cross.
- A small gray town marker labeled "Harpers Ferry" sits ON the river in the lower-left, where it bends.
- A pale-tan mountain ridge runs vertically (north–south) down the CENTER of the map, labeled "South Mountain" — the long barrier between the two armies.
- A small gray town marker labeled "Frederick" sits to the RIGHT (east) of the ridge, in open ground.
- A small gray town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" sits to the LEFT (west) of the ridge, near the top-center, close to the Potomac.

Movements:
- One bold rust-red arrow, labeled "Lee", crosses the Potomac from the lower-right, curves up to Frederick, then bends WEST across South Mountain to Sharpsburg — the main Confederate march.
- A shorter rust-red branch splits off that arrow near Frederick and curves down to the southwest to Harpers Ferry (leave this branch unlabeled).
- One bold BLUE arrow (no label) follows from the lower-right behind Lee, through Frederick, and pushes WEST against South Mountain — McClellan's pursuit forcing the passes.

Six labels only: Potomac, Harpers Ferry, South Mountain, Frederick, Sharpsburg, Lee. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-cornfield

Dawn, September 17, 1862 — the morning fight at the north end of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

In the center, draw a pale-gold rectangle with faint corn-row striping, labeled "The Cornfield" — the contested killing ground. Flank it with two soft sage-green woods: "West Woods" on the left and "East Woods" on the right. A thin gray farm lane runs along the western edge of the field (leave it unlabeled).

The Confederates defend the SOUTH, facing NORTH: draw a row of solid rust-red unit blocks running east–west along the south edge of the Cornfield, its western end anchored in the West Woods. Place a small gray church marker labeled "Dunker Church" just behind (below) the line.

The Union attacks from the NORTH: draw a row of solid blue unit blocks across the top, with one bold blue arrow driving south into the Cornfield, labeled "Hooker". Show the back-and-forth that made this the bloodiest ground of the day with one bold rust-red arrow counterattacking north from the line into the Cornfield.

Five labels only: The Cornfield, Dunker Church, West Woods, East Woods, Hooker. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-west-woods

Mid-morning, September 17, 1862 — the destruction of Sedgwick's division in the West Woods, just west of the Cornfield. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Draw a large soft sage-green wooded block filling the center-left, labeled "West Woods". Place a small gray church marker labeled "Dunker Church" at its lower-right edge, and a pale-gold field with faint corn-row striping in the lower-right corner labeled "The Cornfield" (the ground Sedgwick advanced from).

The Union attack: one bold blue arrow, labeled "Sedgwick", drives WEST from the Cornfield into the West Woods in a tight column.

The Confederate trap: draw rust-red unit blocks on THREE sides of the blue arrow's head — to the west (front), the north, and the south — with three short bold rust-red arrows firing inward at the blue column from those three sides.

Four labels only: West Woods, Dunker Church, The Cornfield, Sedgwick. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-sunken-road

Midday, September 17, 1862 — the fight for the Sunken Road at the center of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Draw a worn farm lane running roughly west-to-east across the center as a sunken track, with a row of solid rust-red unit blocks sheltering in it, labeled "Sunken Road". Place a small gray town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" to the west.

The Union attacks from the NORTH: draw solid blue unit blocks to the north, with two bold blue arrows driving south against the lane, and one bold blue arrow curling around its eastern end to fire down its length.

Two labels only: Sunken Road, Sharpsburg. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-bridge

Afternoon, September 17, 1862 — the fight at Burnside's Bridge at the south end of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Draw Antietam Creek as a light-blue band running top-to-bottom (north–south), with a small gray stone bridge crossing it near the center, labeled "Burnside's Bridge". On the west (far) bank above the bridge, a short line of solid rust-red unit blocks holds the high ground. Place a gray town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" to the northwest.

Show the Union attack as one bold blue arrow forcing across the bridge from the east and continuing up toward Sharpsburg, labeled "Burnside". Show a bold rust-red arrow arriving from the bottom (south) and striking the flank of that blue advance, labeled "A. P. Hill".

Five labels only: Burnside's Bridge, Antietam Creek, Sharpsburg, Burnside, A. P. Hill. Nothing else.
