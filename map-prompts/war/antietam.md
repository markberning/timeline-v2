# War map prompts — Antietam (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style chosen 2026-05-22 (replaced the old
flat-tan schematic).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these (read before drawing)

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — not antique, not a flat color-block schematic, not topographic. Soft, very pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat, even fills only — NO paper texture, NO foxing, NO contour/relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain is shown with simple flat shapes in muted, naturalistic color**, and ONLY what the map's caption names:
   - Woods = soft muted sage-green rounded shapes.
   - Cultivated field = a pale-gold rectangle with faint thin corn-row / furrow striping.
   - Water (river / creek) = a single flat light-blue band.
   - A road or lane = a thin gray line. A town = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (United States / North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force or line is a row of crisp solid rectangular unit blocks (the standard military map symbol) in that side's color. A movement is a bold, smooth, tapered arrow in that side's color. Use no other strong colors. The words BLUE, RUST, and RED name the INK to use — they are instructions to you, NEVER text to print. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **At most SIX labels**, clean modern sans-serif, dark ink, with a subtle thin white halo so they stay legible. Each label is a bare proper name only — never a sentence. Spell each EXACTLY as written below. Add NO label that is not listed for that map (no extra road names, no invented words), and print NO color words. The only text on the image is the labels listed for that map.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** Correct real-world orientation. A defending line FACES the attack — the attacker's arrow strikes the front of the defender's line, never its back or flank unless the caption says so.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text on the image, NO border or frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO decorative ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map antietam-campaign

September 1862 — Lee's first invasion of the North, the Maryland Campaign. A wider operational march map (not a close-up).

Draw the pale ground crossed by a single light-blue river running west-to-east and then bending southeast, labeled "Potomac" — the border the armies cross. Place small gray town markers: "Sharpsburg" just north of the river near the center, "Frederick" to the east, "Harpers Ferry" on the river to the southwest.

One bold rust-red arrow crosses the river from the south and curves up into the land past Frederick, labeled "Lee"; a shorter rust-red branch splits off it down toward Harpers Ferry. One bold blue arrow follows from the southeast, trailing behind toward Frederick, labeled "McClellan".

Six labels only: Potomac, Sharpsburg, Frederick, Harpers Ferry, Lee, McClellan. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-cornfield

Dawn, September 17, 1862 — the morning fight at the north end of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

In the center, draw a pale-gold rectangle with faint corn-row striping, labeled "The Cornfield" — the contested killing ground. Flank it with two soft sage-green woods: "West Woods" on the left and "East Woods" on the right. A thin gray farm lane runs along the western edge of the field (leave it unlabeled).

The Confederates defend the SOUTH, facing NORTH: draw a row of solid rust-red unit blocks running east–west along the south edge of the Cornfield, its western end anchored in the West Woods. Place a small gray church marker labeled "Dunker Church" just behind (below) the line.

The Union attacks from the NORTH: draw a row of solid blue unit blocks across the top, with one bold blue arrow driving south into the Cornfield, labeled "Hooker". Show the back-and-forth that made this the bloodiest ground of the day with one bold rust-red arrow counterattacking north from the line into the Cornfield.

Five labels only: The Cornfield, Dunker Church, West Woods, East Woods, Hooker. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-sunken-road

Midday, September 17, 1862 — the fight for the Sunken Road at the center of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Draw a worn farm lane running roughly west-to-east across the center as a sunken track, with a row of solid rust-red unit blocks sheltering in it, labeled "Sunken Road". Place a small gray town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" to the west.

The Union attacks from the NORTH: draw solid blue unit blocks to the north, with two bold blue arrows driving south against the lane, and one bold blue arrow curling around its eastern end to fire down its length.

Two labels only: Sunken Road, Sharpsburg. Nothing else.

## Map antietam-bridge

Afternoon, September 17, 1862 — the fight at Burnside's Bridge at the south end of the field. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Draw Antietam Creek as a light-blue band running top-to-bottom (north–south), with a small gray stone bridge crossing it near the center, labeled "Burnside's Bridge". On the west (far) bank above the bridge, a short line of solid rust-red unit blocks holds the high ground. Place a gray town marker labeled "Sharpsburg" to the northwest.

Show the Union attack as one bold blue arrow forcing across the bridge from the east and continuing up toward Sharpsburg, labeled "Burnside". Show a bold rust-red arrow arriving from the bottom (south) and striking the flank of that blue advance, labeled "A. P. Hill".

Five labels only: Burnside's Bridge, Antietam Creek, Sharpsburg, Burnside, A. P. Hill. Nothing else.
