# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map spotsylvania-overview

The stage, May 8–12, 1864 — Lee wins the race to the crossroads and digs a four-mile line whose most fateful stretch bulges out into a giant U-shaped salient north of the village. A setting overview, north up.

In the lower-center place a small gray town block labeled "Spotsylvania Court House", with a few thin gray roads meeting at it to show the crossroads. From the UPPER-LEFT (northwest) draw a single thin gray road slanting down toward the town labeled "Brock Road" — the Union approach. Down the LEFT (west) edge draw a flat light-blue river band labeled "Po River". North of the town, draw Lee's entrenched works as a continuous crisp RUST-RED line that swings up into a big upside-down-U salient bulging OUT toward the north (the tip pointing up, the two legs running back down toward the main line on either side) — label this rust-red horseshoe "Mule Shoe". Just south of the Brock Road, on a low pale-tan rise at the west end of the works, mark the anchor of the line labeled "Laurel Hill". Across the TOP, facing the salient from the north, set a row of solid BLUE unit blocks confronting the bulge. No assault arrows on this map — it is the stage, not the fight.

Six labels only: Spotsylvania Court House, Brock Road, Po River, Mule Shoe, Laurel Hill.

## Map spotsylvania-mule-shoe

The fatal geometry, May 10–12, 1864 — a clean diagram of the salient: Upton's narrow May 10 column cracks the west face, then Hancock's whole corps smashes the exposed apex at dawn on May 12. A salient diagram, north up.

Center the map on the salient drawn as a clean RUST-RED upside-down-U (a horseshoe-shaped line of Confederate works, the legs running down toward the bottom of the frame, the rounded tip pointing UP toward the top) enclosing open pale ground. Label the open ground inside the U "Mule Shoe". Put exactly ONE angle label, at the very TIP of the U — the rounded part jutting farthest north — reading "Bloody Angle". This apex tip is the ONLY angle label on the map: do NOT add a second "West Angle" or any east-side angle label — one apex label only, so there is no confusion about which point is the Bloody Angle. On the WEST (left) face of the salient, draw Upton's strike as a single tight, narrow BLUE column arrow — slim and deep — punching straight into the rust-red line, labeled "Upton". From the TOP, aimed squarely DOWN onto the apex tip, draw a single BROAD BLUE assault arrow overrunning the tip, labeled "Hancock". Keep the rust-red works crisp and continuous all the way around the U so the converging exposure of the apex reads at a glance. Every label = bold near-black text on a solid white pill. No terrain clutter — this is a geometry diagram.

Four labels only: Mule Shoe, Bloody Angle, Upton, Hancock.

## Map spotsylvania-bloody-angle

May 12, 1864, close-up — twenty hours of hand-to-hand fighting in the rain at the apex; the Union overruns the tip, and overnight Lee builds a fresh line across the base of the salient and pulls back to it. A close-up of the apex, north up.

Zoom in tight on the tip of the salient. Across the upper part of the frame draw the captured apex works as a contested RUST-RED parapet bent into a sharp angle, labeled "Bloody Angle". From the TOP drive a single broad BLUE assault arrow overrunning that angle, its point pouring over the parapet into the salient. Across the BOTTOM of the frame draw a fresh, straight, continuous RUST-RED line — the new base of the salient Lee's men built overnight — sealing off the bottom of the horseshoe and facing back up toward the apex; this rust-red base line should read as solid and intact while the apex above it is breached. Show the open ground between the breached apex and the new base line as bare pale parchment (the killing ground). Keep it spare: the bent rust-red apex up top, the broad blue arrow crashing into it, the clean rust-red base line holding across the bottom.

Six labels only: Bloody Angle.
