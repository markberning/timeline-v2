# War map prompts — Port Hudson (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map port-hudson-overview

The last Confederate-held lock on the Mississippi, spring 1863 — Port Hudson on its 80-foot bluff above the hairpin bend, Banks's army closing the landward ring while Farragut's fleet works the river. A strategic overview, north up.

Draw the **Mississippi River** as a broad flat light-blue band running down the map from top to bottom, and bend it into a sharp **hairpin** in the center where the channel doubles back on itself in nearly a U-turn. On the EAST (right) bank, hard against the outside of the bend, draw a pale-tan bluff edge and set a small gray fort block on it labeled "Port Hudson" — and along that bluff facing the water place a tight row of rust-red artillery symbols (wheel-to-wheel cannons) labeled "Confederate batteries." Up the river at the TOP edge, where it runs off the top of the map, place a small gray block labeled "Vicksburg" (far upstream, north). Where a smaller light-blue tributary joins the Mississippi from the WEST about a third of the way down, label that junction "Red River." Down the river at the BOTTOM edge place a small gray town block on the east bank labeled "Baton Rouge" (downstream, south).

On the land side EAST of Port Hudson, draw a semicircular arc of rust-red earthwork line bowing out away from the river, both ends anchored back on the riverbank above and below the fort — this is the Confederate landward ring. Wrap Banks's army around it as a broad outer band of BLUE unit blocks investing the arc from the north, east, and south. On the river itself, just below the bend under the bluff guns, set two or three small BLUE warship shapes (Farragut's fleet) on the light-blue water.

Six labels only: Port Hudson, Confederate batteries, Vicksburg, Red River, Baton Rouge.

## Map port-hudson

The first assault, May 27, 1863 — Banks's blue columns funnel into the forested ravines against the Confederate earthworks, while on the far Confederate left by the river the Louisiana Native Guard charge the bluff guns. A tactical view of the May 27 attack, north up, east right.

Draw the **Mississippi River** as a flat light-blue band down the LEFT (west) edge, with a pale-tan **bluff** edge along its east bank and a tight row of rust-red artillery symbols on the bluff facing the water labeled "the bluff guns." Across the middle and right of the map draw the Confederate **earthworks** as a continuous bowed rust-red line, its northern end and southern end both anchored back on the riverbank; inside the line, near its center, mark a small rust-red strongpoint block labeled "Priest Cap," and toward the north end another labeled "Fort Desperate." In front of the earthworks (the open, eastern side) wash a broad soft sage-green band of broken forest and cut it with a couple of darker sage gullies — the deep forested ravines that swallow the attack.

Banks attacks from the NORTH and EAST: draw several bold tapered BLUE arrows driving in toward the earthworks and show them breaking up — thinning and splintering — as they enter the green ravines, so the assault visibly dissolves before it reaches the rust line. Along the river at the far Confederate LEFT (the river end of the line, lower-left), draw a thin gray road running up to the works labeled "Telegraph Road," and send one determined BLUE arrow straight up it against the bluff guns labeled "Native Guard" — the 1st and 3rd Louisiana Native Guard charging where Cailloux fell.

Six labels only: the bluff guns, Fort Desperate, Priest Cap, Telegraph Road, Native Guard.
