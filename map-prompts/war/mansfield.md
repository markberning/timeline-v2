# War map prompts — Mansfield (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map mansfield-overview

The morning of April 8, 1864 — the trap set: a single narrow stage road runs north through dense pine woods toward Mansfield, with Banks's Union army strung out for miles along the one lane while Taylor's smaller Confederate line waits across the road at the head of the column. A setting overview, north up.

Fill almost the entire field with soft sage-green DENSE PINE WOODS, edge to edge, so the woods dominate everything — a forest tunnel with no open ground except one clearing near the top. Down the center of the map draw a single THIN GRAY ROAD running straight TOP-to-BOTTOM (north–south) — this is the only road through the trees. At the very TOP of the road, in a small pale-gold clearing, place a small gray town block labeled "Mansfield". Just below the town, where the road enters the clearing, mark a low pale-tan rise straddling the road labeled "Honeycutt Hill". Across the road on that rise, draw Taylor's Confederate line as a tight row of RUST-RED unit blocks drawn ACROSS the road and facing south down it — waiting, holding the clearing. Down the rest of the road, running far to the BOTTOM edge through the green woods, draw Banks's army as a LONG THIN line of many small BLUE unit blocks single-file in the lane — emphasize how stretched out and strung along the one road it is, the lead blue blocks just reaching the clearing and the rest trailing back miles into the trees. No arrows yet; this is the trap before it springs.

Six labels only: Mansfield, Honeycutt Hill.

## Map mansfield-the-rout

About 4 p.m., April 8, 1864 — Taylor's crescent attacks and envelops the head of Banks's column, which breaks and routs south back down the single road into its own jammed wagon train. A tactical overview of the collapse, north up.

Keep the field filled with soft sage-green dense pine woods and the single thin gray road running straight top-to-bottom down the center, with the small gray town block labeled "Mansfield" at the TOP and the low pale-tan rise labeled "Honeycutt Hill" straddling the road just below it in the clearing. Draw Taylor's Confederate line as rust-red unit blocks bent into a crescent (horseshoe) along the western, northern, and eastern tree lines rimming the clearing. From this crescent send bold tapered rust-red arrows curling around BOTH sides of the lead blue blocks at the head of the column — arrows wrapping the Union flanks from west and east to envelop the head of the column. Show the blue column breaking: the lead blue blocks scattering and a bold tapered blue retreat arrow running SOUTH back down the single road toward the bottom edge. Along the road behind the breaking front, draw a few small gray wagon marks left abandoned in the lane (the captured wagon train jamming the retreat). North up.

The only text anywhere on the map is the two labels below — "Mansfield" and "Honeycutt Hill" — each bold near-black on a solid white pill, spelled exactly. Render NO other text anywhere: no terrain words ("pine woods", "road"), no color words, no instruction words.

Six labels only: Mansfield, Honeycutt Hill.
