# War map prompts — Fredericksburg (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map fredericksburg-overview

December 13, 1862 — the Rappahannock divides the two armies: the Union crosses on pontoon bridges into the town while Lee's army waits dug in on the heights behind it, holding two fronts. A battle overview, north up.

Draw the Rappahannock River as a wide flat light-blue band running diagonally from the UPPER-LEFT (northwest) down to the LOWER-RIGHT (southeast) across the map — label it "Rappahannock River". Place a small cluster of gray town blocks labeled "Fredericksburg" sitting ENTIRELY on the river's SOUTHWEST (lower-left) bank, in the center — the town block must NOT straddle or touch the water; the whole river runs along its northeast edge. From the river's NORTHEAST (upper) bank, draw two short bold blue tapered arrows crossing the water INTO the town (the pontoon-bridge crossings — the Union crosses from the upper bank into the town); add one more blue crossing arrow downstream to the southeast, crossing from the upper bank into the open river plain below the heights. Behind (west and southwest of) the town, draw a long pale-tan ridge band crowned with a row of solid rust-red unit blocks facing the river — the Confederate line. At the NORTHERN end of that ridge, just behind the town, place a rust-red block cluster labeled "Marye's Heights". Several miles SOUTHEAST along the ridge (downstream), at the far end of the line, place a second rust-red block cluster labeled "Prospect Hill". Set a small rust-red command knoll behind the center of the line labeled "Lee's Hill". Blue unit blocks mass between the town and the southern crossing on the river plain below the heights.

Six labels only: Rappahannock River, Fredericksburg, Marye's Heights, Prospect Hill, Lee's Hill.

## Map fredericksburg-maryes

December 13, 1862 — the futile frontal assaults: Union brigades funnel out of the town across open ground and the canal ditch and walk uphill into Confederate infantry sheltered behind the stone wall in the sunken road, wave after wave stopped short. A zoomed view of the Marye's Heights front, north up.

Along the TOP and LEFT (west) of the map draw a pale-tan ridge labeled "Marye's Heights", with rust-red artillery symbols massed along its crest. At the FOOT of the ridge draw a thin gray sunken farm lane fronted by a heavy rust-red line of unit blocks (infantry stacked deep) — label this line "Stone Wall". In the lower-right (east) place a cluster of gray town blocks labeled "Fredericksburg". Between the town and the ridge, draw a thin light-blue ditch running roughly north-to-south across the open ground, crossed by a few narrow bridges — label it "Canal Ditch". Show three or four bold blue tapered arrows emerging from the town, funneling over the canal-ditch bridges, re-forming, and driving UP the open muddy slope toward the Stone Wall — every arrow stopping short of the rust-red line. Scatter the slope between the ditch and the wall with the wreckage of the stalled blue attack.

Six labels only: Marye's Heights, Stone Wall, Fredericksburg, Canal Ditch.

## Map fredericksburg-prospect-hill

December 13, 1862 — the intended main attack on the south front: Meade's blue division breaks THROUGH a gap in Jackson's line at Prospect Hill before unsupported it is driven back across the railroad. A zoomed view of the southern battlefront, north up.

Draw the Rappahannock River as a flat light-blue band along the UPPER-RIGHT (northeast) edge. Across the LEFT and TOP of the field draw a pale-tan wooded ridge held by a rust-red Confederate line facing east — at its southern (lower-left) end place a rust-red block cluster labeled "Prospect Hill", and run a thin gray railroad line in front of the ridge down to a gray junction block in the lower-left labeled "Hamilton's Crossing". Leave one clear break in the rust-red line near the center — a small sage-green swampy wooded patch — and label it "The Gap". From the river plain on the right, draw a bold blue tapered arrow (Meade's division) driving WEST straight into The Gap and punching through the rust-red line behind it. Then show two SINGLE-headed rust-red counterattack arrows closing INWARD onto the breakthrough from both flanks, and a shorter blue arrow pointing EAST — back across the railroad toward the river — showing Meade's unsupported division being driven back.

Six labels only: Prospect Hill, Hamilton's Crossing, The Gap, Rappahannock River.
