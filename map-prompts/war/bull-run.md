# War map prompts — First Bull Run (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these (read before drawing)

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — not antique, not a flat color-block schematic, not topographic. Soft, very pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat, even fills only — NO paper texture, NO foxing, NO contour/relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain is shown with simple flat shapes in muted, naturalistic color**, and ONLY what the map's caption names:
   - Woods = soft muted sage-green rounded shapes.
   - A hill / rise = a soft pale-tan rounded shape.
   - Water (river / creek) = a single flat light-blue band.
   - A road or turnpike = a thin gray line. A town, house, or bridge = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (United States / North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force or line is a row of crisp solid rectangular unit blocks in that side's color. A movement is a bold, smooth, tapered arrow in that side's color. Use no other strong colors. The words BLUE, RUST, and RED name the INK to use — they are instructions to you, NEVER text to print. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **At most SIX labels**, clean modern sans-serif, dark ink, with a subtle thin white halo. Each label is a bare proper name only — never a sentence. Spell each EXACTLY as written below. Add NO label that is not listed for that map, and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack — the attacker's arrow strikes the front of the defender's line.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text on the image, NO border or frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO decorative ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map bull-run-morning

Morning, July 21, 1861 — McDowell's flank march. A tactical overview, north up.

Draw the creek Bull Run as a flat light-blue band running diagonally from the upper-left down to the lower-right (northwest to southeast), labeled "Bull Run". Draw the Warrenton Turnpike as a thin gray road crossing the creek roughly west-to-east at a small gray block labeled "Stone Bridge". Mark "Sudley Ford" as a crossing point on the creek well to the north (upper-left area). Place a soft pale-tan rise labeled "Matthews Hill" just north of the turnpike near the center, and another tan rise labeled "Henry House Hill" just south of the turnpike near the center.

The Confederates hold the south bank of Bull Run: a thin line of solid rust-red unit blocks along the creek, originally facing north. A single short BLUE (Union) arrow pushes west against the Stone Bridge (a feint). The main Union force makes a wide sweep: a long bold blue arrow runs up from the lower area, crosses the creek at Sudley Ford, then curves back south and strikes Matthews Hill from the north — label this arrow "McDowell".

Six labels only: Bull Run, Stone Bridge, Sudley Ford, Matthews Hill, Henry House Hill, McDowell. Nothing else.

## Map bull-run-henryhill

Afternoon, July 21, 1861 — the stand on Henry House Hill. A zoomed-in tactical view, north up. CRITICAL ORIENTATION: the Union attacked this hill FROM THE NORTH (from the top of the frame, driving downward/southward); the Confederates held the hill facing north (up). Do not invert this.

Draw a broad pale-tan plateau filling the center and lower frame, labeled "Henry House Hill", with a small gray house block near its crest labeled "Henry House". Draw the Warrenton Turnpike as a thin gray road running roughly west-to-east across the TOP of the frame, along the north foot of the hill.

The Union attacks from the NORTH (the top): place a row of solid blue unit blocks just below the turnpike at the top of the frame, with two bold blue arrows driving DOWNWARD (south) up onto the hill.

The Confederates hold the hill and face the attack: a firm row of solid rust-red unit blocks across the middle of the plateau, facing up toward the blue arrows, labeled "Jackson".

To the lower-LEFT (the southwest), draw a soft tan rise labeled "Chinn Ridge" with one bold rust-red arrow sweeping up and to the right from it, striking the western (right) flank of the Union blue blocks — the late Confederate counterattack.

Four labels only: Henry House Hill, Henry House, Jackson, Chinn Ridge. Nothing else.
