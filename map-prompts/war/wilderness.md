# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map wilderness-overview

The stage, May 4–5, 1864 — the dense Wilderness thicket where Lee chose to fight blind, laced by two parallel east–west roads and the north–south road that crosses them. A setting overview, north up. No fighting drawn.

Fill almost the entire field with soft sage-green woods — a single unbroken thicket bleeding off all four edges; this dense forest IS the subject. Along the TOP (north) edge draw the "Rapidan River" as a flat light-blue band running west-to-east, and set a small gray crossing block on it labeled "Germanna Ford" (the army crossed in here). Through the woods cut two thin gray roads running straight LEFT-to-RIGHT (west–east), parallel, about a third of the map apart: the northern one labeled "Orange Turnpike", the southern one labeled "Orange Plank Road". Cutting across BOTH of them, running TOP-to-BOTTOM (north–south) through the eastern third of the map, draw a third thin gray road labeled "Brock Road" — it must visibly cross the Turnpike and the Plank Road. Keep the whole field calm — no unit blocks, no arrows, no fire; this map is the stage, not the fight.

Six labels only: Rapidan River, Germanna Ford, Orange Turnpike, Orange Plank Road, Brock Road.

## Map wilderness-may6

May 6, 1864, morning — Longstreet's dawn counterattack drives east down the Orange Plank Road, the artillery at the Widow Tapp farm buys minutes, and a hidden flank march up the unfinished railroad bed rolls up Hancock's line back toward Brock Road. A morning tactical overview, north up.

Fill the field with soft sage-green Wilderness woods. Run a thin gray road straight LEFT-to-RIGHT across the center labeled "Orange Plank Road"; near the RIGHT (east) side draw a thin gray north–south road crossing it labeled "Brock Road". Just behind the center-left of the Plank Road, mark a small pale-gold open clearing with a tight row of rust-red cannon symbols labeled "Widow Tapp farm". South of and roughly parallel to the Plank Road, draw a faint straight gray graded cut through the woods labeled "unfinished railroad". Along the Brock Road on the east, set a north–south line of BLUE unit blocks facing west labeled "Hancock", being pushed back eastward.

Longstreet's counterattack comes from the WEST: send a broad bold tapered RUST-RED arrow driving EAST straight down the Orange Plank Road into the blue line. From the unfinished-railroad cut send a second bold rust-red arrow curling up and EAST into the southern (lower) END of Hancock's line — the flank march folding the blue line sideways back onto Brock Road.

Six labels only: Orange Plank Road, Brock Road, Widow Tapp farm, unfinished railroad, Hancock, Longstreet.

## Map wilderness-the-turn

The night of May 7, 1864 — instead of retreating north across the Rapidan, Grant marches the army southeast down the Brock Road toward Spotsylvania Court House. The hinge of the campaign, north up.

Fill the field with soft sage-green Wilderness woods. Along the TOP (north) edge draw the "Rapidan River" as a flat light-blue band, with a small gray crossing block on it labeled "Germanna Ford" (the bridges taken up — the army is NOT going back). Run a thin gray road down through the field from the center toward the LOWER-RIGHT (southeast) corner labeled "Brock Road". Near that southeast corner, just off the road, place a small gray town dot labeled "Spotsylvania Court House". Draw ONE single bold tapered BLUE arrow (the arrow body and head filled BLUE — Grant is the Union commander) labeled "Grant" starting in the center of the woods and sweeping SOUTHEAST down the Brock Road, its point exiting the lower-right corner toward the Spotsylvania Court House dot. No other arrows, no fighting — one decisive march.

EVERY label on this map — including "Grant", "Brock Road", and "Germanna Ford" — must be BOLD NEAR-BLACK text sitting on its own SOLID WHITE rounded pill. Do NOT tint any label text a color: the "Grant" label is near-black on a white pill like all the others (never red or rust), and the road labels each sit on a solid white pill, not as bare text floating on the road.

Six labels only: Rapidan River, Germanna Ford, Brock Road, Spotsylvania Court House, Grant.
