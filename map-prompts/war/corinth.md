# War map prompts — Corinth (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map corinth-overview

The strategic setting, October 1862 — why one small Mississippi town was worth two armies: the crossing of two great railroads, ringed by lines of field fortifications. A setting overview, north up.

Center the map on a small gray town block labeled "Corinth", with a tiny gray depot dot at its heart (leave that depot dot UNLABELED — print NO "depot" text or any abbreviation near it; the only text anywhere on the map is the six labels listed at the end). Through the town draw two thin gray rail lines crossing at right angles: one running straight TOP-to-BOTTOM (north–south) labeled "Mobile & Ohio RR", the other running straight LEFT-to-RIGHT (east–west) labeled "Memphis & Charleston RR" — the two lines must visibly cross at the depot in the town's center. Wrap the town in the defense rings as faint thin gray arcs: a long sweeping outer arc several miles out across the TOP and upper-left (north and northwest) labeled "Old outer works", and a short tight inner arc hugging the town's northwest face labeled "Inner battery line". In the upper-left, off the northwest edge, place a small gray village dot labeled "Chewalla" with a thin gray road running from it down toward the town through the outer arc. Keep the whole field calm — no unit blocks, no arrows; this map is the stage, not the fight.

Six labels only: Corinth, Mobile & Ohio RR, Memphis & Charleston RR, Old outer works, Inner battery line, Chewalla.

## Map corinth

October 3–4, 1862 — Van Dorn drives in from the northwest down the Chewalla Road, breaks through the old outer works, then bleeds out on October 4 against the inner battery line at Battery Robinett before retreating west. A two-day tactical overview, north up.

Place the gray town block labeled "Corinth" in the lower-center-right, with the two thin gray rail lines crossing at its center (drawn but unlabeled here). Across the TOP and upper-left, draw the long faint gray arc of the field fortifications labeled "Old outer works". Hugging the town's northwest face, draw the short tight inner defensive line as a row of crisp BLUE unit blocks holding a low pale-tan rise labeled "College Hill". On that inner line set two small gray arrowhead-shaped earthwork forts: the forward one astride the road labeled "Battery Robinett", and one to its north-northeast labeled "Battery Powell". From the upper-left (northwest), down a thin gray road, send Van Dorn's attack as bold tapered RUST-RED arrows: a broad arrow breaking through the outer arc and driving southeast, its sharpest point striking the front of Battery Robinett, with a second rust-red arrow curling to hit Battery Powell from the north. Off the LEFT (west) edge, draw a single rust-red retreat arrow pulling away from the town labeled "Chewalla / Hatchie".

Six labels only: Corinth, College Hill, Battery Robinett, Battery Powell, Chewalla / Hatchie, Old outer works.
