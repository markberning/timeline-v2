# War map prompts — Vicksburg (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map vicksburg-overview

Spring 1863 — why Vicksburg was "the key": the Mississippi River winds past the fortress city on its east-bank bluffs, and the last Confederate-held stretch of the river runs from Vicksburg down to Port Hudson. A strategic overview of the prize, north up.

Draw the Mississippi River as a broad flat light-blue band running roughly north–south down the CENTER of the map, snaking in lazy curves, and label it "Mississippi River". At mid-map make the channel double back on itself in a tight hairpin bend; on the EAST bank at that bend place a small gray city block — drawn larger and bolder than any other settlement — sitting on a strip of pale-tan bluff, and label it "Vicksburg". Just opposite the city, inside the hairpin on the WEST (Louisiana) bank, mark the low swampy peninsula in pale sage-green and label it "De Soto Point". Far downstream, near the BOTTOM edge on the river's east side, place a smaller gray fortress block on its own pale-tan bluff and label it "Port Hudson" — the only other Confederate post still corking the river. Render the long stretch of river BETWEEN Vicksburg and Port Hudson in a slightly deeper blue to read as the contested Confederate-held reach, the prize. Off to the EAST (right) place a small gray town block to anchor the interior and label it "Jackson", about forty miles inland from the river.

Mark the city's strength with a short arc of rust-red unit blocks dug in along the Vicksburg bluff facing the water, and place a single rust-red gun symbol on the bluff aimed down at the channel. Up at the TOP edge, where the river enters from the north, show two small blue gunboat shapes on the water to read as the Union-held river above the fortress. Draw a bold blue arrow running DOWN the river from the top, narrowing as it reaches the Vicksburg bend and stopping there — the river the Union wants to open, blocked at the key.

Six labels only: Mississippi River, Vicksburg, De Soto Point, Port Hudson, Jackson.

## Map vicksburg-campaign

April–May 1863 — Grant's bold gamble: Porter's fleet runs the batteries past Vicksburg at night, the army crosses the river at Bruinsburg below Grand Gulf, cuts loose from its base, and drives inland through five battles before bottling Pemberton back inside the city. A campaign-movement overview, north up.

Draw the Mississippi River as a flat light-blue band running north–south down the LEFT-CENTER of the map, with the hairpin bend at upper-left; on the east bank at the bend place a gray city block labeled "Vicksburg", ringed by a small arc of rust-red unit blocks. From the river above the city, send a bold blue arrow of small gunboat shapes running DOWN past Vicksburg hugging the far bank — Porter's run past the batteries — continuing south below the city. Downstream on the east bank mark a small gray bluff block labeled "Grand Gulf" (rust-red gun symbol on it, where the assault failed); just below it, where the blue river-arrow swings to the east bank, mark the crossing point with a gray block labeled "Bruinsburg" and a thick blue ferry-arrow striking ashore there.

From Bruinsburg draw a single continuous bold blue campaign arrow looping inland — first a short hop north to a gray block labeled "Port Gibson", then curving northeast across open country (passing an unlabeled clash at Raymond) all the way EAST to a gray state-capital block on the right side labeled "Jackson". At Jackson show a small cluster of rust-red blocks being shoved further east off the right edge (Johnston driven away). Then bend the blue arrow back WEST, returning toward Vicksburg, and at the point where it crosses a wooded pale-tan rise between Jackson and the city, mark a gray hill labeled "Champion Hill" with rust-red blocks breaking apart under the blue arrow. Continue the blue arrow west to the Big Black River — a short light-blue ribbon just east of Vicksburg — and show Pemberton's rust-red blocks streaming back across it into the Vicksburg works.

Six labels only: Vicksburg, Grand Gulf, Bruinsburg, Port Gibson, Jackson, Champion Hill.

## Map vicksburg-siege

May 18 – July 4, 1863 — the 47-day siege: Grant's blue lines invest the city on the land (east) side, Porter's fleet shells from the river (west), and the Confederate fortifications ring Vicksburg. A close-in siege overview, north up.

Draw the Mississippi River as a broad flat light-blue band down the LEFT (west) edge, curving in the hairpin bend; on its east bank fill a pale-tan bluff strip and set the city as a gray block labeled "Vicksburg" atop it. Ring the city's LANDWARD (east) side with a continuous arc of rust-red fortification — a bold rust-red line of earthworks bowing outward to the east — and label the strongest salient at the arc's center with a small rust-red fort symbol labeled "Stockade Redan", and a second farther along the arc labeled "Third Louisiana Redan". The arc anchors on the river bluff at both its north and south ends.

Outside the rust-red ring, draw the Union investment as a parallel arc of BLUE: a heavy blue line of trenches facing the works, with thin gray zigzag approach-trench lines reaching forward from it toward the redans, and label this blue line "Union siege lines". Pack solid blue unit blocks behind it all the way around the eastern semicircle so the city is plainly surrounded on the land side. On the river to the WEST of the city, place a cluster of blue gunboat shapes with short blue arrows pointing at the bluff — the fleet shelling from the water. Show no escape: the rust-red garrison is sealed between the blue land arc and the blue river.

Six labels only: Vicksburg, Stockade Redan, Third Louisiana Redan, Union siege lines, Mississippi River.
