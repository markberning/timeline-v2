# War map prompts — Champion Hill (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map champion-hill-overview

May 16, 1863 — the ground that decided the Vicksburg campaign: a crossroads on the road home, wedged between Grant's army coming from Jackson in the east and the fortress of Vicksburg in the west. The orientation map, north up.

Lay the field east-to-west. Far on the RIGHT (east) edge place a small gray town block labeled "Jackson" — the wrecked state capital Grant is marching out of. Far on the LEFT (west) edge place a small gray city block labeled "Vicksburg" — the fortress the whole fight is about. Between them, in the center-north of the map, draw a pale-tan rounded height (a bald crest rising over the country around it) labeled "Champion Hill". Down the LEFT-center, behind the height to its west, draw a flat light-blue creek running roughly north-to-south labeled "Bakers Creek" — the water the Confederate army must get across to escape. Now draw the three thin gray roads, all running east-to-west, all converging from the Jackson side toward Vicksburg: the northernmost passes right over Champion Hill's crest — label it "Jackson Road"; a center road below it — label it "Middle Road"; the southernmost — label it "Raymond Road". Where the Jackson Road and the Middle Road meet, just south of the crest, mark the junction plainly.

Show no fighting yet — this is the lay of the land. Keep the three roads clearly parallel and clearly converging westward toward Vicksburg, with Champion Hill commanding the northern road and Bakers Creek lying behind everything to the west.

Six labels only: Jackson, Vicksburg, Champion Hill, Bakers Creek, Jackson Road, Raymond Road.

## Map champion-hill

Midday, May 16, 1863 — the hill of death: Hovey's blue assault carries the crest from the north, Bowen's rust counterattack retakes it, and the ground changes hands several times in a few savage hours. A zoomed-in view of the crest, north up.

Draw the pale-tan crest of "Champion Hill" filling the center of the map, with a small gray house block at its foot labeled "Champion house". Run a thin gray road across the top of the hill labeled "Jackson Road", and just below the crest mark the road junction where it meets the Middle Road. From the NORTH (top) drive a broad bold BLUE tapered arrow up and onto the crest — Hovey's and Logan's men climbing the slope and seizing the height. Then, straight into the nose of that blue arrow, drive a bold RUST tapered arrow back UP over the crest from the southwest — Bowen's Missourians and Arkansans counterattacking, shoving the blue line back down toward the Champion house. Show the two arrowheads colliding right on the crest so the eye reads the ground as contested — taken, lost, and taken again. Place a tight cluster of BLUE unit blocks pressing on the north slope and a tight cluster of RUST unit blocks on the crest itself, lines almost touching.

Six labels only: Champion Hill, Jackson Road, Champion house, Hovey, Bowen, Crocker.

## Map champion-hill-retreat

Afternoon into night, May 16, 1863 — Logan's blue division cuts the Jackson Road behind the Confederate line, forcing Pemberton's beaten army onto the single Raymond Road bridge over Bakers Creek, while Loring's rust division is cut off and never makes it back to Vicksburg. The retreat map, north up.

Draw the flat light-blue creek "Bakers Creek" running north-to-south down the LEFT-center of the map; the Confederate army is east of it and must cross westward to escape toward Vicksburg (off the left edge). Across the NORTH (top), draw a thin gray road labeled "Jackson Road" — and plant a solid block of BLUE unit blocks squarely astride it, west of the old battlefield, labeled "Logan" (the division sitting on the direct route to the crossing, slamming that door shut). Down at the SOUTH (bottom), draw the only crossing left: a small gray bridge over the creek on a thin gray road labeled "Raymond Road bridge". Through that single bridge funnel a bold RUST retreat arrow sweeping from the battlefield southwest across the creek and off the LEFT edge toward Vicksburg — Pemberton's main army escaping over the one bridge. Then, east of the creek and unable to reach the bridge in time, draw a separate RUST division block with its own arrow peeling away to the SOUTH and EAST (down and off the lower-right), curving the long way around — labeled "Loring cut off" — the division that never recrosses the creek and never reaches Vicksburg.

Six labels only: Bakers Creek, Jackson Road, Logan, Raymond Road bridge, Loring cut off, Vicksburg.
