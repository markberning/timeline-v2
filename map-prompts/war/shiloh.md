# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map shiloh

Dawn, April 6, 1862 — the Confederate surprise attack rolls northeast out of the woods toward Pittsburg Landing while Sherman's right fights to slow it. A day-one tactical overview, north up.

Draw the Tennessee River as a flat light-blue band down the RIGHT (east) edge. On its west bank place a small gray landing block labeled "Pittsburg Landing"; a few miles north place a second smaller gray block labeled "Crump's Landing". Draw a light-blue creek along the TOP edge labeled "Owl Creek" and a light-blue creek along the BOTTOM edge labeled "Lick Creek" (these frame the field north and south). In the lower-left place a small gray church block labeled "Shiloh Church" — Union camps sprawl in the woods around it. In the lower-center-left, just south of the camps, mark a small pale-gold farm clearing labeled "Fraley Field" — this is where the first shots fired.

Confederates attack from the SOUTHWEST in three broad rust-red lines: show three bold tapered rust-red arrows sweeping northeast out of the woods, origins near Shiloh Church's south, tips pushing toward Pittsburg Landing. Blue unit blocks begin collapsing toward the river; a final cluster of blue blocks presses against the ridge above the Landing.

Six labels only: Pittsburg Landing, Crump's Landing, Owl Creek, Lick Creek, Shiloh Church, Fraley Field.

## Map shiloh-hornets-nest

Afternoon, April 6, 1862 — the Union center holds on a farm lane for six hours while Johnston falls at the Peach Orchard and Ruggles masses 62 guns. A zoomed-in view of the center battlefield, north up.

Draw the Tennessee River as a narrow flat light-blue band on the RIGHT edge. Across the mid-field draw a thin gray farm lane running roughly west-to-east lined with a solid row of BLUE unit blocks — label this position "Hornet's Nest". Immediately south of the lane draw a pale-gold open orchard rectangle labeled "Peach Orchard". Between the Peach Orchard and the river, mark a small gray farmstead dot labeled "Bell Farm" (Johnston bled to death a short distance south of it). Along the bottom edge of the map, just west of the Hornet's Nest, show a massed row of tightly spaced rust-red artillery symbols (wheel-to-wheel cannons) labeled "Ruggles' Battery".

Confederate assaults come from the west and south: multiple bold rust-red arrows driving east into the blue Hornet's Nest line from across open ground, and additional rust-red arrows curling around both the north and south ends of the line to encircle it. Two small blue gunboat shapes sit in the Tennessee River on the right edge.

Six labels only: Hornet's Nest, Peach Orchard, Bell Farm, Ruggles' Battery, Tennessee River, Pittsburg Landing.

## Map shiloh-day2

April 7, 1862 — overnight reinforcements reverse the battle: Nelson's fresh brigades strike the Confederate left at dawn, the Hornet's Nest area is retaken by noon, and Beauregard's rear guard at Shiloh Church covers the retreat to Corinth. A day-two tactical overview, north up.

Draw the Tennessee River as a flat light-blue band on the RIGHT edge, with a small gray landing block labeled "Pittsburg Landing" on its west bank. Show a dense cluster of fresh BLUE unit blocks massed at Pittsburg Landing (Buell's Army of the Ohio just ferried across overnight). From this mass draw two bold blue arrows: one striking WEST-SOUTHWEST along the southern part of the field labeled "Nelson" (hitting the Confederate left — the southern end of their line); a second blue arrow driving straight west through the center toward the Hornet's Nest area, labeled "Hornet's Nest retaken". In the lower-left place a small gray church block labeled "Shiloh Church" with a compact rust-red unit block in front of it labeled "Breckinridge rear guard". From Shiloh Church draw a single rust-red retreat arrow pointing southwest off the lower-left corner labeled "Corinth".

Six labels only: Pittsburg Landing, Nelson, Hornet's Nest retaken, Shiloh Church, Breckinridge rear guard, Corinth.
