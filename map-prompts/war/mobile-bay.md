# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

---

> NAVAL adaptation — water is the field. The whole frame is a flat light-blue bay; tan land only for the forts' headlands and islands. Ships are small solid blocks (Union = BLUE #1d4ed8, Confederate = RUST RED #b44d3b), never sprite-detailed. The minefield ("torpedoes") is a row of small flat GRAY dots; the marked edge of the live mines is a line of small gray buoy dots. The safe ship channel is a darker-blue lane cut through the lighter bay. All other style rules above hold exactly: flat fills, BOLD near-black labels on SOLID WHITE pills, no outlined text, ≤6 labels, north up / east right, bleeds off all edges, no title/legend/compass/scale.

## Map mobile-bay-the-bay

The stage, August 5, 1864 — the mouth of Mobile Bay, the two forts that guard it, and the minefield and channel between them. A setting overview, north up.

Fill the whole frame with the flat light-blue water of the bay. On the RIGHT (east) edge, draw a tan headland point jutting into the water and set a small gray fort block on it labeled "Fort Morgan" (this is the strong fort, on the eastern point of the entrance). On the LEFT (west) edge, draw a tan island and set a small gray fort block on it labeled "Fort Gaines", with the island itself labeled "Dauphin Island". Between the two forts, cut a darker-blue lane running TOP-to-BOTTOM up the eastern side, hugging close under Fort Morgan — this is the open "channel". Mark the western edge of the safe lane with a vertical line of small gray buoy dots labeled "buoys", and west of that buoy line spread a broad field of small flat GRAY dots stretching toward Fort Gaines labeled "torpedoes" (the minefield fills the water WEST of the buoys; the channel runs EAST of them, under Fort Morgan). Up inside the bay toward the TOP (north of the forts), place a single rust-red ship block labeled "CSS Tennessee" waiting in open water. Off the TOP edge, place a small gray city dot labeled "Mobile" (the city, far up the bay, unreached). No arrows; this map is the stage, not the fight.

Six labels only: Fort Morgan, Fort Gaines, Dauphin Island, torpedoes, CSS Tennessee, Mobile.

## Map mobile-bay-running-the-forts

Dawn to ~7:30 a.m., August 5, 1864 — Farragut's fleet steams north up the narrow channel past Fort Morgan; the lead monitor Tecumseh veers west off the safe lane into the mines and sinks in seconds. The run past the fort, north up.

Fill the frame with the light-blue bay. On the RIGHT (east) edge draw the tan headland with a small gray fort block labeled "Fort Morgan", showing short rust-red gun-flash strokes firing WEST off the fort into the channel. Cut the darker-blue channel as a lane running BOTTOM-to-TOP up the eastern side, hugging close under Fort Morgan. Mark its western edge with a vertical line of small gray buoy dots labeled "buoys", and west of the buoys spread the field of small flat GRAY dots labeled "torpedoes". Up the channel send the Union fleet NORTH as a column of BLUE ship blocks: lead the column on the EAST (Fort Morgan) side with a tight file of four small BLUE blocks labeled "monitors". Break ONE blue block out of the head of the line and steer it WEST across the buoys into the gray dots, sinking — label this stray block "Tecumseh" and mark its sinking point with a small black X among the gray torpedo dots just west of the buoys. Behind and past it, draw a larger BLUE ship block driving on NORTH through the channel labeled "Hartford", with a bold tapered blue arrow continuing north up the bay ahead of it. A line of more blue blocks trails south behind Hartford in the channel.

Six labels only: Fort Morgan, buoys, torpedoes, monitors, Tecumseh, Hartford.

## Map mobile-bay-the-tennessee

Mid-morning, August 5, 1864 — north of the forts in the open upper bay, the lone Confederate ironclad turns and fights the whole Union fleet, which closes from every side and rams her under. The melee in the upper bay, north up.

Fill the frame with the open light-blue water of the upper bay (north of the forts — no land, no channel, no mines here). In the center place a single rust-red ironclad block labeled "CSS Tennessee", alone. Surround her with the Union fleet: several BLUE ship blocks closing in from all sides — north, south, east, and west of her — and draw bold tapered BLUE arrows driving inward from the encircling blocks straight at the Tennessee, ramming her from multiple directions at once. Tag two of the closing blue blocks with their names: one labeled "Hartford" and one labeled "Chickasaw". Show the Tennessee battered and motionless at the center of the converging blue arrows.

Six labels only: CSS Tennessee, Hartford, Chickasaw.
