# War map prompts — Second Fort Fisher (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style chosen 2026-05-22 (replaced the old
flat-tan schematic). Label style = the locked no-box rule (canonical preamble
copied from `wilsons-creek.md` rule 4, 2026-06-01).

Fort Fisher is a giant **L-shaped sand earthwork** at the **tip of a long narrow
peninsula** at the mouth of the Cape Fear River. The **Atlantic Ocean is on the
EAST (right)** and the **Cape Fear River is on the WEST (left)**; show WATER on
both sides on every map. The fort's **land face** runs west-to-east across the
neck of the peninsula (facing NORTH, where the attackers come from); the **sea
face** runs north-to-south down the Atlantic side; the two meet at the **Northeast
Bastion**. The fort's gun line is broken into a row of mounds called **traverses**.
**Battery Buchanan** is a small separate earthwork at the very tip, where the
peninsula meets the river. Porter's **fleet** is a line of blue ship-blocks
standing off the Atlantic shore. The opener section already carries a `{locator}`,
so NO locator map here — tactical maps only.

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map second-fort-fisher-armada

January 13, 1865 — the fleet returns and Terry lands on the beach above the fort. A peninsula-scale tactical view showing the trap that never sprang.

Terrain — this is the whole peninsula tip, with water on BOTH sides:
- A flat light-blue band fills the RIGHT (east) edge, labeled "Atlantic Ocean".
- A flat light-blue band fills the LEFT (west) edge, labeled "Cape Fear River".
- Between them runs the pale sandy peninsula. At the LOWER portion, draw the gray L-shaped sand earthwork of the fort: a long land face running west-to-east across the neck (facing NORTH), and a sea face running down the Atlantic side, labeled "Fort Fisher".

Forces and movements:
- Porter's fleet: a LINE of solid blue ship-blocks standing in the Atlantic Ocean off the fort's sea face, labeled "Porter". (The fleet is offshore in the water on the right, firing toward the fort.)
- Terry's landing: one bold blue arrow comes in from the Atlantic onto the open beach NORTH of the fort and lands a row of solid blue unit blocks there, labeled "Terry".
- Terry's rear-guard line: the blue blocks form a line ACROSS the peninsula FACING NORTH (up the peninsula), guarding their rear against the Confederates above.
- The Confederate force that did not come: up the peninsula to the NORTH, draw a row of solid rust-red unit blocks held back and idle, labeled "Hoke", with a small gray town marker farther up labeled "Wilmington" and the name "Bragg" beside it. No rust-red arrow drives south — the trap never sprang; the rust-red line simply sits north of Terry's line.
- The garrison: a short row of solid rust-red unit blocks manning the fort's land face, facing NORTH toward Terry, labeled "Lamb".

EIGHT labels allowed here (more than six): Atlantic Ocean, Cape Fear River, Fort Fisher, Porter, Terry, Hoke, Wilmington, Bragg, Lamb. Keep each BIG and bold over open pale ground. (Nine total — print all nine; they are the named waters, the fort, and every commander who led or held a position.)

## Map second-fort-fisher-bombardment

January 13–15, 1865 — three days of methodical naval fire that knocks out the fort's guns. A close view of the fort and the fleet offshore.

Terrain:
- A flat light-blue band fills the RIGHT (east) edge, labeled "Atlantic Ocean".
- A flat light-blue band fills the LEFT (west) edge, labeled "Cape Fear River".
- In the center-lower portion, draw the gray L-shaped sand earthwork: the long land face running west-to-east (facing NORTH) and the sea face running north-to-south on the Atlantic side, labeled "Fort Fisher". Along the top edge of the land face draw a row of small gray mounds (the gun positions), labeled "land face".

Forces and movements:
- Porter's fleet: a dense LINE of solid blue ship-blocks standing in the Atlantic Ocean off the sea face, labeled "Porter".
- The bombardment: SEVERAL bold blue arrows fired from the line of ships WESTWARD across the water, striking the fort's land face and sea face (the methodical fire dismounting the guns). The arrows originate from the blue ship-blocks and point INTO the fort.
- The garrison: a short row of solid rust-red unit blocks crouched behind the land face, labeled "Lamb".

SIX labels only: Atlantic Ocean, Cape Fear River, Fort Fisher, land face, Porter, Lamb. Nothing else.

## Map second-fort-fisher-naval-charge

Afternoon of January 15, 1865 — Breese's 2,000 sailors and Marines charge the Northeast Bastion and are slaughtered on the open beach. A close view of the fort's seaward corner.

Terrain:
- A flat light-blue band fills the RIGHT (east) edge, labeled "Atlantic Ocean".
- A flat light-blue band fills the LEFT (west) edge, labeled "Cape Fear River".
- Draw the gray L-shaped sand earthwork with its land face running west-to-east and its sea face running north-to-south; at the corner where they meet (upper-right of the fort) draw a tall gray mound labeled "Northeast Bastion". Label the whole work "Fort Fisher".

Forces and movements:
- The naval assault: on the open beach north of the bastion, draw a packed mass of solid blue unit blocks with one bold blue arrow driving SOUTH along the beach into the Northeast Bastion, labeled "Breese". (This is the sailor-and-Marine charge; show it crowded and aimed at the seaward corner.)
- The defenders: a row of solid rust-red unit blocks on the parapet of the Northeast Bastion, FACING NORTH toward the charge, with short bold rust-red arrows of fire pouring DOWN into the blue mass on the beach, labeled "Lamb".

FIVE labels only: Atlantic Ocean, Cape Fear River, Fort Fisher, Northeast Bastion, Breese, Lamb. (Six total — print all six; they are the named waters, the fort, the named corner, and both commanders who led the charge and held the wall.)

## Map second-fort-fisher-the-fall

Night of January 15, 1865 — Ames's division storms the western end of the land face and fights traverse by traverse to Battery Buchanan, where the fort surrenders. A close view of the land face along its whole length.

Terrain:
- A flat light-blue band fills the RIGHT (east) edge, labeled "Atlantic Ocean".
- A flat light-blue band fills the LEFT (west) edge, labeled "Cape Fear River".
- Draw the long gray sand land face running west-to-east across the peninsula (facing NORTH), broken into a ROW of gray mounds along its length, labeled "traverses". Label the fort "Fort Fisher".
- At the TIP of the peninsula where it meets the Cape Fear River (lower-left), draw a small separate gray earthwork labeled "Battery Buchanan".

Forces and movements:
- The army assault: at the WESTERN (left) end of the land face, the corner nearest the river, draw a row of solid blue unit blocks north of the wall with one bold blue arrow driving SOUTH over the western end of the land face, labeled "Ames".
- The traverse fight: a second bold blue arrow continues EAST-then-SOUTH along the land face, mound by mound, and curls down toward Battery Buchanan, labeled "Curtis" (the brigade that led the way).
- The defenders: a thinning row of solid rust-red unit blocks along the traverses being pushed back toward Battery Buchanan, FACING the blue arrows, labeled "Whiting".

SIX labels only: Atlantic Ocean, Cape Fear River, Fort Fisher, traverses, Battery Buchanan, Ames, Curtis, Whiting. (Eight total — print all eight; this section is the climax, naming both waters, the fort, the traverses, the final battery, and the three commanders who led the storming and the defense.)
