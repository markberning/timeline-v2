# War map prompts — Fort Blakeley (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style copied verbatim from `antietam.md`.
Tactical maps only (the opener already carries a {locator}); no operational/
campaign map. Movements are attributed to the commander who actually led the
column, not the army namesake (Canby is overall; the assaulting columns and the
USCT division go by their own leaders).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map fort-blakeley-siege

Late March to April 9, 1865 — the Union army digs its way up to Fort Blakeley, the last earthwork above Mobile, after Spanish Fort falls. A tactical approach view, NOT the whole campaign.

Terrain:
- Down the RIGHT (east) edge runs a light-blue river band bending south, labeled "Blakeley River". Out in that water, near the lower-right, place two small gray island-battery markers close together, labeled "Huger" and "Tracy".
- The Confederate earthwork is a long curving line running roughly north–south down the CENTER, bowed toward the east (toward its attackers), anchored by small gray strongpoint blocks (the redoubts) spaced along it, labeled "Fort Blakeley". Draw a row of solid rust-red unit blocks holding this line, FACING EAST toward the Union approach.
- Below (south of) Blakeley along the same shore, place a small gray fort marker labeled "Spanish Fort", with a faint thin-gray causeway track leading north from it up toward the Blakeley line (the route Gibson's garrison withdrew along).
- Between the earthwork and the Union lines, fill the cleared ground east of the wall with a pale-gold furrowed strip dotted with tiny felled-tree and stake marks (the abatis and obstacle field). Leave it unlabeled.

Union approach (from the EAST, facing west toward the wall): draw THREE successive lines of solid blue unit blocks parallel to the earthwork, each closer than the last, the foremost just east of the obstacle field. From the southernmost stretch, draw one bold blue arrow climbing up the eastern shore from Spanish Fort toward Blakeley, labeled "Steele" (Steele's column closing in after the march up from Pensacola).

SIX labels only: Fort Blakeley, Spanish Fort, Blakeley River, Huger, Tracy, Steele. Nothing else.

## Map fort-blakeley-storm

Near 5:30 p.m., April 9, 1865 — the whole Union line storms the earthwork at once, with the United States Colored Troops carrying the Confederate right. A zoomed-in tactical view of the assault, NOT the whole siege.

Terrain:
- The Confederate earthwork curves down the CENTER, bowed eastward toward its attackers and anchored by small gray redoubt blocks, labeled "Fort Blakeley". Draw rust-red unit blocks holding it, FACING EAST. A light-blue river band runs down the RIGHT (east) edge behind the upper part of the line, labeled "Blakeley River".
- East of the wall, fill the open ground with a pale-gold furrowed field dotted with tiny felled-tree and stake marks and small burst symbols (the cleared field of abatis, sharpened stakes, and buried subterra shells the attackers crossed). Leave it unlabeled.

The defenders: along the rust-red line, anchor the NORTH-and-CENTER stretch with a denser cluster of blocks labeled "Cockrell" (his hard Missouri and Mississippi brigades). Anchor the SOUTH (right) stretch, nearer the river bend, with a thinner row of blocks labeled "Thomas" (the young Alabama reserves).

The Union storm (charging WEST out of the trenches, all along the front): draw a row of solid blue unit blocks across the EAST, with bold blue arrows driving west across the obstacle field into the wall at several points. On the far Union right (toward the river, against Thomas's stretch), draw one especially bold blue arrow crossing the worst of the obstacle ground and breaking over the parapet, labeled "Hawkins" (the division of United States Colored Troops). The defending line FACES these arrows.

FIVE labels only: Fort Blakeley, Blakeley River, Cockrell, Thomas, Hawkins. Nothing else.
