# War map prompts — Wilson's Creek (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections → `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

---

**LABEL PLACEMENT — applies to EVERY label on EVERY map (critical):** Each label, together with its box/halo, must sit FULLY INSIDE the frame with a clear margin on all four sides. NO label may touch, be clipped by, or run off any edge — pull every edge-hugging name inward toward open ground. No two labels may overlap or touch each other. When a label cannot sit in clear open space right beside the place or arrow it names without colliding with another label, an arrow, troop blocks, or the edge, move it to the nearest open area and draw a THIN GRAY LEADER LINE from the label to its dot/marker so the link stays obvious. River and water labels must be SOLID and STRONG (a deep, readable blue), never pale, faint, or washed-out; if blue-on-water would be low-contrast, set the river name on open ground beside the water with a halo instead.

## Map wilsons-creek-campaign

The Missouri campaign, summer 1861 — a STRATEGIC statewide overview (not a battle close-up), north up. Lyon chases Missouri's secessionist government across the state into the far southwest; a second Union column under Sigel swings down through the southwest and is bloodied at Carthage; the Confederates gather to meet them at Wilson's Creek. The map is statewide and spacious, so spread the labels out and keep every one big and bold.

Draw the Missouri River as a flat light-blue band (UNLABELED) sweeping across the upper third of the frame, entering from the right (east) and curving up toward the left (west). Place a small gray town block on the river in the upper-center labeled "Jefferson City". Place another small gray town block on the river just up and to the left of it (northwest) labeled "Boonville". Place a gray town block low in the center-left (the southwest) labeled "Springfield". Just below and left of Springfield, draw a short flat light-blue creek with a small cluster of solid rust-red unit blocks beside it, labeled "Wilson's Creek". Place a gray town block in the lower-LEFT corner, west of Springfield, labeled "Carthage".

Draw Lyon's march as one bold BLUE arrow that starts at the right edge near the river, runs left (west) along the river past Jefferson City and Boonville, then curves down to Springfield, labeled "Lyon". Draw a SECOND, separate bold BLUE arrow, lower in the frame, that comes in from the right and sweeps down and left to Carthage and on toward Springfield, labeled "Sigel". Draw a bold RUST-RED arrow coming up from the bottom edge (out of Arkansas to the south) to the Wilson's Creek cluster, labeled "McCulloch".

Eight labels: Jefferson City, Boonville, Springfield, Wilson's Creek, Carthage, Lyon, Sigel, McCulloch. No others, and print no color words. Keep them big, bold, upright, and well-separated so none crowd or overlap. Label the arrows ("Lyon", "Sigel", "McCulloch") the same way as the towns: solid filled dark text with only a thin cream halo, no white box behind any label.


**LABEL PLACEMENT (round 2, critical — this map STILL clipped labels at an edge):** EVERY label and its box/halo must sit fully inside the frame with a wide clear margin on all four sides; NONE may touch or run off any edge. Place "McCulloch" well ABOVE the bottom edge, beside the lower part of its red arrow with a wide clear margin below the text — do NOT let the letters touch the bottom border. No two labels may overlap or touch. A leader line is a thin gray line, nothing more.
## Map wilsons-creek-plan

The night march, August 9–10, 1861 — Lyon splits his small army outside Springfield and sends the halves on a converging dawn attack. A strategic overview, north up.

Place a small gray town block labeled "Springfield" in the upper-right (northeast) corner. Draw the "Wire Road" as a thin gray road running from Springfield diagonally down to the lower-left (the southwest). Draw "Wilson's Creek" as a flat light-blue band running north–south in the lower-left third of the frame, with the Wire Road crossing it; cluster a few solid rust-red unit blocks along the creek to mark the Confederate camp.

From Springfield, draw Lyon's main column as a bold BLUE arrow that runs southwest down toward the creek and curls in to strike the camp from the north, labeled "Lyon". Draw Sigel's column as a second bold blue arrow that branches off near Springfield, loops in a wide clockwise sweep south and around the bottom of the frame, and comes up to strike the camp from the south, labeled "Sigel".

Six labels only: Springfield, Wire Road, Wilson's Creek, Lyon, Sigel. Nothing else.

## Map wilsons-creek

Draw "Wilson's Creek" as a flat light-blue band running roughly north–south down the left of the frame. Draw the "Wire Road" as a thin gray road crossing the creek at a ford and running up toward the upper-right. Place a broad pale-tan plateau labeled "Bloody Hill" on the creek's east bank in the center of the frame, ringed with soft sage-green brush.

Set a row of solid BLUE Union unit blocks along the crest of Bloody Hill facing south. Draw Lyon's attack as a bold blue arrow coming down from the north onto the hilltop, labeled "Lyon". Cluster solid rust-red Confederate blocks south and below the hill and draw bold rust-red arrows surging UPHILL into the blue line; label the rust force "Price".

Six labels only: Wilson's Creek, Wire Road, Bloody Hill, Lyon, Price. Nothing else.

Draw "Wilson's Creek" as a flat light-blue band running east–west across the upper part of the frame. Draw the "Wire Road" as a thin gray road crossing the creek and running down toward the lower-left. Mark a small gray house block labeled "Sharp Farm" in the lower-center, just south of the creek.

Show Sigel's blue column as a row of solid BLUE unit blocks strung loosely across the Wire Road near the Sharp farm, labeled "Sigel". Drive a bold rust-red arrow down into the flank of that line from the east, with a row of solid rust-red blocks behind it, labeled "McCulloch". Draw a broken blue arrow breaking away to the rear (toward the lower-left, back up the Wire Road) to show the rout.

Six labels only: Wilson's Creek, Wire Road, Sharp Farm, Sigel, McCulloch. Nothing else.
