# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

---

**LABEL PLACEMENT — applies to EVERY label on EVERY map (critical):** Each label, together with its box/halo, must sit FULLY INSIDE the frame with a clear margin on all four sides. NO label may touch, be clipped by, or run off any edge — pull every edge-hugging name inward toward open ground. No two labels may overlap or touch each other. When a label cannot sit in clear open space right beside the place or arrow it names without colliding with another label, an arrow, troop blocks, or the edge, move it to the nearest open area and draw a THIN GRAY LEADER LINE from the label to its dot/marker so the link stays obvious. River and water labels must be SOLID and STRONG (a deep, readable blue), never pale, faint, or washed-out; if blue-on-water would be low-contrast, set the river name on open ground beside the water with a halo instead.

## Map cold-harbor-overview

The stage, early June 1864 — the road junction 10 miles northeast of Richmond where two armies dug in facing each other, the swampy river crossing the lower field. A setting overview, north up. The stage, lightly drawn — no arrows, no fight.

Place a small gray crossroads DOT in the center-right of the field labeled "Cold Harbor" — render it as a junction where a few thin gray roads meet, NOT a port, NOT a landing, NOT a block on water (there is no water at this crossroads despite the name). Off the LOWER-LEFT (southwest) edge, place a small gray city block labeled "Richmond" with a thin gray road running from it up toward the crossroads. Down the LOWER / southern part of the field draw a sluggish flat light-blue river band, curving across the bottom, labeled "Chickahominy River".

Just WEST of the Cold Harbor crossroads, draw the Confederate position as a single long continuous RUST-RED earthwork arc — a tall sawtooth/scalloped rampart line snaking north-to-south down the field, its toothed face turned EAST toward the crossroads — label this line "Lee's line". Opposite it, a short distance to the EAST, draw the Union position as a parallel row of crisp BLUE unit blocks facing west toward the rust-red works — label this blue line "Grant". Keep both lines calm and lightly drawn: this is the stage being set, not the assault.

Six labels only: Cold Harbor, Richmond, Chickahominy River, Lee's line, Grant.


**LABEL PLACEMENT (round 3, HARD RULE — earlier draws clipped labels at the edges):** Compose the ENTIRE map — all terrain, water, arrows, dots, AND every label — within the central 82% of the canvas, leaving a blank parchment margin around all four edges that NOTHING enters. No label, box, or halo may touch or cross into that outer margin band. Print every place name and every commander name EXACTLY ONCE — never repeat any label anywhere on the map. Where a feature sits toward the edge of the scene, set its label INWARD toward the center and join it to the feature with a thin gray leader line. No two labels may overlap or touch.
## Map cold-harbor-june-3

Dawn, June 3, 1864 — three Union corps charge west into Lee's finished seven-mile trench and break against it; the works hold and the assault fails at the line. A tactical assault view, north up.

Down the center-right of the field draw Lee's line as one long continuous RUST-RED earthwork — a tall sawtooth/scalloped rampart running north-to-south, its toothed face turned EAST (to the right) toward the attackers — label this rampart "works". From the EAST (right) side, send the Union assault as three bold tapered BLUE arrows charging WEST / southwest straight at the rampart, arrayed top-to-bottom: the northern arrow labeled "Smith", the center arrow labeled "Wright", the southern arrow labeled "Hancock". CRITICAL: every blue arrow must BLUNT and break apart AGAINST the front of the rust-red rampart — show the arrowheads flattened, splayed, and stopped at the earthwork face, NONE of them piercing through to the west side. The works stay solid and unbroken along their whole length; the blue arrows pile up and shatter at the line. Make it unmistakable that the charge failed at the trench. Off the LOWER-LEFT (southwest) edge place a small gray city block labeled "Richmond" behind the rust-red line.

Six labels only: works, Smith, Wright, Hancock, Richmond.
