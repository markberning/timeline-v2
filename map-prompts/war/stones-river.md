# War map prompts — Stones River (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map stones-river-overview

The two armies bed down astride Stones River west of Murfreesboro the night of December 30, 1862, while Wheeler's Confederate cavalry rides clear around the Union rear to wreck the supply line back to Nashville. A pre-battle strategic overview, north up.

Draw Stones River as a flat light-blue band running roughly south-to-north up the CENTER of the field, bending gently. On its EAST bank, lower-center-right, place a gray town block labeled "Murfreesboro". Running NW–SE straight across the whole field, crossing the river, draw two thin parallel gray lines a hair apart — a road and a rail line together — and label the road "Nashville Pike" and the rail line "Nashville & Chattanooga Railroad"; this paired axis is the Union lifeline running off the upper-LEFT (northwest) corner toward Nashville. West of the river, set crisp BLUE unit blocks (the Union army) facing east; east and across from them set RUST-RED unit blocks (the Confederate army) facing west — the two lines mirror each other along the river. In the upper-left, well behind the Union line where the pike heads off-map, mark a small gray village block labeled "LaVergne". Draw a single bold RUST-RED arrow looping in a wide clockwise circle from the Confederate position out around behind the Union rear and striking the pike at LaVergne, then curling back — Wheeler's ride around the Union army — and label that sweeping arrow "Wheeler".

Six labels only: Stones River, Murfreesboro, Nashville Pike, Nashville & Chattanooga Railroad, LaVergne, Wheeler.

## Map stones-river

Dawn, December 31, 1862 — Bragg's surprise attack out of the cedars wheels the Union right back three miles like a closing jackknife, until Sheridan's stand and the Round Forest near the pike bend the line into an L that will not break. A day-one tactical overview, north up.

Draw Stones River as a flat light-blue band up the RIGHT (east) side of the field. Running NW–SE across the field, draw a thin gray road-and-rail pair and label it "Nashville Pike". In the lower-LEFT (southwest), draw a light-blue creek labeled "Overall Creek" — the Union right flank hangs in the air past it, anchored on nothing. Fill the left and center with soft sage-green cedar woods. The Confederate attack comes from the SOUTHWEST: draw two or three bold RUST-RED tapered arrows sweeping up out of the woods and then curving hard to the right (northeast), wheeling the Union line back on itself — label the lead arrow "Hardee". Show BLUE unit blocks folding backward in a great arc, hinging from the lower-left around toward the pike, so the surviving Union line forms a bent L with its back to the Nashville Pike. In the cedar woods at the center of that arc, set a dense knot of BLUE blocks standing firm against the rust-red arrows and label it "Slaughter Pen" (Sheridan's four-hour stand). Near the pike on the Union LEFT, where the river and road run close, mark a small sage-green copse held by a tight BLUE block and label it "Round Forest" — Confederate arrows break against it.

Six labels only: Stones River, Nashville Pike, Overall Creek, Hardee, Slaughter Pen, Round Forest.

## Map stones-river-day2

Four o'clock, January 2, 1863 — Breckinridge's charge crosses toward the Union-held hill on the east bank, only to be torn apart at McFadden's Ford by Mendenhall's roughly fifty massed Union guns firing across the river from the west bank. A day-two tactical overview, north up.

Draw Stones River as a flat light-blue band running south-to-north up the CENTER of the field. Mark a shallow crossing where the river narrows, in the LOWER-center, and label it "McFadden's Ford". Run a pale-tan ridge band down the EAST bank (right side); at its SOUTH end, just above the ford, set a row of BLUE Union unit blocks holding the ridge (the division Breckinridge is ordered to drive off). At the NORTH end of that same east-bank ridge, mass RUST-RED unit blocks (Breckinridge's attacking force) and label them "Breckinridge"; from there drive a broad bold RUST-RED arrow charging SOUTH down the ridge into the blue blocks, driving them south toward and across McFadden's Ford. On the WEST bank (left), directly opposite the ford, draw a long row of tightly spaced BLUE artillery symbols, wheel to wheel, facing east, and label that gun line "Mendenhall" — draw their fire as short blue strokes firing EAST across the river into the flank of the rust-red charge as it crowds down to the ford. At the tip of the charge, right at the ford, render a smaller rust-red block breaking apart under the fire and label it "Orphan Brigade". Run the Nashville Pike as a thin gray road-and-rail line across the lower-LEFT of the field and label it "Nashville Pike" on a SOLID WHITE pill.

Six labels only: Stones River, McFadden's Ford, Mendenhall, Breckinridge, Orphan Brigade, Nashville Pike.
