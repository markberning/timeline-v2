# War map prompts — Second Bull Run (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map second-bull-run-campaign

August 25–27, 1862 — Jackson's wing vanishes off the Rappahannock, loops fifty miles around Pope's right, and comes down in Pope's rear to burn the Union supply depot at Manassas Junction while Pope faces the wrong way. A campaign overview, north up.

Draw the **Rappahannock River** as a flat light-blue band down the LOWER-LEFT (southwest), where the two armies had been facing off — place a solid block of BLUE unit blocks along its north bank labeled "Pope" and a cluster of rust-red blocks opposite, facing them so Pope's army looks SOUTH and WEST (the wrong way). Run the **Orange & Alexandria Railroad** as a thin gray crosshatched rail line from the lower-left up toward the upper-right; mark a small gray depot block where the rail line is cut, southwest of the junction, labeled "Bristoe Station". A few miles northeast along the rail line place a larger gray depot block, on fire (a small rust-red flame/smoke mark), labeled "Manassas Junction". From the Rappahannock, sweep one bold tapered RUST-RED arrow in a wide loop — first west and north up off the left edge, then curling back east across the top and dropping down onto Bristoe Station and Manassas Junction in Pope's rear — labeled "Jackson". Keep the arrow clearly OUTSIDE and AROUND Pope's blue blocks, never through them, so the flank march reads as going around his right.

Six labels only: Rappahannock River, Pope, Orange & Alexandria Railroad, Bristoe Station, Manassas Junction, Jackson.

## Map second-bull-run

August 29, 1862 — Jackson's wing is dug in along the unfinished railroad while Pope hurls divisions at it one at a time from the southeast, and Longstreet's wing arrives unseen on Jackson's right. A day-two tactical overview, north up.

Draw the **Warrenton Turnpike** as a thin gray road running west-to-east across the lower-middle of the map, crossing a light-blue creek (Bull Run) at the RIGHT edge over a small gray bridge block labeled "Stone Bridge". NORTH of and parallel to the turnpike, draw the abandoned grade as a thin gray double line lined on its near side with a long solid row of RUST-RED unit blocks facing SOUTH — label this dug-in line "Unfinished Railroad". Just north of the line's western end, mark a pale-gold open farm clearing labeled "Brawner's Farm". From the SOUTHEAST, drive several short bold BLUE tapered arrows up against the railroad line one after another (piecemeal), origins staggered back from the line so they read as separate uncoordinated blows — label this attacking mass "Pope". SOUTH of the turnpike, at the lower-left/center, place a compact body of rust-red unit blocks half-hidden behind a soft sage-green wood patch, facing east toward the Union flank, labeled "Longstreet" — clearly off to the side, not yet engaged.

Six labels only: Unfinished Railroad, Brawner's Farm, Warrenton Turnpike, Stone Bridge, Pope, Longstreet.

## Map second-bull-run-longstreet

August 30, 1862, 4:00 p.m. — Longstreet's five divisions crash into the exposed Union left while desperate stands on Chinn Ridge and Henry House Hill buy time for the army to retreat over the Stone Bridge. A day-three tactical overview, north up.

Draw the **Warrenton Turnpike** as a thin gray road running west-to-east across the middle of the map and crossing a light-blue Bull Run creek at the RIGHT edge over a small gray bridge block labeled "Stone Bridge". From the LOWER-LEFT (southwest), sweep a broad fan of bold tapered RUST-RED arrows northeast and east, slamming into the Union left — make this the dominant force on the map, and label the base of that rust-red mass "Longstreet". In the arrows' path, south of the turnpike, place a pale-tan rise with a thin line of BLUE unit blocks holding against the rust tide, labeled "Chinn Ridge"; behind it and to the east, on a second pale-tan rise just south of the turnpike, a second thin BLUE blocking line labeled "Henry House Hill". From those crumbling blue stands draw one bold BLUE retreat arrow curving NORTHEAST across the turnpike and over the Stone Bridge, off the upper-right corner toward "Centreville" (a small gray town block at the upper-right edge).

Six labels only: Chinn Ridge, Henry House Hill, Warrenton Turnpike, Stone Bridge, Centreville, Longstreet.
