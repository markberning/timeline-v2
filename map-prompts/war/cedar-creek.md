# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map cedar-creek-dawn

Dawn, October 19, 1864 — Early's army comes out of the fog and overruns the sleeping Union camps, his columns sweeping up out of the southeast across the creek and into the Union left. A day-break surprise overview, north up.

Draw Cedar Creek as a flat light-blue band running roughly west-to-east across the LOWER part of the field, labeled "Cedar Creek". Run the Valley Pike as a thin gray road STRAIGHT down the center, TOP-to-BOTTOM (north–south), crossing the creek; label it "Valley Pike". Along the pike, on the NORTH bank of the creek, set the Union camps as a column of crisp BLUE unit blocks stretched out along the road (men asleep in their tents). At the center of the camps, just north of the creek, place a small gray manor-house block labeled "Belle Grove" (Sheridan's headquarters). Where the pike crosses south of the camps, mark a small gray town block labeled "Middletown". From the lower-right (the SOUTHEAST), launch Early's surprise flank attack: bold tapered RUST-RED arrows pouring up out of the southeast, crossing the light-blue creek and slamming into the southern end of the BLUE camp column, RUST arrows overrunning and folding the sleeping BLUE blocks back toward the north. Keep the strike clearly hitting the camps' exposed left (southeastern) end.

Six labels only: Cedar Creek, Valley Pike, Belle Grove, Middletown.

## Map cedar-creek-sheridans-ride

Mid-morning, October 19, 1864 — Sheridan, away at Winchester when his army broke, gallops the dozen miles south down the Valley Pike to the field. Just the ride, north up.

Draw the Valley Pike as a single thin gray road running STRAIGHT down the middle of the map, TOP-to-BOTTOM (north–south), and label it "Valley Pike". At the TOP (north) end of the pike, place a small gray town block labeled "Winchester". At the BOTTOM (south) end, mark the battlefield with a small gray town block labeled "Middletown" and a flat light-blue creek band just north of it labeled "Cedar Creek". Down the length of the pike, from Winchester at the top toward the field at the bottom, draw a single bold tapered BLUE arrow running SOUTH and label it "Sheridan" — one clean galloping arrow, nothing else. No camps, no enemy, no other arrows; this map is only the ride.

Six labels only: Valley Pike, Winchester, Middletown, Cedar Creek, Sheridan.

## Map cedar-creek-counterattack

Late afternoon, October 19, 1864 — the rallied Union army drives the Confederates back south across Cedar Creek in rout while Custer's cavalry curls around the flank. A counterattack overview, north up.

Run the Valley Pike as a thin gray road STRAIGHT down the center, TOP-to-BOTTOM (north–south), labeled "Valley Pike". Draw Cedar Creek as a flat light-blue band running roughly west-to-east across the LOWER part of the field, labeled "Cedar Creek", with the pike crossing it. Mark the small gray town block where the pike meets the field labeled "Middletown". North of Middletown, set the re-formed Union army as a solid line of BLUE unit blocks facing SOUTH. South of them, draw the Confederate line as RUST-RED unit blocks giving way. From the BLUE line send bold tapered BLUE arrows sweeping SOUTH, driving the RUST blocks back across Cedar Creek in a breaking rout (RUST arrows scattering southward off the lower edge). On the far RIGHT (west) end, draw a bold BLUE arrow curling around and down to hook behind the Confederate flank toward the creek crossing, labeled "Custer".

Six labels only: Valley Pike, Cedar Creek, Middletown, Custer.
