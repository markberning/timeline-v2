# War map prompts — Franklin (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map franklin-overview

The stage, late afternoon November 30, 1864 — the town of Franklin tucked inside a bend of the Harpeth River, with Schofield's army dug in behind a semicircle of earthen works facing two miles of open ground to the south. The setting, not the fight. North up.

Place a small gray town block labeled "Franklin" in the upper-center of the map. Draw the river as a flat light-blue band looping around the town's NORTH and EAST — coming down the right (east) side and curving across the top behind the town — labeled "Harpeth River"; the river sits BEHIND the town, with the town on its south bank. South and southwest of the town, draw Schofield's defensive line as a crisp BLUE arc of field works (a row of blue unit blocks forming a semicircle) curving from the river on one side around to the river on the other, the open face of the arc pointing SOUTH toward the bottom of the map; label this blue arc "Schofield". Draw the road as a thin gray line running straight SOUTH-to-NORTH up through the center of the map, passing through a deliberate gap in the blue arc and on into the town; label this road "Columbia Pike". Right at the point where the Pike pierces the blue line, mark a small gray house block on the west side of the road labeled "Carter House" and, just east of the road on the line, a small gray barn-like block labeled "cotton gin" — these two sit at the salient where the works bend toward the enemy. Below the works, fill the entire lower half of the map with calm pale-gold open farmland (the roughly two miles of open ground the attack must cross) — no woods, no walls. Keep the field calm: no arrows, no Confederate units; this map is the stage.

Six labels only: Franklin, Harpeth River, Schofield, Columbia Pike, Carter House, cotton gin.

## Map franklin-the-charge

The assault, dusk November 30, 1864 — some twenty thousand of Hood's men charge north across two miles of open ground straight into the Union works, briefly break the line at the Carter House and cotton gin, and are sealed back out by Opdycke's reserve. A tactical overview, north up.

Place the gray town block labeled "Franklin" in the upper-center, with the Harpeth River drawn (unlabeled) as a flat light-blue band looping behind it to the north and east. South and southwest of the town, draw the Union line as a crisp BLUE arc of works (a semicircle of blue unit blocks) facing SOUTH and holding firm. Draw the Columbia Pike (drawn but unlabeled) as a thin gray line running south-to-north through a gap in the blue arc into the town; at that gap mark a small gray house block on the west labeled "Carter House" and a small gray barn block just east labeled "cotton gin". From the bottom edge, send Hood's assault as several broad bold tapered RUST-RED arrows driving straight NORTH across the open ground into the blue works; label this attacking mass "Hood". Show the rust-red arrowheads briefly piercing the blue line at the gap between the Carter House and the cotton gin (the breach). Just behind the breach, inside the line, place a compact BLUE reserve block with a short blue counterattack arrow stabbing south into the breach to seal it, labeled "Opdycke". The rest of the blue arc holds solid.

Six labels only: Franklin, Hood, Carter House, cotton gin, Opdycke, Columbia Pike.
