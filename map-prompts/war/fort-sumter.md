# War map prompts — Fort Sumter (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map sumter-harbor-approach

The strategic shape of Charleston Harbor in the spring of 1861 — a federal fort alone in the channel, the islands and points of land that ring it, and Lincoln's relief fleet stalled outside the bar. North up; this is a harbor map, NOT a land battle.

Fill most of the frame with a flat light-blue body of water — the funnel-shaped Charleston Harbor — narrowing inland toward the upper-left and opening to the open sea at the lower-right (the southeast). Draw the land that frames the harbor mouth as muted-tan shapes: a long island running along the NORTH side of the entrance, a long island running along the SOUTH side of the entrance, and the water channel running between them out to sea. On the small artificial island in the middle of that channel, near the mouth, draw Fort Sumter as a single solid BLUE (Union) block — alone, surrounded by water. Place a small gray city block where the harbor narrows at the upper-left, labeled "Charleston". Label the north-side island "Sullivan's Island" and the south-side island "Morris Island", and mark the seaward tip of the south island "Cummings Point". Out beyond the harbor mouth, on the open sea at the lower-right, draw a small cluster of BLUE ship marks sitting OUTSIDE a faint tan shoal bar across the entrance, labeled "Relief Fleet" — they sit outside, unable to enter, with no arrow reaching the fort. Keep the shores neutral tan here: this map shows the geography and the trapped fort, not the firing.

Six labels only: Charleston, Fort Sumter, Sullivan's Island, Morris Island, Cummings Point, Relief Fleet.

## Map sumter-harbor

April 12–13, 1861 — the Confederate ring of batteries bombarding Fort Sumter from every shore of Charleston Harbor. A schematic harbor map, NOT a land battle. North up.

Fill most of the frame with a flat light-blue body of water — Charleston Harbor — opening to the sea at the lower-right corner (the southeast) and narrowing inland toward the upper-left. In the center of the water, on a small gray island, draw Fort Sumter as a single solid BLUE (Union) block, labeled "Fort Sumter" — it is alone and surrounded, and it emits NO arrow (it barely fired back).

Ring it with Confederate batteries on the surrounding tan shores, each a solid RUST-RED block firing a bold, smooth, tapered RUST-RED arrow inward at the lone blue fort:
- "Fort Moultrie" on the north shore (upper area, on Sullivan's Island), firing south at the fort.
- "Fort Johnson" on the west shore (left side, on James Island), firing east at the fort.
- "Cummings Point" on the south shore (lower area, the tip of Morris Island), firing north at the fort.

Place a small gray city block labeled "Charleston" at the upper-left (the northwest, head of the harbor). Several rust-red arrows converge on the single blue fort from three sides.

Six labels only: Fort Sumter, Fort Moultrie, Fort Johnson, Cummings Point, Charleston, Charleston Harbor.
