# War map prompts — Fort Sumter (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these (read before drawing)

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — not antique, not a flat color-block schematic, not topographic. Soft, very pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat, even fills only — NO paper texture, NO foxing, NO contour/relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain is shown with simple flat shapes in muted, naturalistic color**, and ONLY what the map's caption names:
   - Woods = soft muted sage-green rounded shapes.
   - Water (harbor / river / creek) = a single flat light-blue fill.
   - A road or lane = a thin gray line. A town or fort = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (United States / North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force or position is a crisp solid rectangular block (the standard military map symbol) in that side's color. A movement or line of fire is a bold, smooth, tapered arrow in that side's color. Use no other strong colors. The words BLUE, RUST, and RED name the INK to use — they are instructions to you, NEVER text to print. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **At most SIX labels**, clean modern sans-serif, dark ink, with a subtle thin white halo so they stay legible. Each label is a bare proper name only — never a sentence. Spell each EXACTLY as written below. Add NO label that is not listed for that map, and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** Correct real-world orientation.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text on the image, NO border or frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO decorative ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map sumter-harbor

April 12–13, 1861 — the Confederate ring of batteries bombarding Fort Sumter in Charleston Harbor. A schematic harbor map, NOT a land battle. North up.

Fill most of the frame with a flat light-blue body of water — Charleston Harbor — opening to the sea at the lower-right corner (the southeast). In the center of the water, on a small gray island, draw Fort Sumter as a single solid BLUE (Union) block, labeled "Fort Sumter" — it is alone and surrounded.

Ring it with Confederate batteries on the surrounding shores, each a solid RUST-RED block firing a bold rust-red arrow inward at the fort:
- "Fort Moultrie" on the north shore (upper area), firing south at the fort.
- "Fort Johnson" on the west shore (left side), firing east at the fort.
- "Cummings Point" on the south shore (lower area), firing north at the fort.

Place a small gray city block labeled "Charleston" at the upper-left (the northwest, head of the harbor). The fort itself emits NO arrow — it barely fired back. Several rust-red arrows converge on the lone blue fort from three sides.

Five labels only: Fort Sumter, Fort Moultrie, Fort Johnson, Cummings Point, Charleston. Nothing else.
