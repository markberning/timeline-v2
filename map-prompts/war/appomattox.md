# War map prompts — Appomattox Court House (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style chosen 2026-05-22 (replaced the old
flat-tan schematic). Label style = the locked no-box rule (canonical preamble
copied from `wilsons-creek.md` rule 4, 2026-06-01).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map appomattox-race-west

April 3 to 6, 1865 — the retreat from Petersburg and Richmond and the running pursuit that ended at Sailor's Creek. A wide operational march map (not a battlefield close-up).

Terrain:
- A small gray city marker labeled "Richmond" near the upper-RIGHT (east), and a small gray town marker labeled "Petersburg" just BELOW it (south), both toward the right edge.
- A small gray town marker labeled "Amelia Court House" in the CENTER, west of Petersburg and Richmond.
- A light-blue stream running short across the CENTER-LEFT, between Amelia Court House and the left edge, labeled "Sailor's Creek".
- Thin gray roads tracing west across open pale ground linking Petersburg and Richmond through Amelia Court House toward the left (west) edge.

Movements (three arrows, each attributed to the commander who led it):
- "Lee": one bold rust-red arrow that BEGINS at Petersburg and Richmond on the right, runs WEST to Amelia Court House, then bends and continues west past Sailor's Creek toward the left edge — the retreating Confederate column.
- "Sheridan": one bold BLUE arrow slashing in from the LOWER edge (south) against the flank and rear of the rust-red arrow near Sailor's Creek — the Union cavalry tearing at the strung-out column.
- "Grant": one bold BLUE arrow running west on a road PARALLEL to and BELOW Lee's, from the lower-right toward the left, overtaking the rust-red arrow — the Union infantry racing to cut the roads ahead.

This operational map carries SEVEN labels (more than the usual six is allowed here) — keep each BIG, bold, and spread over open pale ground: Richmond, Petersburg, Amelia Court House, Sailor's Creek, Lee, Sheridan, Grant. Nothing else.

## Map appomattox-station

Afternoon, April 8, 1865 — Custer's cavalry seizes the supply trains at Appomattox Station, getting in front of Lee's army. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole campaign.

Terrain:
- A thin gray railroad line running across the LOWER portion of the map (east–west), with a small gray station block ON it labeled "Appomattox Station" toward the center.
- A small gray town marker labeled "Appomattox Court House" up and to the RIGHT (northeast) of the station.
- A small gray city marker labeled "Lynchburg" at the LEFT edge (west), beyond the station.
- A thin gray road linking Appomattox Court House down to Appomattox Station and on west toward Lynchburg.

Forces and movements:
- "Lee": one bold rust-red arrow approaching the station from the upper-RIGHT (the Confederate infantry column marching down toward Appomattox Station from Appomattox Court House, reaching for the trains).
- "Custer": one bold BLUE arrow driving INTO Appomattox Station from the LOWER-LEFT (south/west) ahead of the rust-red arrow, its head planted on the station between the Confederates and Lynchburg — the Union cavalry seizing the supply trains first. Place a couple of small rust-red blocks beside the station for the captured Confederate artillery park.

Five labels only: Appomattox Station, Appomattox Court House, Lynchburg, Lee, Custer. Nothing else.

## Map appomattox-last-attack

Dawn, April 9, 1865 — Gordon's last attack west of the village runs into the massed Union infantry. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Terrain:
- A small gray town marker labeled "Appomattox Court House" on the RIGHT (east) side of the map.
- Pale-gold furrowed fields filling the open ground WEST of the village (the fields where the Confederates formed). A low pale-tan ridge running north–south across the CENTER (the high ground the attack pushed over).
- Thin gray roads running west out of Appomattox Court House across the fields toward the left edge.

The Confederates attack WEST (from the right), facing the Union line on the left:
- "Gordon": one bold rust-red arrow driving WEST out of the village across the fields and over the ridge — the lead infantry attack.
- "Fitzhugh Lee": one bold rust-red arrow alongside Gordon's, sweeping west on the Confederate flank — the cavalry.
- "Grimes": one short bold rust-red arrow at the head of Gordon's, leading the foremost divisions over the high ground.

The Union holds and closes (the wall beyond the ridge, on the LEFT/west, facing east toward the attack):
- A row of solid blue unit blocks across the LEFT (west) edge facing the rust-red arrows — the massed infantry that uncovered behind the cavalry. Label two of these "Ord" and "Griffin".
- "Sheridan": one BLUE label on a thin screen of blue blocks just in front of (east of) the main line, sliding aside to uncover it — the dismounted cavalry pushed back.

To the rear (east, behind the village), the Confederate force holds off the closing army:
- "Longstreet": a short line of solid rust-red unit blocks on the RIGHT edge behind Appomattox Court House, facing east, with blue blocks pressing against it.

This map carries SEVEN labels (more than the usual six is allowed here, to attribute every movement): Appomattox Court House, Gordon, Fitzhugh Lee, Grimes, Ord, Griffin, Sheridan, Longstreet. (Eight including Longstreet; keep each BIG and on open pale ground, none on arrows or water.) Nothing else.

## Map appomattox-surrender

Mid-morning, April 9, 1865 — the Army of Northern Virginia surrounded and pinned against the village, where the surrender was signed. A small SITUATION map (the converging armies and the village), NOT an assault.

Terrain:
- A small gray town marker labeled "Appomattox Court House" at the CENTER, drawn as a little cluster of gray blocks for the village.
- One slightly larger gray house block just beside the village center labeled "McLean House" — the parlor where the armies met.
- Thin gray roads running out of the village in several directions across pale ground.

The situation (no assault arrows — show the encirclement as static blocks):
- A tight pocket of solid rust-red unit blocks AROUND Appomattox Court House — the trapped Army of Northern Virginia.
- Solid blue unit blocks closing in on THREE sides of the pocket: to the WEST (left, blocking the road), to the SOUTH (bottom), and to the EAST (right, the army coming up from behind). Leave the blue blocks facing inward toward the village, pinning the rust-red pocket against it. Show no arrows; this is the moment the firing stopped.

Three labels only: Appomattox Court House, McLean House. (Two labels; both BIG, bold, on open pale ground.) Nothing else.
