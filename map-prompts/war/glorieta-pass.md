# War map prompts — Glorieta Pass (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map glorieta-pass-campaign

The New Mexico campaign, February–March 1862 — Sibley's Texans march north up the Rio Grande to take the territory while the Colorado volunteers race down to stop them. A wide operational march map, north up.

Draw the Rio Grande as one flat light-blue river band running top-to-bottom down the LEFT (west) side of the frame, labeled "Rio Grande". String three settlements along it as small gray blocks: low on the river a fort marker labeled "Fort Craig", then climbing north a city marker labeled "Albuquerque", and above it near the top-center a city marker labeled "Santa Fe". Run a pale-tan mountain band down the RIGHT (east) side behind the towns — the Sangre de Cristo range — but leave it UNLABELED. In the upper-RIGHT (northeast), well above and right of Santa Fe, draw a fort marker labeled "Fort Union" (the Union supply depot).

Draw one bold RUST-RED arrow rising from the bottom edge of the frame, following the Rio Grande north past Fort Craig and Albuquerque up to Santa Fe, labeled "Sibley" — the Confederate march. Draw one bold BLUE arrow plunging down from the very top of the frame (out of Colorado) and curving in to Fort Union, labeled "Pike's Peakers" — the Union force-march. The two arrows converge on the gap between Santa Fe and Fort Union.

Six labels only: Rio Grande, Fort Craig, Albuquerque, Santa Fe, Fort Union, Sibley.

## Map glorieta-pass

March 28, 1862 — Glorieta Pass. The Texans win the field at Pigeon's Ranch while a Union column slips over the mesa to strike the rear. A tactical overview of the pass, north up.

Draw the Santa Fe Trail as a thin gray road winding roughly east–west across the middle of the frame, threading a notch between pale-tan mountain shapes (the Sangre de Cristo Mountains), labeled "Santa Fe Trail". At the WEST (left) end of the trail, where the narrow western arm opens, draw a small gray block labeled "Apache Canyon". In the CENTER, on a thin light-blue creek that crosses the trail, draw a small gray block labeled "Pigeon's Ranch" (the main battlefield). At the EAST (right) end of the trail draw a small gray block labeled "Kozlowski's Ranch" (the Union staging point). Draw a broad pale-tan mesa filling the SOUTH (lower) part of the frame, labeled "Glorieta Mesa".

The battle: bold RUST-RED Confederate unit blocks and an arrow pushing EAST along the trail from Apache Canyon toward Pigeon's Ranch; BLUE Union unit blocks at Pigeon's Ranch giving ground eastward toward Kozlowski's Ranch. Show one bold BLUE arrow that leaves the trail, climbs SOUTH up and over Glorieta Mesa, then curves back down toward the west — the flank march bound for the Confederate rear, labeled "Chivington".

Six labels only: Santa Fe Trail, Apache Canyon, Pigeon's Ranch, Kozlowski's Ranch, Glorieta Mesa, Chivington.

## Map glorieta-pass-wagons

March 28, 1862 — the decisive stroke. Chivington's column crosses Glorieta Mesa and burns the entire Confederate supply train at Johnson's Ranch, eight miles behind the fighting. A zoomed tactical view of the western mouth of the pass, north up.

Draw a broad pale-tan mesa filling the lower (south) two-thirds of the frame, labeled "Glorieta Mesa", with the Santa Fe Trail as a thin gray road running east–west across the TOP of the frame, labeled "Santa Fe Trail". At the WEST (left) end of the trail, at the foot of the mesa's steep face, draw a cluster of small gray wagon blocks labeled "Johnson's Ranch" (the Confederate supply depot). Off to the upper-RIGHT, far along the trail, draw a small gray block labeled "Pigeon's Ranch" to show how distant the fighting was.

Draw one bold BLUE arrow climbing from the trail UP onto Glorieta Mesa, tracking west across the tableland, then dropping sharply DOWN the western face onto the wagon cluster at Johnson's Ranch, labeled "Chivington". Show the burning supply train as the wagon blocks struck by that arrow. Draw one thin RUST-RED arrow leaving Johnson's Ranch and trailing back EAST toward Pigeon's Ranch — the wagonless Texan retreat, labeled "Scurry".

Six labels only: Glorieta Mesa, Santa Fe Trail, Johnson's Ranch, Pigeon's Ranch, Chivington, Scurry.
