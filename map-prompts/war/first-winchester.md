# War map prompts — First Winchester (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map first-winchester-valley

May 1862 — Jackson's Valley Campaign: the strategic corridor. A strategic map, north up.

Draw two pale-tan mountain bands running diagonally (southwest-to-northeast) along the left and right edges, framing a pale open corridor between them. Draw a thin gray road running up the center of the corridor labeled "Valley Turnpike". Place gray town dots from south to north: "Strasburg" roughly one-third up the valley, "Winchester" near the top of the valley at its northern entrance. Beyond Winchester place a flat light-blue river band labeled "Potomac R.", and above the river to the upper-right a gray dot labeled "Washington". Off the valley to the southeast, roughly level with Strasburg, place a small gray dot labeled "Front Royal". Show a bold RUST-RED arrow labeled "Jackson" sweeping north up the valley corridor from south of Strasburg toward Winchester.

Six labels only: Valley Turnpike, Strasburg, Front Royal, Winchester, Potomac R., Jackson.

## Map first-winchester

May 25, 1862 — The battle: Taylor's flank turns Bowers Hill. A tactical map, north up.

Place a small gray town block in the center labeled "Winchester". Draw the Valley Turnpike as a thin gray road running north–south through the center of the town. Draw a thin gray road running southeast from Winchester labeled "Front Royal Pike". Draw a soft pale-tan rise to the SOUTHWEST of town labeled "Bowers Hill" and a second rise to the SOUTHEAST labeled "Camp Hill".

The Union (Banks) holds solid BLUE unit blocks: Gordon's brigade on Bowers Hill facing south-southwest, and a second BLUE unit block on Camp Hill facing southeast. The Confederates attack from the south: a bold RUST-RED arrow drives north up the Valley Turnpike toward Winchester (Jackson's center), and a second RUST-RED arrow drives northwest up the Front Royal Pike toward Camp Hill (Ewell's division). A third bold RUST-RED arrow labeled "Taylor" swings wide to the WEST then curves north-northeast up the slope of Bowers Hill, striking the Union right flank from the side. Show the Union collapse with a bold BLUE arrow retreating NORTH through Winchester.

Six labels only: Winchester, Bowers Hill, Camp Hill, Front Royal Pike, Taylor, Valley Turnpike.

## Map first-winchester-pursuit

May 25, 1862 — Banks's retreat: Winchester to the Potomac. A strategic map, north up.

Draw a thin gray road running north from the bottom of the frame to the top — the Valley Turnpike and its continuation north. Place gray town dots along this road from south to north: "Winchester" near the bottom, "Martinsburg" roughly two-thirds up. At the top place a flat light-blue river band labeled "Potomac R." with a small gray block at the crossing point labeled "Williamsport". Above the river to the upper-right place a gray dot labeled "Washington". Draw a bold BLUE arrow labeled "Banks" running north along the road from Winchester through Martinsburg and across the Potomac at Williamsport, showing the day-long retreat. Show a short RUST-RED pursuit arrow starting at Winchester, tapering and ending well south of Martinsburg to indicate the stalled Confederate chase.

Six labels only: Winchester, Martinsburg, Williamsport, Potomac R., Washington, Banks.
