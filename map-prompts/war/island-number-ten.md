# War map prompts — Island Number Ten (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

---

**LABEL PLACEMENT — applies to EVERY label on EVERY map (critical):** Each label, together with its box/halo, must sit FULLY INSIDE the frame with a clear margin on all four sides. NO label may touch, be clipped by, or run off any edge — pull every edge-hugging name inward toward open ground. No two labels may overlap or touch each other. When a label cannot sit in clear open space right beside the place or arrow it names without colliding with another label, an arrow, troop blocks, or the edge, move it to the nearest open area and draw a THIN GRAY LEADER LINE from the label to its dot/marker so the link stays obvious. River and water labels must be SOLID and STRONG (a deep, readable blue), never pale, faint, or washed-out; if blue-on-water would be low-contrast, set the river name on open ground beside the water with a halo instead.

## Map island-number-ten-strategy

Spring 1862 — the strategic river corridor: from Cairo down to Island No. 10. A regional river map, north up.

Draw the Mississippi River as a broad flat light-blue band running from the top of the frame toward the bottom, bending into the double hairpin at Island No. 10. In the upper-right corner, show the Ohio River as a shorter light-blue band joining the Mississippi at Cairo — place a gray town dot there labeled "Cairo". Below Cairo on the west bank, place a gray town dot labeled "Columbus" (the abandoned Confederate stronghold — annotate with a small rust-red battery block, struck through to show abandonment). Roughly 45 miles downstream, draw the sharp double bend; inside the lower loop place a small island with solid rust-red battery blocks labeled "Island No. 10". On the west bank just above the bend, place a gray town dot labeled "New Madrid". Also show the Tennessee River as a narrower light-blue band entering from the east, with a small rust-red fort block labeled "Ft. Donelson" (the flank that unhinged Columbus). Show a bold blue arrow pressing downstream from Cairo toward Island No. 10.

Six labels only: Cairo, Ohio River, Columbus, Island No. 10, New Madrid, Ft. Donelson.


**LABEL PLACEMENT (round 3, HARD RULE — earlier draws clipped labels at the edges):** Compose the ENTIRE map — all terrain, water, arrows, dots, AND every label — within the central 82% of the canvas, leaving a blank parchment margin around all four edges that NOTHING enters. No label, box, or halo may touch or cross into that outer margin band. Print every place name and every commander name EXACTLY ONCE — never repeat any label anywhere on the map. Where a feature sits toward the edge of the scene, set its label INWARD toward the center and join it to the feature with a thin gray leader line. No two labels may overlap or touch.
## Map island-number-ten

April 1862 — Bissell's canal and the Carondelet's gunboat run past Island No. 10. A close-in river map, north up.

Draw the Mississippi River as a broad flat light-blue band making a tight S-shaped double hairpin bend filling most of the frame. In the lower loop of the bend, draw a small island ringed with solid rust-red battery blocks labeled "Island No. 10". On the Tennessee (east) bank opposite the island, draw a small rust-red battery block labeled "Battery No. 2" (where the Carondelet was spotted). On the Missouri (west) bank upstream, place a gray town dot labeled "New Madrid". Running along the Missouri (left/west) side — entirely outside the gun line — draw a thin dashed gray channel looping from upstream of the island down to New Madrid, labeled "Pope's Canal". Show a single bold blue arrow for the USS Carondelet running straight DOWN the river, close to the west bank, past the island's batteries, then curving to tie up at New Madrid — the gunboat gauntlet run.

Five labels only: Island No. 10, Battery No. 2, New Madrid, Pope's Canal, Mississippi River.

## Map island-number-ten-trap

April 7–8, 1862 — the trap springs: Pope crosses and cuts the only road. A close-in river map, north up.

Draw the Mississippi River as a broad flat light-blue band in the same double-hairpin shape as the previous map. Show Island No. 10 in the lower loop with rust-red battery blocks (now surrounded). On the Missouri (west) bank, draw the dashed gray canal channel coming down to New Madrid, with small blue transport silhouettes in the canal. Show a bold blue crossing arrow sweeping from the Missouri bank (west) across the river to Watson's Landing on the Tennessee (east) bank — Pope's army crossing, ~3,000 men, covered by two gunboats. On the Tennessee bank south of Watson's Landing, place a gray town dot labeled "Tiptonville" with a thin gray road running north from it toward the island — the garrison's only escape road. Show a bold blue infantry arrow cutting across that road just north of Tiptonville, blocking it. Place a rust-red retreat arrow pointing south from Island No. 10 toward Tiptonville — arriving too late.

Five labels only: Island No. 10, Watson's Landing, Pope's Canal, Tiptonville, Mississippi River.
