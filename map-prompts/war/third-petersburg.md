# War map prompts — Third Petersburg (The Breakthrough) (modern battle-map house style)

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

---

**LABEL PLACEMENT — applies to EVERY label on EVERY map (critical):** Each label, together with its box/halo, must sit FULLY INSIDE the frame with a clear margin on all four sides. NO label may touch, be clipped by, or run off any edge — pull every edge-hugging name inward toward open ground. No two labels may overlap or touch each other. When a label cannot sit in clear open space right beside the place or arrow it names without colliding with another label, an arrow, troop blocks, or the edge, move it to the nearest open area and draw a THIN GRAY LEADER LINE from the label to its dot/marker so the link stays obvious. River and water labels must be SOLID and STRONG (a deep, readable blue), never pale, faint, or washed-out; if blue-on-water would be low-contrast, set the river name on open ground beside the water with a halo instead.

## Map third-petersburg-breakthrough

Before dawn, Sunday April 2, 1865 — the Sixth Corps wedge smashes through the Confederate line southwest of Petersburg while the rest of the Union army hits the works at once. A zoomed-in tactical view of the assault, NOT the whole siege.

Terrain:
- A gray town block labeled "Petersburg" sits at the upper-right (northeast), against a light-blue river running along the top edge labeled "Appomattox River".
- The Confederate line is a continuous arc of solid rust-red unit blocks curving around the south and west of the town, FACING outward (south and west, toward the attack).
- A small gray fort block labeled "Fort Fisher" sits in the open ground in front of (southwest of) the rust-red line, in the Union sector — this is the work the signal gun fired from.
- A small gray fort block labeled "Fort Mahone" sits ON the rust-red line on its eastern stretch (south of the town).

Movements (three blue arrows, each attributed to the commander who led it):
- "Wright": one bold BLUE arrow in the southwest, drawn as a single broad wedge driving NORTHEAST from the Union ground past Fort Fisher straight through the rust-red arc, breaking the line wide open (the arrowhead crosses the rust-red blocks). This is the Sixth Corps spearhead.
- "Parke": one bold BLUE arrow on the EAST, driving north against the eastern stretch of the line by the Fort Mahone block (Ninth Corps).
- "Humphreys": one bold BLUE arrow on the WEST, driving in against the rust-red line west of Wright's wedge (Second Corps).

This map carries SEVEN labels (more than the usual six is allowed here, stated per rule 4) — keep each BIG, bold, and over open pale ground: Petersburg, Appomattox River, Fort Fisher, Fort Mahone, Wright, Parke, Humphreys. Print the label "Fort Mahone" EXACTLY ONCE (beside its fort block only); do not repeat any label. Nothing else.


**LABEL PLACEMENT (text-readability sweep 2026-06-01):** Every label, with its box/halo, must sit FULLY INSIDE the frame with a clear margin on all four sides — none may touch, be clipped by, or run off any edge. These labels currently touch or run off an edge and MUST be pulled inward with a clear margin (use a thin gray leader line to their dots if that is the only way to keep them clear): Humphreys; Fort Mahone. Render "Appomattox River" in SOLID strong blue (not pale or faint), set beside the water on open pale ground with a halo so it reads cleanly. No two labels may overlap or touch.
## Map third-petersburg-fort-gregg

Early afternoon, April 2, 1865 — Gibbon's corps storms the last two forts covering the town while the Sixth Corps wheels to cut the South Side Railroad. A zoomed-in tactical view, NOT the whole battle.

Terrain:
- A gray town block labeled "Petersburg" at the upper-right (northeast), with a light-blue river along the top edge labeled "Appomattox River".
- Two small gray fort blocks standing in the open ground southwest of the town, facing the attack (facing southwest): a larger one labeled "Fort Gregg" and a smaller one just behind/beside it (to the north) labeled "Fort Whitworth". Garrison each fort with a small cluster of solid rust-red unit blocks.
- A thin gray rail line running east–west across the LOWER-LEFT of the map into Petersburg, labeled "South Side Railroad".

Movements (two arrows):
- "Gibbon": bold BLUE arrows (draw two converging heads) driving NORTHEAST from the open ground into Fort Gregg, the Twenty-Fourth Corps assault hitting the fort head-on.
- "Wright": one bold BLUE arrow sweeping from the breakthrough ground (lower-left) and curling down onto the "South Side Railroad" rail line, cutting it — draw a short break/gap in the gray rail line where the arrowhead crosses it.

Six labels only: Petersburg, Appomattox River, Fort Gregg, Fort Whitworth, South Side Railroad, Gibbon. (Wright's arrow is attributed in-line by the second blue arrow striking the railroad; if a seventh label fits over open ground, add "Wright".) Nothing else.

## Map third-petersburg-fall-and-flight

That night and after, April 2–3, 1865 — Lee abandons Petersburg and marches west along the Appomattox River while Grant's columns pursue toward Amelia Court House. A wider operational retreat map, NOT a battlefield close-up.

Terrain:
- A light-blue river runs diagonally across the map from the lower-right up toward the upper-left, labeled "Appomattox River".
- A gray town block labeled "Petersburg" sits on the SOUTH bank of the river at the lower-right.
- A gray town block labeled "Richmond" sits in the upper-right, north of Petersburg.
- A gray town block labeled "Amelia Court House" sits toward the upper-left (west), near the river.

Movements (two arrows, each attributed):
- "Lee": one bold rust-red arrow leaving Petersburg after dark, running WEST along the NORTH bank of the Appomattox River toward Amelia Court House — the arrow must clearly cross the blue river from the south bank to the north bank near Petersburg, then follow the north bank northwest.
- "Grant": one bold BLUE arrow starting south/southeast of the rust-red arrow and racing WEST on a parallel track below it, pursuing and reaching to get in front of the retreat.

Five labels only: Appomattox River, Petersburg, Richmond, Amelia Court House, Lee. (Add "Grant" as a sixth if it fits over open pale ground beside the blue arrow.) Nothing else.
