# War map prompts — Opequon (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map opequon-overview

The setting, dawn September 19, 1864 — Sheridan's army massed east of Opequon Creek must thread the one road west into Winchester while Early's line waits before the town. A setting overview, north up.

Place a small gray town block labeled "Winchester" in the CENTER of the map. Down the RIGHT (east) side draw a flat light-blue water band running top-to-bottom labeled "Opequon Creek". Draw a thin gray road running WEST from the creek toward Winchester labeled "Berryville Pike"; partway along its run, between the creek and the town, pinch it through a narrow pale-tan ravine (high ground squeezing in from both sides) labeled "Berryville Canyon" — the road visibly chokes to a single thread there. Draw a second thin gray road running straight TOP-to-BOTTOM (north–south) through Winchester labeled "Valley Pike". Just EAST of the town, between Winchester and the canyon, draw the Confederate defensive line as a crisp row of RUST-RED unit blocks facing EAST toward the creek (Early's line waiting for the approach). East of Opequon Creek, off toward the right edge, mass a dense cluster of BLUE unit blocks (Sheridan's army gathering before the crossing). No attack arrows yet — this map is the stage before the blow.

Six labels only: Winchester, Opequon Creek, Berryville Pike, Berryville Canyon, Valley Pike.

## Map opequon-the-flank

The afternoon break, ~3 p.m. September 19, 1864 — Union infantry grind west along the Berryville Pike while the massed cavalry sweeps down from the north against Early's left, collapsing his line south through Winchester. The flanking blow, north up.

Place the gray town block labeled "Winchester" in the CENTER, with the thin gray "Valley Pike" running straight TOP-to-BOTTOM through it. Draw the thin gray "Berryville Pike" running in from the RIGHT (east) toward the town. East of Winchester draw the Confederate line as a row of RUST-RED unit blocks; show it bending and breaking — its blocks ragged, no longer a clean line. From the EAST, along the Berryville Pike, drive Union infantry as bold tapered BLUE arrows pushing WEST into the front of the rust-red line. From the TOP (north) sweep a broad bold tapered BLUE cavalry arrow DOWN onto the open northern end of the Confederate line (Custer the spearhead of the massed horse). Just north of the town, on that crumbling left flank, set two small gray earthwork blocks labeled "Fort Collier" and "Star Fort". Show Early's force routing: bold tapered RUST-RED retreat arrows streaming SOUTH out of Winchester down the Valley Pike off the lower edge.

Six labels only: Winchester, Berryville Pike, Valley Pike, Fort Collier, Star Fort.
