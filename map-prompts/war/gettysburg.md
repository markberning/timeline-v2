# War map prompts — Gettysburg (modern battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps** drawn in a **clean modern textbook style** — the Hal
Jespersen / National Park Service look: legible on a phone, beside prose, with
real terrain, troop blocks, and bold arrows. Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections, writes
`public/war-img/<slug>.png`). House style chosen 2026-05-22 (replaced the old
flat-tan schematic).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these (read before drawing)

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — not antique, not a flat color-block schematic, not topographic. Soft, very pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat, even fills only — NO paper texture, NO foxing, NO contour/relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain is shown with simple flat shapes in muted, naturalistic color**, and ONLY what the map's caption names:
   - Woods / a wooded hill = a soft muted sage-green rounded shape.
   - Cultivated field = a pale-gold shape with faint thin furrow striping.
   - Water (river / creek) = a single flat light-blue band.
   - A road or lane = a thin gray line. A town = a small gray block or dot. A mountain ridge = a soft gray band.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (United States / North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force or line is a row of crisp solid rectangular unit blocks (the standard military map symbol) in that side's color — or, on a WIDE OVERVIEW map, a single bold continuous band in that color. A movement is a bold, smooth, tapered arrow in that side's color. Use no other strong colors. The words BLUE, RUST, and RED name the INK to use — they are instructions to you, NEVER text to print. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **At most SIX labels**, clean modern sans-serif, dark ink, with a subtle thin white halo so they stay legible. Each label is a bare proper name only — never a sentence. Spell each EXACTLY as written below. Add NO label that is not listed for that map (no invented words), and print NO color words. The only text on the image is the labels listed for that map.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** Correct real-world orientation. A defending line FACES the attack — the attacker's arrow strikes the front of the defender's line.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text on the image, NO border or frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO decorative ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map gettysburg-day2

Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, July 2, 1863 — the "fishhook" day. A wide tactical overview.

Place a small gray town marker labeled "Gettysburg" near the top center. The Union army holds a hooked defensive line — draw it as a single bold blue band: a curled northern barb at "Culp's Hill", bending west over "Cemetery Hill" just south of the town, then running straight south down a low ridge to a hill at the southern tip, "Little Round Top". The Confederate army wraps the OUTSIDE of the hook as a looser bold rust-red band to the west and north of the Union line.

Show exactly TWO bold rust-red attack arrows striking the hook: one from the south/southwest driving against the southern tip near Little Round Top, labeled "Longstreet"; one from the north curling against the barb at Culp's Hill, labeled "Ewell".

Six labels only: Gettysburg, Culp's Hill, Cemetery Hill, Little Round Top, Longstreet, Ewell. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-lrt

The fight for Little Round Top, late afternoon July 2, 1863. A zoomed-in tactical view of the southern tip of the battlefield, NOT the whole battle.

On the right (east), draw a rounded sage-green wooded hill labeled "Little Round Top". Hold its crest with a blue line of unit blocks facing west and south; at the southern (lower) end place a small blue block labeled "Chamberlain" — the far left flank of the whole Union army. On the left (west), lower down, draw a small cluster of gray boulders labeled "Devil's Den".

Show the Confederate assault as ONE bold rust-red arrow labeled "Hood", coming from the west and southwest, sweeping over Devil's Den and up the slope toward the blue line on Little Round Top.

Four labels only: Little Round Top, Chamberlain, Devil's Den, Hood. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-wheatfield

The Wheatfield and the Peach Orchard salient, evening July 2, 1863. A zoomed-in tactical view of the center-left of the battlefield, NOT the whole battle.

Draw a pale-gold field labeled "The Wheatfield" (center) and a small sage-green orchard just northwest of it labeled "Peach Orchard". Draw a blue line of unit blocks bent forward into an exposed angle around the Peach Orchard — the over-extended salient — with a small blue block labeled "Sickles" at the bend.

Show the Confederate attack as TWO bold rust-red arrows converging on the salient from the west and south, labeled "McLaws".

Four labels only: The Wheatfield, Peach Orchard, Sickles, McLaws. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-culps

The fight for Culp's Hill, dusk July 2, 1863. A zoomed-in tactical view of the far northern barb of the fishhook, NOT the whole battle.

On the right, draw a sage-green wooded hill labeled "Culp's Hill", held by a blue line of unit blocks with one small blue block labeled "Greene" — a single brigade dug in behind breastworks.

Show the Confederate attack as ONE bold rust-red arrow labeled "Ewell", pressing the hill from the north and east at dusk.

Three labels only: Culp's Hill, Greene, Ewell. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-campaign

The road to Gettysburg, June 1863 — Confederate Virginia up into southern Pennsylvania. A wide operational march map.

Draw a soft gray mountain band running diagonally from lower-center up toward the top — the Blue Ridge / South Mountain ridge line — but leave this band UNLABELED. West of that band, label the open corridor "Shenandoah Valley" in gray. Place a small gray town marker labeled "Gettysburg" in the north (upper area), and a marker labeled "Washington" in the southeast (lower right). Show the Potomac as one thin light-blue line the armies cross.

Draw exactly TWO movement arrows running south-to-north, each labeled exactly once: ONE rust-red arrow on the WEST side of the mountains, up the Shenandoah Valley, curving north and then east toward Gettysburg, labeled "Lee"; and ONE single blue arrow on the EAST side of the mountains, staying between the rust arrow and Washington, also moving north toward Gettysburg, labeled "Meade". Do not draw a second blue arrow and do not repeat any label.

Six labels only, each appearing once: Gettysburg, Washington, Potomac, Shenandoah Valley, Lee, Meade. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-day1

Gettysburg, July 1, 1863 — the opening day. A tactical view.

Place a small gray town marker labeled "Gettysburg" in the center. Just west of it, draw a blue line of unit blocks on a low ridge, labeled "McPherson's Ridge", with a small blue block on it labeled "Buford" — the dismounted cavalry making the morning stand. South of the town draw a blue hill position labeled "Cemetery Hill".

Show the Confederate attack as one bold rust-red arrow driving east into McPherson's Ridge from the west, labeled "Heth". Show one short blue arrow falling back from the ridge, through the town, south to Cemetery Hill — the Union retreat at evening.

Five labels only: Gettysburg, McPherson's Ridge, Buford, Cemetery Hill, Heth. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-day3

Gettysburg, July 3, 1863 — Pickett's Charge. A tactical view.

Draw two parallel north-south ridge lines about three-quarters of a mile apart with open ground between them. The eastern (right) line is a blue line of unit blocks labeled "Cemetery Ridge" — the Union center. The western (left) line is a rust-red line labeled "Seminary Ridge" — the Confederate line. Place a small gray town marker labeled "Gettysburg" at the north end between them.

Show one single very bold rust-red arrow crossing the open ground from Seminary Ridge straight into the middle of Cemetery Ridge, labeled "Pickett's Charge". Mark the point where it strikes the blue line with a small notch labeled "The Angle".

Up to six labels: Gettysburg, Cemetery Ridge, Seminary Ridge, Pickett's Charge, The Angle. Nothing else.
