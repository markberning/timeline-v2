# War map prompts — Gettysburg (battle-map house style)

These are **battle maps**, not territorial-civ maps. The style is deliberately
minimal so it reads on a phone, beside prose: a flat schematic, a few bold
arrows, very few labels. Generated by `scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses
`## Map <slug>` sections, writes `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these (read before drawing)

**CRITICAL BATTLE-MAP RULES:**

1. **This is a clean, flat, schematic battle map — NOT a topographic or terrain map.** Draw NO contour lines, NO hill shading, NO relief, NO textured ground, NO forest stippling, NO elevation tinting. The base is a single flat pale tan (#e7ddc8) land area and, where water is shown, a single flat light blue. Nothing else fills the background.
2. **Use side colors for forces.** Union (United States / North) positions, lines, and arrows are BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) positions, lines, and arrows are RUST RED (#b44d3b). Use no other strong colors. A force line is a simple thick colored band or arc; a movement is a bold tapered arrow in that side's color.
3. **Very few labels — at most SIX, large and legible.** Each label is a bare proper name only (e.g. "Cemetery Hill"), in a clean sans-serif, in dark ink, with a thin white halo so it stays readable over the map. Never write a sentence on the map. Never label more than six things.
4. **No road network, no creeks, no railroads, no county lines** unless a single one is explicitly named below. A battlefield is shown by the two armies' shapes and the arrows between them, not by terrain detail.
5. **Spell every label EXACTLY as written below. Invent no words, no extra labels, no annotations.** The only text on the image is the labels listed for that map — nothing more.
6. **North is at the top. East is to the right.** Place everything in correct real-world orientation. Do not rotate or tilt.
7. **NO title bar, NO chapter number, NO caption text on the image.** The map carries no title — the page caption describes it. Render no header bar.
8. **NO frame.** Draw no border, box, rectangle, or hairline anywhere. The map bleeds off all four edges.
9. **No ornaments, no compass rose, no scale bar, no legend, no north arrow, no inset.** Do not print the words North/South/East/West or the letters N/S/E/W. Do not draw a decorative star or sparkle.

## Map gettysburg-day2

A clean schematic battle map of Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, on July 2, 1863 — the "fishhook" day.

The Union army holds a hooked defensive line (the famous fishhook). Draw it as a single bold BLUE curving band: starting at the curled northern barb at Culp's Hill, bending west over Cemetery Hill just south of the town, then running straight south down a low ridge to two hills at the southern tip. Place a small town marker labeled "Gettysburg" at the inside top of the hook. Along the blue hook, label only three points: "Culp's Hill" (the curled north barb), "Cemetery Hill" (the bend), and "Little Round Top" (the southern tip).

The Confederate army wraps the OUTSIDE of the hook as a longer, looser RUST arc to the west and north of the Union line.

Show exactly TWO bold rust attack arrows striking the hook:
- One from the south/southwest driving up against the southern tip near Little Round Top, labeled "Longstreet".
- One from the north curling against the barb at Culp's Hill, labeled "Ewell".

Six labels total: Gettysburg, Culp's Hill, Cemetery Hill, Little Round Top, Longstreet, Ewell. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-lrt

A clean schematic close-up of the fight for Little Round Top, Gettysburg, late afternoon July 2, 1863. This is a zoomed-in tactical view of the southern tip of the battlefield, NOT the whole battle.

On the right (east), draw a single rounded hill shape labeled "Little Round Top". Hold its crest with a thin BLUE defensive line facing west and south. At the southern (lower) end of that blue line, place a small blue marker labeled "Chamberlain" — the far left flank of the whole Union army.

On the left (west), lower down, draw a small cluster of boulders labeled "Devil's Den".

Show the Confederate assault as ONE bold RUST arrow labeled "Hood", coming from the west and southwest, sweeping over Devil's Den and up the wooded slope toward the blue line on Little Round Top.

Four labels only: Little Round Top, Chamberlain, Devil's Den, Hood. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-wheatfield

A clean schematic close-up of the Wheatfield and the Peach Orchard salient, Gettysburg, evening July 2, 1863. A zoomed-in tactical view of the center-left of the battlefield, NOT the whole battle.

Draw a flat open area. Label one patch "The Wheatfield" (center) and another patch just northwest of it "Peach Orchard". Draw a thin BLUE line bent forward into an exposed angle around the Peach Orchard — the over-extended salient — with a small blue marker labeled "Sickles" at the bend.

Show the Confederate attack as TWO bold RUST arrows converging on the salient from the west and south, labeled "McLaws" (striking the Peach Orchard).

Four labels only: The Wheatfield, Peach Orchard, Sickles, McLaws. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-culps

A clean schematic close-up of the fight for Culp's Hill, Gettysburg, dusk July 2, 1863. A zoomed-in tactical view of the far northern barb of the fishhook, NOT the whole battle.

On the right, draw a single wooded hill shape labeled "Culp's Hill", held by a thin BLUE line with one small blue marker labeled "Greene" — a single brigade dug in behind breastworks.

Show the Confederate attack as ONE bold RUST arrow labeled "Ewell", pressing the hill from the north and east at dusk.

Three labels only: Culp's Hill, Greene, Ewell. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-campaign

A clean schematic march map of the road to Gettysburg, June 1863 — Confederate Virginia up into southern Pennsylvania.

Draw a simple flat tan land area. Show a single soft gray mountain band running diagonally (the Blue Ridge / South Mountain ridge line) from lower-center up toward the top. Place a small town marker labeled "Gettysburg" in the north (upper area). Place a small marker labeled "Washington" in the southeast (lower right). Show the Potomac River as one thin light-blue line the armies cross.

Two bold movement arrows running south-to-north:
- A RUST arrow on the WEST side of the mountains (the valley), curving north and then east toward Gettysburg, labeled "Lee".
- A BLUE arrow on the EAST side of the mountains, staying between the rust arrow and Washington, also moving north toward Gettysburg, labeled "Meade".

Up to six labels: Gettysburg, Washington, Potomac, Lee, Meade. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-day1

A clean schematic battle map of Gettysburg on July 1, 1863 — the opening day.

Place a small town marker labeled "Gettysburg" in the center. Just west of it, draw a thin BLUE defensive line on a low ridge, labeled "McPherson's Ridge", held by a small blue cavalry symbol. South of the town draw a BLUE hill position labeled "Cemetery Hill".

Show the Confederate attack as TWO bold RUST arrows converging on the town from the west and the north, labeled "Heth" (from the west, against McPherson's Ridge). Show one short BLUE arrow falling back from McPherson's Ridge south to Cemetery Hill, indicating the Union retreat at evening.

Up to six labels: Gettysburg, McPherson's Ridge, Cemetery Hill, Heth. Nothing else.

## Map gettysburg-day3

A clean schematic battle map of Gettysburg on July 3, 1863 — Pickett's Charge.

Draw two parallel north-south ridge lines about three-quarters of a mile apart with flat tan open ground between them. The eastern (right) line is BLUE, labeled "Cemetery Ridge" — the Union center. The western (left) line is RUST, labeled "Seminary Ridge" — the Confederate line. Place a small town marker labeled "Gettysburg" at the north end between them.

Show one single very bold RUST arrow crossing the open ground from Seminary Ridge straight into the middle of Cemetery Ridge, labeled "Pickett's Charge". Mark the point where it strikes the blue line with a small notch labeled "The Angle".

Up to six labels: Gettysburg, Cemetery Ridge, Seminary Ridge, Pickett's Charge, The Angle. Nothing else.
