# War map prompts — Chickamauga (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map chickamauga-overview

Mid-September 1863 — after Rosecrans maneuvered Bragg out of Chattanooga without a fight, the two armies converge in the woods along West Chickamauga Creek. A campaign-approach overview, north up.

Draw West Chickamauga Creek as a flat light-blue band running roughly north–south down the RIGHT-of-center of the field, angling slightly NE–SW, labeled "West Chickamauga Creek". West of and parallel to it draw a thin gray north–south road, the spine of the whole field, labeled "LaFayette Road" — running it north (off the TOP-LEFT edge) leads to the city, so place a small gray city block at the upper-left labeled "Chattanooga" with a thin gray road connecting up to it. At the SOUTH end where the road meets the creek, mark a small gray mill block labeled "Lee & Gordon's Mills". On the creek, draw two thin gray bridge crossings: a northern one labeled "Reed's Bridge" and a southern one labeled "Alexander's Bridge". Fill the ground between the creek and the road with soft sage-green woods.

The armies converge on the creek: show BLUE unit blocks forming up along and west of the LaFayette Road (the Union line), and RUST RED unit blocks east of the creek with bold tapered rust-red arrows pushing WEST across the two bridges toward the road. A single bold blue arrow runs north up the LaFayette Road toward Chattanooga, marking the lifeline.

Six labels only: West Chickamauga Creek, LaFayette Road, Chattanooga, Lee & Gordon's Mills, Reed's Bridge, Alexander's Bridge.

## Map chickamauga

September 20, 1863, mid-morning — Wood's division is pulled out of the Brotherton field exactly as Longstreet's massed column strikes the gap, and the Union right routs northwest down the LaFayette Road. A breakthrough-moment view of the center, north up.

Draw the thin gray LaFayette Road running north–south down the center of the field, labeled "LaFayette Road" — its north end (TOP-LEFT) is the road to the city, so place a small gray city block at the upper-left corner labeled "Chattanooga". Fill the field with soft sage-green woods. In the CENTER, on the road, mark a small gray cabin block in a pale-gold clearing labeled "Brotherton field" — this is the fatal gap. Show the BLUE Union line as a row of unit blocks along the road, but with a clear quarter-mile HOLE in the line at the Brotherton field; draw a blue arrow pulling one blue block NORTH up out of the line away from the gap, labeled "Wood withdraws".

From the EAST (right), aim an enormous RUST RED assault column straight at the Brotherton gap: stack several bold tapered rust-red arrows in deep parallel lines driving WEST through the hole, labeled "Longstreet". Beyond the breach, show shattered blue blocks dissolving and streaming NORTHWEST up the LaFayette Road in a single bold blue rout arrow toward Chattanooga, labeled "Union rout".

Six labels only: LaFayette Road, Chattanooga, Brotherton field, Wood withdraws, Longstreet, Union rout.

## Map chickamauga-snodgrass

September 20, 1863, afternoon — Thomas rallies the broken army on the high ground at the northwest of the field, the "Rock of Chickamauga," holding against repeated assaults while Granger marches to the guns and the army escapes north. A last-stand view, north up.

Draw West Chickamauga Creek as a flat light-blue band down the RIGHT (east) edge, labeled "West Chickamauga Creek". Draw the thin gray LaFayette Road running north–south at the RIGHT-of-center, just west of the creek, labeled "LaFayette Road" — its north end (TOP) is the escape road, so place a small gray city block at the upper-right-of-top labeled "Chattanooga" with the army's withdrawal running toward it. In the NORTHWEST quarter of the field, draw a pale-tan band of steep wooded knolls (woods sage-green at their feet) as the high ground, labeled "Snodgrass Hill" along its crest and "Horseshoe Ridge" along its western shoulder. Crown the high ground with a tight defending arc of BLUE unit blocks FACING south and east. From the NORTH, draw a bold blue arrow of fresh troops marching down to the ridge, labeled "Granger".

Against the blue arc, drive repeated RUST RED assaults: several bold tapered rust-red arrows coming UP the wooded slopes from the south and east, breaking against the crest. Show a thin bold blue arrow slipping NORTH off the high ground up the LaFayette Road toward Chattanooga (the army escaping in order).

Six labels only: LaFayette Road, Chattanooga, Snodgrass Hill, Horseshoe Ridge, Granger, West Chickamauga Creek.
