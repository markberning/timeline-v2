# War map prompts — Shiloh (modern battle-map house style)

Battle maps in the clean modern textbook style (Hal Jespersen / NPS look). Generated by
`scripts/generate-war-maps.mjs` (parses `## Map <slug>` sections -> `public/war-img/<slug>.png`).

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen), DARK ink, each label sitting on a crisp solid white pill/halo so it stays sharp against the map. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or hard to read. Each label must read like a printed STICKER — BOLD near-black text inside a SOLID WHITE rounded box — NEVER thin, gray, or faint text floating directly on the terrain (that is the failure to avoid). Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; OUTLINED text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. At most SIX labels per map. Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written; add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map jonesborough-overview

The strategic stakes, late August 1864 — why one rail line south of Atlanta decided everything: the city's last open supply line runs down through Jonesborough, and Sherman's armies are swinging in from the west to cut it. A setting overview, north up.

At the TOP (north) place a large gray city block labeled "Atlanta". Roughly 15 miles below it, in the lower-center, place a smaller gray town block labeled "Jonesborough". Connect them with a single thin gray rail line running straight NORTH–SOUTH, passing through Jonesborough and up into Atlanta, labeled "Macon & Western RR" — this is the city's last open artery. To the WEST (left) of the town, draw the "Flint River" as a flat light-blue band running top-to-bottom down the left side. Covering the town and the railroad, draw a crisp rust-red defensive line of unit blocks dug in facing west, labeled "Hardee" — the entrenchments shielding the rail line. From the LEFT (west), across the Flint River, send Sherman's armies as bold tapered BLUE arrows advancing eastward toward the railroad, labeled "Sherman". Keep it a clean stage: the city and rail line north, the town and works below, the river and the blue advance to the west.

The only text anywhere on the map is the six labels below, each bold near-black on a solid white pill, spelled EXACTLY as written — in particular "Hardee" (one r, one d, ends "-dee"). Render NO other text, no color words, no terrain or instruction words.

Six labels only: Atlanta, Jonesborough, Macon & Western RR, Flint River, Hardee, Sherman.

## Map jonesborough-the-break

September 1, 1864 — Davis's XIV Corps assault breaks Hardee's thin line, overrunning the salient where Govan's brigade is swallowed whole, and the Federals cut the Macon & Western behind it. The railroad cut means Atlanta is doomed. A breakthrough view, north up.

Draw Hardee's defensive line as a long rust-red row of unit blocks running roughly north–south, facing west, with a sharp angle jutting toward the enemy at one point — a salient. Mark the apex of that salient with a small rust-red block labeled "Govan". Behind the line (to the east) draw the thin gray "Macon & Western RR" running north–south. From the WEST (left), drive a bold tapered BLUE arrow labeled "Davis" straight into the salient, punching clean THROUGH the rust-red works and overrunning Govan's apex; STOP the arrow at the broken line, its point planted on the overrun salient — do NOT carry the arrow across or over the "Macon & Western RR"; the railroad runs intact just behind the shattered line, now exposed (the breakthrough threatens it, the arrow does not cross it). Place the gray city block labeled "Atlanta" at the very TOP (north), up the railroad, to show what the lost line dooms. The shattered rust-red line buckles around the overrun point.

The only text anywhere on the map is the five labels below, each bold near-black on a solid white pill, spelled EXACTLY as written — in particular "Hardee" (one r, one d, ends "-dee") and "Govan". Render NO other text, no color words, no terrain or instruction words.

Six labels only: Hardee, Govan, Davis, Macon & Western RR, Atlanta.
