# War map prompts — Five Forks (modern battle-map house style)

## Style — every battle map MUST obey these

1. **CLEAN MODERN CARTOGRAPHY** — pale parchment-white background (#f3eee2). Flat even fills only — NO paper texture, NO relief shading, NO 3-D, NO drop shadows, NO glow, NO noise.
2. **Terrain = simple flat shapes in muted color**, only what the caption names: woods = soft sage-green; a hill/ridge/plateau = pale tan; a mountain range = a pale-tan band; water (river/creek/sea) = a flat light-blue band or fill; a road/trail = a thin gray line; a town, city, fort, church, house = a small gray block or dot.
3. **Forces use side colors.** Union (North) = BLUE (#1d4ed8). Confederate (South) = RUST RED (#b44d3b). A force/line = crisp solid rectangular unit blocks; a movement = a bold smooth tapered arrow. No other strong colors. BLUE/RUST/RED name the INK, never text. Never render a color name as a label.
4. **LABELS MUST BE LARGE, BOLD, AND EASY TO READ ON A PHONE** — this is critical. Use a HEAVY modern sans-serif at a GENEROUS size (think a clean textbook map seen on a small screen) in DARK, near-black ink, placed DIRECTLY ON THE MAP. Give each label only a thin soft cream halo hugging the letters (a narrow outline the colour of the pale background) for separation — do NOT draw a filled white box, pill, sticker, or rounded rectangle behind the text. Prefer FEWER, BIGGER labels over many small ones. NO label may be small, thin, faint, or gray. Use SOLID, FILLED glyphs — NEVER outlined, hollow, or stroked-only letterforms; hollow/outlined text is illegible and is the exact failure to avoid. Position each label over open, pale ground (not on top of arrows, water, or dark fills) so it reads cleanly without a box. At most SIX labels per map (more only if the map's own section explicitly lists them). Bare proper names only, spelled EXACTLY as written, upright (not italic or rotated); add NO unlisted label and print NO color words.
5. **North is at the TOP, east to the RIGHT.** A defending line FACES the attack.
6. **NO title bar, NO caption text, NO border/frame, NO compass rose, NO scale bar, NO legend, NO N/S/E/W letters, NO ornament.** The map bleeds off all four edges.

## Map five-forks-dinwiddie

March 31, 1865 — Pickett pushes out of Five Forks and drives Sheridan's cavalry back toward Dinwiddie Court House through the rain-soaked woods. A tactical view of the day's fighting.

Terrain:
- Soft sage-green wooded blocks across most of the map (this was dense pine country); leave open pale-gold field gaps between them.
- A small gray crossroads marker near the TOP-CENTER where several thin gray roads meet in the woods, labeled "Five Forks".
- A thin gray east–west road running through the crossroads, labeled "White Oak Road".
- A small gray town marker near the BOTTOM-CENTER, labeled "Dinwiddie C.H.".

Movements:
- The Confederate attack: one bold rust-red tapered arrow, labeled "Pickett", BEGINS at Five Forks at the top and drives SOUTH through the woods toward Dinwiddie C.H., its head pressing in near the courthouse.
- The Union defense giving ground: a row of solid blue unit blocks below the arrow's head, facing NORTH toward the attack, with a short bold blue arrow bending BACK (southward) toward Dinwiddie C.H., labeled "Sheridan", to show the cavalry yielding ground slowly toward the courthouse.

Five labels only: Five Forks, White Oak Road, Dinwiddie C.H., Pickett, Sheridan. Nothing else.

## Map five-forks-line

Overnight, March 31 into April 1, 1865 — Pickett pulls back to Five Forks and entrenches along the White Oak Road, facing south, with the western end bent back at a right angle: "the return". A tactical view of the defensive position.

Terrain:
- Soft sage-green wooded blocks framing the scene, with open pale-gold ground in the center-south where the attack will come from.
- A small gray crossroads marker at the CENTER where thin gray roads meet, labeled "Five Forks".
- A thin gray east–west road running across the center through the crossroads, labeled "White Oak Road".

The Confederate position (defends, facing SOUTH):
- A line of solid rust-red unit blocks running EAST–WEST along the White Oak Road, facing south (down). At its WESTERN (left) end, bend the last stretch of the line BACK at a right angle so it faces WEST — draw those blocks turned to face left. Label this bent-back angle "The Return".
- Place "Pickett" as a label over the rust-red line near the center.

No Union arrows yet — this map shows only the dug-in line and its weak western hinge.

Four labels only: Five Forks, White Oak Road, The Return, Pickett. Nothing else.

## Map five-forks-assault

About 4:15 p.m., April 1, 1865 — Sheridan throws everything forward at once: Warren's V Corps wheels into the bent-back return while the cavalry pins the front. The line breaks. A tactical view of the assault.

Terrain:
- Soft sage-green wooded blocks framing the field, open pale-gold ground in the center.
- A small gray crossroads marker at the CENTER, labeled "Five Forks".
- A thin gray east–west road across the center, labeled "White Oak Road".

The Confederate position (defends): a line of solid rust-red unit blocks along the White Oak Road facing SOUTH, with its WESTERN (left) end bent back at a right angle facing WEST, labeled "The Return".

The Union assault (strikes the front and the bent-back western flank at once):
- One bold blue tapered arrow driving in from the WEST/southwest straight into the bent-back return, labeled "Warren", with a second bold blue arrow just south of it wheeling LEFT (east) into the same angle, labeled "Ayres".
- One bold blue tapered arrow pressing NORTH against the front of the line from the south, labeled "Merritt", to show the cavalry pinning the front.
- A short bold blue arrow on the FAR western flank curling around the open end, labeled "Custer".

Six labels: Five Forks, White Oak Road, The Return, Warren, Ayres, Merritt. (Custer is a seventh label on the far western flank — keep all seven big and bold over open pale ground; this is the climactic map and needs them.) Seven labels: Five Forks, White Oak Road, The Return, Warren, Ayres, Merritt, Custer. Nothing else.
